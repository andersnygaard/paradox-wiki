# Don Elliott's "Illusion"

**"Illusion"** is [Don Elliott](/people/don-elliott.md)'s own unbuilt design, entirely separate from [Paradox](/paradox.md): a 3 metre micro-sailboat, rectilinear in style, resembling a Bolger "Brick" dinghy or a Puddle Duck Racer.[1] She is not to be confused with three other boats that share or nearly share her name: [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)'s similarly named "[Elusion](/matt-layden/elusion.md)" (a different boat by a different designer, sometimes misspelled "Illusion" in a 2010 mailing-list thread), the unrelated one-design keelboat class raced under the name "Illusion" at several English sailing clubs and a third, unconnected "Illusion" built and sailed by another owner.[1] Don was working on Illusion at his death in December 2008; complete plans were never published.[1]

## The design, and her lineage from "Squeezebox"

Illusion grew out of an earlier concept of Don's own, **"Squeezebox,"** first conceived as a lifeboat, and was developed through extended discussion on a separate Yahoo "Smallboats" group.[2] That correspondence survives online, though the Smallboats group itself receives only spam traffic today. Don died before the design could be completed, but an open web directory of his own illustrations, most of his Illusion work among them, remains publicly accessible, needing only a builder to pick the design up and carry it forward.[2]

## Design intent: a protected-water boat

Reading Don's own account of the conditions Illusion was designed for, one correspondent called her "an amazing design" and asked whether she was meant for protected water only, and whether a pointier bow could be substituted without undermining the underlying idea.[3] The answer held that keeping her under 8 ft would cost too much displacement, and that the workable compromise for a boat this small is a hull pointed on the waterline and flat above it, in the manner of Sand Flea or John Welsford's Sherpa: a boat of this kind is not something to take far from land regardless, since Illusion herself is designed primarily for exploring where the water and land meet.[4]

## Two surviving drawings: a removable topper, and adjustable ballast

Two mechanical details survive in Don's own drawings. One shows a removable "topper" canopy (drawing `DES8.jpg`): a rigid shelter sealed to the hull with rubber-type compressible gasketing, snugged down with nylon wing nuts and fitted with a roll-back canvas enclosure carrying vinyl windows for viewing forward and upward while sailing inside. Left off entirely for day-sailing in good weather, the topper was read as adaptable to almost any hull, car-topped bare and fitted only at the launch site, with a folding dolly to reach the water and a small van the best vehicle for carrying it all.[5] A further drawing or two seemed to show **adjustable ballast on the sides**, noticed in the course of a wider discussion about how to sail a light, boxy sail-inside hull flat at all without the option of shifting crew weight to windward as dynamic ballast.[6]

## Reception, and an unfinished legacy

The same discussion produced an independent echo of Don's own thinking: Neil Barratt separately suggested a cabin-aft, flush-deck-forward layout, already aware Don had worked out something along these lines.[7] Once the surviving drawings themselves were shared, Neil found them engrossing enough to burn a meal he was cooking at the same time, lamented that most of the hundreds, perhaps thousands, of illustrations Don produced in his career were never saved and are now lost, and judged the surviving record, combined with what was already known about PDRacers, probably enough on its own to build an interesting small boat.[8] A further correspondent later asked whether Wanda Elliott, already patiently fulfilling Building Manual orders, might also be willing to furnish some of the wider body of Don's drawings and designs beyond Illusion alone, reasoning that the effort behind them deserved to have its legacy preserved.[9]

Complete plans for Illusion were never published.[1]
