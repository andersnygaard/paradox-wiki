# Whaleback (Paul V.)

**"Whaleback"** is a trailerable sharpie designed, built and sailed by her own designer, **[Paul V.](/people/paul-v.md)**, an American builder who also built a [Paradox](/paradox.md) to the plans in the same years. First proposed in February 2002 as a dory-hulled alternative to Paradox's own chine-keel hull, she resurfaced in 2006 as a stitch-and-glue sharpie fitted with chine runners directly inspired by [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)'s boats, and was confirmed under construction that August by an independent, third-party account. Design pages for the boat were kept at `cruisenews.net/whaleback`.

## A dory hull chosen over Paradox's own chine-keel, 2002

Paul proposed Whaleback as a trailerable Great Lakes cruiser. He considered Paradox's own chine-keel hull and set it aside, worried it could trip the boat in steep breaking waves; self-righting came first among his priorities for the new design, then full flotation, following [Bill Serjeant](/people/bill-serjeant.md)'s advice on hull form, and he chose a dory hull instead. He raised Sven Yrvind's "Bris" hull shape for comparison at the same stage, and had a chine-height model under construction to test the shape he had settled on.[1]

## A chine-runner sharpie, and construction confirmed, 2006

By July 2006 the design had picked up chine runners, inspired directly by Matt Layden's own boats: a stitch-and-glue sharpie of Paul's own design, built after trying out ideas of his own rather than following anyone else's plans. His advice to other builders weighing the same choice was to follow the plans for a boat known to work well, and to design one's own only once a few boats' worth of experience stood behind it.[2] By late August he had proposed an open-topped outboard-bracket box for the transom, sized to hold a 2-gallon fuel jug, with the outboard clamped to its aft edge and its controls extended forward to the cockpit; see [auxiliary outboard power](/operations/auxiliary-outboard-power.md#a-transom-mounted-petrol-outboard-fuel-stowage-and-trim).[3] The next day he named her "Whaleback" outright, in a message noting how closely her own building problems mirrored Paradox's, and asked to discuss the build here too, since a similar post elsewhere had drawn no interest.[4]

## Design questions answered, and a boundary kept

[Chris Curtis](/people/chris-curtis.md), then working on his own enlarged Paradox, asked why Whaleback's sides ran flatter than Paradox's own and why Paul had not simply built a larger Paradox instead; Paul used the exchange to compare the two designs directly.[5] John Pertalion offered design commentary of his own, a slightly wider beam, fuller ends and a wider transom to help her stand up to her rig, then wrote back a day later to apologize, having only just realized Paul was already well into building the boat: "nice progress."[6][7] Rather than continue the design comparison on the Paradox-specific correspondence, Paul redirected both men to a separate correspondence about small-boat design generally, judging that questions about construction method belonged here but that design changes to Paradox herself belonged there.[8]
