# Similar boat designs

Boats and designs that are **not the Paradox** but are discussed alongside it — separate microcruisers and small cruisers by other designers, from-scratch derivatives, and cross-design comparisons. This branch is kept apart from the Paradox-specific tree so the core stays focused on the boat itself; content here is about *other* designs, weighed against or inspired by Paradox.

# Concepts

* [KSDuo](ksduo.md) - The KSDuo is a from-scratch 17 ft two-person microcruiser design expanding a solo "KS2" hull and Paradox alike — strip-built in Western Red Cedar with epoxy/glass laminate, a raised pop-top cabin, formed toed-in bilge keels for a very shallow, dry-out draft, and a carbon-sparred gaff yawl rig.
* ["Hogfish Maximus"](hogfish-maximus.md) - Hogfish Maximus is a roughly 38 ft flat-bottomed cruising sharpie built by Chris Morejohn, described in the archive as "basically a 38 foot version of Paradox," which crossed the Atlantic in 2004 with its Paradox-style chine runners proven at ocean scale; its designer holds it a separate "Modified Sharpie" design, not a scaled-up Paradox.
* [Chris Morejohn's 16′ minimal cruiser](morejohn-16-minimal-cruiser.md) - A 16 ft minimal-cruiser design concept sketched by Chris Morejohn on his Hogfish Design blog in 2016 — his idea of the smallest boat that works well at sea — that keeps Paradox's spirit but adds a ballasted daggerboard, an exterior cockpit, a lowerable mast, and a main-plus-mizzen rig for heaving-to on a sea anchor.
* ["MULLUSK 16"](morejohn-mullusk-16.md) - MULLUSK 16 is a small-boat design concept Chris Morejohn posted on his Hogfish Design blog in 2017 — his own take on a Paradox, "a bit longer," keeping Matt Layden's roller-furling lug sail but adding a ballasted keel, a lowerable mast, and deck seating for good weather.
* [Paradox compared to other small-boat designs](comparisons.md) - How Paradox has been weighed against other designers' small cruisers — Jim Michalak's AF3, Scram Pram, Robbsboat, and Frolic2, Phil Bolger's Black Skimmer, Martha Jane, and Cormorant, John Welsford's Swaggie and Fafnir, and a speculative Goat Island Skiff hybrid — on stability, seaworthiness, interior room, draft, and build cost.
* [Alternatives for a larger sailor](alternatives-for-a-larger-sailor.md) - Designs other than stock Paradox considered by sailors who do not fit her — Jim Michalak's Caroline, the from-scratch Paramour redesign, and Bolger's Micro/Birdwatcher and bateau.com's "Adelie" — and the case for choosing a different boat rather than modifying this one, plus a counterpoint that Paradox may already be too seaworthy for two-up sailing.
* [Paramour](paramour-design.md) - Paramour is a 16 ft stitch-and-glue microcruiser designed from scratch in the UK, sharing Paradox's rig and rudder but with a flatter hull run computed, via the PLYBOATS design program, to be lighter and slightly faster than a same-length Paradox.
* [Gorfnik (a Puddle Duck Racer "Paraduck")](gorfnik-paraduck.md) - A home-built "Paraduck" — a Puddle Duck Racer hull fitted with a fully enclosed, sitting-headroom cabin resembling a smaller Paradox — a crossover between the two designs rather than a Paradox proper, built by a Paradox owner and about a seventh of a Paradox's weight.
* [Don Elliott's "Illusion"](don-elliotts-illusion.md) - Don Elliott's own unbuilt design, a 3 metre rectilinear micro-sailboat conceived alongside his Paradox work but never completed, distinct from three other boats that share or nearly share its name.
* [Lucky Town 7](lucky-town-7.md) - A 14 ft trailerable microcruiser designed, built and sailed by "A Speck on the Sea" author William H. Longyard over roughly ten years, sharing Paradox's managed-from-below layout but substituting a lifting keel and a polytarp junk rig for chine runners and a lug sail.
* [Whaleback (Paul V.)](whaleback.md) - A dory-hulled, chine-runner sharpie designed, built and sailed by Paradox correspondent and moderator Paul V., proposed in 2002 as a self-righting alternative to Paradox's own chine-keel hull and confirmed under construction by 2006.
