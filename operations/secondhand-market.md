# Secondhand Paradox market

A secondhand Paradox is a rare find. Owners have consistently reported, across more than a decade of the mailing list's history, that built Paradoxes seldom come up for resale at all, and prospective buyers who go looking for one — in the United States or the United Kingdom alike — routinely come away empty-handed. Asked to sell or trade a Paradox outright, an owner's usual answer is no: a boat built by hand tends to be kept for the long haul, or passed quietly to another builder or list member, rather than advertised on the open market. The practical consequence, repeated to newcomer after newcomer who asks about buying rather than building, is consistent: anyone who wants a Paradox should expect to build one rather than buy one used.

## Owners rarely sell

Only one sale of a built Paradox was ever recalled when a newcomer asked, in 2005, whether a used boat might be available.[1] A UK owner who went looking, in 2012, for a second boat "similar to" his own Paradox found none at all for sale in the country and bought a conventional boat instead.[2] Demand for a ready-built boat has also come from the opposite direction: at least one prospective builder, already committed to building his own, separately wanted to buy a second, already-built Paradox — and never found one.[3] At least one owner asked directly whether he would sell or trade his finished Paradox declined outright, preferring to keep her rather than part with a boat already suited to his own use.[4]

When a Paradox has changed hands, it has typically been through direct, informal contact between list members rather than public advertising. The earliest recorded sale, in 2001, went to a member who spent several weeks trying to reach the seller through the mailing list before a deal was struck.[5][6][7] Before committing to restore the boat, he checked her wood for hidden rot using nothing more than a simple ice-pick probe — a still-useful way to judge any secondhand hull's soundness beyond her cosmetic condition (see [checking a used Paradox for rot](/construction/rot-inspection-ice-pick.md)).[8] [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)'s older, related design [GJAC](/matt-layden/gjac.md) — a sibling design rather than a Paradox hull herself — surfaced for resale through the same kind of informal, list-based contact around the same period; no record survives of her actually selling.[9] When an unfinished hull's builder could no longer continue, other list members have sometimes stepped in to help find a buyer informally — for instance by adding a stalled builder's contact details to a UK builders' registry — though such help has not always led to a completed sale (see "RHXII," below).

## A want-to-buy inquiry from Texas

A newcomer asked, in December 2005, whether a finished or in-progress Paradox — or a similar boat — might be for sale, and how to order study plans. The reply was direct: Paradox owners rarely part with their boats, with only one sale ever heard of, and pointed instead to [study plans](/references/study-plans.md) at microcruising.com and to the full [building plans](/references/building-plans.md) as the realistic way in.[1] Asked where he was located, the correspondent answered Texas.[10]

## Build vs. buy secondhand: a UK newcomer's cost-benefit hesitation

A recurring question behind every listing on this page is really its reverse: is building a Paradox from scratch even worth it, against simply buying something ready-made? One UK correspondent weighing exactly that tradeoff gave the archive's fullest first-person statement of the hesitation. He was drawn to the Paradox specifically because she can be sailed entirely from inside her cabin, avoiding sitting exposed to wet and cold in an open cockpit — but no secondhand boat on the conventional market answered to that preference, since none of them can be sailed from inside. Buying secondhand, in other words, would still mean accepting an open-cockpit "open air life," regardless of which boat was chosen. Against that, a from-scratch Paradox build represents a substantial commitment of time and money, with an uncertain resale market by the time it eventually comes to be sold.[11]

The same exchange also touched on why an unconventional, high-performing small-boat design like Paradox is hard to sell either new or used — a question of marketing and boat-buying culture more than of actual sailing performance, addressed at length in [chine runners and windward performance](/chine-runners/windward-performance.md#a-recurring-skeptics-question-does-british-water-sail-differently) — and drew a separate build-cost comparison against a bigger boat, covered on [Faith](/boats/faith.md#build-cost-comparison-and-a-hull-assembly-milestone) and in [build effort and workspace](/construction/build-effort.md).

## An unfinished hull for sale: "Swash" (July 2004)

A part-built Paradox, **"Swash,"** was offered for sale in 2004. The hull was largely complete — foam installed, ready to be skinned in plywood, her yuloh finished and her rudder started — but not yet glassed, with her chine runners still only unblocked bottom extensions. The asking price was set to recover roughly what had been spent on materials, rather than to profit from the labor already invested.[12] A third party expressed interest and asked for more photos and a completion estimate, offering a trailer and cash if a deal could be struck,[13] but the sale never went through: the builder grew increasingly reluctant to let the boat go, and ultimately finished her himself.[14] See [boats/swash.md](/boats/swash.md) for her fuller build and ownership history.

## An abandoned build for sale: "RHXII" (April 2006)

An Amsterdam builder's part-built hull, known to the list only by her working name "RHXII," could not be finished when family circumstances forced him to abandon the build and move on; he offered the unfinished boat for sale.[15] Other list members offered to help find a buyer by adding his contact details to a UK builders' registry.[16] No sale or further progress is recorded in the archive after that point — a 2017 review of the group's own registry reached the same conclusion, finding no confirmation the listing had ever been completed and prompting a fellow member to consider removing the stale entry.[17]

## A further want-to-buy inquiry (February 2009)

A newcomer's first post, under the subject "NOLI," expressed simple admiration for [Jeff UK's boat](/boats/noli.md) — she was "so easy on the eyes" that he wanted to "downsize" to something like her — and four days later he asked outright whether a Paradox was available for sale.[18][19] Neither post drew a reply on record, consistent with the pattern above.

## An unfinished hull for sale: "Telestai" (March 2011)

A part-built Paradox under construction in Kansas City, Missouri since 2004, later recorded in an independent census as **"Telestai,"** was offered for sale unfinished in March 2011 by her builder, [Steve (Kansas City)](/people/steve-kc.md), rather than completed by him. The lot included beams laminated in Douglas fir (all but one still untrimmed), a laminated Douglas fir mast wired for a masthead light but not yet faired, both yards faired, several larger pieces already cut from marine plywood, silicon bronze nails, a halyard sheave and axle, a rudder-stock post fitting, Douglas fir framing stock, the plans, Don Elliott's instruction booklet, and about 100 lb of lead ballast cast from wheel weights. The marine plywood, without a Lloyds of London pedigree, came from a Kansas City specialty lumberyard, Shutte Lumber. The asking price was $500 for the whole lot, with delivery offered along a periodic Kansas City to East Texas driving route.[20]

## The "Minnow" campaign: an eBay listing, a tiered offer structure, and a full inventory (2014)

The fullest single secondhand-Paradox sale on record involved an already cruising-equipped boat, listed on eBay in the UK. The listing itemized her full inventory — sail, rudder, running rigging led into the cabin, navigation instruments, solar power, ground tackle, an auxiliary outboard, a yuloh, and a road trailer — and structured the sale in tiers, with gear removable from the package to suit a buyer's budget.[21] The listing drew genuine interest over several months, including prospective buyers who travelled a considerable distance to view her in person, before she finally sold.[22][23] One visit fell through for a reason unrelated to price or condition: a disabled prospective buyer found he could not safely get in and out of the forecabin from a dinghy given his disability.[24]

## Valuing a used Paradox

Asked directly what a used Paradox is worth, the consistent answer on the list has been to weigh her against what it would cost to have an equivalent boat built professionally from scratch — adjusted for her actual condition and the quality of her workmanship, and for the fact that Paradox, never having been a production boat, is rare to begin with. The final word, though, is the oldest rule of any secondhand market: a boat is worth whatever a buyer is willing to pay, or an owner is willing to accept.[25] In the one fully documented case of a complete, cruising-equipped Paradox reaching the open market (the "Minnow" sale above), the asking price for the whole package — boat, trailer, and gear together — worked out to well under half of what building an equivalent boat from scratch would cost over a comparable span of time, even before counting the builder's own labor at all.[26]

Buying a used Paradox from overseas adds a further, often decisive complication: importing a boat internationally brings freight, customs, duty, and handling costs on top of the purchase price itself — expensive and involved enough that at least one interested overseas buyer chose to build a Paradox derivative from scratch rather than import a used one,[27][28] and a later would-be importer found that shipping a part-built hull across the Atlantic added roughly as much again to the purchase price.[29]

## A 2010 want-to-buy inquiry, and a renewed trade offer

An 84-year-old Massachusetts sailor wanting to downgrade from a Peep Hen asked, in August 2010, whether anyone knew of a Paradox for sale.[30] The same owner already on record above declining a Peep-Hen trade in 2007 answered by renewing that same offer directly — trading his own Paradox for a good used Peep Hen, on the same terms as before; see [Whisper — a 2010 renewal of the trade offer](/boats/whisper.md#a-2010-renewal-of-the-trade-offer) for the fuller record.[31] The only other reply judged it hard to say whether the trade would be a step up or down, wishing both parties luck; no completed trade, and no other Paradox offered, survives on record.[32]

## TARDIS priced at $3,000, and a fuller valuation framework (January 2013)
The valuation framework above was restated on the list itself in January 2013, prompted directly by the seller of a nearly finished, unlaunched Paradox asking outright what a fair price would be, having listed the boat at $3,000; full text and citation also at [people/lezlie-henson.md](/people/lezlie-henson.md#citations) and [boats/scout.md](/boats/scout.md#citations).[33] One reply held that a used or homebuilt boat is worth only what a buyer is willing to pay — builders rarely recover their true investment of time or materials, since such projects are undertaken as a labor of love — but that a boat's own reputation and track record add a premium, and recalled two recent Paradox sales, one in Texas and one in Washington, both in the $2,000–3,000 range.[34] A fuller framework followed: price ultimately sits at the margin of what a buyer will pay, but three concrete factors bear on it. Comparable recent sales matched the same $2,000–3,000 range, against a notably higher price point recalled from a few years earlier. Replacement cost pointed the same way — a 15 ft Enigma, a comparable Matt Layden minicruiser, had cost $3,000–4,000 in materials alone when built a few years before, and a Paradox, carrying more material, would cost more still before labor was even counted. Supply and demand cut both ways: real interest in the unique design, but few with the time, skill, or budget for a professional build. On that basis, the $3,000 asking price looked barely enough to cover materials for a new, well-finished, unlaunched hull with a trailer already included, and could reasonably have been set higher.[35]

## A further cost comparison: a UK import estimate and a real build-cost account (January 2013)

The same "TARDIS for sale" thread that produced the $3,000 asking price above also drew a fuller cost comparison over the following weeks. A UK correspondent, drawing on his own blog research, restated a total of roughly **£4,000** to import the boat from Texas to Britain, including the boat and trailer together, and judged she "couldn't be built for less in the UK" — the same import breakdown recorded in full at [Scout — an unfinished "Tardis," and a UK import cost estimate](/boats/scout.md#an-unfinished-tardis-and-a-uk-import-cost-estimate-january-2013).[36] A separate reply gave a real, itemized total from having built a Paradox from scratch — about $12,000 AUD all in, more than most might guess even before counting labor — recorded in full at [Small Fry — a full build-cost account, and a buy-vs-build comparison](/boats/small-fry.md#a-full-build-cost-account-and-a-buy-vs-build-comparison-2013).[37] Weighing the two, one prospective builder concluded the cost would not deter him either way, since it would be spread across the two or three years a build takes rather than paid all at once — and buying a Paradox complete, to him, would almost feel like "cheating."[38]

## A "12-foot Paradox" listed in Ottawa, and a naming question (February 2013)

A boat advertised as a "12-foot Paradox" was listed for sale in Ottawa, Canada, in early February 2013, at what one correspondent, simply forwarding the link, judged "kind of high" an asking price.[39] The listing drew a skeptical question rather than interest: a stock Paradox measures close to twenty feet, so a genuine twelve-footer would have to be either a mislabelled Enigma or a Paradox built to plans deliberately shortened — and, if the latter, why a builder would want the smaller boat was not obvious. No answer is on record.[40]

## A Canadian owner's Paradox offered for sale (December 2014)

A finished Paradox was offered for sale in Canada in December 2014, once a different boat arrived at the property and made her surplus. The listing included a trailer with new wiring and submersible lights and new bearings and tires; the boat itself was kept tarped over through the winter to keep the snow out.[41] Interest came within hours from as far off as central New York state, and from two further prospective buyers elsewhere in North America;[42][43][44] by the next afternoon the boat had already sold, going to a buyer in Quebec, leaving at least one interested party with regret at having missed her.[45][46] A further inquiry arrived three weeks later, evidently unaware the sale had already closed.[47]

## A renewed want-to-buy inquiry, and a scarcity observation (January 2016)

Just over a year after his own missed inquiry above, the same central New York correspondent, "Deric," resurfaced when a new owner reported buying "[Raffles](/boats/raffles.md)" secondhand from Folsom, California — recalling the boat had been listed online at an asking price of about **$7,000**, and observing that few Paradoxes turn up for sale at all, especially on the **East Coast of the US**, where he was himself still looking to buy one.[48]

The same week he went further back through the archive itself for a lead, replying directly to a three-year-old listing from Michael Beebe's own first Paradox to ask for a current phone number — a sign of just how thin the market really is, though unanswered on record.[49]

## Don Elliott's own Paradox offered for sale (2017)

One of the earliest-built Paradoxes, [Don Elliott](/people/don-elliott.md)'s "[The Edge](/boats/edge.md)," was offered for sale by his family in 2017 — overall sound shape, needing only minor toe-rail and rudder attention, priced at **$2,800 OBO** with a trailer and some cruising gear included. She sold within about two weeks, to a new owner unnamed in the archive. Full record at [The Edge — offered for sale (2017)](/boats/edge.md#offered-for-sale-2017).[50][51]

## "Small Fry" offered for sale in Western Australia (September 2018)

Geoff Davis offered "[Small Fry](/boats/small-fry.md)" for sale on the mailing list in September 2018, drawing interest from as far away as Brisbane. Answering the interest, he gave a fuller construction and electrical-system account than recorded elsewhere in the archive and set an asking price of **$6,000 AUD, negotiable** — roughly half what materials, a trailer and a sail alone would cost new. Full record at [Small Fry — the 2018 sale listing](/boats/small-fry.md#the-2018-sale-listing-construction-electrical-system-and-price).[52][53]

## A California listing, price-reduced, then sold (2020)

A Paradox was listed for sale in **Eureka, California**, from August 2020, asking **$5,950**, reduced to **$3,950** by late October, and sold by mid-December — equipped with a 2012 trailer, an electric outboard and battery, a depth sounder, a VHF radio, and an anchor roller. The notice reached the mailing list secondhand, forwarded with "no affiliation" and no builder or prior owner named, so this hull cannot be matched to any other Paradox on this record.[54]

## A build-or-buy inquiry, and Thor offered for sale in South Australia (September 2021)

A newcomer weighing whether to build or buy asked the list in September 2021 for a current estimate of materials cost and build time to weigh against building: he had bought Don Elliott's Builder's Manual, which put the average build at four months and its own wood cost, twenty years earlier, at $200, and the seller of a boat he had found locally guessed materials might now run to $2,000.[55]

A fellow owner answered the underlying question directly from long experience with both build and buy: a secondhand Paradox is genuinely hard to find, "one comes up every couple of years," and even for a builder with several boats already behind him, a Paradox from scratch would still take at least six months — his own similarly-scoped Enigma had just taken 4 1/2 months at 4-5 hours a day and cost over $3,000 in materials alone, without a trailer. His verdict: "Buying is by far the best option, at any price, if you can get your hands on one." Full account, including his own cross-continent purchase: [people/andre-francois-bourbeau.md](/people/andre-francois-bourbeau.md#question-an-enigma-ketch-of-his-own-design).[56]

Nick Bell answered with his own figures for "[Thor](/boats/thor.md)" — wood and resin A$2,000, a professionally made sail A$1,000, the jet-ski trailer A$900, and a Honda 2.3 hp outboard A$1,200[57] — and, in the same breath, offered her for sale in South Australia, describing her as built to plan and "very sturdy," with Oregon chine logs and runners and a marine-plywood hull under a heavy fibreglass layup.[58] Told the distance from Phoenix, Arizona ruled out buying her, and asked whether the prices were in Australian dollars, when she was built, what the materials really cost, how long the build took, and whether the trailer was custom-built,[59] Bell gave a fuller answer the next day: an all-up cost of A$7,000, a build six years earlier that took eight months (helped by being retired), all marine plywood and top-quality products throughout, and a birdsmouth mast made deliberately light "for my ageing old muscles to lift easily"; he restated that she is shy to windward in rough water and rights herself readily, "rather suddenly too," even from a full knockdown, and closed with "Stick to the plan. You'll love it."[60]

A separate reply warned that quality timber prices might already have doubled from what earlier builders had paid, owing to a supply-and-demand imbalance, and that a well-built secondhand Paradox in rated marine plywood and epoxy was likely to come out cheaper than building one over the following year or two — advising against lesser materials regardless of price, and suggesting the design itself, "way overbuilt" by ordinary scantling standards, might be due for a lighter redesign now that wood had grown so expensive.[61]

The listing drew a further inquiry the following week from a Tasmania-based correspondent,[62] which prompted Bell's fullest account of the boat's condition, construction, and price yet — full detail at [boats/thor.md — sold across the country, and renamed "Pamela B"](/boats/thor.md#sold-across-the-country-and-renamed-pamela-b-2022).[63]

Full account of Thor's own build and sale: [boats/thor.md — materials cost](/boats/thor.md#materials-cost-and-a-modified-jet-ski-trailer) and [boats/thor.md — sold across the country, and renamed "Pamela B"](/boats/thor.md#sold-across-the-country-and-renamed-pamela-b-2022).
