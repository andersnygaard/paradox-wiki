# Clew

The clew is the aft lower corner of the Paradox's sail, along with its associated fittings.

## Trim: sheeting the clew

Working to windward, the sail is sheeted from the clew as if the boat were junk-rigged rather than sheeted flat and pinched: the boom is kept over the leeward quarter and the luff kept just taut, with the boat sailed free rather than pointed too high, since pointing too high stalls the sail while sheeting in too tightly stops the wind flowing cleanly off the leech — the luff should sit almost on the point of shaking rather than be hauled hard in.[1] This matches the separate finding, from a different owner, that the sail performs best close-hauled when treated the same way, with the wind meeting the cloth at only two or three degrees of incidence rather than sheeted hard in (see [sailing characteristics — a new owner's lee-helm and immersion questions](/operations/sailing-characteristics.md#a-new-owners-lee-helm-and-immersion-questions)).
