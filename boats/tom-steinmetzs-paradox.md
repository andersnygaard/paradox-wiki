# Tom Steinmetz's Paradox

**Tom Steinmetz** built this Paradox to Matt Layden's plans and later converted her, removing her chine runners and fitting a Bermuda rig and a leeboard in their place, a conversion the archive documents from a real, sailing boat and nowhere else.[1][2] No message of Steinmetz's own survives on this record; everything known about the boat comes from other builders studying a photograph, and later a video, of a hull none of them had sailed.[1][3][2] The archive knows her only as "Tom's Paradox," the description a correspondent gave her the first time she surfaced, in August 2015; her builder was not named until nearly two years later, and no later record of her survives.[1][2]

## Sighted first as a photograph, missing her chine runners

A correspondent shared a photograph in August 2015 asking whether the boat in it, described only as "Tom's Paradox," still counted as a Paradox for the community's registry; she carried a triangular sail and appeared to be missing her chine runners entirely.[1] Another correspondent studying the same photograph corrected the sail's identification from Marconi to Bermudan, the term Marconi properly describing how a mast is stayed rather than a sail's shape, and noted her centre of effort seemed to have moved a long way forward; he wondered, without ever getting an answer, whether this had left her with lee helm.[4] The correspondent who first shared the photograph confirmed there seemed to be no chine runners on her at all.[5] A third correspondent, working through the consequences, wondered how leeway would be controlled on a hull built this way, a leeboard perhaps, or a centreboard, since neither showed in the picture.[6]

## Converted, one way only

The question was answered eight months later, when the same correspondent who had first shared the photograph reported having researched the boat's history: her chine runners had been removed first, and a leeboard fitted afterward, a genuine conversion rather than a hypothetical one, and, once made, irreversible, "there is no way back."[7] Revisiting the photograph the following day, a further correspondent noted her rig showed no shrouds at all, and wondered whether that made it difficult to hold luff tension in the foresail.[3] A further correspondent answered the observation on the engineering: converting to a Bermuda rig ordinarily needs shrouds and stays fitted, short of something like an aerorig; fitting leeboards, by contrast, does not itself require removing the chine runners at all, only battens added to give the board clearance to bear against.[8]

## Her builder named, two years on

The same photographed boat resurfaced fourteen months later again, this time in her own right rather than as an illustration for someone else's argument, in a discussion about a possible Bermuda-rig conversion for a different Paradox entirely. Her builder was named for the first time, **Tom Steinmetz**, with a video of her under sail linked, though the photographs once shared online of her had by then gone down.[2] Recalling what he had once learnt of her history, the correspondent believed the chine runners had been cut off specifically to reduce lee helm, framing it as something he recalled rather than something he had confirmed himself; he also recalled having seen a further video showing her leeboard, but could not relocate it.[2] Nobody who built, owned or sailed her ever confirmed the reason on this record, and no later account of her survives.
