# David Nemo's Paradox

**David Nemo's Paradox** is an unnamed **28 ft by 8 ft live-aboard**, built by [David Nemo](/people/david-nemo.md) in the United Kingdom by translating every measurement on Matt Layden's stock plans to double scale, against the stock 13 ft 10 in length and roughly 4 ft beam.[1] She keeps the stock skeg variation, with a small open cockpit added aft, the one departure visible from outside and the detail by which she was later recognised on the water as a scaled-up Paradox.[1][2] By January 2009 her hull, decks and sides were already built and glassed. She was complete and sailing by December 2009, having crossed the English Channel and set off, under electric power, via France's Canal du Midi for the Mediterranean.[2] The general design question a double-scale build like hers raises, whether Paradox's qualities survive such enlargement at all, is treated at [design/larger-paradox.md](/design/larger-paradox.md#a-far-larger-unsanctioned-build-doubling-every-stock-dimension-for-a-live-aboard).

## The build

The hull, sides and decks were largely built and glassed by January 2009. Divinycell foam core replaced plywood for the sides and decks; the bottom stayed plywood but carries a thin bonded Divinycell inner lining for insulation and extra flotation. Framing is reclaimed hardwood salvaged from a church, joined with peg-and-hole, mortise-and-tenon and dovetail joints, then double-bonded with fibreglass and resin. The chine runners are hand-carved from two solid pieces of oak, hollowed and filled with cast lead ballast before being glassed into the hull, combining ballast and lateral plane in one member.[1]

## Departures from the plans

She follows the stock plans' skeg variation, with a small open cockpit added aft, the one departure visible from outside and the detail by which she was later recognised on the water as a scaled-up Paradox.[1][2] Beyond the skeg and cockpit, every dimension on the stock plans was translated to double scale rather than redrawn, with no scale-up document consulted and no sanction sought from the designer. Construction continued through the winter of 2008-09.[1][3]

## Cost

Some £8,000 had gone into materials and tools by January 2009, with most of the hull, sides and decks already built and glassed.[1]

## Sailing record

By December 2009 the boat had crossed the English Channel and was under way, slowly, on electric power, headed via France's Canal du Midi for the Mediterranean. A fellow sailor who crossed the Channel aboard her that month judged the completed hull "a real miracle" of a build, recognisable at a glance as a scaled-up Paradox with a rear cockpit added.[2]
