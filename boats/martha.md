# Martha

**"Martha"** is [Christian Skjutare](/people/christian-skjutare.md)'s [Paradox](/paradox.md), announced to the list in August 2013 as his first solo boatbuild, undertaken after a 13-year break from sailing.[1] Built in Sweden, she was expected from the outset to raise a run of language-barrier questions rather than construction ones as such, an American-drafted plan set read by a non-native English speaker.[1]

## A metric-versus-imperial plywood mismatch

With the plans in hand, the first real hurdle turned out to be material thickness rather than technique: standard Swedish plywood stock runs about 1mm over the US thicknesses the plans specify, a mismatch already met and worked around on a prior kayak build by simply using the thicker sheet.[2] The alternatives — a special order in the exact spec thickness, or planing 1mm off a standard sheet by hand, already tried once on a small model kayak and found miserable — were both judged worse than accepting the extra millimetre and the resulting slight weight gain.[2] An outside data point from an experienced small-boat builder, uninvolved with Paradox specifically, confirmed the extra thickness would make the hull stronger and only a little heavier, with planing standard stock down to spec judged not worth the cost and time of the machinery it would take.[3] Published figures from an outside plywood retailer put the weight penalty for exceeding spec by a millimetre at only about 2 lb a sheet.[4] The decision settled on the thicker stock throughout, particularly since it was the one thickness reliably stocked locally for the side panels — the dimension of most concern going in.[5]

## A second Swedish builder makes contact

A second prospective Swedish builder, based in **Gothenburg** and about to order the building manual and plans, wrote in — answering, in effect, Christian's own opening question about whether any other Swedish Paradox builders were on the list.[7] Christian gave his own location as **Hörby**, in central **Skåne**, welcoming the news that more Swedes were taking up the design.[8]

## A third Swedish builder makes contact, and a mast-first build-order question

A third prospective Swedish builder made contact in August 2014, introducing himself first in Swedish and then, an hour later, in English for the wider list: living outside Växtorp, on the border between Halland and Skåne, he had bought the Paradox plans and building manual but not yet started, having just finished his first boat — an outrigger sailing canoe, a Gary Dierking-designed Wa'apa — the previous summer, sailing it that season with a Paradox build a possibility further down the road.[9][11] Christian reported his own build, by then a year further along, still going entirely to plan but at a slow pace: roughly 80-90% of the work remained, though enough had taken shape by then to see how big she would become, and he offered to send photographs once he could motivate himself off the sofa.[10] The newcomer expected his own build to run at a similarly low intensity and planned to start with the mast; Christian, at the same slow pace himself, asked in return why start with the mast rather than the hull, and whether the newcomer already had the building manual.[12][13]

Answering the next day, the newcomer confirmed the manual was already in hand, and explained his reasoning: another forum member had once started with the mast, a choice he traced to enthusiasm — seeing something appealing tends to spur him into action, as had happened with his previous build, leaving little room afterward for anything else fun. Wanting to avoid repeating that, and to take his own build at a genuinely relaxed pace this time, he judged starting with the **mast and rudder** — smaller, self-contained pieces — the better fit.[14]

## Bending oversize plywood and chine logs

Thicker plywood of this kind is more prone to resist bending on the bottom and deck panels, where the plans' curve is already a demanding one even for standard stock. The fuller technique for working around it — laminating two thinner sheets rather than forcing a single over-thick panel, and a hot-weather, pre-soaked bending method for the chine logs — lives at [chine log vs. taped-seam construction — a 2013 recurrence](/construction/chine-log-vs-taped-seam.md#a-2013-recurrence-laminating-over-thickness-plywood-and-a-hot-weather-bending-technique).[6]
