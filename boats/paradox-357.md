# Paradox 357

**Paradox 357** is **[OkieBob](/people/okiebob.md)'s** own Paradox, built in **Oklahoma City** from **July 2010**. She takes her name from her own arithmetic: built at **85 percent** of the stock 420 cm hull, she measures almost exactly 357 cm. That settled figure came after an earlier, unreconciled one: a single 2010 statement put the scale at 90 percent, a figure never repeated once the 85 percent measurement was confirmed in 2015 and restated again in 2016. The build's own record on this list runs from 2010 to January 2016, when its builder moved the project to a different group.

## The build, 2010-2015

OkieBob cut wood for the hull for the first time in July 2010, starting with the hull-bottom scarph joint. Unfamiliar with a block plane, he tried a series of backsaw kerfs instead and abandoned the method after the first kerf caught the plane on every pass.[1] The joint was clamped on a plastic-sheeted garage floor, butted against a wall and held with bolts and washers rather than nails, then weighted, refined into using the hull's own ballast lead as the weight instead.[3] It was glued with resorcinol rather than epoxy, ordered from CP Adhesives; the fuller trial and the comparison against epoxy that followed over the next several months is told in full at [construction/fastening-and-gluing.md](/construction/fastening-and-gluing.md#a-resorcinol-alternative-to-epoxy).[2] A public build blog followed at the end of that month, its own record of the build kept separately from this archive.[4]

Five years into the build, in August 2015, the side panels were dry-fitted to the bulkheads with a neighbour's help and trued using the diagonal-comparison method described in full at [construction/hull-assembly-sequence.md](/construction/hull-assembly-sequence.md#truing-the-hull-the-diagonal-comparison-method).[6] A month later, drawing the bow's side panels together at the stem with a ratchet strap left them bulging outward at the middle rather than meeting in a point; the fix was zip-ties applied partway up the bow, full technique at [construction/hull-assembly-sequence.md](/construction/hull-assembly-sequence.md#pulling-a-hulls-sides-together-with-a-spanish-windlass). A fellow builder answered with an alternative stem-fitting method, cross-linked at [people/rob-kellock.md](/people/rob-kellock.md#citations) rather than repeated here.[7][8]

## The 85% scale-down

By September 2010 the scale had been settled, for the time, at 90 percent of the plan rather than the stock 100 percent, a decision made after the bottom panel had already been cut to the plans' own 3/4 in thickness rather than a proportionately thinner one; the extra material was accepted as ballast, sitting low in the hull exactly where ballast is wanted.[5]

The figure changed. The August 2015 dry-fit restated the scale as 85 percent, with bulkheads moved fore and aft from their stock positions for sleeping space; the record does not explain the change from the earlier figure.[6] The following month, still building at 3/8 in panels rather than the stock 1/2 in, bulkhead 2 was moved forward and bulkhead 4 aft, and bulkhead 1 was eliminated in favour of a temporary mold to hold the shape. A first stem cut from scrap white wood was replaced with one cut from Douglas fir, unwilling to risk a cheaper timber failing years later over a small saving. The hull measured almost exactly 357 cm, the figure behind her own "Paradox 357" name and a second confirmation of the 85 percent scale-down.[7] Prompted by that stem-fitting exchange and by other builders' own stem wear-protection techniques, cross-linked at [components/stem.md](/components/stem.md#protecting-the-stem-from-wear), the plan was to sheathe the hull in fiberglass and go a little thicker at the stem, curious after a sprayed-on truck-bed liner seen used on a different build, and expecting the boat to take damage sooner or later.[9] In January 2016 the figure was restated a third time, the arithmetic done explicitly: 85 percent of the stock 420 cm comes to 357 cm, the arithmetic behind the boat's own name.[10]

Whether the 85 percent scale-down worked is honestly thin on this record: no launch or completion of Paradox 357 is on record on this archive.

## Where the record ends

In January 2016, OkieBob explained a choice made at the outset of public build documentation: he had debated recording the build here or on the separate `enigma_sailboat` group, and kept it on this list since the hull is basically a small Paradox rather than an Enigma, reasoning it might help a future builder working through scale modifications in either direction.[10] Leaning by then toward further modifications that might distract from this group's own core purpose, he planned to move his build album, titled "Po.," to the `microcruising` group instead; that move is where this archive's own record of the build stops.[10]

In June 2018, reflecting on the years since, he described himself, in his own words, as "a one displacement boat guy" who happened to own two displacement boats, his other being an '87 Rob Roy 23, and turned his attention there instead.[11] No later account of Paradox 357 appears on this record.
