# Endeavour

**"Endeavour"** is [Greg Lockhart](/people/greg-lockhart.md)'s Paradox, plans ordered and construction begun in June 2011.[1] He had already admired the finished, teak-decked "[Odyssey](/boats/odyssey.md)" — particularly her electrical panel and decking — and named her one of the boats he intended to use as his own example.[1]

## Plans, first materials, and a name given in "Zoë"'s memory

His plans arrived within a week of ordering, credited to a fast turnaround from [Dave Bolduc](/people/bolduc.md); that same weekend was spent at the local lumber yard picking out material for deck beams and bulkheads.[2] The build began in the same week Glen Maxwell's "[Zoë](/boats/zoe.md)" was destroyed by fire in transit to her new owner — a loss "Endeavour" was named in memory of, built to [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)'s plans with "Zoë" herself held up as the standard to be compared against.[2] Progress was to be documented with a newly bought digital camera, and a build blog was expected to be running within the week.[2][3] "Endeavour" is his fourth build, following three earlier boats; a working tool list drawn from that experience, shared with a fellow prospective builder the same month, lives at [the builder's toolkit — a 2011 recurrence](/construction/builders-toolkit.md#a-2011-recurrence-a-fourth-builders-own-tool-list).[4]

## First deck beams and bulkheads cut

By late June 2011 all seven deck beams were complete and the first two bulkheads cut, awaiting their cleating.[5] The build's basic layout, cutting, and assembly stage is judged the easy part next to the sanding and glassing still ahead, with no fixed deadline and every part meant to be as close to perfection as achievable — "This is my 'Mount Everest'.....and the climb has begun..."[5]

A few days later, final assembly work on the bulkheads — cleats, deck beams, and sole risers — was finished, and the pieces sanded and shaped, with a suitable camera still being sought to document the build going forward.[6]

## Epoxy coating complete on the bulkheads and deck beams (July 2011)

By late July, all bulkheads and exposed deck beams were epoxied, sanded, faired, and painted, with a working three-dimensional hull hoped for by 1 September; a week away was expected to slow progress, and photographs remained scarce with the build budget going toward plywood, lumber, and epoxy instead.[7]

## A tabernacle mast-step proposal for bulkhead #2, and a mixed reaction

Studying the finished bulkheads laid out in their station positions, Greg floated a genuine design change rather than a stock build: on bulkhead #2 — carrying the forward "stateroom" hatch and the vent trunk — replacing the angled vent trunk and its surrounding cabinet with a square hollow column running the full height of the bulkhead, serving as a tabernacle for the mast, with sail-control lines still led in through it exactly as through the stock vent trunk, just rearranged vertically rather than at an angle; separately wanted to add two small dorade vents at the forward cabin corners for fresh air underway.[8] The idea, floated explicitly as a way to set his own boat apart from a "cookie cutter" build, drew a skeptical one-liner from a UK owner — "If it ain't broke, why fix it? It would be easy to change Paradox, a lot harder to improve it" — and a more encouraging reply from a fellow correspondent, framing any change as a straightforward gain/loss calculation and encouraging him to make the boat his own.[9][10] Alberto Callejon, whose own "[Cachivache](/boats/cachivache.md)" already carries a widened cabin, answered with a real-world reference point rather than an opinion — a photograph of Cachivache's own vent trunk, alongside the note that he too had modified the cabin to make it wider; see [Cachivache — a 2011 postscript: the cabin widened after all](/boats/cachivache.md#a-2011-postscript-the-cabin-widened-after-all) for that build's own fuller account.[11] No further word on whether the tabernacle idea was actually built is on record.

## A digital camera secured, and the assembly target moved to October (October 2011)

After a long gap without posting, Greg reported Endeavour still taking shape and a digital camera finally in hand to document her construction going forward, with that day's project photographing and posting pictures; his revised target was to have the hull assembled into three dimensions by the end of October, later than the 1 September goal set that July (see [above](#epoxy-coating-complete-on-the-bulkheads-and-deck-beams-july-2011)). He also welcomed a new list member — his own uncle, an accomplished aircraft and boat builder with several completed builds to his credit — who had taken an interest in the Paradox once Endeavour's own construction began.[12]

## A photo album, and the cabin windows and hatch style chosen (October 2011)

A ten-photograph album followed days later, documenting progress to date and, most notably, an almost-completed cabin top. **Port-hole style windows** and a **one-piece "lift out" hatch** were chosen over the stock sliding hatch — see [components/hatch.md — port-hole windows and a one-piece lift-out hatch, chosen for one 2011 build](/components/hatch.md#port-hole-windows-and-a-one-piece-lift-out-hatch-chosen-for-one-2011-build) for that choice set against the design's other hatch variants. His uncle was thanked by name for his help getting the pictures posted.[13] Asked why the continuous side window called for in the plans was traded for several separate ports, the choice was explained as a deliberate personal touch to set the boat apart — the finished oval ports, dressed with trim rings, look "salty," with visibility unaffected; the same reasoning carried over to the companionway hatch, whose own oval port keeps the boom visible from inside with the hatch closed.[20][21]

## Hull scarfing, the stem, and a first dry fit (October 2011)

Hull scarfing began in mid-October, with progress photos posted alongside a portrait of Greg standing next to the hull to gauge her size against his own.[14] Partway through, he sought help interpreting the plans' stem construction, having found the drawings unclear — see [stem — notching for chine logs and sheer clamps](/construction/stem-notching.md) for the resulting technique.[15][16][17] The scarfs were reported curing two days later,[18] and by 15 October the scarfing was complete and the hull itself cut out and faired that same morning, with bulkhead and deck-beam locations marked on the panels and a first three-dimensional "dry fit" targeted for the following Tuesday.[19]

## First three-dimensional pictures posted (October 2011)

The dry fit planned above delivered what it promised: the first three-dimensional pictures of Endeavour finally went up, three or four months later than first hoped, with more to follow in the following days.[22]

## The sheer clamp finished, the hull rolled, and both chine logs laminated from doubled runners (late October-November 2011)

Most of a day went into the top-side sheer clamp: the bulkheads were prepared to receive it and the "peanut buttering" of their joints mostly finished, leaving fiberglass tape on those joints and the sheer clamp itself as the next steps before rolling the hull to start the bottom — with all of the bottom's epoxying, taping, fiberglassing, fairing, and painting planned for one continuous stretch while she was rolled, so that righting her again would leave the exterior hull finished from the sheer clamp down.[23] The sheer clamps went in more easily than expected and proved unexpectedly stout once fitted — "hell for stout."[24]

By early November the rollover was complete: the starboard chine log was finished and the port chine under way, with both the sheer clamp and the chine built by laminating two thinner runners in place rather than bending a single piece — easier for the twisted contortions along Paradox's hull lines, and expected to add strength from the extra glue joints once glassed. The week's goal was to finish the chines and begin covering the bottom with plywood and glass.[25]

## A return after a long hiatus (July 2013)

After roughly a year and a half without a build update — a difficult stretch that included the death of Greg's father — construction resumed in earnest with help from his uncle, Ronny Johnston (a different first name from William Johnston, the uncle who joined the list in 2011; not resolved on record whether this is the same relative under another name or a second uncle), and new progress photographs were posted for comment.[26]
