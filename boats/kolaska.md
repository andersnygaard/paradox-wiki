# Kolaska

**"Kolaska"** is Richard's Paradox, under construction near **Montauban**, in France's **Tarn-et-Garonne** department. He first appeared on the mailing list in December 2010 running the archive's most rigorously controlled resorcinol-versus-epoxy strength comparison, then spent the next several years building two other small boats before returning to the Paradox; by August 2014 the hull was far enough along to be completing the beams, with the rudder next.[1]

## A controlled resorcinol-versus-epoxy strength test

In December 2010 he described a scarf-joint strength comparison run earlier that year: a 3 m length of clear, straight, seasoned Douglas fir was ripped lengthwise into two identical 70 x 60 mm pieces, each given an 8:1 diagonal scarf cut, then bonded — one with resorcinol (Bostik), the other with unthickened Sicomin epoxy — at the manufacturers' stated 14-15°C minimum application temperature, curing over 7 days as the ambient temperature fell to 10°C. Loaded afterward with lead weight across the joint, the epoxied piece failed at about 65 kg, wood fiber giving way beyond the glue line rather than the joint itself; the resorcinol piece cracked audibly at about 75 kg but did not fail outright, and testing was abandoned around 85 kg for lack of a way to weight it further accurately. His conclusion: resorcinol stays more flexible than epoxy under stress at low temperature, spreading load more evenly into the surrounding wood, at the cost of needing considerably more clamping pressure to cure well — noting the Paradox plans themselves specify either adhesive as acceptable. He went on to glue all of his own spars and the yuloh with resorcinol, finding it no harder to apply or clean up (soluble in water while uncured) than epoxy. Full text and citation also at [construction/fastening-and-gluing.md](/construction/fastening-and-gluing.md#a-resorcinol-alternative-to-epoxy).[2]

The same message separately recommended **SICOMIN**, a French supplier, for a dedicated drinking-water/aquarium/food-container-rated epoxy — not listed on the supplier's general product pages but found in its downloadable catalogue as "Aquapox 8500 2324" — alongside two years' trouble-free use of the same supplier's other epoxy products; judged it cost-competitive with UK or US brands once shipping, tax, and customs are added, being French himself, and offered to share the French-language datasheet on request. Full text and citation also at [construction/water-tank-lining.md](/construction/water-tank-lining.md#epoxy-mixed-opinions).[3]

Replying to a fellow correspondent's location question in the same thread — Mark Balogh, who never went on to build a Paradox himself — he gave his own build's location for the first time, France's Tarn-et-Garonne department, explaining the "82" in his `rkg_82` handle.[4]

## Trailer research

By mid-2011 he was in discussion with a trailer maker in his own area about the dimensions and outfitting of a custom-made trailer. Congratulating "[JoHanna](/boats/johanna.md)"'s launch, he asked how her trailer configuration worked, in particular compared against Little Jim's own, wanting especially the launch angle, the bed length needed to float a Paradox, and the optimal recovery angle to get her back onto the trailer.[5] Following up a month later on the detailed reply this drew, he noted the trailer's own 580 kg rated capacity and asked how close to it JoHanna runs loaded for the road, and, from his own reading of the group's history, observed that the trailer is unbraked, like several other Paradox owners' own trailers — unanswered on record. Full text and citation also at [operations/trailering.md](/operations/trailering.md#a-swing-beam-roller-trailer-for-johanna-july-2011).[6]

## Two other boats, then back to the Paradox, and a boat name at last (August 2014)

Nearly four years passed with no further Paradox report on record. Replying in August 2014 to a newer correspondent's own introduction on the mailing list, he offered help sourcing materials and supplies to anyone who needed it, and revealed for the first time that his own Paradox now had a name: "**Kolaska**," building in the southwest of France.[7] Mark Balogh — the same correspondent whose 2010 question had first drawn out his location — asked where in the southwest of France the build was.[8] He gave it precisely: a couple of miles from Montauban, in the Tarn-et-Garonne (82). In the interval since their last exchange he had built a **Selway-Fisher 50/50 sailing canoe** and a **Nick Schade Wood Duck kayak**, the latter converted for sailing with an add-on rig by Mike Storer; by his own account it was now "back to the Paradox," with the beams being completed and the rudder next in line.[1] Mark Balogh, replying, recalled the earlier exchange, but had since decided against a Paradox build of his own for the time being, having turned to other projects instead — a plywood-epoxy cabin for a gypsy wagon and a planned trailerable cruising proa — and asked whether any build photographs had been posted.[9]
