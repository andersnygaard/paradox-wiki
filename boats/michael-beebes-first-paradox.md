# Michael Beebe's First Paradox

**Michael Beebe's first Paradox** was built at **Rockport**, on the Texas Gulf coast near Corpus Christi, begun in July 2011 and launched on 25 February 2012 with her interior and cabin top still unfinished, the cabin top left deliberately removable.[1] She ran the 2012 Texas 200 that June, filmed making a difficult tack under sail alone, in close quarters, at the Padre Island Yacht Club in Corpus Christi.[2] Beebe pushed her side decks out to the sheer line and lengthened her tiller so he could sit sideways and steer as on a conventional sailboat, a change his second Paradox did not repeat.[3] By January 2017, when Beebe sold that second Paradox to [Bobby (Oklahoma City)](/people/okiebob.md), who named her "[Oogway](/boats/oogway.md)," this first one had already passed to another owner.[4]

## Departures from the plans

Beebe pushed her side decks out to the sheer line and lengthened her tiller so he could sit sideways and steer as on a conventional sailboat.[3] Sailing alongside her for six days in cold rain, a fellow owner judged the change had cost more than it gave: the side decks' usual storage for the yuloh and mast was gone, and climbing on and off the cabin top became harder, though visibility from the wider cabin was never a complaint.[3] Her mast was an ordinary aluminum flagpole.[5]

## Sailing record

She ran the 2012 Texas 200, filmed making a particularly difficult tack under sail alone, in close quarters, at the Padre Island Yacht Club in Corpus Christi, a maneuver few other boats in the fleet managed that day.[2]

## Cost and sale

Beebe listed her for sale in December 2012 at $2,000, pointing to the Texas 200 video as evidence of how she sailed.[6] A year later, still unsold, the listing was repeated as a bargain: a completed Paradox for less than her materials alone would cost to build.[7] She had passed to a new owner by January 2017, when Bobby (Oklahoma City), who had tried unsuccessfully to buy her in 2012, bought Beebe's second Paradox instead.[4]
