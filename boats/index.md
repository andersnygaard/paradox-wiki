# Boats

Individual, known **Paradox** builds / hulls — specific instances of the
[Paradox](/paradox.md) design, as distinct from designer Matt Layden's other
designs (which live under [matt-layden](/matt-layden/index.md)). Each concept
cross-links its builder/owner under [people](/people/index.md). Concepts are
added here as they are sourced from the archive.

# Concepts

* [Original Paradox (Matt Layden's prototype)](original-paradox.md) - Matt Layden's original prototype Paradox — built in about four months and launched down a ravine to the Salmon River in 1993, cruised thousands of miles including a tandem Bahamas passage with the Bolducs' Little Cruiser, and won the ~300-mile 2003 WaterTribe Everglades Challenge outright, finishing at Key Largo well ahead of the entire paddled field.
* [Zoë (Glen C. Maxwell)](zoe.md) - Zoë is Glen C. Maxwell's professionally built Paradox, launched in late 2001 and cruised for years afterward, whose build hours, ballasting, interior fit-out, and Cumberland Sound sea trial supply some of the best-documented real-world figures for the design.
* [Walden (Chris Becker)](walden.md) - Chris Becker's Paradox "Walden", built in Brisbane, Australia, from 9 mm plywood starting in 2001 — the earliest Australian Paradox build reported to the list, though an earlier, 2000-built example later came to light (see [Aussie Paradox](aussie-paradox.md)).
* [The Edge (Don Elliott)](edge.md) - Don Elliott's heavily built Paradox "The Edge", one of the first two Paradoxes known to have sailed, with a distinctive lead-ballast installation technique.
* [Whisper (David Beard)](whisper.md) - David Beard's secondhand Paradox, bought from original builder Bob Archibald and refitted, renamed "Whisper," known for a hard-won lesson in tacking a loaded lug-rigged Paradox, a jet-ski collision that proved the hull, and a parallel from-scratch build.
* [Little Jim (Alastair Law)](little-jim.md) - Alastair Law's own Paradox, built in a Yeovil, England garage and launched in Poole Harbour in 2003, whose build cost, weigh-ins, launch trials, and cruise reports (including a 27-mile passage to Portland) form the fullest single UK builder's record.
* [Aussie Paradox (Andrew Graham), named "Zero"](aussie-paradox.md) - Andrew Graham's Brisbane-built Paradox, launched as "Zero" on 29 November 2003 — briefly credited as Australia's first Paradox to sail before an earlier, 2000-built secondhand example came to light — notable for its Australian-ply-sheet-optimized cutting layout, widened transom, omitted mid bins, and other deliberate plans deviations.
* [Petite Lady (Hugh Miller)](petite-lady.md) - Hugh Miller's Paradox, built in New Zealand and completed in 2003 as the archive's first New Zealand build — round birdmouth spars aside, her only recorded deviations from the plans are curved fore-and-aft cabin ends and shorter windows, and a January 2007 haul-out after four unmaintained years afloat found her sound throughout.
* [The "BRIS Paradox" (Kjell Karlsson)](bris-paradox.md) - A Swedish two-person Paradox variant built by Kjell Karlsson in Sven Yrvind's "BRIS" foam-sandwich composite method rather than plywood — a beamier, taller hull with a bronze-sheathed bottom for ballast and rock protection, no water tank, and a carbon-fibre rig, built with the blessing of both Sven Yrvind and Matt Layden; now owned and sailed year-round on Sweden's west coast by Johan Bloom as "Tetris." Karlsson also translated Yrvind's own book chapter on Paradox into English — see [Sven Yrvind's book](/references/yrvind-book.md) for that saga.
* [Jill (Jack Gardiner)](jill.md) - Jack Gardiner's Rhode Island-built Paradox, launched as "Jill" at Gaspee Point, Narragansett Bay on 11 September 2004 — a stitch-and-glue hull filmed extensively under sail and power, including two days in a steady 20-35 mph wind and a close-quarters test against passing powerboat wakes.
* [Enuf (Derek Clark)](enuf.md) - Derek Clark's UK-built Paradox — renamed "Enuf" from an earlier working name, "So-So," on his wife's suggestion — built with a fully worked-out ballast layout, a fabricated tack-strut/boom-axle pivot joint, and a three-tone green paint scheme with a yellow bottom; sold within a month of launch, departing for a new owner in Norway.
* [Swash (David C. Smith)](swash.md) - David C. Smith's Paradox, built strictly to the plans from Joubert Okoume BS-1088 plywood and Honduras mahogany, moored downriver from Deer Island, Oregon on the Columbia River. Briefly listed for sale in July 2004, before David C. Smith chose to finish her himself.
* [Ladikan (Bengt Genell)](ladikan.md) - Bengt Genell's plywood-built Paradox, launched on Lake Mälaren in central Sweden in July 2006 after a two-year build, with a modified two-part cabin for easier boarding, no water tanks, and a scratch-built wood reefing/furling gaff copied from the Australian-built Paradox "Zero."
* [Raffles (Mark Waters)](raffles.md) - Mark Waters' Los Angeles-built Paradox, completed in 2006 strictly to the plans and later trailered to Portland, Oregon — one of the best-finished hulls in the fleet, with a mahogany interior, steel weight-lifting ballast in place of lead, and a rudder-head-mount self-steering wind vane that took two years to build.
* [Faith (Bill Serjeant)](faith.md) - Bill Serjeant's UK Paradox, built strictly to Matt Layden's original plans after a kit deal fell through — started August 2005, launched mid-June 2007, cruised to the West Country, the Isles of Scilly, and Chichester Harbour, then sold in 2009.
* [Defiant (Sean Herron)](defiant.md) - Sean Herron's Paradox, under construction as of December 2006 with slight modifications from Matt Layden's stock design. This page is also the canonical home for Sean Herron himself.
* [JoHanna (Pete Martin)](johanna.md) - Pete Martin's UK Paradox, built in Cheltenham, England from 1 December 2006 and launched 11 June 2011.
* [Irene May (Ian Lang)](irene-may.md) - Ian Lang's UK Paradox, under construction in North London from late June 2007 and named for his mother.
* [Noli (Jeff, UK; ex-"Paranigma")](noli.md) - A lighter, shallower-draft Paradox/Enigma hybrid built on the Isle of Wight, England, from 2005 onward by Jeff, the same length as stock Paradox but roughly 500 lb lighter and drawing only 7 in, restated by her builder as "a Paradox-sized Enigma."
* [Ironsides (Stephen Yahn)](ironsides.md) - Stephen Yahn's Puget Sound-built Paradox "Ironsides," started July 2006 in Manchester, Washington — a steel-reinforced bulkhead 3 for a wider berth, a side-mounted outboard fabricated to spare his hands, a shop-built vent trunk drain, machined rudder gudgeons, a proposed pendulum self-steering weight, a glued-2x4 mast with an internal wiring/lightning-ground conduit, her launch on Puget Sound on 15 October 2008, and her sale in January 2013 to André-François Bourbeau of Québec.
* [Auspice (Jimmy Lu)](auspice.md) - Jimmy Lu's Taiwan-built Paradox, launched 18 February 2008 after rapid, professionally finished construction that drew unprompted praise from several established builders; named "Auspice," a name that did not surface on the list itself until six months after her launch.
* [Small Fry (Geoff Davis; later Gary Nicholls)](small-fry.md) - Geoff Davis's Western Australian Paradox, registered out of Fremantle and cruised north along the WA coast to Jurien Bay, the Abrolhos Islands and Shark Bay — the only documented Western Australian build, sold in July 2020 to Perth sailor Gary Nicholls; also the sole canonical page for Geoff Davis himself.
* [Scout (Sean Mulligan; ex-"Tardis," built by Lezlie Henson)](scout.md) - The Paradox built in Texas by Lezlie Henson as "Tardis," sold almost finished and launched by Sean Mulligan as "Scout" on Lake Havasu, Arizona, then sailed for years by Chris Curtis in North Idaho and the Puget Sound and in 2024 sold on to Lee of Maine — documenting a fast trailer-to-sailing routine, heavy-wind and 2014 Texas 200 sailing, a 2013 UK-import cost estimate, and the boat's full four-owner history.
* [Planktos (Roselt Croeser)](planktos.md) - Roselt Croeser's Western Australian Paradox, built 2013–2016 across four locations for about $2500 while he was simultaneously owner-building a house, and converted from the stock standing-lug rig to a junk rig — the archive's only junk-rig Paradox built by converting the stock lug, and the only one cruised, shaken down on a passage to Shark Bay.
* [Cachivache (Alberto Callejon)](cachivache.md) - Alberto Callejon's Paradox, begun May 2008 on Argentina's Patagonian coast at Rawson, Chubut, and finished around 2011 with the help of master shipwright Néstor Siri — "el Paradox mas austral," the most southerly Paradox on record, sailed on the Río Chubut and the open Atlantic with her transom rudder slot closed against the South Atlantic.
* [Wizard Spawn (Rodney Cannon)](wizard-spawn.md) - Rodney Cannon's Paradox, under construction in Venice, California from August 2008 and later identified, in a 2014 census, as "Wizard Spawn."
* [THU (Eerik Mee)](thu.md) - Eerik Mee's Paradox, built in Estonia from 2015 and launched in 2017 — scaled up 20% on every plans dimension and carrying a purpose-designed cambered Chinese junk rig, making it the archive's only built-and-sailed example of the 20% larger-Paradox scale-up and the only non-conversion junk rig on the design; ~240 kg of cast-lead-and-water ballast, an electric outboard, and a full electrical system.
* [Odyssey (Alexander Fadeev)](odyssey.md) - Alexander Fadeev's Paradox "Odyssey", launched with a teak deck on 12 May 2011 out of Barnegat Bay, New Jersey, and sailed a 250-nautical-mile, eight-day maiden cruise up the New Jersey and Long Island coast to Point Judith, Rhode Island.
* [Miramis (Anders Nygaard)](miramis.md) - The Paradox that Anders Nygaard is building in southern Norway — a 10% scaled-up hull in exterior-grade Baltic birch to Simas Butavičius's lighter plywood scantlings, distinguished by extensive 3D-printed build aids and fittings, a home-sewn cambered sail, and a decade-long correspondence with Sven Yrvind, who first pointed him at the design.
* [Endeavour (Greg Lockhart)](endeavour.md) - Greg Lockhart's Paradox "Endeavour," construction begun June 2011, the same month Glen Maxwell's "Zoë" was destroyed in transit; he hoped his own boat might one day merit the comparison.
* [Nebula (Edgardo Filon)](nebula.md) - Edgardo Filon's Argentine Paradox, launched and photographed by October 2011.
* [Moxie (Andy)](moxie.md) - A Paradox under construction through 2012, built by Andy and documented only through his photograph-caption messages and, by August, a finished bottom lay-up schedule.
* [Manatee (Gunther Battista)](manatee.md) - Gunther Battista's 20%-enlarged Paradox, pre-named "Manatee" in March 2013 and put under construction in Austria that May, sourced in wide Okoume plywood sheets against a deliberate survey of the archive's other builds.
* [Martha (Christian Skjutare)](martha.md) - Christian Skjutare's Paradox, announced in August 2013 under the working name "Martha," complicated from the outset by Swedish plywood running 1mm over the plans' US-standard thicknesses, launched for a first test in the water in July 2017 under the name "Gripen" (the Griffon), and offered free to the group in 2020 after a house move left her chine runners and bottom damaged and unrepaired.
* [SaSa (Stephen Barwell)](sasa.md) - Stephen Barwell's Paradox "SaSa," under construction on North Stradbroke Island, Queensland since June 2013 — a curved transom, a planned double-tiller self-steering system, and a considered switch from loose water ballast to flexible bladders.
* [Pathfinder (Benjamin Bloss)](pathfinder.md) - Benjamin Bloss's Paradox, under construction north of Seattle since December 2021 and still unfinished — built to plan in ABX marine plywood with laminated deck beams, laid out first in CAD, and documented in a continuing series of video build logs that record the hull-assembly stage as it happened.
* [Miko (Tony Barlow)](miko.md) - Tony Barlow's Paradox, built and launched at Phuket, Thailand in March 2024, fitted for light airs and heat with twin outboards, a cut-down electric trolling motor on lithium, extra solar and air conditioning.
* [Bye Bye Love (newingtonnate)](bye-bye-love.md) - A Paradox under construction in British Columbia as of December 2013, her builder known only by his handle, distinguished by eliminating the vent box entirely for extra interior room.
* [Kolaska (Richard)](kolaska.md) - Richard's Paradox "Kolaska," under construction near Montauban in France's Tarn-et-Garonne department — completing the beams with the rudder next as of August 2014, after a controlled resorcinol-versus-epoxy test and two other small-boat builds along the way.
* [Mini (Rob Kellock)](mini.md) - Rob Kellock's Paradox, built at his home workshop in Kirwee, Canterbury, New Zealand from September 2014 and launched in December 2017, converted from a standing lug to a four-sided sprit rig in 2018 and sailed and measured more methodically than almost any other Paradox on record, from a GPS-tracked hull-speed trial to repeated week-long South Island cruises.
* [Quixotic (Terry Joseph)](quixotic.md) - Terry Joseph's Paradox, begun in March 2015 within a week of receiving the plans — bulkheads and transom complete and the deck beams under way at a deliberately unhurried, one-task-a-day pace.
* [Thor (Nick Bell; later "Pamela B," Mark Brothers)](thor.md) - Nick Bell's Paradox "Thor," built in South Australia through 2014-15 with a boat builder's professional help on a birdsmouth mast and launched on the Murray River in June 2015 — sold in 2022 to Mark Brothers of New South Wales, who renamed her "Pamela B" and made her the reference boat for the Mk3 sail.
* [Tom Steinmetz's Paradox](tom-steinmetzs-paradox.md) - A Paradox built by Tom Steinmetz and converted to a Bermuda rig with her chine runners removed and a leeboard fitted in their place, the only such conversion the archive documents from a real, sailing boat, known to it only through other builders' study of a photograph and a video.
* [Pequeno (Alan Ferris; later André-François Bourbeau)](pequeno.md) - A Paradox built by Alan Ferris in Meaford, Ontario, Canada, launched in 2014 as "Al's Boat," and later acquired by André-François Bourbeau of Saguenay, Québec as his second Paradox alongside "Ironsides" (renamed "Iota").
* [Michael Beebe's First Paradox](michael-beebes-first-paradox.md) - Michael Beebe's first Paradox, built and launched at Rockport, on the Texas Gulf coast, in February 2012, with her side decks widened to the sheer line and her tiller lengthened for sideways steering, sold before he built a second, later Oogway.
* [Oogway (Michael Beebe; later OkieBob)](oogway.md) - Michael Beebe's second Paradox, built in Rockport, Texas to the stock cabin plan and fitted with bulkhead-mounted hatch seats, a removable coachroof, and an all-locker subfloor — sold in January 2017 to OkieBob, who towed her home to Oklahoma and named her "Oogway" after the wise old tortoise of the Kung Fu Panda films.
* [Rahänd (Jörgen Agardtson)](rahand.md) - A Paradox under slow, continuing construction by Jörgen Agardtson in Sweden since at least 2017, with a two-layer 12mm plywood bottom in place of the plans' single 18mm sheet.
* [Paw Paw (Dave Skaife; later Steve Collins)](paw-paw.md) - A Paradox built by Dave Skaife in Hawaii and sailed engine-free across four seasons of Pacific, Gulf, Bahamas, and Cuba cruising, sold in March 2018 to Steve Collins of the Gulf Coast, who renamed her "Paw Paw."
* [Prescott Eagle (Brian Prescott; continued by Cory Sellars)](prescott-eagle.md) - A Paradox begun by Brian Prescott in Kincardine, Ontario, and rescued from a burn pile after his death by Cory Sellars, who is finishing her toward a planned Bruce Peninsula touring trip on the Great Lakes.
* [Paradox 357 (OkieBob)](paradox-357.md) - OkieBob's own scaled-down Paradox, built in Oklahoma City from 2010 at 85 percent of the stock 420 cm length, the arithmetic behind her own "Paradox 357" name, with the build's own record on this list ending in 2016.
* [David Nemo's Paradox](david-nemos-paradox.md) - An unnamed 28 by 8 foot live-aboard Paradox built by David Nemo in the United Kingdom at double the stock plans' scale, foam-cored in Divinycell over reclaimed church-hardwood framing, complete and sailing for the Mediterranean by December 2009.
