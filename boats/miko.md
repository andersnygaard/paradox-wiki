# Miko

**"Miko"** is the [Paradox](/paradox.md) that **[Tony Barlow](/people/tony-barlow.md)** built and launched at **Phuket, Thailand**, in **March 2024** — the archive's only recorded build in tropical Asia, and a boat fitted from the start for the conditions she works in: persistent light airs, heat, and thunderstorms.[1] She was launched the day after the build was announced complete, sailed briefly in almost no wind, and motored to her new mooring ball.[1]

## Trim at launch

The launch exposed one immediate imbalance. With the **battery and outboard both on the port side**, Miko sat leaning to port, and Barlow's first job afloat was to add **lead or other weight to starboard** to bring her upright — a practical reminder that on a hull this narrow the [ballast](/components/ballast.md) budget has to account for the systems as well as the plans' pigs.[1]

## Power: an electric installation, then two outboards

A year before launch Barlow mocked up an **electric trolling-motor drive** copied from another owner's boat, and documented it in enough detail to rebuild: the motor's **shaft cut down** (following the YouTube method), the **control head relocated under the tiller** with its wiring run through a hose in a hole in the transom, and the **outboard bracket modified** — springs removed and two of the arms reversed — so it lifts and drops smoothly. Power comes from a **100 Ah lithium battery** housed between **bulkhead 4 and the transom**. He installed it explicitly as a provisional arrangement: the plan was "to sail as much as possible," and if his sailing and yuloh skills came on far enough, the motor would come off again.[2] The same photographs introduced a **stainless-steel rudder gudgeon** — his epoxy casting had come out full of bubbles — and an **alternative to the sliding hatch** that frees deck area for more solar panels.[2]

In practice the light Phuket airs won. By 2025 she carried **two petrol outboards**, a working motor and a back-up for longer trips, which Barlow posted with an apology to "sailing purists" and the note that he "would sail the entire way if I could"; the cheaper **Parsun** motors he rates as a Yamaha copy.[3] A **blue tube led to the outboard** lets him put it in and out of gear more easily from the helm.[4] See [auxiliary outboard power](/operations/auxiliary-outboard-power.md).

## Fitted for the tropics

Miko carries **air conditioning** — a Cybertake S1 Pro drawing about **100 W in eco mode** — which is unique in the archive and entirely rational at 8° north.[5] Barlow has also worked on a **seat that stays comfortable with the boat heeled**, putting his attempt to the group for better ideas, and rigged a **winch setup** for recovery at a ramp that looks like open beach but has a **concrete slip** running under the sand.[6][7] During the build he asked the group for photographs of how others fixed the plans' **lightning grounding plate** — a 75 mm × 1500 mm strip — and routed the associated mast wiring, a question the archive had not previously recorded from a builder in a lightning-prone climate.[8]

He tested the [furling system](/components/rig/furling.md) on a **mock-up mast in the garden** before committing it to the boat, working from a method demonstrated on the smallwoodenboats YouTube channel and reporting that it worked really well; [Nickolas Bell](/people/nickolas-bell.md) added the working technique from his own years with the gear — lift the sail on the halyard, furl a little, lift again, and repeat, which furls easily enough except in strong winds under way.[9]

She sails out of Phuket, where Barlow also records the free anchoring spots and the day-to-day of keeping a small boat in the tropics.[10]
