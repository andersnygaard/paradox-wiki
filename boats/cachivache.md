# Cachivache

**"Cachivache"** — Spanish slang for a piece of junk or a knick-knack — is [Alberto Callejon](/people/alberto-callejon.md)'s [Paradox](/paradox.md), under construction from May 2008 at **Rawson, Chubut**, on Argentina's Patagonian coast. Alberto styled her on the list as **"el Paradox mas austral,"** the most southerly Paradox on record.

## Starting construction on the Patagonian coast

Before starting, Alberto weighed whether the design could handle his home coast's strong wind and sea, a stretch of open South Atlantic at 43°21′S, 65°03′W with almost no local sailing tradition beyond rowing and occasional windsurfing; see [blue-water suitability — a high-latitude query from Argentine Patagonia](/design/blue-water-suitability.md#a-high-latitude-query-from-argentine-patagonia) for the reassurance this drew. Construction began in May 2008, with early progress photos posted to a "Cachivache" folder in the group's photo section.[1] Alongside starting the hull, he raised the recurring question of building the bottom and sides in sheet steel rather than plywood — see [aluminum and steel Paradox (speculative) — a 2008 recurrence, from a builder in Patagonia](/design/ideas/aluminum-construction-idea.md#a-2008-recurrence-from-a-builder-in-patagonia) for the sizing advice this drew, and Alberto's own non-technical contributions (an offered instant-messenger contact for Spanish-speaking builders, and a search for Don Elliott's out-of-print construction manual) on [people/alberto-callejon.md](/people/alberto-callejon.md).

## Enlarging the cabin to the hull's edge, considered and answered

While laying out Cachivache's bulkheads, before cutting, Alberto considered enlarging the cabin out to the hull's edge and asked whether doing so would affect any of the boat's other design parameters, echoing an idea raised once before and left untested at the time. A reply the next day answered directly against the idea: the stepped-in side decks stow the lowered sail and the yuloh, a wider cabin top makes it harder to reach the anchor or fend off from forward, it adds windage especially to windward, and the cabin's usable interior space would not even increase unless the internal side bins were removed too — so, on balance, little was seen to recommend it. See [flush cabin sides for Paradox (speculative)](/design/ideas/flush-cabin-sides-idea.md#a-2008-recurrence-answered) for the fuller record of both messages.[2][3]

## A drain-pipe material question, where PVC is hard to find

Asking about the PVC pipe shown on drawing 9 of the plans, Alberto drew an answer that read the question as one of materials sourcing: the pipe carries waste water, and where PVC itself proves hard to obtain locally, copper pipe — or the lead pipe once standard before PVC became common — would work as a substitute.[4][5]

## Reinforcing the hull for Patagonian seas

Construction advanced further that July: panels were reinforced with angle iron and stainless steel bolts and laminated with fiberglass on both sides — extra strengthening measures taken specifically against the rough conditions of the Patagonian sea (see [blue-water suitability — a high-latitude query from Argentine Patagonia](/design/blue-water-suitability.md#a-high-latitude-query-from-argentine-patagonia) for the conditions themselves) — with new build photographs due in the group's "CACHIVACHE" folder within a few days.[6] New photographs duly went up in the album later that month, drawing further encouragement from the list, with more images following within days.[7][8][9]

## A sail-geometry question for the newer rig

Framing Cachivache's rig, Alberto asked for the distance between the mast and the forward end of the yard on Paradox's newer sail — almost certainly the [Mk2 sail](/components/rig/mk2-sail.md) current at that date, since the design's plans were not revised again until the 2023 Mk3. The question drew no dimension in immediate reply, only a pointer to the group's own Files section, where a "New Sail Plan" folder already held the drawing itself.[10][11] A day later, a fellow builder measured the drawing directly and supplied the missing figure: about 800 mm from the mast centerline to the sail's forward top corner along the top edge, or 900 mm to the halyard attachment point along the same edge — a dimension not otherwise given on the plan (see [Mk2 sail — locating the sail's forward top corner](/components/rig/mk2-sail.md#locating-the-sails-forward-top-corner-and-tuning-balance-through-the-sleeve-gap) for the full figure and the sail-balance-tuning technique it enabled).[12]

## A personal website for the build photos, and an access problem

By mid-August 2008, a fuller set of building photographs was posted to a personal website rather than the group's own photo folder.[13] The link proved unreachable for at least one overseas list member, whose browser simply timed out trying to reach it; contacted directly, Alberto reported the site working fine from his own end, leaving the access problem unresolved on record.[14] Two further list members confirmed the site loaded without any trouble from their own connections — one had checked it several times with no issue, the other praised the photographs directly — narrowing the access problem to that one report rather than any fault with the site itself.[15][16] Revisiting the question weeks later, the browser-timeout report was updated rather than resolved: the site was eventually reached, though it still usually times out, tentatively put down to the long internet path between the UK and Argentina rather than to any fault with the site itself.[11]

## A polyester-and-epoxy question

Weighing how best to impregnate Cachivache's wood, Alberto asked whether a diluted coat of polyester resin applied first would still let epoxy resin be laminated over it afterward. The answer: not reliably, since polyester resin typically leaves a wax on the surface that must be removed before epoxy will adhere at all, and diluting the polyester does not impregnate the wood in any case — only the solvent penetrates, not the resin. A professional's caution, from prior experience at a marine-resin manufacturer, put it more bluntly still: never put epoxy over polyester; seal the wood with an epoxy sealer first, then laminate with epoxy resin. Full exchange and the fallback advice for a tight budget: [epoxy brands, chemistry, and cure behavior — epoxy over polyester](/construction/epoxy-brands-and-chemistry.md#epoxy-over-polyester-wax-impregnation-and-a-professionals-caution).[17][18][19] Alberto settled on laminating with epoxy resin just above the waterline, painting everywhere else.[20] A further reply arrived a day later, after the thread had already effectively closed, offering yet another combination — epoxy resin itself diluted with acetone in place of a polyester pre-coat, sidestepping the adhesion problem altogether.[21]

## Continued progress into the Patagonian winter

By late August 2008, the hull's sides were finished, with the remaining work hoped to be done by the end of the Patagonian winter so assembly could begin.[22] New photographs of the finished sides went up in the "Cachivache" folder the following day.[23] The build photos drew a further, unprompted compliment: another builder credited the site directly with the design of his own beam-laminating jigs, judging the method shown there considerably easier than the one given in the construction manual — see [Wizard Spawn — starting on the beams](/boats/wizard-spawn.md#starting-on-the-beams-jigs-sizing-confusion-and-a-lesson-in-plywood) for the fuller account of that build.[24] A further photo went up the following week, along with a wry aside that the Patagonian cold had returned just as it seemed to be easing, with a hope that shifting weather patterns would not draw the low temperatures out any further.[25]

## A cheap imported tape measure, and a full bulkhead redo

Posting in machine-translated Spanish under a subject line that only makes sense once decoded, Alberto described a construction mishap: bulkhead dimensions carefully marked out with a "Made in China" tape measure turned out not to agree with a second measuring tool once cross-checked against it — a seamstress's own tape among them — revealing the cheap import's centimetre scale ran short of true, reading only "99" where it should have read 100. The mismatch went uncaught until after the bulkheads were already cut, forcing a full redo of the lot.[26] Some of the machine-translated phrasing resists confident decoding — a "hammer of 20 kgs" in the subject line, apparently some idiom for the shock of the discovery, and an aside about "the subway seamstress" (Spanish "metro" serves for both "tape measure" and "subway," a likely source of the translator's confusion) — but the underlying gist survived plainly enough for [Nels](/people/nels-arvidson.md) to restate it back in plain English without correction: a scale error on the imported measuring device had thrown off the bulkhead dimensions.[27] Replying the same day, in equally garbled machine translation, Alberto confirmed the gist: he was able to recover the material and get back on track, though it cost him the lost time, with the mate (the drink) close at hand throughout the ordeal — a further phrase, translated as having "gone over gander" to recover the pieces, resists confident decoding, consistent with the same translation difficulty already flagged above rather than a new fact.[28]

A message the following week settles what the "hammer of 20 kgs" subject line actually meant: after "many hours of meditation," the offending tape measure itself met a hammer, rather than merely another cross-check — a literal detail confirming the earlier garbled phrase referred to smashing the faulty tool, not to a literal 20 kg tool of any kind. With a corrected measure in hand, construction of Cachivache continued, every measurement now checked consistent with the plans; new photographs went up at the same personal-website gallery used before, drawing further encouragement from the list.[29][30]

## Considering an aluminum mast

Weighing a wood-to-aluminum substitution for Cachivache's own mast, Alberto had a **63.5 mm** diameter aluminum tube on hand and asked whether it would serve. The general engineering answer he drew — conditional on the tube's wall thickness, with a specific technique offered for reinforcing its lower end — is set out in full at [components/rig/mast.md — aluminum as a mast material](/components/rig/mast.md#aluminum-as-a-mast-material-engineering-fit-not-just-substitution); his own tube's wall proved to be **3 mm**, and no further reply settling whether that was thick enough is on record.[31][32][33]

Raising the same substitution again independently a few days later — restating the tube's diameter as **63 mm** rather than 63.5 mm — Alberto drew two further opinions, both leaning toward wood. One judged the tube better suited to a boom than a mast, recommending a larger diameter (roughly 73 or 88 mm, with a 5 mm wall) if aluminum were used at all, and said he would use wood himself since it floats in the event of real disaster. The other questioned substituting aluminum for wood at all, seeing no advantage besides ease of construction, judged the tube undersized, and suggested testing it by laying the tube across two sawhorses with a central weight and comparing the deflection against a wood box-section built to the mast's own dimensions — predicting wood would win clearly. Full exchange: [components/rig/mast.md — aluminum as a mast material](/components/rig/mast.md#aluminum-as-a-mast-material-engineering-fit-not-just-substitution).[34][35][36]

## Continued photographs, and an open filleting question

A further round of building photographs went up at the same personal-website gallery a few days later, on 4 November 2008.[37] Separately, Alberto asked whether phenolic (resorcinol-type) wood glue and sawdust, rather than epoxy, could be used to make fillets — no reply settling the question is on record.[38] A further progress update, again pointing to the same gallery, followed the following week.[39] A further update followed two weeks later still, again linking the same gallery.[40]

## Building the sail: a broadseaming question answered

Turning to the sail, Alberto asked whether the cloth should be cut and sewn perfectly flat or given some bag or curve — Spanish "vela" (sail) coming through machine translation as "the candle." The answer: a Paradox sail is genuinely three-dimensional, its shape built in by **broadseaming** — varying the width of overlap along the seams joining each panel, with more overlap at each end than at the centre — following [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)'s own sewing instructions in the group's "Files" section under "New Sail Plan," whose measurements differ from the sail shown on drawing 1 of the plans. The fuller technique, including a caution about working from a translated copy of the instructions, is at [Sail — broadseaming](/components/rig/sail.md#broadseaming-how-the-sails-three-dimensional-shape-is-sewn-in).[41][42]

## Insulating the aft deck above the stove burners

Studying photographs of other Paradoxes, Alberto could not find any builder who had fitted insulation against heat between the stove's burners and the underside of the aft deck above them.[43] The question drew a firsthand answer from build experience on Little Cruiser and Swamp Thing — copper flashing, foil bubble wrap, and a proposed woven-roving/metal-flashing fireproofing method. See [cabin heating — insulating the aft deck above the stove burners](/operations/cabin-heating.md#insulating-the-aft-deck-above-the-stove-burners).[44]

## Don Elliott's death, and a personal note of thanks

News of [Don Elliott](/people/don-elliott.md)'s death reached the list in early December 2008; Alberto's own reply credited "the care and aid received from Don" with having helped him build Cachivache. Full record, including Wanda Elliott's own announcement, at [people/don-elliott.md](/people/don-elliott.md#after-his-death).[45]

## Stays, and a foam-insulation alternative

Alberto separately asked why no stays or shrouds appear in any photograph or drawing of the design, worried by the strong, unpredictable gusts common on the Patagonian coast — answered that the design relies on reefing and on the hull's own narrow-rolling behavior instead of standing rigging. See [mast — why the mast carries no stays or shrouds](/components/rig/mast.md#why-the-mast-carries-no-stays-or-shrouds).[46] Days later he also asked whether a polyethylene bubble-wrap film, seen replacing foam insulation on another boat, would work aboard a Paradox — unanswered on record.[47]

## A Christmas 2008 progress update

A further holiday-season update — "we remain firm in 'Cachivache', every day looks more like something" — pointed once more to the same personal-website gallery.[48]

## A second polyester question, as epoxy prices rise

With epoxy's price "gone to the clouds," Alberto asked again whether polyester resin could simply substitute for it in laminating fiberglass generally, having heard "the epoxy did not adhere" — the same wax-adhesion question already settled the previous September, this time driven by cost rather than by unfamiliarity with the chemistry. Unanswered on record. See [epoxy brands, chemistry, and cure behavior — epoxy over polyester](/construction/epoxy-brands-and-chemistry.md#epoxy-over-polyester-wax-impregnation-and-a-professionals-caution).[49]

## The tiller finished, and the aluminum mast idea abandoned

Construction reached the tiller in late January 2009; before shaping it, Alberto asked whether its length to bulkhead 4 (700 mm) was correct and how many degrees the rudder blade should turn — see [rudder and chine experiments — tiller construction](/components/rudder.md#tiller-construction-laminated-wood-versus-bent-metal-tube) for the roughly-30-degree answer this drew, scaled from the same sheet-4 template used elsewhere in the archive.[50][51] Replying with the tiller laminated and ready for sanding, Alberto also settled the aluminum-mast question raised the previous November: pricing the tube proved prohibitively expensive, so he built the mast in wood instead, and posted further building photographs to the same personal-website gallery.[52]

## A recurring access problem, and a likely explanation

The photo-gallery access problem recurred five months later, in late January 2009, when a second New Zealand list member found the same personal-website gallery unreachable — his browser loaded for a long time before showing only a blank screen, even though the status line reported the download complete — despite being sent the gallery's two alternate URLs and a re-post using Yahoo's own link-shortening button.[53][54][55][56] The New Zealand builder who had reported the original August 2008 timeouts judged it likely a symptom of simply being in New Zealand, since he experienced the identical intermittent timeout himself, and recommended bookmarking the page and trying again every so often until it loads.[57] Two independent New Zealand-based readers hitting the same symptom against the same site, while list members elsewhere reported no trouble reaching it, points to a routing or bandwidth problem between New Zealand and Argentina rather than any fault in the site itself.

## A further question: chine runners on Sven Yrvind's design

Turning briefly from Cachivache's own build to a wider design question, Alberto read of a resolved chine-runner design issue on [Sven Yrvind](/people/yrvind.md)'s own site and wondered whether something similar could be applied to Paradox's own runners — unanswered on record within the mailing list's remaining span. See [Sven Yrvind — Paradox and the chine runner](/people/yrvind/paradox-and-the-chine-runner.md) for Yrvind's own firsthand relationship to the feature.[58]

## Fiberglassing resumes and finishes with the Patagonian spring (September 2009)

With the Patagonian sun's return after winter, fiberglassing resumed in early September 2009, drawing brief encouragement from the list along with a warning that sanding cured glass can be an itchy job.[59][60] Separately, Alberto asked whether any logo identifies the Paradox design — unanswered on record.[61] Within two weeks the glassing was finished outright — deck, sides, transom, and cabin all laid up — with further progress photographs posted to the same personal-website gallery.[62]

## A combined putty-and-primer product (October 2009)

Progress continued into the Patagonian spring: an epoxy product combining both **putty and primer** in one, applied by brush, roller, or spray, was used for fairing and priming the hull, with further photographs posted to the same personal-website gallery.[63]

## Taking color (November 2009)

By mid-November 2009 the hull was **taking color** — the paint stage under way — with further photographs posted to the same personal-website gallery.[64]

## Ballast: railroad track on hand

Nearing launch, Alberto turned to the ballast question, reporting he had obtained sections of railroad track weighing **20 kg per metre** and asking how best to install it.[65] The answer: start with the track pieces close to bulkhead 3 without fixing them down permanently, since some will likely need repositioning to get the trim right once test-sailing begins — better to have too much ballast aboard than too little.[66]

## Nearing launch: a low-bridge mast-hinge question

By December 2009, Cachivache was soon to be launched: a few blocks from home the Chubut river offered interesting sailing, the only obstacle being a series of low bridges, prompting the question of whether the mast could be made to hinge for repeated lowering and raising.[70] The suggested answer looked to a hinged-tabernacle arrangement documented in a separate junk-rig group's files, needing some custom metalwork, with a copy then added directly to the Paradox group's own files for easier reference; see [Mast — a 2009 question: hinging the mast to clear low river bridges](/components/rig/mast.md#a-2009-question-hinging-the-mast-to-clear-low-river-bridges) for Paradox's own lift-off arrangement and the fuller exchange.[71][72]

## A January 2010 launch announced

By January 2010, Alberto reported the launch itself only days away, with further progress photographs posted to the same personal-website gallery.[73]

## A June 2010 echo sounder failure

After the launch, an echo sounder aboard Cachivache failed outright — its display broken, with no replacement part obtainable anywhere in Argentina, leaving the question of where to source one open on record. See [GPS and navigation instruments — a remote-region echo sounder failure](/operations/gps-navigation.md#a-remote-region-echo-sounder-failure-no-local-parts-market) for the fuller parts-scarcity picture this adds to.[74]

## Completion, and the finished boat (Facebook record)

The mailing-list record above stops in January 2010 with the launch reported just days away; the [Paradox Sailboat Facebook group](/references/paradox-facebook-group.md) supplies the completion the list never recorded. Cachivache was **finished and sailing** — Alberto dates the build to about 2011 and credits the work of a **master shipwright, Néstor Siri** — and by 2019 he confirmed her first-hand as **"the southernmost Paradox in the world,"** sailed on the **Río Chubut** and the open **Atlantic** at Rawson, and that he was "really happy with it."[67][68] One departure from the plans, made deliberately for his home waters: the **through-transom rudder slot is closed**, not left open, "the waters of the South Atlantic [being] rough enough to leave it open."[69]
