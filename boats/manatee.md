# Manatee

**"Manatee"** is [Gunther Battista](/people/gunther-battista.md)'s 20%-enlarged [Paradox](/paradox.md), under construction in Austria from May 2013. Introducing himself to the list in March 2013 — 44 years old, new to sailing but working toward a license, already holding the plans — he pre-named the boat "Manatee" for the quiet, cozy character the name suggested to him, envisioning eventual Mediterranean and Atlantic cruising.[1] Weighing the list's usual caution against enlarging the design (see [larger Paradox — a March 2013 recurrence](/design/larger-paradox.md#a-march-2013-recurrence-a-newcomers-20-plan-and-renewed-caution-from-an-experienced-builder-and-a-standard-size-owner)), he settled on the sanctioned 20% step, widening the existing bays by only a few centimetres apiece rather than adding new bulkheads, for the extra stores and gear capacity a genuinely extended stay aboard would need.[2]

## Materials and sourcing

Construction began two months later. Plywood and epoxy were on order for delivery within the week, with mast timber to follow in June. Okoume plywood in the wide sheet size a 20% scale-up needs — 3100 x 1530 mm — proved hard to source in Austria except in large bulk quantities; two 18 mm sheets of that size were eventually secured for the bottom panel. Before cutting, the archive's other documented hulls were studied in detail — [Odyssey](/boats/odyssey.md) singled out as a particularly successful boat, alongside [Johanna](/boats/johanna.md), [Faith](/boats/faith.md), [Cachivache](/boats/cachivache.md), [Ironsides](/boats/ironsides.md), the Taiwan Paradox, ParaPlastic, [Zoë](/boats/zoe.md), and the Aussie Paradox.[3]

## Construction begins

By late May 2013 the side panels were cut, with the bulkheads next. Weighing the wider debate over doubling the design's every dimension instead of the sanctioned 20% (see [larger Paradox — the wider debate](/design/larger-paradox.md#the-wider-debate-a-do-not-scale-doctrine-and-why-the-interest-in-enlarging-paradox-specifically-persists)), doubling was judged too big a boat for this build; a catamaran, not a further-enlarged Paradox, would be the choice at that scale instead.[4] Photographs of the cut side panels were posted under the same "Paradox Max (Manatee)" listing the following week.[5]

## A skin reaction to epoxy pauses the work (August 2013)

A skin reaction to epoxy — hands reddened and swollen, "like the back of a toad" — forced a short break from the build; a milder itch was judged manageable by scratching, and work with epoxy resumed carefully a few days later. See [epoxy safety and personal protection — a firsthand skin allergy](/construction/epoxy-safety-and-ppe.md#a-firsthand-skin-allergy-2013) for the general hazard this confirms.[6] A month later the same reaction's full day-by-day course was set out in detail — see [epoxy safety and personal protection — a firsthand skin allergy](/construction/epoxy-safety-and-ppe.md#a-firsthand-skin-allergy-2013) for the fuller record.[7]

## A February 2014 update: eight months in, and a stalled pace

About eight months after work began, replying in the same "Paradox Max" thread, Gunther Battista confirmed the boat's specs again — Manatee, 20% larger than stock, with a write-up filed under the group's Files section as "Larger Paradox.doc" — and gave a personal update on progress: building almost entirely alone, he had made real progress but only slowly, with a recent marital separation and a February deadline to move the boat out of cellar storage and into the open both adding stress. The epoxy allergy documented above (see [above](#a-skin-reaction-to-epoxy-pauses-the-work-august-2013)) had by then eased substantially, largely resolved after roughly ten episodes. Remaining work — a sliding hatch, epoxy-coating the deck, and a few smaller items — continued slowly, with four children now to support; the build would go on regardless, "an optimist."[8]

## A further February 2014 exchange: the allergy's treatment, and scaling specifics confirmed

Asked how the epoxy allergy above was treated, the answer: it went away on its own, treated with a single anti-itch cream containing no cortisone; across roughly ten occurrences as a rash, it was already noticeably weaker by the eighth. Asked separately how the 20% enlargement had been carried through the design, the answer confirmed a uniform 20% increase across all three principal dimensions — length, width, and height — with material thickness left to personal discretion rather than scaled in step, insulation increased to 50mm, and every bulkhead kept at its plan position, shifted only by the same 20% factor. Further data was offered to anyone who wanted it. For the resulting displacement and draft implications, worked out from these figures, see [larger Paradox — a worked example](/design/larger-paradox.md#a-worked-example-cubic-displacement-scaling-and-the-draft-increase-it-implies-february-2014).[9]

## No further word since February 2014

By April 2015, over a year after the last update above, a fellow correspondent reviewing old build photographs noted the hull had looked very nearly complete — windows, finishing, foils, and the rig still wanting — and that the epoxy allergy seemed to have resolved; given the pace of progress by then, he had expected the boat to be sailing already, but no further photographs or messages had appeared since February 2014.[10]

## A February 2017 recurrence

Nearly three years after the last confirmed update, "Manatee" resurfaced by name in a mailing-list exchange about enlarged Paradoxes generally — recalled as the go-to example of a beautiful enlarged Paradox that came close to finishing before its builder simply disappeared.[11] A reply confirmed the boat meant was indeed "Manatee," misremembering Battista as South American rather than Austrian but describing the same charging-along-then-silence arc.[12] The exchange confirmed which boat was meant rather than supplying any news of Battista himself, whose silence since February 2014 remained unbroken.

## Updates since 2017

Two and a half months after the February 2017 recurrence above, Battista replied to the mailing list himself for the first time since February 2014 — correcting the guesses made about him in the exchange above: "No I am not a type from Germany and I am not from South America, I am an Austrian." "Manatee is still in my possession," he confirmed, "unfortunately has not gone much since 2014" — a divorce in the meantime, and four children now to support, explaining the slow pace. He planned to buy a boat trailer the following year and then resume building.[13] Two and a half years after that, in November 2019, he confirmed the boat was still under construction under the same "Paradox Max (Manatee)" name, with new pictures expected soon.[14] Two days later he added that he had not been able to work on the boat at all between 2014 and 2019, with progress since resuming slowly but steadily — "it's just going on slowly but it continues, I have time."[15] The same thread drew an admiring reply asking how much larger than stock Manatee is, and whether a 20% linear enlargement takes roughly 20% more plywood or as much as 50% more once the plans' 8-by-4 ft sheet layout is accounted for.[16] The size was confirmed the next day — 20% larger, built stronger throughout so she should continue out to sea — though the plywood-quantity half of the question remains unanswered on record.[17]

Further pictures followed over the next two days, documenting window preparation: the pad that takes the glazing was built up, then a second step was drilled oversized and refilled with epoxy, a technique repeated for the toe-rail fastening holes; the interior was painted white, and construction of the vent trunk began.[18] The set closed with insulation fitted and cover plates added over the same drilled holes, the floor plates prepared and left for painting the following year, and the side fans' own design still being worked out — "it still works in my head" — with the note that the build would continue again next year.[19]
