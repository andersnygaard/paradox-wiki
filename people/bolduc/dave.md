# Dave Bolduc

*Part of the [Bolduc](/people/bolduc.md) record; see also his cruising partner [Mindy Bolduc](/people/bolduc/mindy.md) and the couple's shared [Matt Layden firsthand record](/people/bolduc/matt-layden-authority.md).*

**Dave Bolduc** distributes the [Paradox building plans](/references/building-plans.md) and, with his wife [Mindy](/people/bolduc/mindy.md), cruises [Little Cruiser](/matt-layden/little-cruiser.md), a boat "very similar to Paradox."[1] A carpenter by trade, he is one of the archive's deepest firsthand authorities on [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md) and his boats. He has never built a Paradox himself, but has known Matt since 1992, owns three of his designs, cruised the Bahamas in his company and has examined most of Matt's boats in person, discussing each at length with the designer. The full relationship is told in the couple's [Matt Layden firsthand record](/people/bolduc/matt-layden-authority.md). He joined the Paradox builders' community in September 2001 and later founded the companion [Micro Cruising](/references/micro-cruising-group.md) group, which members were encouraged to join.[2][3] For Paradox-specific building details he defers to others as "the real experts."

## Biography

Per his own site, [microcruising.com](/references/microcruising-website.md), Dave is a carpenter and Mindy a pharmacist; the couple have micro-cruised for roughly 30 years.[4] His first boat was a $100 secondhand 12-foot Wildflower Snark sailed on the Hudson River during graduate school, replaced by a $100 Sparkman & Stephens Bluejay he christened "Wet Feet": "very appropriately named," since she leaked continuously and left her crew wet and a little miserable, especially when it rained; by contrast, cruising Little Cruiser has meant cruising in luxury ever since: a dry place to sleep, wine in the bilge for evening toasts and, in later years, an iPhone for calls and browsing underway.[5][6] A 2006 interview dates that early sequence: he came to sailing "late," by way of **sea kayaking in the early 1980s**, bought the $100 Snark around **1984** after his family moved to New York within reach of the Hudson, and moved up to the Bluejay about a year later, a boat that leaked badly enough that its crew woke every couple of hours to pump, and aboard which he first took [Mindy](/people/bolduc/mindy.md) camp-cruising. He met her in **September 1986**; they married in **1989**.[31] In the same interview he described his own work as "basically a handyman" doing "all kinds of odd jobs": a looser self-description than the "carpenter" of the written record, and the reason the couple's cruise dates always turned on Mindy's leave rather than his.[31] He met Matt Layden in 1992, "he seemed really out there... cruising full-time in these little dinky home-built boats, and he was having a blast doing it," and bought [Little Cruiser](/matt-layden/little-cruiser.md) that same year.[7] He and Mindy went on to make roughly six two-up winter Bahamas cruises aboard her, averaging about 800 miles each.[8] He later bought Matt's own Enigma, racing her through the 2010 Everglades Challenge (coming through a frontal passage on the Bahama Bank of over 50 knots) and in 2013 made an engine-free solo Bahamas crossing in her, his seventh island trip overall.[9][10] He holds an amateur-radio license, is a competitive marathon kayaker and in 2014 co-wrote a SAIL magazine article on small-boat cruising with Mindy; a decade earlier, Furled Sails ran a two-part podcast interview with the couple.[9][11][1] That article, ["Go Small, Go Now"](/references/sail-magazine.md), is as published written in Dave's own first-person singular voice, with the photographs credited to both of them, and it is the fullest single account of the couple's cruising life that he wrote.[34] As of around 2014 he still owned all three of his micro-cruisers: Little Cruiser, Swamp Thing and Enigma.[9] His one formal community role was a brief stint as co-moderator, volunteering alongside another member to help close sign-ups against a wave of spam accounts.[12] See also [sculling and yuloh technique](/operations/sculling-and-yuloh-technique.md), which draws heavily on his account of learning to scull.

## Joining the WaterTribe, and why he bought Enigma to race

Dave's racing came directly out of Matt Layden's example. By **2008** he had been inspired to try the marathon adventure races run by the **WaterTribe**, where Matt, competing his own tiny home-built cruisers ("some as small as 8 feet long"), had consistently placed at or near the top. The format's appeal was its simplicity: entrants launch from **above the high-water mark** at the start and then paddle or sail to the finish, over courses running from **65 miles** up to the **1,200-mile race around Florida**; meet the safety requirements, and if you and your crew can drag your craft into the water, "you were good to go."[34] See [Everglades Challenge](/merits/everglades-challenge.md) and [Ultimate Florida Challenge](/merits/ultimate-florida-challenge-2010.md).

That launch rule is the reason he owns a second boat. **[Little Cruiser](/matt-layden/little-cruiser.md)'s 1,600 pounds could not be moved off a beach single-handed**, so he bought one of Matt's older micro-cruisers to race instead: **[Enigma](/matt-layden/enigma.md), 180 pounds unloaded and 11 ft 10 in long**. In **September 2009** he won the inaugural **90-mile North Carolina Challenge** in the solo sailboat class, and in **2010** entered the **300-mile Everglades Challenge from Tampa to Key Largo**. It was the confidence built over those races that led him to take *Enigma* to the Bahamas solo in 2013.[34]

Both races were also seen from outside, by a fellow competitor who wrote them up. In the North Carolina Challenge, Dave sailed past his anchored boat in the night (the rest of the sailing fleet having stopped at the checkpoint on the race manager's warning of the forecast) and was seen next morning "beating close to the beaches" around Harkers Island in the 20–25 knot wind that drove the observer out of the race altogether; the outside record is the one that shows *how* he won it, by keeping going.[35] Six months later, at the Everglades Challenge start at Fort DeSoto, the same writer noted him on the beach as "Freebyrd (David Bolduc), a veteran of cruising the Bahamas in Matt's Paradox design," his *Enigma* drawn up near Matt's new [Elusion](/matt-layden/elusion.md).[36] His own account of the race that followed (chasing Matt in Matt's old boat and being left behind once Matt started paddling) is at [The 2010 Ultimate Florida Challenge](/merits/ultimate-florida-challenge-2010.md#fort-clinch-dave-bolducs-chase-and-the-spray-skirt-question-narrows).

## Design and performance across Layden's boats

Dave sailed [Little Cruiser](/matt-layden/little-cruiser.md) and [Paradox](/paradox.md) side by side for two and a half months, and gave the archive its account of *why* Paradox tracks stiffly and tacks hard (a deliberate trade-off Matt made for maximum directional stability, not a flaw) against Little Cruiser and [Swamp Thing](/matt-layden/swamp-thing.md)'s easier but less stable tacking. He is the source most of the archive's chine-runner, rudder, rig and ballast pages already credit by name for their firsthand comparisons across Matt's designs: the chine-extension and rudder experiments Matt tried and dropped; the quantified chine-runner sea trial Matt's own data supplied; the shape evolution that carried Swamp Thing's small square runners into the larger triangular ones now used on Paradox; the furling and reefing comparison that showed operator skill mattered as much as gear; and the standing advice to ballast a Paradox all the way up to her full 9 in cruising draft.

That comparison ran to Matt's other boats too. Little Cruiser's foredeck-stepped mast, unlike Paradox's, folds back on two hinged struts once a pin at its foot is pulled, a Little-Cruiser-specific detail with no Paradox equivalent. Cruising Enigma himself through the Exumas in 2013, partway through his engine-free solo crossing, Dave reported her "very spartan" next to Paradox from lived, day-to-day experience (everything stored behind leecloths for want of bilges, resupply needed often for want of water tanks or rain catchment) and reaffirmed the verdict all the same: "Paradox is pretty much the finest all weather microcruiser out there," even while having a great time in "the smaller sistership." Full comparison at [Enigma's 2013 update](/matt-layden/enigma.md#a-2013-update-cruising-enigma-firsthand-and-a-reaffirmed-verdict).[32] See [sailing characteristics](/operations/sailing-characteristics.md), [chine runners and windward performance](/chine-runners/windward-performance.md) and [rudder](/components/rudder.md#rudder-variety-across-layden-boats) for the fuller technical record these comparisons feed.

## Construction and materials

Dave is the archive's source for how Matt built the *original* Paradox (hauling materials to a remote building site by bicycle trailer, launching her down a ravine, in about four months of full-time work), testimony about watching Matt work, not a technique any builder could replicate. Full account at [the build and launch of the original Paradox](/boats/original-paradox.md).[2]

His own building has the same resourceful, frugal streak running through it: casting lofting weights and trim ballast from scrap tire lead rather than buying them,[16][17][18] judging a $35 eBay roll of 4 oz E-glass (meant for topsides, with only minor flaws) good enough to use, and demonstrating it the same day on his own toe-rail work,[19] building an $18 DIY all-round LED masthead light from parts sourced online[20] and hacking a GPS unit's firmware to swap its startup screen for a "REWARD $$$" anti-theft message instead of the stock warning.[22] Matt's own habit of field-repairing his boats with whatever lay to hand (a broken rudder blade replaced with a scrap of plywood, a splintered yard with a tree branch) left Dave with a standing resolution of his own: he now carries a foot-square piece of quarter-inch plywood and coarse sandpaper aboard for exactly that kind of repair.[33]

## Operations and cruising practice

The yuloh is "a MUST" aboard both his boats, not an occasional backup: sculling Little Cruiser runs 1.5-2 kt typically, up to 3 kt in sprints.[21] Going engine-free himself, by his own account four years before a 2010 restatement of the practice, built exactly this skill: he grew confident enough sculling Enigma to complete both the NC Challenge and the EC Challenge under sail and oar alone, the run-up to his fully engine-free 2013 solo Bahamas crossing.[30] He takes a more relaxed view of engines than either Matt or Sven Yrvind: he sees Matt's arguments and concedes a motor-free sailor becomes the better sailor for it, but personally likes an outboard as another "tool," a real point of difference from the purism the rest of the Bolducs' Matt Layden record records.[14]

## Building advice and the "first boat" question

Dave does not oversell Paradox as a good first boat for someone without prior woodworking experience: the hull itself is not necessarily complex, but the complete boat is detailed and time-consuming, and a part-timer should expect three to four times the roughly 3.5 months Matt took building the original full-time.[13][23] He is also, in his own words, a patient and unshowy mentor: a discouraged first-time builder was reassured that slow, iterative, mistake-and-redo work is normal and nothing to apologize for, a builder may be more capable than they think and won't know until trying.[24]

## Stories and community notes

**The Bolduc-Maxwell meeting.** Returning from the 2003 Bahamas cruise, Dave and Mindy spent a few days with a fellow correspondent and his wife, the archive's only recorded meeting between Dave and Mindy and [Glen Maxwell](/people/glen-maxwell.md) in person, at which Little Cruiser and "[Zoë](/boats/zoe.md)" were seen firsthand by each other's owners for the first time.[27] Dave also chronicled Matt's own boat in detail around that time: reporting the contemporaneous record of Matt's entry in the 2003 Everglades Challenge, guessing (correctly, as it turned out) that Matt would meet the race's above-high-tide launch rule by floating her off at high tide and hauling her back with a block and tackle, then later photographing the new sculling oar's bronze-rod tips and a string run over the cleats to keep the sheet from catching.[15][25][26]

**A second small-boat-design thread, beyond Layden.** Dave flagged a for-sale 40 ft version of [Chris Morejohn](/people/chris-morejohn.md)'s "Hogfish" sharpie design, remarking on its Paradox-style chine runners, and has himself sailed aboard Morejohn's original "Hogfish" in the Bahamas, in a stiff 30-plus-knot breeze and 8 ft seas she handled admirably; he and Mindy have long been tempted to buy her whenever she comes on the market, though living far inland they have no real need for a liveaboard boat.[28][29]

**His plans blurb, quoted verbatim on an outside weblog (September 2008).** The American small-craft weblog [70.8%](/references/70point8-blog.md) reproduced Dave's own ordering blurb in full and named the Bolducs "the conduit for Matt['s] plans": the fullest statement of his ordering terms made to a general readership rather than the mailing list. Full ordering mechanics at [building plans](/references/building-plans.md).[37]

**New photo pages, and a Tongue of the Ocean sighting.** After getting hold of a film scanner, he posted two further pages of digitized Paradox photographs to microcruising.com, with more site updates expected to follow, and in a follow-up separately pointed out a picture of Paradox cruising through the Tongue of the Ocean on the site's own "Poem" page.[38][39]

[34] [SAIL Magazine](/references/sail-magazine.md) - "Go Small, Go Now: A Cruising Couple and their Micro-Cruisers," David Bolduc, 2014-07-19: "By 2008 I was inspired by Matt to try some marathon adventure races organized by a group called the Watertribe. Competing his tiny home-built cruisers, some as small as 8 feet long, Matt had consistently placed at or near the top in these events"; the format (launch from above the high-water mark, then paddle or sail to the finish, over events "as short as 65 miles, or… as long as their 1,200-mile race around Florida") - "If you met the safety requirements and you or your crew could drag your craft into the water, you were good to go!"; "Since I knew I could not move Little Cruiser's 1,600 pounds off the beach by myself, I bought one of Matt's older micro-cruisers to race. This boat, Enigma, weighed only 180 pounds unloaded and was just 11ft 10in long"; "In September of 2009 I surprised myself by winning the inaugural 90-mile North Carolina Challenge race in the solo sailboat class. In 2010 I entered the 300-mile Everglades Challenge, from Tampa to Key Largo." The article is written throughout in the first person singular ("my wife, Mindy, and me"), with the closing credit "Photos courtesy of David and Mindy Bolduc"; the page's "By Webmaster" byline is a site-migration artifact.
