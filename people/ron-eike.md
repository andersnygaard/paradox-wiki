# Ron Eike

**Ron Eike** was a sailing-canoe sailor, with firsthand experience of Dabbler's lug sails and a close acquaintance with the small-craft sailmaking world.[1][2] Racing the 2007 Everglades Challenge himself, he saw Matt Layden's new boat, Sand Flea, pass mid-race.[3]

## Small-craft sailmakers

Sailing-canoe sailmaking was where his knowledge counted, and it served Paradox builders needing a lug sail. He had used **Dabbler**, of Virginia, himself, and knew **Douglas Fowler**, of upstate New York, as the sailing-canoe community's favorite, credited with at least one set of Caledonia Yawl sails; **Todd Bradshaw**, author of *Canoe Rig*, belonged to the same small-craft sailmaking world.[1][2] See [Sail: buying vs. building](/components/rig/sail.md#buying-vs-building-a-sail-recommendations-and-firsthand-accounts).

## The WaterTribe Everglades Challenge

As a fellow competitor in that race, he saw Sand Flea up close mid-race: a decked-over multichine pram with a single leeboard, among rivals at least twice her size as she neared the finish in Florida Bay.[3] See [Sand Flea: race record](/matt-layden/sand-flea.md#race-record).

## Rig materials

A composite mast built elsewhere gave him a data point for the Paradox mast's own spruce-core question: a gaff laid up over a tapered PVC-pipe mandrel, glassed and wrapped in two layers of tubular carbon fiber, gained little strength from the PVC core itself, while a spruce core, of the kind proposed for a Paradox mast, would add plenty.[4] See [Mast: a birdmouth-and-carbon composite mast, revisited](/components/rig/mast.md#a-birdmouth-and-carbon-composite-mast-revisited).
