# Andreas Westerberg

**Andreas Westerberg** is a builder in Sweden who began a [Paradox](/paradox.md) in late 2021, working from plans bought from Wanda Elliott after an email to Dave Bolduc went unanswered.[1] He built a 3D model of the whole boat from the plans' drawings before cutting anything, then CNC-routed the bulkheads and other parts rather than shaping them by hand, test-fitting a set of bulkhead outlines within weeks of buying the plans.[2]

## Modelling before cutting

Westerberg models before he cuts. Working out the hull's parts and their fit in software first, he machined the bulkheads and other pieces on a CNC router rather than laying them out and cutting them by hand.[2] The model had to account for the small curve built into three of the four bulkheads' sides, a detail a hand layout leaves implicit but a CAD model has to represent explicitly; the fuller geometry of that curve is recorded at [construction/panel-layout.md](/construction/panel-layout.md#bulkhead-side-curves).[3] He modelled the mast components the same way, finding them harder to picture correctly from the drawings than the hull had been.[4]

The same instinct for checking rather than trusting a claim carried over to the bench. Warned that clamping an epoxy joint too hard can starve it of resin instead of strengthening it, he tested the claim directly: clamping a dab of unthickened epoxy as hard as he could between two scrap pieces of plywood, reasoning this should produce the worst possible case if the risk were real. The general lesson it confirmed is recorded at [construction/fastening-and-gluing.md](/construction/fastening-and-gluing.md#controlling-epoxy-squeeze-out).[5]

## The boat

A regional plywood shortage left only 12mm stock available where 18mm was specified for the bottom; judging the difference small, he used 12mm for the bottom and the deck alike.[6] He sealed the compartments forward of bulkhead 1 and aft of bulkhead 4 for buoyancy, each closed with its own hatch,[7] and avoided cutting the transom for a tiller shaft by rerouting the steering as a block and tackle from each side of the rudder through pipes into the cockpit instead, a conversion whose rudder-horn fitting is recorded at [components/rudder.md](/components/rudder.md#rudder-horns-for-a-tiller-less-pipe-routed-steering-conversion).[8] For ballast he salvaged an old steel drill rod buried on his property since the 1980s, about 250 kg, planned as cut bar stock; see [weight and ballast](/design/weight-and-ballast.md#ballast-material-choice-lead-against-concrete-iron-and-other-alternatives) for how that compares with other builders' ballast choices.[9]
