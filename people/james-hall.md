# James Hall

**James "Jim" Hall** was an English Paradox owner and sailor who bought **"[Faith](/boats/faith.md)"** from her builder, [Bill Serjeant](/people/bill-serjeant.md), around 2008 — introducing himself to the mailing list for the first time in July 2011 as "the chap who bought Faith from Bill Serjeant a few years ago," and describing himself plainly as a Paradox *user* rather than a Paradox *builder*.[1] He sailed her for eight years out of Poole Harbour and along the South West coast, mostly alone, until his death in August 2016.

## Sailing Faith, mostly alone

Hall's own pattern of cruising set him apart from the other UK owners he knew: rather than day sails or short weekends, he favoured long solo passages, typically around ten days at a stretch, and had managed four such cruises in a single year by 2012 — "'Nobby No Mates' - that's me - an anti social loner....."[2] He knew [Alastair Law](/people/alastair-law.md) well enough to cross paths with him while sailing, but had, as of that year, still not met [Pete Martin](/people/pete-martin.md) or seen his boat in person.[2] A twelve-day cruise that same summer took Faith through the Solent and Chichester Harbour, launched and recovered at Ridge Wharf; one hard beat back to Poole from Oxey Lake against a Force 5-6 westerly sent him looking afterward for a better-shaped replacement sail.[3][4] He timed his launches deliberately around the tide rather than his own convenience: the boatyard he used only allowed its slipway and storage compound to be reached while staff were on site, so he would launch onto the dry slipway itself while the tide was still out, secure the car and trailer in the compound before the water returned, and be away over the horizon by the time the yard locked up for the night — the same technique, for a different reason, that Alastair Law used habitually aboard "[Little Jim](/boats/little-jim.md)."[5]

He never built a Paradox himself and said so plainly when asked for construction advice he could not give, but he spoke of his own boat's builder with real respect: asked in 2015 for chine-log technique he had no firsthand knowledge to offer, he added that Bill Serjeant "made an excellent job of building her."[6] Faith's original yuloh, made by Serjeant during the build, was still the one aboard in 2013, its curve very slightly more pronounced than when new.[7]

## Keeping an older boat going

Faith came to Hall without foam flotation at all — a choice Serjeant had made deliberately while building her, trading the buoyancy for storage and living space — and Hall improvised his own emergency buoyancy in its place: the forward compartment, awkward to reach for gear storage anyway, packed with empty plastic screw-top bottles; the stern end carrying hollow plastic fenders, a part-full water can, a home-made portable toilet, and snap-lidded dry-food containers for buoyancy that varied with how much food and water happened to be aboard. Years of accumulated gear under the cabin sole — a 15 kg take-apart storm anchor and 15 m of chain, tools, spares, a Honda outboard, a large battery — had made the boat steadily heavier despite his own wish to stop adding to it, and he fitted a manual bilge pump under the sole as a result.[8]

A serious crack turned up at both ends of the trailer's axle beam in 2011, traced to the "indispension" independent suspension units' near-total lack of springing under load — a ride harsh enough, he found, to scatter the boat's own contents "as if a bomb has gone off." Rather than patch it, he built a replacement trailer around a rear axle salvaged from a scrapped van, mostly from steel angle to avoid trapped, rusting sea water, with a tipping bed that lets the boat run off the rollers under gravity even on a level, dry surface — finished and running by December 2011.[1][9] He kept a charcoal heater built from copper pipe for cold-weather cruising, supplemented on occasion by an unvented kerosene pressure lantern for extra warmth on short spells with the hatch cracked to vent the fumes; asked why he bothered with a heater at all when another owner felt no need for one, his answer was that Faith was already "fully insulated by the junk all around the inside."[10] In 2015 he epoxied a strip of stainless steel to the stem's curved lower section to protect it from wear.[11]

## A blog and a running voice on cruising

Hall kept a cruising blog, `jimtimes.wordpress.com`, posting accounts of his Faith cruises from 2014 on, and asked practical questions of his own — among them whether anyone had tried an electronic tiller pilot aboard a Paradox, and how well it had worked.[12][13] He was remembered on the mailing list as a familiar, down-to-earth voice on cruising and sailing, both in his own advice and through the blog.[14]

## Death (August 2016)

James Hall died on 23 August 2016, having felt unwell while cruising Faith around Poole Harbour and returning home, where he died soon after.[14]
