# Greg Lockhart

**Greg Lockhart** (Yahoo handle `lockhart_greg`) is a Paradox builder who introduced himself to the list in June 2011, admiring the finished, teak-decked "[Odyssey](/boats/odyssey.md)" and naming her one of the boats he intended to use as his own example.[1] His plans arrived within a week of ordering; that same weekend he bought material for deck beams and bulkheads, and named his boat **"Endeavour."**[2] Full build account: [boats/endeavour.md](/boats/endeavour.md).

## Building in "Zoë"'s memory

Construction began the same week [Glen C. Maxwell](/people/glen-maxwell.md)'s "[Zoë](/boats/zoe.md)" was destroyed by fire in transit to her new owner; he hoped "Endeavour" might one day merit comparison to her, building to [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)'s plans with "Zoë" herself as his example.[2] He planned to document the build with a newly bought digital camera and expected a build blog running within the week.[2][3] "Endeavour" is his fourth build, following three earlier boats; a working tool list drawn from that experience, shared with a fellow prospective builder the same month, lives at [the builder's toolkit — a 2011 recurrence](/construction/builders-toolkit.md#a-2011-recurrence-a-fourth-builders-own-tool-list).[4] By late June, all seven deck beams were complete and the first two bulkheads cut, and final assembly work on the bulkheads (cleats, deck beams, sole risers) followed within days; by late July all bulkheads and exposed deck beams were epoxied, sanded, faired, and painted, with a working three-dimensional hull hoped for by 1 September. A digital camera finally in hand that October, he moved his own assembly target to the month's end and welcomed his uncle, William Johnston — an accomplished aircraft and boat builder — as a new list member drawn in by the build.[12] A photo album followed days later, showing the almost-completed cabin top with port-hole style windows and a one-piece "lift out" hatch chosen over the stock sliding hatch.[13] Hull scarfing began the same month and reached a first three-dimensional "dry fit" by mid-October, with a question about the stem's own construction resolved along the way; asked why he chose separate oval ports over the plans' continuous window, he explained wanting a personal touch that still preserved visibility, extending the same choice to the lift-out hatch so the boom stays visible from inside.[14][15] Full build account: [boats/endeavour.md](/boats/endeavour.md).[5][6][7]

## A tabernacle mast-step proposal, and a mixed reaction

Studying the finished bulkheads, Greg floated a genuine design change to set his own boat apart: on bulkhead #2, replacing the angled vent trunk and its cabinet with a square hollow column running the full bulkhead height, serving as a tabernacle for the mast, plus two small dorade vents at the forward cabin corners. The idea drew a skeptical "if it ain't broke, why fix it?" from [Alastair Law](/people/alastair-law.md) and a more encouraging "make it your own" from a fellow correspondent, plus a real-world reference point from [Alberto Callejon](/people/alberto-callejon.md), whose own "[Cachivache](/boats/cachivache.md)" already carries a widened cabin. Full exchange and citations: [boats/endeavour.md](/boats/endeavour.md#a-tabernacle-mast-step-proposal-for-bulkhead-2-and-a-mixed-reaction).[8][9][10][11]
