# Greg Lockhart

**Greg Lockhart** found the [Paradox](/paradox.md) by chance. A comment he left in May 2011, under a UK weblog's photographs of [Alastair Law](/people/alastair-law.md)'s "[Little Jim](/boats/little-jim.md)," records three earlier boats, the discovery of the design, a retirement boat "perfect for a sailing bachelor like myself" and an intended cruise to Australia and French Polynesia.[1] He had also been admiring another finished Paradox, "[Odyssey](/boats/odyssey.md)," for her teak decks and electrical panel, and set both boats as his example when he ordered plans and began building the following month.[2] He named his own Paradox **"[Endeavour](/boats/endeavour.md)"** and began construction the same month [Glen Maxwell](/people/glen-maxwell.md)'s "[Zoë](/boats/zoe.md)" was destroyed by fire in transit to her new owner, hoping "Endeavour" might one day merit the comparison.[3] Full build account: [boats/endeavour.md](/boats/endeavour.md).

## Building his own way

An experienced builder by then, on his fourth boat, he carried forward a "nut-and-bolt, meat-and-potatoes" tool list from the three before her.[4] He treated the plans as a starting point rather than a template: the continuous side window became a row of individual oval ports finished with trim rings, and the stock sliding hatch gave way to a lift-out one fitted with its own port so he could watch the boom from below, changes made for a personal touch rather than a functional gain.[5] On the same reasoning he proposed reworking a bulkhead's vent trunk into a mast tabernacle. Full account of the build's departures from the plans: [boats/endeavour.md](/boats/endeavour.md#a-tabernacle-mast-step-proposal-for-bulkhead-2-and-a-mixed-reaction).[6]
