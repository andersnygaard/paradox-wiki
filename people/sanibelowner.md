# sanibelowner

**sanibelowner** posted to the mailing list under that Yahoo account — no location is ever given, and a first name, "Andy," surfaces only once, in a November 2013 message. His messages on record document his Paradox, **"[Moxie](/boats/moxie.md)"**, through 2012: construction-photograph captions in February, then the bottom's finished lay-up schedule in August — the archive preserves his captions and material lists, not the images themselves.[1][2][3]

## Two photographs, and a caption correction

His first message apologized for photographs being "over due," having lost most of them, but shared "a few."[1] The next day he corrected one caption: a picture he had labeled "sheer clamps in place" should instead have read "pre-sheer clamps in place" — showing the hull just before, not after, that stage of construction.[2]

## The bottom's lay-up schedule (August 2012)

Six months on, he gave the bottom's full material recipe: 3/4 in high-grade plywood ("consolation for marine grade"), three layers of 24 oz woven roving, over 2 gallons of epoxy in three coats (one mixed with 407 filler), 3 1/2 coats of grey undercoat, and two coats of black bottom paint.[3] A month later, answering a fellow builder's question about substituting thinner plywood, argued for keeping to the plans' own thickness — calling it the boat's structural foundation and part of what gives her her character. See [plywood grade and foam flotation debate — a 2012 recurrence: the 9mm-substitution question restated, and a structural-weight/ballast tradeoff](/construction/plywood-grades-and-foam-flotation.md#a-2012-recurrence-the-9mm-substitution-question-restated-and-a-structural-weightballast-tradeoff).[4]

## A November 2013 return: a sheet-hole plan, and a sail of his own

After a year's silence, he asked a fellow builder's opinion on drilling his own mainsheet sheet hole at the upper transom, in the same spot the builder had mounted a pair of chafe-curing fairleads; full exchange at [boats/scout.md](/boats/scout.md#a-transom-fairlead-fix-for-the-mainsheet-refined-with-a-fellow-builders-questions).[5] He signed the message "Andy" — the only time a first name appears anywhere in his messages on record — and mentioned plans to sew his own sail that winter, incorporating a visibility window into its design.[5]
