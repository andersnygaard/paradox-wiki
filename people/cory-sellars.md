# Cory Sellars

**Cory Sellars** is a carpenter in **Kincardine, Ontario, Canada**, finishing a Paradox rescued from a burn pile after the death of her original builder, **Brian Prescott** — not Prescott's first attempt at a boat. Sellars named her **"[Prescott Eagle](/boats/prescott-eagle.md)"** in his memory, aiming for an August 2019 maiden voyage on the Great Lakes.[1]

## Continuing another builder's hull

Towing the boat home on a trailer in a snowstorm, Sellars took his own measurements and found the hull tracked closely to the plans, with screws used in place of nails for the sides and bottom; the hull itself was complete but for epoxy sheathing and some cut-outs, with the interior only just begun. He brought his own tools to the finishing work, including a CNC machine and a 2 ft x 3 ft duplicator.[1] Full build account: [boats/prescott-eagle.md](/boats/prescott-eagle.md).

His first list question — a sharp bow in place of the plans' snub nose, answered with no performance difference expected either way — and the wave-handling discussion it led to are covered in full at [boats/prescott-eagle.md — a first design question](/boats/prescott-eagle.md#a-first-design-question-snub-nose-versus-sharp-bow).

He came to the Paradox as an already-experienced Great Lakes dinghy sailor, mostly on Lake Huron and Georgian Bay, and set out to build her specifically for touring Ontario's **Bruce Peninsula**. By January 2021 he described the build as roughly half complete, and by then knew of two other Paradoxes already sailing the Great Lakes — one on Lake Ontario, one on Georgian Bay — along with an unconfirmed report of an Enigma 360 hull somewhere in southwestern Ontario whose completion status he did not know.[2]
