# Nickolas Bell

**Nickolas Bell** is a builder and sailor in **South Australia** who built the [Paradox](/paradox.md) **"[Thor](/boats/thor.md)"** and launched her on the Murray River in June 2015.[1] He sailed her hard along the Yorke Peninsula and Spencer Gulf coast for seven years before selling her in 2022 to [Mark Brothers](/people/mark-brothers.md), who renamed her **"Pamela B."**[2] Bell is known for a fastening method that puts no screw into raw wood, and for what he did after selling Thor: a tiny home-built pontoon houseboat, ridden 1,550 km down the Murray. Full build and sailing record: [boats/thor.md](/boats/thor.md).

## A fastening method: nothing driven into raw wood

Bell's hull went together dry, screwed rather than glued and checked and rechecked until it was symmetrical. Only then was it glued, and days later every screw came back out, its hole backfilled with a wooden dowel, so that no metal fastener was left in the wood to heat and crack the ply as it cured. Any screw a fitting genuinely needed later went into a hole drilled oversize and backfilled with resin and powder, driven into the cured resin rather than raw wood: "the long way around but it never leads to cracks."[3] The core of it, a temporary screw pulled once the glue cures, is [Glen Maxwell](/people/glen-maxwell.md)'s own idea, first recorded in 2001; the wooden-dowel backfill and the two-stage resin-plug method for a later screw are Bell's own additions to it (see [fastening and gluing](/construction/fastening-and-gluing.md#a-builders-alternative-removable-screws)). The same laminating jig that built the deck and cabin beams also gave him a chair for the local café, built from lumber-yard offcuts and leftover resin.[4]

## A second boat, and a jammed sail

Thor was not Bell's only boat. His first build, simpler and quicker than a Paradox, was a Stevensons Pocket Cruiser, kept mainly to hone building skills; he judged her a lakes-and-rivers craft rather than an offshore one, wet and uncomfortable in rougher water despite her own built-in flotation.[5] Her rig gave him one bad afternoon: motoring in toward a marina, the sail locked fast up the mast, its blocks bound tight by rope pressure after being laced too tightly. He forced the anchor over the side to slow his approach, narrowly avoided a collision with a large cruiser, then climbed the mast to free the sail by hand. Staggering the halyard blocks so they angle apart fixed the problem for good.[6]

## Sailing by testing, not by trust

Bell measured before he believed. He tested the flotation of every kind of wood in the build by standing a cut stick of each in a jar of water and reading how much stayed above the surface, sizing the built-in foam and ballast to match,[7] and he sought a university engineering department's advice on where to place the ballast rather than take a rule of thumb on faith.[8] Sailing Thor hard taught him exactly where and why a Paradox broaches: running before a sea, she will round up and roll if the helmsman's attention wanders, and his rule is to reef hard and steer well off the wind rather than press on when tired.[9] Full sailing and knockdown record: [boats/thor.md](/boats/thor.md) and [self-righting and capsize recovery](/design/self-righting-and-capsize-recovery.md#firsthand-knockdown-accounts).

## After Thor: a pontoon houseboat down the Murray

Bell sold Thor in 2022: "I had a paradox, I sold it after years of adventure."[10] He then built a tiny pontoon houseboat and rode it the length of the Murray, launching at Yarrawonga and covering 1,550 km downstream in 79 days with the motor idling for steering only. He judged that a Paradox could have made the same trip just as well, the shallows if anything favouring the smaller boat.[10]
