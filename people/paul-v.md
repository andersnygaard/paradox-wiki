# Paul V.

**Paul V.** was an American builder who worked through a wood-heated "north woods" winter to build a Paradox to the plans, then went on to design and build a boat of his own, **"[Whaleback](/similar-boat-designs/whaleback.md)"**, a dory-hulled chine-runner sharpie confirmed under construction by 2006. Active on the Paradox builders' correspondence from 2001 to 2006, he volunteered alongside **[Dave Bolduc](/people/bolduc/dave.md)** as one of its two moderators, keeping new membership open through a run of spam. He also filed some of the liveliest real-time reporting on record for the 2003 Everglades Challenge, watching **[Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)** take an early lead.

## Building a Paradox in a frozen workshop

He is first found on the Paradox builders' correspondence wondering what let [Swamp Thing](/matt-layden/swamp-thing.md) sail better than [Paradox](/paradox.md) or [Little Cruiser](/matt-layden/little-cruiser.md), a keel, a narrower beam, a lighter displacement, recalling her only as "the one with oar holes on the sides."[1]

By the autumn of 2002 he was building a Paradox to the plans, through a winter cold enough that his woodburner "tries to heat my frozen workshop," as he put it, thanking a fellow builder for a morale-boosting trip report of a Paradox flying through the water while his own deck beams went together.[2] His stated principle for the build was that "a boat is far more than the sum of its parts": changing one part of a refined design changes the whole system, so a pram hood was fine to consider but a stern extension or a rig change would need a full, independent redesign of its own. He had thought about designing a boat of his own from scratch and ruled it out, judging it would take at least three attempts to work the bugs out of a new design. His own planned departures were smaller: a hinged, watertight hatch in place of the stock sliding hatch, lead ballast in place of the built-in water tank, plus extra insulation and bug protection, aimed at a planned cold-climate trip toward mandow.ca.[3] The same caution carried into his plans for open water: he called himself "a real chicken" about big water, meant to prove any boat of his own first on Lake Michigan's sheltered water, and wondered whether Quebec authorities would require a guide for a colder run further north.[4]

The build itself progressed through deck-beam lamination, six layers of thin plywood and epoxy per beam, a technique he defended against a doubt about plywood grain, documented only as far as he knew it worked, in a public "Build Book," a discipline he carried over from writing build documentation years earlier at S2 Yachts.[5][6] By January 2003 he was gearing up to start cutting out bulkheads, still keeping the Build Book and anticipating "epoxy mixing weather" within a few months.[7] A search for a building sequence documented beyond the plans themselves turned up Don Elliott's paid "Building Paradox" series and a spare plans CD from [Charles Wilson](/people/charles-wilson.md).[8][9][10] He questioned whether the transom's vent baffle, built for the tiller ropes, was even necessary, and said plainly he was not sure he trusted the rope tiller as the boat's main steering system.[11][12] He also worked out a lower-tech way to ballast the chine runners, packing lead birdshot into thickened epoxy by hand rather than casting or buying pre-shaped weights; see [chine runner construction](/chine-runners/construction.md#lower-tech-casting-lead-birdshot-in-epoxy).[13]

## Volunteering as a moderator

By late September 2002 a run of spam carrying bare links prompted him to propose requiring a moderator's approval before new members could join.[14] [Glen Maxwell](/people/glen-maxwell.md) judged the idea workable but said it would need a second or third volunteer moderator to cover it, since he expected to be away sailing for a couple of months himself, and floated a digest format as a lighter alternative while he asked for volunteers.[15] A follow-up suggested trying the digest first, based on another community's report that it had cut spam back to only occasional.[16] [David LeBlanc](/people/david-leblanc.md) argued against switching to a web-based reading interface instead, given the extra advertising it carried on top of what already ran through the e-mail digest.[17] Glen Maxwell's own words settled the outcome: his call for volunteer moderators was answered by [Dave Bolduc](/people/bolduc/dave.md) and Paul V., covering the correspondence between them well enough that new membership could finally be closed to unapproved signups.[18]

## Watching the 2003 Everglades Challenge

News that [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md) had entered [Paradox](/paradox.md) in that year's Everglades Challenge drew an early, curious question from him: whether her shoal draft would let her cut shortcuts through water too shallow for the rest of the fleet.[19] He went on to file some of the liveliest real-time checkpoint reporting on record for the race, following it through the public WaterTribe results site. Two days in, over the roughly 300-mile course, Layden was still some ten hours behind the paddling kayakers at Checkpoint 2, though he had managed a good night's sleep sailing offshore: "there is a long way to go."[20] By the third checkpoint the gap had closed entirely: "Matt's in front!", logged in at 14:40 and, as of 22:15 that Monday, the only competitor to have reached it at all.[21] Once Paradox had won, he allowed he had "never thought of the Paradox as a racing boat, more of a slow but steady cruiser," and wondered aloud why no Hobie-cat catamarans had entered a race that suited them so well.[22]

## Designing and building "Whaleback"

He had been sketching a boat of his own since February 2002, a dory-hulled sharpie proposed as a self-righting alternative to Paradox's own chine-keel hull.[23]

Having just spent a winter proving he could build someone else's design faithfully to the plans, he went back to a design of his own: by 2006 she had picked up chine runners inspired by Matt Layden's boats, and he named her "Whaleback," confirming she was under construction and working through many of the same problems Paradox's own builders faced.[24] Design questions from other builders followed once the build went public; he kept the two conversations apart, answering construction questions here and sending design-change discussion of Paradox herself elsewhere. Her full design and construction record is at [Whaleback](/similar-boat-designs/whaleback.md).

## Disambiguation

His citations carry three related account labels, and his own signature confirms they are one person.[25] He is distinct from [Paul (NZ)](/people/paul-nz.md), an earlier, unrelated member from southern New Zealand, and from "kayaker37," **Paul Raymond**, per his own October 2021 display name and corroborating the signature "Paul" he had used throughout. Paul Raymond is a separate, long-running correspondent, first appearing in a February 2002 exchange titled "Design"; see [blue-water suitability](/design/blue-water-suitability.md).
