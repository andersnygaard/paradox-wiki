# Nels Arvidson

**Nels Arvidson** is a small-boat sailor from **British Columbia, Canada**. He never built a Paradox: both the stock hull and Matt Layden's smaller Enigma ran too small for the crew he sometimes sails with, so he chose a Bolger "Micro" instead.[1] The Micro's four-person day-sailing capacity, centerline engine and cat-yawl rig suited him better. He sailed her for more than a decade.[2] The wish for a Paradox of his own, simplified with "no water tanks and a few other changes," dates to 2002.[3] His own cruising ground lies in British Columbia's interior. Firsthand northern travel grounded his caution about colder water: years spent servicing weather equipment and training radio operators by light plane along Hudson Bay's western coast.[4]

## Hull materials, chine runners and Paradox variants

His own preference ran toward Paradox's proven plywood-and-glass construction over a steel or welded-aluminum hull, tougher for her size and cheaper to build. See [Aluminum and steel Paradox](/design/ideas/aluminum-construction-idea.md).[5] He also treats a chine runner's grip as coming from a box hull's own resistance to sideways slippage; on that reasoning, having no keel below the hull is itself a safety feature off a lee shore, letting a boat slide sideways rather than trip. See [How chine runners work](/chine-runners/how-they-work.md).[6] A 20 percent scale-up of Paradox, approved in principle by Matt Layden, is described at [Building Paradox larger](/design/larger-paradox.md).[7] A motorsailer variant of Paradox for inland cruising closer to home, proposed in 2002, called for a slipper stern enclosing a small outboard and a pedestal-mounted mast raised on a forestay for clearing bridges among the changes discussed, judged in the end to need a full redesign rather than a modification of the stock boat. See [A Paradox motorsailer](/design/ideas/motorsailer-idea.md).[8]

## Organizing the Yrvind book translation

He organized a volunteer project to translate Sven Yrvind's book into English, working out a chapter-by-chapter pipeline and lining up the translators himself, though the translation work was done by others. See [Sven Yrvind's book](/references/yrvind-book.md).[9]

## A life of small boats: a Bolger Micro, a Robbsboat and wilderness travel

His Bolger "Micro," kept outdoors year-round, carried him for more than a decade; her Cetol-finished brightwork and painted interior showed the wear of a dozen years in the weather.[10] In later years he took on a second project apart from Paradox, modifying a Jim Michalak "Robbsboat" and gathering cast-iron gym weights for her ballast.[11] Small boats had been a fixture of his life well before that. Years of wilderness canoeing, some days covering 20 to 25 miles, came first, followed by a run of small hulls carried and stored through a series of apartments during years of work travel.[12][13] His home river runs too swift to paddle against for any distance, so his own trips are planned downstream rather than up.[14]
