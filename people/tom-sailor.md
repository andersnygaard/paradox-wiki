# Tom (squared bow)

**Tom** restarted a long-shelved [Paradox](/paradox.md) in February 2020 and carried it from stored, already epoxy-coated bulkheads to a flipped hull by the end of May.[1][2] He had built before: a small Wharram outrigger canoe in stitch and glue. The Paradox proved the easier build of the two.[2]

## Returning to the design

He began the Paradox years earlier, drawn to its submarine feel, shoal draft and interior space as well as what he called its sheer quirkiness, though less taken with its lines and furling system. He drifted instead toward a Harmonica shanty boat, engineless and shorter than the Paradox but similarly enclosed and single-handed, then toward a Wharram Pahi 26 as a retirement dream until her scale, cost and complexity proved too much and her frames were left behind, unused. The Paradox kept its pull; he came back to it simply because, as he put it, "it's just so practical."[1] By mid-2019 he already had the frames and transom built and a stock of CD-grade 19 mm plywood on hand for a strong bottom, with extra ballast in mind.[3]

## Building loosely and true

He placed himself in the tradition of 1970s Californian surfers Don Elliott describes in his own construction manual, builders who worked to looser tolerances and let epoxy, roving and mat carry the strength: "That's me, not fine [joinery]."[2] Dry-fitting the hull needed real force: he found the sides had to be pressed from the outside to bring them true, put down to the two sides carrying different degrees of stiffness; he settled the fit against a centreline, square bulkheads and diagonals held within a centimetre, catching a bad bevel cut and a broken scarf before gluing. Full account: [hull assembly, truing the hull](/construction/hull-assembly-sequence.md#truing-the-hull-the-diagonal-comparison-method).[4] The hull was flipped on 30 May 2020.[2]

## Departures from the plan

His build does not follow the plans exactly. He squared up the bow rather than keeping its standard profile, in the manner of traditional timber boats or a modern catamaran, roughing out the change in April 2020.[5] He intends a [junk rig](/design/ideas/junk-rig.md) for the ease and speed of reefing, in place of the stock furling gear he found off-putting, having earlier weighed a bowsprit and gaff main to make more of the boat's short mast.[1][6] Before restarting, he had also weighed building in Divinycell foam core after reading Sven Yrvind, noting that Yrvind's own Exlex was under construction in 30 mm Divinycell and epoxy, while judging the lighter, longer and narrower [Enigma](/matt-layden/enigma.md) closer to Yrvind's own ideal than the Paradox.[7]
