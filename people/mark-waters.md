# Mark Waters

**Mark Waters** is a Paradox builder who introduced himself to the group in February 2006 from Los Angeles, California, registering his boat with a UK build registry; his Paradox — later named **"[Raffles](/boats/raffles.md)"** — was already 90% complete at the time, built strictly to the plans. He had previously built a Selway-Fisher 50/50 sailing canoe.[1] He relocated to Portland, Oregon by October 2008, where a 2014 community census records him and the boat; his 2006 Los Angeles self-identification and the census entry are a genuine move six years apart rather than a conflict.[2][3] By November 2010, replying to a West Coast newcomer's inquiry, he confirmed he still had his Paradox and was then living in the Sacramento, California area — a further waypoint between the 2008 Portland report and the 2014 Portland census entry, not otherwise reconciled or explained in the record.[19] By January 2011 he had sailed only Folsom Lake since that move, and proposed sailing together on the San Francisco Bay with "[Zoë](/boats/zoe.md)"'s new owner that summer.[20] His finished interior became well regarded within the group, and a 2014 retrospective called his Paradox "one of the best built."[4]

The full build, modification, ballasting, sailing, and wind-vane record for his boat lives at [boats/raffles.md](/boats/raffles.md) — including his own detailed owner's assessment of how she sails, what he would change, and how the stock size suits a 5'10", 160 lb sailor.[12]

## The self-steering wind vane

Waters' longest single project was a rudder-head-mount self-steering wind vane for "Raffles," pursued from August 2008 to its completion in June 2009 — opening with a search for design sources and a *Self-Steering Under Sail* PDF, working through Cape Horn and Walt Murray Model 90-93 geometry, building a model over a weekend and then two rival models at once, questioning the standard 5 in x 32 in x 9 in blade size, and finishing with a mounting problem that risked "an unplanned swim" every time the vane needed adjustment.[5][6][7][8][9][10] The build is narrated in full at [boats/raffles.md — the self-steering wind vane](/boats/raffles.md#the-self-steering-wind-vane), and the general technique it drew out lives on [operations/self-steering.md](/operations/self-steering.md).

## Contributions

- **Epoxy.** Built the whole of "Raffles" with System Three epoxy, recommending it to other builders for its price, easy mixing, and customer service, and pointed to Aircraft Spruce in Corona as a Southern California supplier stocking it. See [epoxy brands, chemistry, and cure behavior — MAS epoxy validated for general use, and a California supplier](/construction/epoxy-brands-and-chemistry.md#mas-epoxy-validated-for-general-use-and-a-california-supplier).[15]
- **Scarfing and plywood joinery.** Advised backing a plain butt joint with a butt block rather than relying on tape and epoxy alone,[16] agreed the plywood veneers themselves are a good visual guide to a scarf's flatness and straightness, recommended the Gougeon Brothers' *Gougeon Brothers on Boat Construction* as the most comprehensive epoxy-plywood boatbuilding book available, and encouraged new builders to dive in without fear of early mistakes, practicing first on cheap construction-grade fir before committing to costlier finished plywood.[17] See [construction/scarf-joints.md — choosing between a scarf, a butt block, and a taped butt joint](/construction/scarf-joints.md#choosing-between-a-scarf-a-butt-block-and-a-taped-butt-joint).
- **Interior joinery.** His mahogany interior finish was influential in its own right: a newer California builder, admiring the work in photographs, e-mailed him directly to ask what wood he had used and adopted the same choice. See [boats/wizard-spawn.md — mahogany for the interior, inspired by another builder's finish](/boats/wizard-spawn.md#mahogany-for-the-interior-inspired-by-another-builders-finish).[18]
- **Ballast.** Voted for 300 lb ballast in an August 2006 group poll, one of only two responses,[13] and settled on steel weight-lifting weights rather than lead for his own boat — a choice made to keep toxic lead away from his young son. See [weight, ballast, and displacement — ballast material choice](/design/weight-and-ballast.md#ballast-material-choice-lead-against-concrete-iron-and-other-alternatives).[14]
- **Auxiliary power.** Contributed to the pedal-drive-outboard discussion with a question about propeller sizing and getting drive through the hull without major penetrations, and reported the sculling oar effective but hard to use except in a near-dead calm. See [auxiliary outboard power](/operations/auxiliary-outboard-power.md#a-pedal-drive-outboard-conceived-for-ironsides-and-the-alternatives-it-drew).[11]

## Sale of "Raffles" (January 2016)

By January 2016, "Raffles" had been sold — bought from Folsom, California by Curtis Clark and moved to Scottsdale, Arizona. Full account, including Clark's own decade-earlier history with the group, at [boats/raffles.md — sale to Curtis Clark](/boats/raffles.md#sale-to-curtis-clark-and-a-move-to-scottsdale-arizona-january-2016).[21] Three years later, having seen photographs of the boat sailing on under a new name, **"Melandro,"** Waters wrote back to the group simply to say he was happy she was still under sail — and Clark himself answered within the hour, confirming the boat is one and the same: "Hi Mark I have your boat."[22][23] Full account: [boats/raffles.md — Waters resurfaces](/boats/raffles.md#waters-resurfaces-seeing-raffles-sailing-on-as-melandro-january-2019).

## Disambiguation: vs. Mark Brothers

Mark Waters is not **[Mark Brothers](/people/mark-brothers.md)** of Newcastle, New South Wales, Australia, who owns "Pamela B" and commissioned the larger, higher-peaked Mk3 sail from Really Simple Sails. The two share only a forename and must not be merged.
