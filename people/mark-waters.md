# Mark Waters

**Mark Waters** is a Paradox builder from **Los Angeles, California**, whose boat was already 90 percent complete when first recorded in February 2006, built strictly to Matt Layden's plans, after previously building a Selway-Fisher 50/50 sailing canoe.[1] He named her **"[Raffles](/boats/raffles.md)"** and by 2008 had relocated to Portland, Oregon.[2] Her finish, a mahogany interior especially, was admired: Bill Serjeant's 2014 retrospective called her "one of the best built" Paradoxes.[3][4] Full build account: [boats/raffles.md](/boats/raffles.md). He is not to be confused with [Mark Brothers](/people/mark-brothers.md), the Newcastle, New South Wales, Australia owner of "Pamela B."

## Building to the plans

Raffles was built entirely with System Three epoxy, chosen for its price, easy mixing and customer service.[5] For ballast Waters settled on steel weight-lifting plates instead of lead, bulkier for the same weight but available in a range of sizes and easy to rearrange and re-secure for trim.[6] Her mahogany interior was admired enough in photographs that another California builder adopted the same wood for his own boat.[7]

His own practice for plywood joinery was to back a plain butt joint with a butt block rather than trust tape and epoxy alone.[8] For a first scarf, the plywood's own veneers are a good guide to how flat and straight the joint is turning out, and the way to learn is to practice on cheap construction-grade fir before committing to costlier finished plywood.[9] See [construction/scarf-joints.md](/construction/scarf-joints.md#choosing-between-a-scarf-a-butt-block-and-a-taped-butt-joint) on choosing between a scarf, a butt block and a taped butt joint.

## The self-steering wind vane

Waters' one long post-launch project on Raffles was a self-steering wind vane, pursued from August 2008 to June 2009. The search opened with design sources and a comparison against the vane already fitted to "[Zoë](/boats/zoe.md)", before settling on a rudder-head-mount design after weighing the commercial Cape Horn gear against the Walt Murray site's Model 90-93.[10][11] A weekend spent building a model brought the breakthrough, "the penny finally dropped", and two rival versions went ahead at once to compare which performed best, with the standard vane blade's size itself in question for light air.[12][13] The gear was finished at last in June 2009, five times slower than first expected, and immediately posed a mounting problem: set far enough aft to clear the boom, it was awkward to reach for adjustment without risking "an unplanned swim".[14] Full account at [boats/raffles.md](/boats/raffles.md#the-self-steering-wind-vane); the general technique is at [operations/self-steering.md](/operations/self-steering.md).

## Raffles changes hands

By January 2016 Raffles had been sold, bought from Folsom, California by Curtis Clark and trailered to Scottsdale, Arizona.[15] Three years later, photographs surfaced of a Paradox sailing under a new name, "Melandro". Raffles and Melandro turned out to be the same boat: "Hi Mark I have your boat."[16][17]
