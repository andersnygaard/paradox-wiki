# Tony Barlow

**Tony Barlow** built and launched **"[Miko](/boats/miko.md)"**, a [Paradox](/paradox.md), at **Phuket, Thailand**, in **March 2024**, to the stock design with adaptations for the tropics.[1] Full build account: [boats/miko.md](/boats/miko.md).

## Boat

Barlow worked out the rudder's shape, the method for turning the heavy, bottom-fitted hull and a first electrical fit-out from as early as June 2022, as a first-time builder. Full account: [boats/miko.md](/boats/miko.md#the-build-2022).[2]

Miko carries the fit-out her climate calls for: two outboards against Phuket's persistent light winds, a cut-down electric trolling motor on a lithium battery, extra solar freed up by an alternative to the sliding hatch and air conditioning running at about 100 W in eco mode. She sails out of Phuket. Full account: [boats/miko.md](/boats/miko.md).

## Approach

Motoring is built into the plan rather than kept as a last resort: light winds are the Phuket norm, so Miko carries a working outboard and a back-up for longer trips. Barlow apologises "to sailing purists" for it, but says plainly, "Would sail the entire way if I could."[3]

Launching Miko, he said there would be no second epoxy boat: "Next time Aluminium, me and epoxy can't be in the same town."[1] He developed an allergy to epoxy during the build; pricing a twenty-foot follow-on, he has weighed vinylester and polyester resin over a foam core instead, doubting only polyester's adhesion to plywood.[4] He would also like to build a second Paradox a fifth larger in welded aluminium, following [Alex McGavin](/people/alex-mcgavin.md)'s [aluminium Paradox](/design/variants/aluminium-paradox.md) for exactly that reason.[5]
