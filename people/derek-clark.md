# Derek Clark

**Derek Clark** is a UK Paradox builder from **Wolverhampton**, in the English Midlands. He built **["Enuf"](/boats/enuf.md)**, sold her within a month of her maiden season and saw her go on to a new owner in Norway.[1] [Bill Serjeant](/people/bill-serjeant.md) later called Derek's the second Paradox built in the United Kingdom, after [Alastair Law](/people/alastair-law.md)'s "Little Jim."[2] Full build and ownership record: [boats/enuf.md](/boats/enuf.md).

## Building "Enuf"

Derek launched "Enuf" in August 2006 and sailed her for the first time over three days in Poole Harbour, in company with Alastair Law's "Little Jim."[3] He painted her a distinctive three-tone green with a yellow bottom, choosing the colours from the colour wheel to complement a bright red sail he had bought for visibility.[4] He built in exterior-grade plywood throughout, reasoning the hull would be fully covered in epoxy and fibreglass regardless of the ply's own rating.[5]

## A resourceful builder

Derek favoured working around what he had or could source locally, rather than chasing the part specified by the plans. Needing a brass fork for the boom axle's pivot joint, he judged shipping brass pipe from the United States would carry a disproportionate carbon cost for so small a part and built the fitting instead from galvanised steel pipe bought locally, sanded and coated in epoxy against rust.[6] For the tiller he bypassed the plans' laminated plywood or bent ash entirely: a length of one inch galvanised pipe, worked cold through a hole drilled in a block of wood until it took the right curve, then given a touch up bend in a vice, became the finished tiller itself.[7]

## A beginner's guide, twice published

Once his own build was largely finished, Derek condensed its practical lessons into a free, copyright free "Beginners guide to building a Paradox," revised within a day of readers' first suggestions. Full account: [references/building-plans.md](/references/building-plans.md#a-community-written-beginners-guide).[8]

Three months later he published a longer version as a feature article for [Duckworks Magazine](/references/duckworks-magazine.md), "Building a Paradox by a Beginner," written from Wolverhampton before the boat was launched.[9] In it he rated himself "a mediocre to moderate woodworker" who had not cut a dovetail in twenty years, held that the design needed no cabinetmaker standard work anywhere and judged the many small departures he had made from the plans safe under modern epoxy technique, so long as nothing structural was changed.[9] Looking back at his own build order, he named its greatest frustration: having built the hull first, he was left making the mast, yard, boom, yuloh, rudder and their fittings with a finished hull sitting in the workshop waiting for them.[9]

## Selling "Enuf"

A month after her maiden outing in Poole Harbour, Derek put "Enuf" up for sale.[10] She sold within days and set off by road toward her new owner in Norway.[1]
