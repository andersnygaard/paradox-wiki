# phil_e.stine

**phil_e.stine** posted to the mailing list under that Yahoo account beginning December 2013 — no real name or location is given, beyond his own description of a career "spent in a technical field."[9] He introduced himself as interested in the Paradox but concerned, with bad knees and shoulders, about cockpit and cabin egress, and elsewhere describes himself as "a bit of a freak" — a six-footer's torso on legs with a 27 in inseam and very wide (EEEEE) feet.[1][12] No plans confirmed ordered, no construction started, and no boat of his own is on record.

## Cockpit and cabin egress, and footwell dimensions

Getting up from the bare cabin sole looked like a major ordeal to him; a stool no higher than about 9 in seemed practical, though untested, and his long torso meant anything taller would need a cabin modification. He floated a footwell immediately forward of the seat, covered with a panel for sleeping, as a third alternative to raising the roof or lowering the sole, and asked for the sole-to-hull distance and grab-handle dimensions.[1] Given concrete figures in reply, he judged the footwell made the worst-case exit feasible, "if not as easy as it would have been fifty years ago."[2] Full figures and exchange: [components/interior.md](/components/interior.md#cockpit-and-cabin-egress-with-limited-knees-or-shoulders-and-footwell-dimensions-december-2013).

## The boom-securing and topping-lift questions

The same week, unable to find a photograph showing how the boom is secured at its mast end, he doubted a bare deck fitting could hold it against the wind on its own — answered with the flexible-gooseneck mechanism and video documentation, which he found satisfied the question.[3][4] He then asked whether the topping lift is normally kept under tension; told it runs slack and plays no part in setting the sail, and that Matt Layden fits none on his own boat and privately advised against fitting one, he judged from the boom's long moment arm that he would still give serious thought to fitting one himself.[5] Full exchange: [components/rig/furling.md](/components/rig/furling.md#the-topping-lift-question-answered-zero-tension-and-matt-laydens-own-private-advice-against-fitting-one-december-2013).

## An Enigma-inspired mainsheet-routing proposal

Watching video of Dave Bolduc's Enigma, he noticed her sheet routed through a block at the rear centre of the aft deck and proposed the same routing on a Paradox — a deck block, a fairlead over the tiller baffle, a second block below deck, then forward to the outboard — untried and unanswered on record.[6] A further look through other boats' photos turned up at least one Paradox, believed to be "Johanna," already using a deck rather than a transom fairlead.[7] Full exchange: [components/mainsheet-outboard-fouling.md](/components/mainsheet-outboard-fouling.md#a-further-december-2013-proposal-routing-the-sheet-past-the-tiller-baffle-inspired-by-dave-bolducs-enigma).

## Organizing the sail-insignia photo album

He proposed dreaming up an actual insignia to put to Matt Layden and created a photo album for candidate designs, opening with his own entry rendered in mathematical notation — "arcane enough to be appropriate."[8] He kept the album open to public entries, professing a limited artistic eye of his own,[9] had initially thought of the yin-yang himself but suspected he had seen it used on a sail before,[10] endorsed the already-suggested black swan idea as suited to his own sense of humor and separately proposed a bumble bee,[11] recalled two further candidates since lost (a trademarked yin-yang/swan combination, and a swan in flight),[12] restated the yin-yang idea as only ever a vague notion and, having found just one real-world example — a yacht-charter operator's logo — objected to it chiefly as ubiquitous,[13] and rendered a fellow correspondent's Penrose-triangle proposal in black lines for the album.[14] The same message asking after two lost candidates also asked a fellow owner's own size, for a better sense of his own likely fit against the boat.[12] Full thread: [components/rig/sail.md](/components/rig/sail.md#whether-an-official-sail-insignia-exists).

## Opening the "Mast is offset?" question

Having read that the mast sits offset to port but unable to relocate the source, he asked whether that was true and, if so, why the sail is rigged to the mast's own port side — further from the centerline than the seemingly more logical starboard side — and whether anyone had tried the sail to starboard.[15] Answered with the vent-box drainage mechanism, a firsthand account of Matt Layden's own starboard test sails, and a force-vector verdict that the lateral position makes little practical difference, he restated the standing boom-to-starboard question once more before the thread settled.[16] Full exchange, including a new correspondent's own build: [components/rig/mast.md](/components/rig/mast.md#why-the-mast-and-the-sail-on-it-are-offset-to-port-rather-than-centered).

## Reaching out to a new British Columbia builder, and an unanswered rigged-hull-weight question

Replying to a new correspondent building a Paradox named "[Bye Bye Love](/boats/bye-bye-love.md)," who had eliminated his own vent box, he asked whether the freed space was meant for sleeping or storage and suggested cutting the forward-compartment opening down flush with the sole instead — as already done on "[Little Jim](/boats/little-jim.md#the-forward-stateroom-access-cut-down-for-bike-stowage)" to stow a folding bicycle — while warning that closing the hatch without the vent box's airflow could fog the ports.[17] Days later he asked for a ballpark figure for a Paradox hull's weight, rigged but without stores, ballast, water, or ground tackle — unanswered on record. See [weight, ballast, and displacement](/design/weight-and-ballast.md#an-unanswered-2013-question-hull-weight-rigged) for the closest figures on record.[18]

## A ballast-tank storage proposal, and preserving rain catchment

Answering a fellow correspondent's proposal to convert the built-in ballast tanks to general storage, he identified the real cost — pulling out the plumbing would give up the ability to refill drinking water from rain — and the fix: disconnecting the fill hoses at their lower ends and using them directly to refill water bottles instead. Days later, in the same thread, he proposed a movable alternative of his own: since sand weighs about 100 lb per cubic foot against water's 65, sand-and-watertight bags weighing about 20 lb each when filled could be made up in advance, filled just before launch, stowed after launch to trim the boat, and dumped again while waiting to use the ramp. Full exchange: [weight, ballast, and displacement](/design/weight-and-ballast.md#ballast-placement-outboard-vs-centered).[19][20]

## A wood-stove chimney-routing question

When a fellow correspondent shared a link to an ammo-can wood-stove design, he set the stove itself aside to ask where the chimney was actually meant to run through the hull — venting it out through the open hatch would give radiant heat but little else, since any heated air would simply escape upward, replaced by cold air flowing down and pooling around the body. Full exchange: [cabin heating for cold-weather sailing](/operations/cabin-heating.md#a-2014-recurrence-an-ammo-can-stove-idea-and-a-flue-placement-technique).[21]

## A cubic-scaling displacement calculation, and the name "Mark"

He signed one message "Mark" — the only name he has given on record — while following the 20%-enlarged "[Manatee](/boats/manatee.md)" build with anticipation.[22] He went on to work out the displacement and draft implications of a 20% scale-up from first principles, judging a build Manatee's size would carry roughly 2400 lb of new displacement, "a BIG little boat," with draft increasing proportionally by about another inch and a half. Full working: [larger Paradox — a worked example](/design/larger-paradox.md#a-worked-example-cubic-displacement-scaling-and-the-draft-increase-it-implies-february-2014).[23][24] Weighing that displacement against a comparison boat, he judged it not particularly heavy — without the draft concern, he would be looking at Welsford's Swaggie instead. Full exchange: [larger Paradox — Welsford's Swaggie compared](/design/larger-paradox.md#a-march-2014-exchange-welsfords-swaggie-compared-and-the-correct-scaling-law-for-stability).[25] Days later he caught a further arithmetic mixup in the same thread: a claimed 40% rise in the sail centre of effort's height in fact describes the increase in sail area, with the vertical dimension itself holding at 20%, and a separate rig-dimension figure had been computed from the cubed 170% displacement multiplier rather than the correct 120% linear scale factor. Full exchange: [larger Paradox — a further correction to the sail-rig arithmetic](/design/larger-paradox.md#a-march-2014-exchange-welsfords-swaggie-compared-and-the-correct-scaling-law-for-stability).[26]

## A 2015 reappearance: a sail-balance correction

Over a year after his last recorded message, he corrected a fellow correspondent's tacking theory on general sail-balance principle: moving a boat's centre of effort further aft helps her tack, not further forward — the further forward it sits, the harder she is to turn into the wind. Full exchange: [operations/sailing-characteristics.md](/operations/sailing-characteristics.md#reefed-sail-tacking-trouble-and-a-chine-runner-as-keel-theory-2015).[27]

## Trunk cabin visibility, and a rope-stretch note (August 2015)

Weighing in on why Paradox uses a trunk cabin rather than a raised-deck design, he offered that it gives much better visibility, particularly forward, when buttoned up for heavy weather.[28] See [components/pilothouse.md — trunk cabin versus a raised-deck cabin](/components/pilothouse.md#trunk-cabin-versus-a-raised-deck-cabin). Answering a question about substituting nylon or polypropylene rope for the dacron the plans specify, noted that nylon stretches considerably more than dacron, linking a cordage-materials reference page.[29] See [components/rig/running-rigging.md — rope materials: what is actually discussed](/components/rig/running-rigging.md#rope-materials-what-is-actually-discussed).
