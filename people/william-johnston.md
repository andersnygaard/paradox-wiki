# William Johnston

**William Johnston** built a [Paradox](/paradox.md)'s rig through 2015 and 2016, a first-time sailor by his own account who had never sailed before.[1] Working through the stock furling gear's reefing trouble with help from established owners, he reached a working rig by November 2015.[2] The next year he hinged the mast on a tabernacle for easier handling alone,[3] then rebuilt it lighter still once it came out heavier than most builders' masts.[4]

## Rebuilding rather than patching

His first boom warped after resting too long across saw horses. Rather than try to straighten it, on [André-François Bourbeau](/people/andre-francois-bourbeau.md)'s advice that dry wood holds its shape while a warped piece "remembers" its own, he remade it from scratch in dry Douglas fir.[5] The same instinct showed again with the mast: sized up from the plans to suit a substitute wood, it came in at 32 lb against other owners' more typical 22 lb, and he rebuilt it rather than carry the extra weight.[4] Full technical detail on both rebuilds, and on the furling and rope-material questions his build also raised, is at [components/rig/furling.md](/components/rig/furling.md#a-newcomers-rigging-troubles-and-a-further-compound-halyard-purchase-july-2015), [components/rig/mast.md](/components/rig/mast.md#easing-mast-raising-and-lowering-for-an-aging-or-less-able-sailor-january-2016) and [components/rig/running-rigging.md](/components/rig/running-rigging.md#a-crowded-masthead-and-rigging-friction-lessons-2015).

He closed out the boom rebuild with the rig at last reefing and furling as intended: "I guess I am out of excuses and will have to put it in the water soon."[2]
