# Frederic Tallman

**Frederic Tallman**, who signs himself **Deric**, is a sailor from **Central New York**, near **Syracuse and Oneida Lake**, and has been part of the Paradox community since 2012. He held the [Paradox](/paradox.md) plans and [Don Elliott](/people/don-elliott.md)'s own build manual for years, watching secondhand boats pass him by while he kept an existing Herreshoff-designed cat ketch of his own on the water, before buying Don's completed Paradox, **["The Edge,"](/boats/edge.md)** from Don's widow and son in July 2017. He restored her and learned to sail her over the seasons that followed, and in the years after built a wood boat of his own: a [Gorfnik](/similar-boat-designs/gorfnik-paraduck.md) he named **"Cracker Jack."** Since then he has become one of the fleet's steadier presences, welcoming newcomers to sailing the Great Lakes, registering "The Edge" for the 2020 [Texas 200](/merits/texas-200.md) and passing on a stream of small practical fixes to the builders who followed him.

## Years spent watching, before a Paradox of his own

He compared Matt Layden's "Wizard" nickname to Nathanael Herreshoff's own early on, in 2012, and wished for a museum someday to hold Matt's designs, boats, tools and history.[1] For years afterward he held the Paradox plans and Don Elliott's build manual, sailing a Herreshoff-designed cat ketch he called "Great Ketch," and repairing fiberglass boats, alongside a Windrider RAVE hydrofoil trimaran he also owned.[2] Twice in 2014 he expressed interest in secondhand Paradoxes that sold before he could act, one in Canada and one in the UK, and inquired unsuccessfully after a third in 2016, the same year he congratulated Curtis Clark on buying Mark Waters' own boat while noting how few Paradoxes ever come up for sale on the US East Coast.[3][4][5][6]

In April 2015 he set out to start a scratch build of his own, planning to work the yard first since its taper makes the centerline easier to drill true before shaping, and reaching out to other regional builders: he expected the whole project to take five to seven years around other commitments, aimed at his own retirement.[7] The build never got past sourcing materials. A local lumberyard talked him toward Hem-fir before a fellow builder redirected him to genuine Douglas fir, and he chased down a kiln-dried supplier, asked a beginner's question on shaping a laminated mast and worked out the yard's own metric-to-imperial conversion; the fuller wood-sourcing account is at [construction/hull-planking.md](/construction/hull-planking.md#alder-and-hemlock-for-structural-and-joiner-work), [construction/build-effort.md](/construction/build-effort.md), [components/rig/index.md](/components/rig/index.md#yard-material-douglas-fir-remains-standard-aluminum-a-proven-alternative) and [components/rig/mast.md](/components/rig/mast.md).[8][9]

That same July he wrote to André-François Bourbeau, calling his new "Gorfnik" design very creative and lighter than a Paradox, and said he looked forward to learning more about it.[10] It was a brief exchange, but the seed of a friendship that would eventually put a Gorfnik of his own on the water.

## Buying Don Elliott's own Paradox

In July 2017 Tallman bought Don Elliott's own completed Paradox from Don's widow Wanda and son Josh, towing her home across five states from Wisconsin, and began her restoration: sourcing a boom, a Sailrite sail kit and repairing fiberglass and rot at the rudder.[11][12][13][14] Weighing his own straight-stitch sewing machine against a zigzag one for the new sail, and later working out how to finish the yard's own ends, he asked around for advice on both, and once it came, the yard's own repair took its final shape.[15][16][17] The fuller restoration account is at [boats/edge.md: sold to Deric, and restoration begins (2017)](/boats/edge.md#sold-to-deric-and-restoration-begins-2017).[18]

Three years on, with solar power and a new electrical panel added, he reported "The Edge" back on the water after a repaint, sailing the Chesapeake for the first time and remaining, in his own words, "very grateful to have acquired Don's boat."[19][20]

## Learning to sail The Edge

Through that first winter, watching a fellow owner's own newly launched Paradox video while shovelling snow in Central New York, Tallman still had the boom to finish and the rudder, rudder block and cabin top to redo, but hoped to have The Edge sailing that year.[21] Reading a fellow owner's own written account of a maiden voyage the same week, he praised its candor and its description of tack-to-tack and downwind handling, particularly taken with the word "ghosting" for a becalmed drift.[22] By August the rudder, boom and yuloh were finished, the new sail had arrived and the yuloh's own hand position and socket placement had been worked out over a season of testing on open water; the fuller technical account is at [boats/edge.md](/boats/edge.md#the-boom-and-yuloh-finished-and-the-sail-arrives-august-2018) and [components/yuloh.md](/components/yuloh.md).

He sailed her for the first time that same August. A terse note two days later simply recorded the fact, and a fuller account written two days after that described sculling out on the yuloh past several motor yachts, hoisting sail on a starboard tack at André-François Bourbeau's advice and holding steady through a mid-sail gust to 15 knots as the chine runner gripped the water. After several years quietly waiting in a field, "The Edge" finally had "a life of her own again," running free "in her natural habitat."[23][24] A leak into the water tanks' dry storage space turned up soon after and was traced to a loose filler-tube connection, cured with a new tube and a second clamp.[25] The same weekend, a second, distinctly separate sail on Oneida Lake tested the boat's stability standing in the cabin before an accidental Chinese gybe beating home was cleared at the dock with a friend's help, the sail undamaged.[26] Reflecting on it once other builders shared their own experience of the same failure, he took it as a first lesson in how differently a lug rig wants to be sailed compared to a Bermudian one, finding something elegant in a sail left untethered to the mast.[27]

## Building a Gorfnik in wood: "Cracker Jack"

The Paradox Tallman owns is one he bought already built. Two and a half years after first writing to André-François Bourbeau about the "Gorfnik" design, he introduced himself more fully, in January 2018, to Bourbeau's fellow French-speaking builders, recalling a visit to Bourbeau in Canada where he toured his Paradoxes and sailed his smaller boats (he names a sailing companion "Andrea" two sentences after naming André-François, very likely the same person under a differently rendered name, though the discrepancy remains unresolved), and hoped the two might sail together that coming summer, each in his own boat.[28] On that same visit he learned to pole a yuloh in Bourbeau's own canoes, a technique he later tried himself in shallow water at home, though he settled back on plain yulohing once he had tried it.[29]

By June 2018 a Gorfnik build of his own was one of several boats he had running at once, alongside The Edge's own restoration and a Herreshoff-designed cat ketch he was re-gelcoating; the sail for The Edge went to a professional loft rather than to his own sewing machine partly for that reason.[30][31] He finished the Gorfnik by March 2021, reporting "my dox and Gorfnik is ready," and named her **"Cracker Jack."**[32] Writing again in January 2023, he called Paradox, Enigma and Gorfnik three related designs, each with its own distinct virtues, and signed himself "Gorfnik SV Cracker Jack" alongside "Paradox SV Edge."[33] The fuller build record is at [gorfnik-paraduck.md, a third Gorfnik: Tallman's "Cracker Jack"](/similar-boat-designs/gorfnik-paraduck.md#a-third-gorfnik-tallmans-cracker-jack).

## A steady voice for the fleet

By January 2021 Tallman had become one of the fleet's steadier voices, welcoming newcomers who asked about sailing a Paradox on the Great Lakes and judging it far more a matter of watching weather and conditions than any limit of the boat's own design, some stretches being considerably more interesting to sail than others. His own boat was by then kept nearer the City of Oswego, on Lake Ontario's south-eastern shore, and he looked ahead to sailing Lake Champlain that spring, inviting fellow owners within reach to join him.[34] The same month he weighed in on the long-running question of whether official plans for Matt Layden's Enigma exist, judging that they never did (Layden only ever offered plans for the Paradox) and guessing that any Enigma plans in circulation would have to come from someone who had reverse-engineered an existing hull's own specifications; the fuller account, and how the question was finally settled, is at [matt-layden/enigma.md: a January 2021 answer](/matt-layden/enigma.md#a-january-2021-answer-no-official-plans-but-a-builders-own-notes-online).[35] He had tried once before, in 2018, to reach Matt Layden directly, through the Everglades Challenge's own founder, without success: the nearest he came to firsthand contact with the designer whose "Wizard" nickname he had compared to Herreshoff's own, six years earlier.[36]

Twice, in February and September 2021, he pointed newer builders toward Josh Elliott's own website, built by Don Elliott's son to make ordering his father's building manual easier to process; the fuller account of both recurrences is at [references/building-plans.md: a February 2021 recurrence](/references/building-plans.md#a-february-2021-recurrence-and-the-manuals-own-new-order-route).[37][38] In February 2020 he registered "The Edge" for that June's [Texas 200](/merits/texas-200.md), inviting other owners to join him, and that same week thanked William Nix for personally offering to help fund a proposed cross-Paradox distance race, calling the offer "very generous."[39][40] That October he also planned to attend the Annapolis Sailboat Show without a boat of his own in tow.[41] The same month he worked through, alongside other owners, how a Paradox might be taken off her trailer entirely for winter storage or a future beach launch.[42][43]

A stream of smaller fixes rounded out his contribution to the fleet: a rudder leading-edge protection, a cam-cleat angle fix, a window-bedding technique, a hatch-rail refinish and two settled methods for filling and draining the water tanks, each already told in full on its own topic page.[44][45][46][47][48] He set out, in 2018, to sail "The Edge" without a motor at all, unlike his other boats, a plan later borne out, by his own account, over three years on fresh water with no current or tide to fight.[49][32] In 2023 he gave "The Edge" a permanent line running bow to stern and judged that knowing when it is safe to go on deck matters more than any single piece of gear, preferring, where he can, to sail close to shore and step off beside the boat instead; his own full on-deck routine is at [operations/personal-safety-gear.md: technique for going forward, rather than hardware](/operations/personal-safety-gear.md#technique-for-going-forward-rather-than-hardware).[50]
