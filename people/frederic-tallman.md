# Frederic Tallman

**Frederic Tallman**, who signs himself **Deric**, is a sailor from **Central New York**, near **Syracuse and Oneida Lake**, and has been part of the Paradox community since 2012. He held the [Paradox](/paradox.md) plans and [Don Elliott](/people/don-elliott.md)'s own build manual for years, watching secondhand boats pass him by while he kept an existing Herreshoff-designed cat ketch of his own on the water, before buying Don's completed Paradox, **["The Edge,"](/boats/edge.md)** from Don's widow and son in July 2017. He restored her and learned to sail her over the seasons that followed, and in the years after built a wood boat of his own: a [Gorfnik](/similar-boat-designs/gorfnik-paraduck.md) he named **"Cracker Jack."** Since then he has become one of the fleet's steadier presences, registering "The Edge" for the 2020 [Texas 200](/merits/texas-200.md) and passing on a stream of small practical fixes to the builders who followed him.

## Years spent watching, before a Paradox of his own

Early in his involvement with the design he likened Matt Layden's "Wizard" nickname to Nathanael Herreshoff's own and wished for a museum someday to hold Matt's designs, boats, tools and history.[1] For years afterward he held the Paradox plans and Don Elliott's build manual, sailing a Herreshoff-designed cat ketch he called "Great Ketch" and repairing fiberglass boats, alongside a Windrider RAVE hydrofoil trimaran he also owned.[2] Twice in 2014 he missed secondhand Paradoxes, one in Canada and one in the UK, that sold before he could act, and inquired unsuccessfully after a third in 2016, the same year Curtis Clark bought Mark Waters' own boat, a reminder of how few Paradoxes come up for sale on the US East Coast.[3][4][5][6]

In April 2015 he set out to start a scratch build of his own, planning to work the yard first since its taper makes the centerline easier to drill true before shaping, and reaching out to other regional builders: he expected the whole project to take five to seven years around other commitments, aimed at his own retirement.[7]

The build never got past sourcing materials. A local lumberyard steered him toward Hem-fir before a fellow builder redirected him to genuine Douglas fir; he tracked down a kiln-dried supplier, worked through how a laminated mast is best shaped, strip by strip or glued first and converted the yard's own dimension from the plans' millimetres to lumber-yard inches; the fuller wood-sourcing account is at [construction/hull-planking.md](/construction/hull-planking.md#alder-and-hemlock-for-structural-and-joiner-work), [construction/build-effort.md](/construction/build-effort.md), [components/rig/index.md](/components/rig/index.md#yard-material-douglas-fir-remains-standard-aluminum-a-proven-alternative) and [components/rig/mast.md](/components/rig/mast.md).[8][9]

That same July he first encountered André-François Bourbeau's new "Gorfnik" design, finding it very creative and lighter than a Paradox and looking forward to learning more about it.[10] It was a brief exchange, but the seed of a friendship that would eventually put a Gorfnik of his own on the water.

## Buying Don Elliott's own Paradox

In July 2017 Tallman bought Don Elliott's own completed Paradox from Don Elliott's widow and son, towing her home across five states from Wisconsin, and began her restoration: sourcing a boom, a Sailrite sail kit and repairing fiberglass and rot at the rudder.[11][12][13][14] Weighing his own straight-stitch sewing machine against a zigzag one for the new sail, and working out how to finish the yard's own ends, he drew on other builders' experience for both, and the yard's own repair took its final shape.[15][16][17] The fuller restoration account is at [boats/edge.md: sold to Deric, and restoration begins (2017)](/boats/edge.md#sold-to-deric-and-restoration-begins-2017).[18]

Three years on, with solar power and a new electrical panel added and a fresh coat of paint inside and out, "The Edge" was sailing the Chesapeake for the first time, and he remained, in his own words, "very grateful to have acquired Don's boat."[19][20]

## Learning to sail The Edge

Through that first winter, with the boom still to finish and the rudder, rudder block and cabin top still to redo, Tallman hoped to have The Edge sailing that year, drawing encouragement from a fellow owner's account of a newly launched Paradox's maiden voyage, one description of a becalmed drift staying with him afterward.[21][22]

He sailed her for the first time that August, and after several years quietly waiting in a field, "The Edge" finally had, in his own words, "a life of her own again"; the fuller account of that first sail, the yuloh testing around it and a water-tank leak traced and fixed the same month is at [boats/edge.md](/boats/edge.md#the-first-sail-and-the-water-tanks-in-service-august-2018).[23][24][25] A second sail days later on Oneida Lake, after an accidental gybe was cleared undamaged at the dock, taught him how differently a lug rig wants to be sailed compared to a Bermudian one; he found something elegant in a sail left untethered to the mast; the fuller account is at [boats/edge.md](/boats/edge.md#a-near-miss-under-sail-and-a-cold-water-squall-recalled-september-2018).[26][27]

## Building a Gorfnik in wood: "Cracker Jack"

The Paradox Tallman owns is one he bought already built. By January 2018, two and a half years after he first learned of Bourbeau's new "Gorfnik" design, he had visited him in Canada, touring his Paradoxes and sailing his smaller boats, and hoped the two might sail together that coming summer, each in his own boat.[28] On that same visit he learned to pole a yuloh in Bourbeau's own canoes, a technique he later tried himself in shallow water at home, though he settled back on plain yulohing once he had tried it.[29]

By June 2018 a Gorfnik build of his own was one of several boats he had running at once, alongside The Edge's own restoration and a Herreshoff-designed cat ketch he was re-gelcoating; the sail for The Edge went to a professional loft rather than to his own sewing machine partly for that reason.[30][31] He finished the Gorfnik by March 2021, by his own account ready to sail, and named her **"Cracker Jack."**[32] By January 2023 he considered Paradox, Enigma and Gorfnik three related designs, each with its own distinct virtues.[33] The fuller build record is at [gorfnik-paraduck.md, a third Gorfnik: Tallman's "Cracker Jack"](/similar-boat-designs/gorfnik-paraduck.md#a-third-gorfnik-tallmans-cracker-jack).

## A steady voice for the fleet

By January 2021 Tallman had become one of the fleet's steadier presences. He treats sailing a Paradox well on the Great Lakes as a matter of watching weather and conditions rather than any limit of the boat's own design, with some stretches considerably more interesting to sail than others. His own boat was by then kept on Lake Ontario's south-eastern shore, and he looked ahead to sailing Lake Champlain that spring, inviting fellow owners within reach to join him.[34]

He had tried, in 2018, to reach Matt Layden directly through the Everglades Challenge's own founder, without success, the nearest he came to firsthand contact with the designer whose "Wizard" nickname he likened to Herreshoff's own.[35]

In February 2020 he registered "The Edge" for that June's [Texas 200](/merits/texas-200.md), inviting other owners to join him.[36]

A stream of smaller fixes marked his years with "The Edge": a rudder leading-edge protection, a cam-cleat angle fix, a window-bedding technique, a hatch-rail refinish, a technique for taking a Paradox off her trailer entirely for winter storage and two settled methods for filling and draining the water tanks, each already told in full on its own topic page.[37][38][39][40][41][42][43] He set out, in 2018, to sail "The Edge" without a motor at all, unlike his other boats, a plan later borne out, by his own account, over three years on fresh water with no current or tide to fight.[44][32] In 2023 he gave "The Edge" a permanent line running bow to stern. He treats knowing when it is safe to go on deck as more important than any single piece of gear, and where he can, prefers to sail close to shore and step off beside the boat instead; his own full on-deck routine is at [operations/personal-safety-gear.md: technique for going forward, rather than hardware](/operations/personal-safety-gear.md#technique-for-going-forward-rather-than-hardware).[45]
