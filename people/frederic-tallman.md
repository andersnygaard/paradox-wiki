# Frederic Tallman

**Frederic Tallman** — who signs the mailing list "Deric," and whose account also posts under the display names "Deric Tallman" and "Frederic Tallman" — is a sailor from **Central New York**, near **Syracuse and Oneida Lake**, who held [Paradox](/paradox.md) plans and [Don Elliott](/people/don-elliott.md)'s build manual for years before finally becoming an owner by buying Don's own completed boat, ["The Edge,"](/boats/edge.md) from the Elliott family in July 2017.[1][10] He owns a RAVE sailboat and has repaired fiberglass boats, though never built one in wood.[2]

## Years of watching, sourcing wood, and missing secondhand boats (2012-2016)

Deric followed the mailing list for years before becoming an owner. In 2012 he compared Matt Layden's "Wizard" nickname to Nathanael Herreshoff's own, and wished for a museum someday to hold Matt's designs, boats, tools, and history.[1] Twice in 2014 he expressed interest in secondhand Paradoxes that sold before he could act — one in Canada, one in the UK — and inquired unsuccessfully after a third in 2016, the same year he congratulated Curtis Clark on buying Mark Waters' own boat while noting how few Paradoxes ever come up for sale on the US East Coast.[3][4][5][6]

Opening "Paradox Sailors Near New York State US?" in April 2015, he set out to start his own scratch build, planning to work the yard first since its taper makes the centerline easier to drill true before shaping, and reaching out to other regional builders — expecting the whole project to take five to seven years around other commitments, planned as a retirement project.[2][7] The build never got past sourcing materials: a local lumberyard talked him toward Hem-fir over Douglas fir, before a fellow builder redirected him toward genuine Douglas fir as the better wood; he chased down a kiln-dried supplier hours from home, asked a beginner's question on shaping a laminated mast, and worked out metric-to-imperial conversions for the yard — the full wood-sourcing saga at [construction/hull-planking.md](/construction/hull-planking.md#alder-and-hemlock-for-structural-and-joiner-work), [construction/build-effort.md](/construction/build-effort.md), [components/rig/index.md](/components/rig/index.md#yard-material-douglas-fir-remains-standard-aluminum-a-proven-alternative), and [components/rig/mast.md](/components/rig/mast.md).[8][9]

## Buying "The Edge" (2017)

In July 2017 Deric bought Don Elliott's own completed Paradox from Don's widow Wanda and son Josh, towing her home across five states from Wisconsin, and began her restoration — sourcing a boom, a Sailrite sail kit, and repairing fiberglass and rot at the rudder. Weighing his own straight-stitch sewing machine against a zigzag one for the new sail, and later working out how to finish the yard's own ends, he put both questions to the mailing list directly, and thanked the group once the advice let the yard's own repair take shape. Full account: [boats/edge.md — sold to Deric, and restoration begins (2017)](/boats/edge.md#sold-to-deric-and-restoration-begins-2017).[10][11][12][13][14][15][16][17]

## Toward a 2018 launch

Watching a fellow owner's own newly launched Paradox video that winter, while taking breaks from shovelling snow in Central New York, still had the boom to finish making and the rudder, rudder block, and cabin top to redo, but hoped to have The Edge sailing that year.[18] By June, the sail question was settled by commissioning a professional loft — Quantum Sails of Rochester, NY — to cut it to the original design in 6 oz cloth, rather than sewing it himself given how much boat work he had running at once, that "once" including a Gorfnik build and a separate cat-ketch re-gelcoating job alongside The Edge's own remaining checklist of paint and reassembly. Full account: [boats/edge.md — the sail ordered, and a launch checklist (June 2018)](/boats/edge.md#the-sail-ordered-and-a-launch-checklist-june-2018).[21][22] By August, the rudder and rudder block were reconditioned, the boom and yuloh built with a friend's help, and the sail itself arrived; rigging her for the first time turned up the same furling friction other builders report, resolved with the same half-topping-lift fix already worked out on Iota and Pequeno. The newly built yuloh was tested successfully in the marina days later, its pivot-socket position estimated by standing in the water in sculling posture and found within three inches of the plans once measured. Full account: [boats/edge.md — the boom and yuloh finished, and the sail arrives (August 2018)](/boats/edge.md#the-boom-and-yuloh-finished-and-the-sail-arrives-august-2018).[23][24][25]

## Rudder-up testing and a loaded-trim plan (August 2018)

Days after the yuloh's first successful test, put André-François Bourbeau's rubber-hose pivot suggestion into practice — sealing the socket with epoxy and fitting a length of hose inside — and planned a further test with the rudder up, having so far only tried it down; also planned to add weight and fill the water tanks to get a better sense of the boat's loaded motion, and, watching a YouTube video believed to show Dave Bolduc yulohing with a U-shaped oarlock, looked forward to the sailing test still to come. Full account: [boats/edge.md](/boats/edge.md#the-boom-and-yuloh-finished-and-the-sail-arrives-august-2018) and [components/yuloh.md](/components/yuloh.md#building-the-yuloh-itself-blade-curvature-and-the-pivot-boltsocket).[26]

## The first sail, and continued yuloh testing (August 2018)

Days later, sailed the boat for the first time — an outing that taught him a great deal about her — after filling the water tanks from a marina spigot ahead of the sail, and found the boat's four-inch inspection ports too small for his own reach, unlike Don Elliott's own. Continued testing the yuloh's hand position and body stance, finding sweet spots for a sustained stroke, and planned a further modification extending the blade deeper into the water. Full account: [boats/edge.md — the first sail, and the water tanks in service](/boats/edge.md#the-first-sail-and-the-water-tanks-in-service-august-2018) and [components/yuloh.md — hand position on the handle, and body stance while sculling](/components/yuloh.md#hand-position-on-the-handle-and-body-stance-while-sculling-2018).[27][28]

## Introducing himself to the francophone builders, and hopes for a joint 2018 sail

In January 2018 Deric introduced himself, via machine translation, to the mailing list's French-speaking builders — restating his purchase of Don Elliott's boat and the boom and yuloh it still needed — and recalled visiting [André-François Bourbeau](/people/andre-francois-bourbeau.md) in Canada, touring his Paradox(es) and sailing his own smaller boats; hoped to sail together that summer, each in his own boat (the message names this partner "Andrea," two sentences after naming André-François — very likely the same person under a differently rendered name, though the discrepancy is unresolved).[19] The same week, replying to a fellow owner's written maiden-cruise report, praised its candor about seasickness and its account of hot tea in a dry, rain-lashed boat, and — with his own first sail expected that summer — was particularly struck by its description of tack-to-tack and downwind/gybing behaviour, and by the term "ghosting."[20]

## A first sail, further yuloh testing, and a tank leak (August-September 2018)

Sailed "The Edge" for the first time days after the rudder-up test, an outing recounted in full as "Being on the Edge" — sculling out on the yuloh, hoisting sail on André-François Bourbeau's recommended starboard tack, and finding her heel and hold steady on a chine runner through a mid-sail gust to 15 knots. Continued yuloh testing with a second, handle-ward socket and open-water runs on the Erie Canal, and later found and began tracing a leak into the water tanks' dry storage space, planning to test each tank in turn to localize it precisely. Full account: [boats/edge.md — the first sail, and the water tanks in service](/boats/edge.md#the-first-sail-and-the-water-tanks-in-service-august-2018), [boats/edge.md — a further yuloh socket, and open-water testing on the Erie Canal](/boats/edge.md#a-further-yuloh-socket-and-open-water-testing-on-the-erie-canal-september-2018), and [boats/edge.md — a leak found in the water tanks](/boats/edge.md#a-leak-found-in-the-water-tanks-september-2018).[29][30][31][32]

Found and fixed the tank leak days later, tracing it to a loose filler-tube connection cured with a new tube and a second hose clamp, and a further solo sail on his home waters, Oneida Lake, tested the boat's stability standing in the cabin before an accidental "Chinese gybe" beating home was cleared with a friend's help at the dock, the sail undamaged. Full account: [boats/edge.md — a leak found in the water tanks](/boats/edge.md#a-leak-found-in-the-water-tanks-september-2018) and [boats/edge.md — a near-miss under sail, and a cold-water squall recalled](/boats/edge.md#a-near-miss-under-sail-and-a-cold-water-squall-recalled-september-2018).[33][34]

## Learning a lug rig, a topping lift set aside, and a first trailer question (September 2018)

Reflecting on the Chinese gybe once other builders shared their own experience of the same failure, framed it as a first lesson in how differently a lug rig wants to be sailed compared to a Bermudian one, and found something elegant in a sail left untethered to the mast. Tried a topping lift for the first time days later, abandoned it again after its line snagged in a partially reefed sail while working the boat between docks, and took his first grounding entirely in stride. Separately raised a question new to the mailing list: "The Edge" carries no bow U-bolt for hoisting onto her trailer, unlike many other Paradoxes, and hooking the trailer cable around the bow cleats instead costs extra time at the ramp. Four builders answered with their own placements; thanked them for the advice and for a Coast Guard towing story besides, and resolved to add the fitting as a winter project — also inviting anyone wanting to sail together off the East Coast that season to get in touch. Full account: [boats/edge.md — a near-miss under sail](/boats/edge.md#a-near-miss-under-sail-and-a-cold-water-squall-recalled-september-2018), [boats/edge.md — a topping lift tried and set aside, and a first grounding](/boats/edge.md#a-topping-lift-tried-and-set-aside-and-a-first-grounding-september-2018), and [boats/edge.md — a trailer bolt considered, and answers from four builders](/boats/edge.md#a-trailer-bolt-considered-and-answers-from-four-builders-september-2018).[35][36][37][38]

## A furling technique adopted, a boat show, and a trailer-removal question (October 2018)

Said he would try the continuous single-line furling technique too, his own halyard already being thicker line than his furling line, so no equipment change was needed to make the switch; the half topping lift was still working well for him besides.[39] Planned to attend the Annapolis Sailboat Show that October without his own boat yet in tow, hoping to meet fellow Paradox owners there.[40] Revived the trailer question a few days later with a broader one: whether a Paradox is ever taken bodily off her trailer for storage, since "The Edge"'s own bunk-and-pivot trailer has no rollers, and — with a possible future Everglades Challenge entry in mind — whether she could be gotten onto a beach above the high-tide mark the same way. Full discussion, with three builders' methods and the practical answer that followed: [boats/edge.md — removing "The Edge" from her trailer, and a beach-launch question](/boats/edge.md#removing-the-edge-from-her-trailer-and-a-beach-launch-question-october-2018) and [operations/trailering.md — removing the boat from the trailer entirely](/operations/trailering.md#removing-the-boat-from-the-trailer-entirely-for-storage-or-a-beach-launch-october-2018).[41][42][43]

Had already tried, a month earlier, to reach Matt Layden directly through the Everglades Challenge's own founder without a reply, and wondered whether Matt might be off on another challenge of his own.[44] Continued the trailer conversation the same day: admired Alastair Law's own trailer, seen on his website, as "a work of art," described floating "The Edge" on and off her trailer at a ramp in fresh water as his own workable practice so far, expected ramps and launch environments elsewhere to differ, and asked how far past the wheels the trailer extends — answered with the same trailer specifications already on record (see [operations/trailering.md](/operations/trailering.md#a-2016-recurrence-transverse-versus-longitudinal-bunks)), together with news that the winch itself had since been upgraded to a braked unit.[45][46] Days later, having ordered a new two-speed, 2,500 lb winch with a brake to replace his own single, non-braking one, planned a longer winch stand — 24 to 32 in — to raise it to the height of the bow cleats, with the V-block set lower to rest the bow; his next task after that was set as lowering the boat from the trailer to the ground on small rollers, or a dolly with wheels.[47] Reported days later that his old winch, still in use while the new one was on order, was rusty and worn but trusted through six trips regardless, and began repainting the trailer and re-sealing the mast in two-part epoxy over surfaces already sanded and stripped. Full account: [boats/edge.md — a winch upgrade planned, and a trailer-length question](/boats/edge.md#a-winch-upgrade-planned-and-a-trailer-length-question-october-2018).[48]

Restated, replying on a fellow owner's grounding thread, his intent to sail "The Edge" without a motor at all, unlike his other boats which all carry some auxiliary help. Separately reported having already tried poling his own yuloh in shallow water the previous summer — a technique first learned visiting André-François Bourbeau in Canada, tried out in Bourbeau's own canoes — though he still preferred plain yulohing once he had tried it. Full account: [boats/edge.md — a no-motor sailing plan restated, and a poling technique tried](/boats/edge.md#a-no-motor-sailing-plan-restated-and-a-poling-technique-tried-october-2018).[49][50]

## Protecting the rudder's leading edge (November 2019)

A year later, asked the group for ideas protecting the rudder's leading edge from rocks and hidden objects, since an unprotected edge too often becomes "the default depth finder." Five techniques came back — rope, PVC conduit, half-round bronze, Teflon rub rail, and epoxy-soaked webbing — and he summarized them as three broad kinds (a Teflon rub rail, copper or galvanized pipe, and epoxy with tape or rope), reporting he had the winter ahead to make the improvement. Full technique detail: [components/rudder.md — a proven copper-pipe leading edge, and a dacron rope alternative](/components/rudder.md#a-proven-copper-pipe-leading-edge-and-a-dacron-rope-alternative).[51]

## Registering for the 2020 Texas 200

The following February, posting under the display name "Captain Ketch," registered "The Edge" for the June 2020 [Texas 200](/merits/texas-200.md), inviting other Paradox owners to join him and pointing to `www.texas200.com` for details, noting that Sean Mulligan and Michael Beebe had both already sailed the event in their own Paradoxes. On the same list that week, thanked William Nix for personally offering to fund a proposed cross-Paradox distance race, calling the offer "very generous" and saying he looked forward to seeing it happen; full account of the proposal at [merits/gatherings.md — a proposed 12-hour distance race](/merits/gatherings.md#a-proposed-12-hour-distance-race-february-2020).[52][53]

Two months later, still posting as "Captain Ketch," agreed with other owners that the cam-cleat angle for the lines coming into the cabin was awkward — locking them in meant shifting forward, at times pulling with both hands while pressing a line into the cleat with a foot — and expected a new block and angle change to ease it. Full account: [boats/edge.md](/boats/edge.md#citations).[54]

That July, reported both fixes had worked well in practice, added a DC panel and solar system, and confirmed "The Edge" back on the water after a repaint, having sailed the Chesapeake for the first time the previous year. Full account: [boats/edge.md — a planned 2020 Texas 200 entry](/boats/edge.md#a-planned-2020-texas-200-entry).[55][56]

Ten days later, offered his own window-bedding technique on a "Replacing windows" thread opened by a fellow owner: paint the surrounding wood in one-part poly, white or black, then bed the window in butyl tape sold by Rod Collins/"Maine Sail," elastic enough not to break off the surface, giving a leak-free bed the window can still be pulled again later — "keep it simple: no goop." See [window glazing materials — bedding sealant](/construction/window-glazing-materials.md#bedding-sealant-a-cost-comparison) for the fuller range of options the fleet gave.[57]

A month later, on a "hatch" thread, reported the same butyl tape and two-part polyurethane finish, first worked out for windows, carried over to the SS bolts through the hatch's outer rail: refinished and repainted, then bedded in the tape, with no leaks. Shares a black sunscreen material spread over the hatch opening while sailing seated on the cabin floor, for fresh air with the sun's heat cut down. See [components/hatch.md — the outer rail's carriage bolts](/components/hatch.md#the-outer-rails-carriage-bolts).[58]

## Welcoming newcomers on the groups.io list (January 2021)

Answering newcomers asking about sailing a Paradox on the Great Lakes, judged it far more a matter of watching weather and conditions than any limit of the boat's own design, some stretches of the Great Lakes being considerably more interesting to sail than others. His own boat was by then kept nearer the **City of Oswego**, on Lake Ontario's south-eastern shore, still rigged with the original design sail shape; he looked ahead to sailing **Lake Champlain** that spring or summer, and invited fellow owners within reach to sail together.[60]

## A permanent bow-to-stern line, and an on-deck safety routine

Answering a newcomer's question about lifelines and pulpits, gave "The Edge" a permanent line running bow to stern, useful when anchoring, and set out his own routine for the rare occasions a Paradox sailor needs to be on deck underway: stay on the centerline, lie down to keep weight low, keep the boom clear with the sail struck, brace against the mast, wear a life jacket, and trail a rescue line — judging that knowing when it is safe to go on deck matters more than any single piece of gear, and preferring, where he can, to sail close to shore and step off beside the boat instead. Had not seen a Paradox fitted with lifelines in the traditional sense, and read the design's whole arrangement as built to keep the sailor within the cockpit and cabin rather than send them onto an open deck. Full account: [operations/personal-safety-gear.md — technique for going forward, rather than hardware](/operations/personal-safety-gear.md#technique-for-going-forward-rather-than-hardware).[64]
