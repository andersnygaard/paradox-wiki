# People

The designer and notable builders/contributors in the [Paradox](/paradox.md)
community. Concepts are added here as they are sourced from the archive.

> The **designer, Matt Layden**, has his own top-level branch at
> [/matt-layden/index.md](/matt-layden/index.md) (his biography and his other
> designs). Individual built Paradox hulls live under
> [/boats/index.md](/boats/index.md).

# Concepts

* [Glen C. Maxwell](glen-maxwell.md) - Glen C. Maxwell, a professional boatbuilder from Live Oak, Florida, founded the Paradox builders' mailing list, built his Paradox "Zoë" and in 2016 became the first American in England's Jester Azores Challenge.
* [The Bolducs: David & Mindy Bolduc](bolduc.md) - David and Mindy Bolduc, North Carolina micro-cruisers, distributors of the Paradox plans and owners of Little Cruiser, who met Matt Layden in 1992 and cruised the Bahamas in his company.
* [Sven Yrvind](yrvind.md) - Sven Yrvind, a Swedish designer and ocean voyager known for tiny safety-first blue-water boats and a personal friend of Matt Layden, sailed Paradox in 1997 and called the chine runner a landmark innovation.
* [Don Elliott](don-elliott.md) - Don Elliott, an early Paradox builder from Tomah, Wisconsin, authored the sixteen-part Building Paradox construction guide, built "The Edge" to Matt Layden's plans, and died in 2008.
* [Alastair Law (Yeovil, England)](alastair-law.md) - A Yeovil, England CAD engineer who built and has sailed the first UK-built Paradox, "Little Jim," single-handed since 2003, and has since published and spoken about the design in public.
* [Chuck Leinweber](chuck-leinweber.md) - Chuck Leinweber, founder and publisher of Duckworks Magazine, an online small-boat magazine and plans outlet, who built his own Paradox, Tennessee, by 2001 and raced a Frolic2 in the WaterTribe Everglades Challenge.
* [Chris Becker](chris-becker.md) - A Paradox builder in Brisbane, Queensland, Australia, who began building "Walden" from scratch in 2001.
* [Paul (New Zealand)](paul-nz.md) - A prospective builder in the south of New Zealand, drawn to Paradox's enclosed cabin and simple rig for sailing in the region's rough weather.
* [Bert Vercauteren](bert-vercauteren.md) - A Belgian boat designer who bought the Paradox plans as inspiration for his own micro cruiser and independently verified Matt Layden's stability calculation using his own boat-design software.
* [Chris Curtis](chris-curtis.md) - Sandpoint, Idaho sailor and third owner of the Paradox "Scout," part of a run of Layden and Layden-influenced boats including an Enigma 460 and the Hogfish Maximus, lived aboard since 2022.
* [Chris Morejohn](chris-morejohn.md) - Chris Morejohn, a Florida Keys boatbuilder, designed and built a family of liveaboard sharpies with chine runners of his own design, including the Atlantic-crossing "Hogfish Maximus," and later sketched two smaller Paradox-inspired cruiser concepts.
* [Jeff](jeff-doryman.md) - Jeff, a builder from the Isle of Wight, England, who drew the from-scratch microcruiser "Paramour" before building "Noli," a lighter Paradox and Enigma hybrid, around 2005 to 2008.
* [David Beard](david-beard.md) - A first-time boatbuilder from Tupelo, Mississippi, who bought and refitted a secondhand Paradox named "Whisper" while building a second one from scratch, known for hands-on fixes to her chine runners, rudder and rig.
* [Charles Wilson](charles-wilson.md) - An early Paradox plans customer in the United Kingdom who went on to build a different boat.
* [Paul V.](paul-v.md) - Paul V., an American builder from the Great Lakes region who built a Paradox to the plans, then designed and built a chine-runner dory sharpie of his own, Whaleback, under construction by 2006.
* [Nels Arvidson](nels-arvidson.md) - A small-boat sailor from British Columbia, Canada, who sailed a Bolger Micro rather than building a Paradox, proposed a motorsailer variant and organized a volunteer translation of Sven Yrvind's book.
* [Pete Hodges](pete-hodges.md) - A Florida builder-planner who worked out a 10 percent enlarged "Paradox 1.1" sized to his own frame in 2001 and 2002 and hammer-tested pressure-treated wood for the bottom and chine runners.
* [Jeff Forrest](jeff-forrest.md) - A Paradox builder in Southern Oregon, USA, who began building from scratch in December 2001, chose stitch-and-glue construction for the hull and weighed enlarging her by 10 to 15 percent.
* ["guylita"](guylita.md) - Known only by the name "guylita," someone drawn to the design in November 2001 whose interest centred on self-steering, rig balance across points of sail and the placement of the outboard ballast.
* [Mat](mat-man22.md) - Mat researched metal chine-runner shoes, set a practical hull-scaling rule and worked out plywood scantlings and resin choices for the Paradox in the early 2000s.
* [Harald Lillevang](harald-lillevang.md) - A prospective builder in Denmark, drawn to the design after finding its sketches online in mid-2001 and hoping to see a Paradox sail Scandinavian waters.
* [David LeBlanc](david-leblanc.md) - David LeBlanc, of Seattle, Washington, a longtime amateur radio enthusiast and former US Navy radioman, drew on wide reading in small-boat literature for detailed knowledge of plywood, antifouling, refit, rig and lights.
* [William H. Longyard](william-longyard.md) - William H. Longyard, of Winston-Salem, North Carolina, is the author of A Speck on the Sea, a survey of small-boat voyages, and designed, built and sailed Lucky Town 7, his own 14 ft microcruiser.
* [Ron Eike](ron-eike.md) - Ron Eike, a sailing-canoe sailor, had used Dabbler's lug sails himself and knew the small-craft sailmaking world, and, racing the 2007 Everglades Challenge, saw Matt Layden's Sand Flea pass mid-race in Florida Bay.
* [David C. Smith](david-c-smith.md) - A Pacific Northwest professional boatbuilder of twenty years who built his own Paradox, "Swash," strictly to the plans and held that fidelity to a design's specifications is a matter of respect for its designer.
* [Andrew Graham](andrew-graham.md) - A Brisbane, Australia builder whose Paradox "Zero" launched 29 November 2003, her build carefully adapted to standard Australian plywood and checked against the plans throughout.
* [Kjell Karlsson](kjell-karlsson.md) - Kjell Karlsson, a Swedish builder from the Stockholm archipelago, built "Paraplastic" in Sven Yrvind's "BRIS" composite method, and translated Yrvind's book chapter on Matt Layden into English.
* [Jack Gardiner](jack-gardiner.md) - A Rhode Island carpenter and welder who built the stitch-and-glue Paradox "Jill," launched at Gaspee Point, Narragansett Bay, in 2004, after weighing a steel hull against wood.
* [Steve (Kansas City)](steve-kc.md) - A Kansas City, Missouri builder who worked through the Paradox plans in strict sequence from 2004, building the appendages before the hull, and offered her unfinished parts and materials for sale in 2011.
* [Derek Clark](derek-clark.md) - Derek Clark, a UK Paradox builder from Wolverhampton, built and sailed "Enuf," authored a free community beginners' guide and a Duckworks Magazine build article, and fabricated a jig-free bent-pipe tiller.
* [Bill Serjeant](bill-serjeant.md) - Bill Serjeant, Essex builder on the River Crouch, ran a UK Paradox website and registry from 2003 to 2009, kept the blog Bill's Log and built and sailed the Paradoxes Faith and Minnow.
* [James Hall](james-hall.md) - James Hall, an English sailor from Dorset who had already built and sailed a yacht, bought "Faith" from her builder Bill Serjeant in 2008 and sailed her from Poole Harbour until his death in 2016.
* [Mark Waters](mark-waters.md) - A Los Angeles, California builder whose Paradox "Raffles," completed in 2006 strictly to the plans, later sailed on under a new owner in Arizona.
* [Matthew E. Noonan](matthew-noonan.md) - A Paradox builder from Ithaca, New York, who began building to the stock plans in June 2006 after two folding kayaks and a PDRacer, and kept a public daily build log of his first week.
* [Stephen Yahn](stephen-yahn.md) - Stephen Yahn, a Puget Sound, Washington builder, built and sailed the Paradox "Ironsides" from 2006 to 2012, working out fastening, rot-inspection and rigging technique.
* [Bengt Genell](bengt-genell.md) - A builder from Västmanland, in central Sweden, who spent two years building a budget plywood Paradox, launched on Lake Mälaren in July 2006 as "Ladikan."
* [Sean Mulligan](sean-mulligan.md) - Sean Mulligan, a firefighter and pilot from Lake Havasu City, Arizona, finished and sailed the Paradox "Scout" from 2013 and founded the Havasu Pocket Cruisers Convention. Not to be confused with [Sean Herron](/boats/defiant.md).
* [Pete Martin](pete-martin.md) - A Cheltenham, England builder, originally from Northern Ireland, who built and launched the Paradox JoHanna in 2011 and has since sailed her, including a 140-mile river cruise mostly under yuloh.
* [Ian Lang](ian-lang.md) - A North London, England builder who gathered plywood and timber quotes through spring 2007 and started construction on his Paradox, named "Irene May" after his mother, in late June 2007.
* [Robert Biegler](robert-biegler.md) - Robert Biegler bought Derek Clark's former Paradox "Enuf," shipped her to Norway, sailed her out of Trondheimsfjord without an outboard, and repaired her rudder and furling gear before selling her on in 2013.
* [James (Bradford)](james-bradford.md) - A Bradford, Yorkshire builder who designed a lighter, car-topping Paradox/Enigma hybrid in October 2007 and began cutting plywood for his own version in January 2008.
* [John Gilbert](john-gilbert.md) - John Gilbert, a sailor from Nanaimo, British Columbia, reasons about the Paradox from physics and first principles, covering ballast, scaling, rig classification, sculling, self-steering and rudder loads.
* [John H. Wright](john-h-wright.md) - John H. Wright, of central Texas, entered WaterTribe's Everglades Challenge six times as Karank, finishing in 2012, built his own roller boom and inspected Matt Layden's Elusion before the 2010 Ultimate Florida Challenge.
* [John Jamieson](john-jamieson.md) - John Jamieson, a shipwright of thirty-five years and a former schooner officer in northwest Montana, brought a tradesman's judgment to plywood grades and joinery.
* [Dave Raftery](dave-raftery.md) - Dave Raftery, from eastern Massachusetts, compiled the Dave's Wiki technical reference on Matt Layden's Paradox and Enigma designs, preferred the Enigma 460 to an enlarged Paradox and began his own Enigma build in 2013.
* [Jim Baltaxe](jim-baltaxe.md) - Jim Baltaxe, from Wellington, New Zealand, weighed building a Paradox in foam and glass after a composite-construction workshop before ordering stock plywood plans in 2008, with ideas on chine runners, cabin safety and small-boat rigging.
* [Jimmy Lu](jimmy-lu.md) - A first-time boatbuilder in Taipei, Taiwan, who built the Paradox "Auspice" in under eight months, launching her in February 2008.
* [Roselt Croeser](roselt-croeser.md) - A Western Australian boatbuilder who converted the junk-rigged Paradox "Planktos," built 2013 to 2016, from the stock lug rig, and presented her build and Shark Bay cruise to the Amateur Boat Builders' Association in 2021.
* [Dave Skaife](dave-skaife.md) - Dave Skaife built a Paradox in Hawaii and sailed her engine-free from 2014 to 2018 across Hawaii, the Gulf Intracoastal Waterway, the Bahamas and Cuba, blogging the whole voyage.
* [Alberto Callejon](alberto-callejon.md) - Alberto Callejon, a journalist and online translator from Rawson, Chubut, on Argentina's Patagonian coast, built and sailed "Cachivache," billed by him as the most southerly Paradox on record, active from 2008 to 2021 and the founder, in 2011, of the Paradox builders' Facebook group, a role he was still publicly claiming as of 2019.
* [Alan (New Zealand)](alan-nz.md) - Alan, of New Zealand's South Island, worked out engineering solutions for masts, batteries, self-steering and cabin hatches, including a year-long field test of a solar garden light as an anchor light.
* [Rodney Cannon](rodney-cannon.md) - Rodney Cannon, a Los Angeles builder and former professional magician, took up Paradox in 2008 after Matt Layden's story drew him to the design, and built two hulls, one in California, one in New Orleans.
* [David Nemo](david-nemo.md) - David Nemo, a UK builder, built an unnamed 28 by 8 foot live-aboard Paradox at double the stock scale, foam-cored in Divinycell over reclaimed church-hardwood framing, sailing for the Mediterranean by December 2009.
* [Eerik Mee](eerik-mee.md) - Eerik Mee is a boatbuilder in Tartu, Estonia, who built and sailed "THU," a Paradox enlarged 20% and rigged with a cambered junk sail designed from scratch, launched in 2017.
* [Lezlie Henson](lezlie-henson.md) - The Houston-area, Texas builder of the stock Paradox "Tardis," documented step by step on her own blog and sold unfinished in 2013 to the owner who renamed and sailed her as "Scout."
* [Tim McKinney](tim-mckinney.md) - A Texas sailor who acquired a secondhand Paradox built by Michael Beebe, founded the Paradox Sailboat Facebook group in 2019 and converted, then reverted, her steering after a Texas 200 attempt cut short by a snake bite.
* [André-François Bourbeau](andre-francois-bourbeau.md) - André-François Bourbeau, a retired wilderness-survival professor in Québec, Canada, owns two Paradoxes, designed the Gorfnik and built an Enigma ketch of his own design, bringing decades of wilderness-travel skill aboard.
* [Simas Butavičius](simas-butavicius.md) - Simas Butavičius, an amateur Paradox builder in Lithuania who began building in the winter of 2019, using exterior-grade Baltic birch plywood thinned to 12mm bottom, 9mm sides and 6mm deck to offset its extra weight.
* [Simon B](simon-b.md) - Simon B, a Paradox builder in Vilnius, Lithuania, began building in the winter of 2019 in Baltic birch plywood thinned to 9mm bottom and 6mm sides, weighed against his own earlier Puddle Duck Racer.
* [Andreas Westerberg](andreas-westerberg.md) - Andreas Westerberg, a builder in Sweden who 3D-modelled his Paradox from the plans before cutting anything, then CNC-routed the bulkheads and other parts rather than shaping them by hand.
* [Christian Blohm](christian-blohm.md) - Christian Blohm, a builder in north-east Ohio, USA, who began a Paradox named "Rebel" in 2023, built her bulkheads, beams and side panels with careful preparation, and was still building her as of 2026.
* [Erik Blohm](erik-blohm.md) - Erik Blohm, a builder in northeast Ohio, USA, on Lake Erie, who began a Paradox, later named "Rebel," in February 2023, researching each material choice before committing to it.
* [Dave Fahy](dave-fahy.md) - Dave Fahy, a builder on the Gold Coast, Queensland, Australia, who built and launched an Enigma 3.6, "Goliath," in 2021, then progressively increased her ballast to tame a tender hull.
* [Mark Brothers](mark-brothers.md) - Mark Brothers, a former racing sailor in Newcastle, New South Wales, Australia, returned to sailing in 2022 by refitting Nick Bell's secondhand Paradox "Thor" as "Pamela B," for which Storer later drew the Mk3 sail.
* [Johan Bloom](johan-bloom.md) - Johan Bloom owns and sails "Tetris," a Divinycell foam-sandwich Paradox built by Kjell Karlsson in Sven Yrvind's "BRIS" method, out of the Bohuslän fjords on Sweden's west coast, year-round.
* [Greg Lockhart](greg-lockhart.md) - A Paradox builder on his fourth boat, "Endeavour," begun in June 2011, who hoped she might one day merit comparison to Glen Maxwell's "Zoë," lost to fire that same month.
* [Andres Espino](andres-espino.md) - Andres Espino is a Glen-L sharpie builder in the United States who stretched his own "Fancy Free" from 21 to 26 ft himself; his sharpie-type and scaling knowledge applies to Paradox from outside it.
* [Edgardo Filon](edgardo-filon.md) - A builder from Mar del Plata, Argentina, who requested the Paradox and Enigma plans by post in 2009 and launched his own Paradox, "Nebula," by October 2011.
* [Bobby (Oklahoma City)](okiebob.md) - An Oklahoma City builder who scaled his own Paradox to 85 percent as "Paradox 357," kept the Paradox builders' registry from 2015 to 2018 and later bought a second Paradox, "Oogway," from Michael Beebe.
* [Richard (Tarn-et-Garonne)](richard-tarn-et-garonne.md) - A builder in France's Tarn-et-Garonne department, who tested resorcinol against epoxy under controlled conditions before building his own Paradox, "Kolaska," completing her beams by August 2014 after two other small-boat builds.
* [Perry Phillips](perry-phillips.md) - A southern Alabama builder who kept the Paradox bottom at full thickness after grounding his West Wight Potter, then built a stock-design Paradox in cypress and okoume plywood from 2009.
* [John Lehman](john-lehman.md) - A Milwaukee, Wisconsin builder who built and sailed a stitch-and-glue Paradox in the United Kingdom, sold her on returning to the United States and began a second Paradox at home in 2008.
* [sumedho1000](sumedho1000.md) - sumedho1000 began building a Paradox in April 2009, found the plans' scarf, drain and lightning-ground details held as drawn before cutting, and had the bulkheads made and hull panels cut within a month.
* [Michael Beebe](michael-beebe.md) - A builder on the Texas Gulf coast who launched his first Paradox in 2012, later built a second, sold and renamed Oogway, and became a recurring "Meanderings" columnist for Messing About In Boats.
* [Andy](sanibelowner.md) - Andy built a Paradox, "Moxie," through 2012 and held to the plans' own plywood thickness as the boat's structural foundation.
* [Gunther Battista](gunther-battista.md) - An Austria-based first-time builder and sailor who pre-named his 20%-enlarged Paradox "Manatee" in 2013, studied other builders' documented hulls before cutting a single panel, and resumed her build in 2019 after standing idle since 2014.
* [Christian Skjutare](christian-skjutare.md) - Christian Skjutare, a first-time solo builder from Skåne, southern Sweden, built the Paradox "Martha," launched in 2017 as "Gripen," and offered her free in 2020 after a house move damaged her.
* [Stephen Barwell](stephen-barwell.md) - A builder in Moreton Bay, Queensland, Australia, who began building the Paradox "SaSa" in 2013 and had the hull half-built by 2016, and hosts "the Jollybod," a shallow-water sailing event on North Stradbroke Island held each winter and summer.
* [Anders Nygaard](anders-nygaard.md) - A recreational boatbuilder in Porsgrunn, southern Norway, building "Miramis," a Paradox scaled up 10% with 3D-printed fittings and a home-sewn sail, and the compiler of "A guide to the Paradox sailboat" (2023).
* [Tony Barlow](tony-barlow.md) - Tony Barlow, builder of "Miko," a Paradox launched at Phuket, Thailand in March 2024, fitted out for the region's light winds and heat.
* [Benjamin Bloss](benjamin-bloss.md) - Benjamin Bloss, a builder north of Seattle, Washington, with a maritime career behind him, began building "Pathfinder" in December 2021 and had reached her sail by August 2025, documenting the build in his own video log.
* [Kevin Williamson](kevin-williamson.md) - Kevin Williamson, a Perth, Western Australia owner who bought Roselt Croeser's junk-rigged Paradox "Planktos" in 2023, renamed her "Skye" and fitted a folding mast so her rig can be struck without unrigging.
* [Alex McGavin](alex-mcgavin.md) - Alex McGavin, an aluminium boatbuilder who welded an aluminium Paradox hull in 3 mm 5005 plate, with a companion 4 mm hull built alongside for a friend.
* [Rob Kellock](rob-kellock.md) - A Paradox builder and owner from Canterbury, New Zealand, near Christchurch, who changed his boat's rig after sailing and comparing both versions, and built a GPS-tracked course to test the design's hull-speed claims.
* [Tom (squared bow)](tom-sailor.md) - A builder who restarted a long-shelved Paradox in February 2020 and carried her from stored bulkheads to a flipped hull within four months, squaring the bow and planning a junk rig.
* [Nickolas Bell](nickolas-bell.md) - Nickolas Bell, a South Australian builder, sailed the Paradox "Thor" from her 2015 launch until selling her in 2022, then rode a home-built houseboat 1,550 km down the Murray River.
* [Darren (Victoria, BC)](darren-victoria.md) - A sailing instructor in Victoria, British Columbia, building a Paradox with a split lug sail drawn from an AYRS junk-rig design, planned in 2013 to circumnavigate Vancouver Island after a season of trials.
* [phil_e.stine](phil-e-stine.md) - phil_e.stine worked out from first principles whether a Paradox could be meaningfully enlarged, tracked the question to its first completed example and compiled the design's sail-insignia photo album.
* [newingtonnate](newingtonnate.md) - A British Columbia, Canada builder who left out the stock vent box for extra room and launched his Paradox, "Bye Bye Love," by March 2014.
* [Rick Langer](rick-langer.md) - Rick Langer, a boat builder from the northeastern United States, sought the Paradox plans and building manual in 2009 intending to build that winter, and devised a scrap-plywood screw-clamping technique for setting deck panels.
* [John Starkie](john-starkie.md) - A Norfolk, England sailor with no Paradox of his own, known for explaining navigation rules by the rule number and reasoning chine runner and hull geometry from first principles, active since 2013.
* [Terry Joseph](terry-joseph.md) - A builder from Newcastle, New South Wales, who discovered the Paradox in 2015 and began building "Quixotic" within a week, working at a deliberately unhurried, one-task-a-day pace.
* [Brian ("Brian in PA")](brian-in-pa.md) - An American builder from Clearfield County, Pennsylvania, who built a Gorfnik, a Puddle Duck Racer hull with a Paradox-style cabin, as its second builder, and named his own copy "Kinfrog."
* [William Johnston](william-johnston.md) - A first-time sailor who built a Paradox's rig through 2015 and 2016, rebuilding a warped boom and, the next year, a too-heavy mast, hinging it on a tabernacle for easier handling alone.
* [Jolyn Barley](jolyn-barley.md) - Jolyn Barley, a solo sailor from Vancouver Island, British Columbia, entered the Race to Alaska on a Hobie Tandem Island in 2024 and on a Paradox in 2026, the smallest boat in that year's fleet.
* [Doug Cameron](doug-cameron.md) - A WaterTribe racer and small-boat writer from Sewanee, Tennessee who races as Ridgerunner, is the author of the Small Craft Advisor profile of Matt Layden and recorded several sightings of his boats at shared races.
* [Tom Sorensen](tom-sorensen.md) - Tom Sorensen, known as TDSoren, built Jim Michalak's "Robbsboat" and a cambered junk rig trimaran, gaining practical experience in steam bending, ballast plumbing and junk rig conversion.
* [Jack Brubaker](jack-brubaker.md) - Jack Brubaker, a builder from Bloomington, Indiana, has enlarged a Paradox to 18 ft since 2015, with ballast tanks in the sides and a foredeck hatch and anchor locker of his own design.
* [Sybe Starkenburg](sybe-starkenburg.md) - Sybe Starkenburg, a retired mariner and navigator from Montagu, South Africa, built an unnamed Paradox from 2017 to about 2021 and released StarFinder, a celestial navigation Android app, in December 2021.
* [Frederic Tallman](frederic-tallman.md) - A Central New York sailor who signs himself Deric, who bought Don Elliott's completed Paradox, "The Edge," in 2017 and later built a wood-hulled Gorfnik, "Cracker Jack."
* [Stéphane Cordier](stephane-cordier.md) - A Belgian professional woodworker who began building a Paradox around November 2017, drawn to the design after following Lezlie Henson's "Tardis" build blog and already the owner of an ocean-going steel cruiser.
* [Jörgen Agardtson](joergen-agardtson.md) - A Swedish builder of "Rahänd," a Paradox under slow, continuing construction, who built the mast himself in 2017 and gave the bottom panel two layers of plywood in place of the plans' single sheet.
* [Steve Collins](steve-collins.md) - A sailor on the Gulf Coast of the United States who became the second owner of Dave Skaife's Hawaii-built Paradox in the spring of 2018, renaming her "[Paw Paw](/boats/paw-paw.md)."
* [Cory Sellars](cory-sellars.md) - A carpenter in Bruce County, Ontario, Canada, finishing a part-built Paradox rescued from a burn pile after her original builder's death and renamed "[Prescott Eagle](/boats/prescott-eagle.md)" in his memory, to tour the Bruce Peninsula.
* [Kreig (Missouri)](kreig-missouri.md) - A Blue Springs, Missouri builder who finished his own Paradox in Douglas fir, laminating the sheer and chine logs in two staggered-scarf pieces, and first sailed her in the fall of 2022.
