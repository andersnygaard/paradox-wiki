# Jolyn Barley

**Jolyn Barley** is a solo sailor from **Vancouver Island, British Columbia, Canada**. Barley has entered the **[Race to Alaska](/merits/race-to-alaska.md)**, the roughly 750-mile engine-free race from Port Townsend, Washington, to Ketchikan, Alaska, twice under the team name Forget Me Knot: in 2024 on an 18 ft 6 in Hobie Tandem Island, and in 2026 on a **[Paradox](/paradox.md)**, the smallest boat registered in that year's fleet.[1][2] The Hobie attempt ended in withdrawal after leaks in its hull and both amas and other equipment failures. The retirement statement the race published afterward closed, "I will be just that much more prepared for next time!"[3] The Paradox entry two years later was that return.

## Smallest boat in the fleet

Barley registered for the 2026 race on 9 April as Team Forget Me Knot, declaring a Paradox of thirteen feet ten inches, crewed by one, and appeared on the printed team cheat sheet as boat number 66.[2][4] The race's own fleet statistics, published four days later, made a point of the contrast: "Smallest boat: 13 feet. Team Forget Me Knot ... sailing a Paradox. What's a Paradox? Google it," against a largest boat of 35 feet in a fleet that also ran to 16 trimarans, 37 monohulls, 2 catamarans, an outrigger sailing canoe, 4 kayaks, 4 rowboats, a paddleboard and a pedal craft.[5]

The declared human propulsion was a Hobie Mirage Drive and a [yuloh](/components/yuloh.md) together. A yuloh is standard equipment on a Paradox, and the design's builders had examined a Mirage Drive for the boat twice, in 2006 and again in 2008, rejecting it both times: the drive unit would block the flat cabin floor, its trunk would have to rise above the waterline and a fin drive was judged ineffective on a hull as heavy as a Paradox (see [pedal power and why a Mirage Drive does not suit Paradox](/operations/auxiliary-outboard-power.md#pedal-power-and-why-a-mirage-drive-does-not-suit-paradox)). Barley's entry carried the Mirage Drive alongside the yuloh rather than instead of it, declared for a race where engines are barred outright and a calm is settled by muscle.[2]

## Preparation and result

Preparation was still under way nine days before the start: local press placed Forget Me Knot among three Vancouver Island teams in their last week of readying for a race leaving Port Townsend on 14 June.[6] The 2026 race's official Stage One results record the entry as DNS, did not start, the only such result among seventy-one rows, and the team does not appear in the Stage Two results in any category.[7] No reason is given. A Paradox was entered, registered and prepared for one of the toughest engine-free races in North America but did not leave the start line.
