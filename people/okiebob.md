# OkieBob

**OkieBob**, who signs himself "Bobby" or "Bobby, Oklahoma, USA," is a first-time boatbuilder from **Oklahoma City** who took up the Paradox design in 2010 and stayed active in the community through 2018. He cut his first scarf joint that July, ultimately built at **85 percent** of the plan scale, and named the result "**Paradox 357**" after doing the arithmetic himself: 85 percent of the stock 420 cm hull comes to 357 cm. In 2015 he built the community's own successor builders' registry and kept it current for three years before handing it to the moderators in 2018. In January 2017 he bought a second, already-finished Paradox from **[Michael Beebe](/people/michael-beebe.md)** and named her "**[Oogway](/boats/oogway.md)**" after the *Kung Fu Panda* films.

## Building "Paradox 357"

He first asked how to order the Paradox plans, drawing a reply from Pete Martin, who pointed him to Dave Bolduc and to Pete's own "Johanna" build, then under way.[1] Four weeks later, still before his own build began, he asked whether anyone was seriously pursuing an [Elusion](/matt-layden/elusion.md), calling Matt Layden's boats "fascinating."[2] Pete Martin's "Johanna," seen through video and blog, kept drawing his admiration in the weeks that followed.[3] That June he committed himself outright: "Well mates, I'm in!" He had never built a boat before, though he had "dreamed about it since I was a teenager."[4]

He cut wood for the first time that July, starting the hull-bottom scarph joint; unfamiliar with a block plane, he tried a series of backsaw kerfs instead and abandoned the method after the first kerf caught the plane on every pass.[5] The joint was glued with resorcinol rather than epoxy, and the comparison against epoxy he kept running over the following months became the archive's fullest firsthand resorcinol-versus-epoxy account, told in full at [construction/fastening-and-gluing.md](/construction/fastening-and-gluing.md#a-resorcinol-alternative-to-epoxy).[6] He started a public build blog at the end of that month, explicit that he was paying forward the help he had drawn from other builders' own sites and would document the project "probably more likely by showing what not to do."[7] Yuloh-handling tips picked up from other builders proved useful on a separate rowing boat he was fitting out at the same time.[8]

The build itself, her scale and the departures she took from the stock plan are told in full at [boats/paradox-357.md](/boats/paradox-357.md). The scale he settled on, after an unreconciled early figure, was 85 percent; in September 2015, fitting the stem, he measured the finished hull at almost exactly 357 cm and named her for it, restating the arithmetic in his own words that January: 85 percent of the stock 420 cm comes to 357 cm.[9]

## The Paradox registry

In July 2015 he proposed a community-maintained registry to succeed the aging 2014 census, noting a fellow builder's own build blog was informative but never meant to serve as one.[10] Two days later he had built a first phase himself, drawn from members' own photo albums, calling it "a starting place" and inviting corrections, which arrived within a day.[11] The corrections that followed, and the registry's own mechanics, are kept at [references/builders-and-owners-census-2014.md](/references/builders-and-owners-census-2014.md), which tells the fuller story of what he built and who helped fix it. His own registry work turned up at least one real discrepancy: a Paradox sold in 2001 by Bob Archibald to David Beard came back named "Minnow" rather than "Whisper," a discrepancy explored in full at [boats/whisper.md](/boats/whisper.md#a-2015-registry-thread-discrepancy-minnow).[12] Within its first week he judged the new registry about ninety percent complete, the launch dates for a few boats the main remaining gap.[13] A registry question about Don Elliott's own Paradox went unanswered, though the answer already existed elsewhere: she survived him as "[The Edge](/boats/edge.md)."[14]

A year on, having drifted out of touch after switching to a Mac, he renewed the registry call, asking whoever could still edit it to look it over; he had, by then, also agreed to buy a second, already-built Paradox (see [Buying and sailing Oogway](#buying-and-sailing-oogway) below).[15] Revisiting the registry again that June, he wondered about removing a stale entry for an unfinished Paradox that had been offered for sale years earlier and never confirmed completed, a question that went unanswered. The original listing is at [operations/secondhand-market.md](/operations/secondhand-market.md#an-abandoned-build-for-sale-rhxii-april-2006).[16]

In June 2018 he stepped back, restricting further edits to the community's own moderators and recommending that newly built boats not be added until actually launched, and closed with his own reflection on the years since: he described himself as "a one displacement boat guy" who happened to own two displacement boats, his other being an '87 Rob Roy 23, and settled on making the Rob Roy his focus going forward, a turn he called surprising for a self-described "boat-whore."[17] A later question about where to find the registry drew the direct answer: it was kept on the community's own website, under Database, entry four.[18][19]

## Buying and sailing Oogway

OkieBob met [Michael Beebe](/people/michael-beebe.md) in person for the first time at the 2012 Texas 200, his first close-up look at a finished Paradox, "very exciting for me"; thick mud at the anchorage kept him from actually climbing aboard, though Beebe offered.[20] He had followed [Lezlie Henson](/people/lezlie-henson.md)'s "Tardis" since, hoping to see her at that year's event; when she came up for sale that December he reacted at once: "Congratulations Lezlie on your new journey! Tardis looks like a very nice boat. Somebody looking to own a brand new paradox without having to build one will be in luck. These aren't available very often."[21] Watching her sail the following September as "[Scout](/boats/scout.md)" under her new owner, he admitted he was "kicking myself for missing the brief window she was for sale."[22] He had tried, unsuccessfully, to buy Beebe's own first Paradox years earlier; the closest documented trace is his own December 2013 relay of her for-sale listing.[23] A guided look at Beebe's second Paradox at the 2015 Port Aransas festival found him admiring, fifteen months ahead of time, the boat he would end up buying.[24]

In January 2017, renewing his registry call, he announced that Beebe had agreed, the day before, to sell him that second Paradox, making him an owner of the design at last.[15] A week later he confirmed the deal complete: a 1,200-mile round trip towed the boat home "like a dream," and a slower look at her afterward found "stout construction" and a workboat finish he judged himself free to treat roughly.[25] Chris Curtis, who had met him years before at a Lake Eufaula gathering, congratulated him warmly; full account at [people/chris-curtis.md](/people/chris-curtis.md).[26] Days later, prompted by his daughter playing a Disney game, he settled on a name: "**Oogway**," from the *Kung Fu Panda* films, "fits perfect, and now I got that monkey off my back."[27]

He sailed Oogway for the first time that May, a nervy but successful first departure that ended in a memorable retrieval, drawing a standing ovation from a crowd gathered onshore for a fishing tournament.[28] A furling line that proved incredibly difficult to pull drew tips from three further builders that June; his own eventual fix combined mast rotation, extra line on the spool, and releasing the mainsheet before furling, and he judged most such problems came down to user error.[29][30] Full record of the boat, her modifications and her first season under his ownership is at [boats/oogway.md](/boats/oogway.md).

## Disambiguation

OkieBob used three separate accounts over the life of the build, each signed with the same standing "Bobby, Oklahoma" signature and continuing, without a break, the same first-person build narrative: one account took up an order announced days earlier by another, still in the first person.[6] A third account first appears in November 2011 and recurs through the following year.[31][32] None of the three carried a distinct display name of its own, so the identification rests on that unvarying signature and the narrative continuity, not on any header field. The account-level detail is in the citations.
