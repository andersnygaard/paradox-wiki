# OkieBob

**OkieBob** (Yahoo handle `o_k_i_e_bob`, signing "Bobby" or "Bobby, Oklahoma, USA") is a first-time boatbuilder from Oklahoma City who cut wood for his own Paradox for the first time in July 2010, decided partway through to build at **90% of the plan scale**, and documented the project on a public blog, `okieboat.blogspot.com`.[2][6] No boat name is on record. He posts across at least three distinct Yahoo accounts over the life of the build — the standing `rscockerham@...`, an occasional `okiebob@...`, and a further `okiebobby@...` — each carrying the same "Bobby, Oklahoma"/"Okc" signature and the same first-person build narrative; treated throughout the archive as one person on that basis, not three (see [Three accounts, one builder](#three-accounts-one-builder) below).

## Building at 90% scale

His first cut, a hull-bottom scarph joint, went wrong the ordinary way a first cut does: unfamiliar with a block plane, he tried a series of backsaw kerfs instead and abandoned the method after the first kerf caught the plane on every pass.[2] He asked, in the same message, whether the 1/2 in side panels should be scarfed as full sheets or from leftover offcuts, worried that anything less than a full panel was beyond his skill; full text and the answers he drew are at [scarf joints — full-panel vs. leftover-offcut scarfing](/construction/scarf-joints.md#full-panel-vs-leftover-offcut-scarfing-for-the-side-panels).[3] For clamping the bottom-panel scarph itself, he proposed laying the panels on a plastic-sheeted garage floor, butted against a wall and held with bolts and washers rather than nails, then weighted with bricks or lead — refined in reply into using the boat's own ballast lead as the weight instead; full exchange at [scarf joints — clamping and weighting a scarf joint through the glue's cure](/construction/scarf-joints.md#clamping-and-weighting-a-scarf-joint-through-the-glues-cure).[4] For the next joint he planned circular-saw crosscuts ahead of the plane, to clear the bulk of the waste first.[5] He started his build blog at the end of July 2010, explicit that he was paying forward the help he had drawn from other builders' own sites and would document the project "probably more likely by showing what not to do."[6]

By September 2010 the scale decision was settled and explained: building at 90% rather than the stock 100%, having already committed to the plans' full 3/4 in bottom-panel thickness before making the call, and accepting the resulting extra weight as ballast rather than switching to a thinner panel.[7][8] Full account at [a 2010 real-world 90% scale-down, mid-build](/design/ideas/scaled-down-idea.md#a-2010-real-world-90-scale-down-mid-build).

## The resorcinol trial

OkieBob supplied the archive's fullest firsthand resorcinol-versus-epoxy comparison. Having ordered a gallon from CP Adhesives for the bottom-panel scarph,[1] he used it for the first time that August: short of the instructions' recommended 25-75 lb/sq² clamping pressure (which would have needed roughly 7,500 lb over his ~300 in² joint), he substituted stacked water-filled buckets for about 1 lb/sq², treated that week's ~100°F local heat as an aid since resorcinol reportedly favors heat and pressure, and left the joint fully undisturbed through the full six-day cure. Working notes: it goes on like wine, then thickens like jam, cleans up easily with a damp towel before hardening, and needs comparatively little volume for a whole joint. He judged himself sold on it for structural wood-to-wood joints if a planned boil-and-strength test held up, while still favoring epoxy for anything involving fiberglass.[9] That test was dropped a month later once the cured joint proved strong enough on its own, and further 1/2 in side-panel scarphs went ahead with the same glue.[10] Thanking a French correspondent's own controlled resorcinol-versus-epoxy strength test in December 2010, he added that his own hot Oklahoma summers — and plans for trips to hotter climates still — made resorcinol's heat tolerance the more useful property for him; an earlier, more loosely scarfed joint had made settling noises that stopped on their own, and none of his glued boards could be pried apart.[10] Full account at [fastening and gluing — a resorcinol alternative to epoxy](/construction/fastening-and-gluing.md#a-resorcinol-alternative-to-epoxy).

## Other contributions

Before his own build began, he asked whether anyone was seriously pursuing an [Elusion](/matt-layden/elusion.md), calling Matt Layden's boats "fascinating" — the archive's earliest sign of his interest in the designs, six weeks ahead of his first plans question.[11] In October 2011, well into his own build, he floated a hypothetical "Paradox 140%" — the largest hull his own garage could store — with a small mizzen and a small forward-placed centerboard while keeping the chine runners, and, once pointed to the `microcruising` Yahoo group's files section, found a "Big Cruiser" sketch there he had not expected.[12][13] Full exchange at [larger Paradox — a 140% hypothetical](/design/larger-paradox.md#a-140-hypothetical-a-small-mizzen-forward-centerboard-and-chine-runners-kept).

## A reefing-drum and swivel-rod fabrication project (2012)

In early 2012 he worked through a full reefing-drum/swivel-rod fabrication project step by step: after a shop-hopping search for equipment to bend the plans' brass rod came up empty, he built his own jig at home from wood clamps and a steel cheater bar, and finished the fitting with a hardware-store clevis pin and key ring in place of the plans' bolt. Full account at [components/rig/tack-strop.md](/components/rig/tack-strop.md#a-2012-how-to-thread-adhesive-rod-material-and-three-further-bending-methods).[15][16] Weeks later, answering a fellow builder's own question about the same swivel rod's material, he reported using Naval Brass sourced from Duckworks for the fitting, pleased with the results so far; full account at the same page.[17] Separately building a practice boat of his own — a Puddle Duck Racer scaled to Paradox proportions — he asked in the same message whether it would be ready in time for that year's Texas 200, uncertain himself, while also asking whether "[Tardis](/boats/scout.md)" would be finished before the same event.[17] Lezlie Henson answered directly, still hoping to have Tardis finished herself despite a grandchild due the same week (full account at [people/lezlie-henson.md](/people/lezlie-henson.md#citations)); he in turn confirmed the exchange had tipped him toward attending that year's Texas 200 himself, meant to watch for her and Tardis at Seadrift if he made it, and thanked her again for sharing a building blog he found "helpful, inspiring, and entertaining."[18]

## Meeting a fellow builder in person at the 2012 Texas 200

That June, having wondered months earlier whether he would make it to the event at all, he did: at the **2012 Texas 200** he ran into [Michael Beebe](/people/michael-beebe.md) and Lezlie Henson in person, getting his first close-up look at a finished Paradox — "very exciting for me." Thick mud at the anchorage kept him from actually climbing aboard, though Beebe offered. He filmed Beebe's boat executing a particularly difficult tack into a strong wind, in close quarters, at the Padre Island Yacht Club in Corpus Christi, judging the boat sailed well and Beebe a smooth sailor.[19] After the video went up, he posted a second YouTube link as a backup, pointing searchers to his own handle or "Mike's Paradox 2012 TX200" if the first didn't work.[20]

## Watching "Tardis" become "Scout" (2013)

Congratulating Sean Mulligan on the finished "[Scout](/boats/scout.md)" (ex-"Tardis") the week of her second shakedown sail, OkieBob admitted to "kicking myself for missing the brief window she was for sale" — the same boat he had asked after, hoping to see her at the Texas 200, back in February 2012.[21]

## A further 2014 recurrence: a mast-step-height alternative

Resurfacing the cabin-top-height thread two months on, proposed raising the mast step itself rather than lengthening the mast, to gain rig height without the mast growing too long to stow inside the hull, and asked how much stowage clearance a stock Paradox actually leaves and whether navigation lights need removing for it. Full exchange, and the replies it drew: [crew size and cabin fit — a 2014 exchange: the boom, mast, and hatch consequences of raising the cabin](/operations/crew-size-and-fit.md#a-2014-exchange-the-boom-mast-and-hatch-consequences-of-raising-the-cabin).[23]

## A 2015 deck-carlins question

Nearly five years into public build documentation, he asked whether the stock plans really call for only two deck carlins — both running the side decks — leaving the fore- and aft-decks supported by deck beams alone, having recalled build photos that seemed to show more deck support than the plans drew; confirmed correct, with the further explanation that heavier-looking photos usually show one of Matt Layden's later, lighter designs, which need more structure under a correspondingly lighter deck. Full exchange: [deck and cabin beam lamination — carlins versus deck beams](/construction/deck-and-cabin-beams.md#carlins-versus-deck-beams-what-actually-supports-the-fore-and-aft-decks).[24]

## A 2015 registry and census proposal

Floated the idea of a group-maintained database to track completed Paradoxes — year completed, builder, current name, and the boat's location — noting a fellow builder's own [build blog](/references/pete-martin-blog.md) is informative but was never intended to serve that purpose, and asking whether such a census already existed that let builders and owners register changes themselves, reasoning that keeping it current long-term needs tracking at the group level rather than by one person's blog. Recent group photos, in his own reading, showed "an explosion of newly and nearly completed Paradoxes." See [the 2014 census](/references/builders-and-owners-census-2014.md) for the archive's own most complete existing registry.[25] Two days later he had actually built one: a first phase compiled from members' own Yahoo Groups photo albums, with dates guessed where not otherwise known and owner/builder pairings left unverified, calling it "a starting place" and inviting corrections — which [Alastair Law](/people/alastair-law.md) supplied within a day. See [references/builders-and-owners-census-2014.md — a short-lived successor registry](/references/builders-and-owners-census-2014.md#a-2015-proposal-for-a-group-maintained-update-and-a-short-lived-successor-registry) for what those corrections added.[26] He went on to ask, unanswered on record, who had bought [Don Elliott](/people/don-elliott.md)'s own Paradox after his 2008 death — already recorded elsewhere in the archive as kept by his family, sailing her as "[The Edge](/boats/edge.md)."[27] Separately he asked after the fate of the Paradox Bob Archibald sold to David Beard in 2001 — long since named "Whisper" and fully documented in the archive — drawing a reply that instead named her "Minnow," recorded as an unresolved discrepancy at [boats/whisper.md](/boats/whisper.md#a-2015-registry-thread-discrepancy-minnow).[28]

Two days later he judged the registry about 90% accurate and 10% incomplete, with launch dates the main remaining gap — all but a handful were filled in, the rest concentrated among boats whose completion status was itself unclear, other than "[Raffles](/boats/raffles.md)," whose launch date he still wanted specifically, asking whether anyone stayed in touch with [Mark Waters](/people/mark-waters.md).[29] Reading the group's own recent traffic, he had not expected so many completed Paradoxes, and named five more he hoped to add soon — Quest, "Mini," "[Planktos](/boats/planktos.md)," "[Moxie](/boats/moxie.md)," and "[Manatee](/boats/manatee.md)" — while continuing his own slow progress; from Oklahoma City, he hoped for a chance to see "Quest" in Dallas and [Michael Beebe](/people/michael-beebe.md)'s new Paradox in Rockport. The same message noted [Dave Skaife](/people/dave-skaife.md), by then rounding the Gulf of Mexico by the Intracoastal Waterway from Port Isabel, Texas, as making full use of his own new coastal cruiser.[29] Thanked [André-François Bourbeau](/people/andre-francois-bourbeau.md) for posting his own registry corrections, and, noting Bourbeau owns two Paradoxes, asked whether there is much difference between them — unanswered on record.[30] Separately, still working toward finishing his own Paradox as of mid-July 2015, found the group's yuloh tips applicable to rigging oarlocks on a rowing boat he was setting up in parallel.[31] A month later, a fellow builder's registry question about an unfamiliar photographed boat (see [people/john-starkie.md](/people/john-starkie.md#a-modified-paradox-with-no-chine-runners-and-a-keel-theory-of-tacking)) prompted him to add that boat's own entry to the registry, though only partially, hoping its builder or someone who knew him could supply more complete and accurate information.[32] The following week, replying on a thread whose own opening message is not preserved in the archive, asked whether it was time to add that correspondent's own Paradox to the registry too — unanswered on record.[33]

## Dry-fitting the hull, and a scale discrepancy (August 2015)

Five years into the build, side panels were dry-fitted to the bulkheads with a neighbour's help, trued using the section-by-section diagonal-comparison method described in full at [hull assembly — truing the hull](/construction/hull-assembly-sequence.md#truing-the-hull-the-diagonal-comparison-method). The same message restated the build's scale as **85%** — moved bulkheads fore and aft from their stock positions for sleeping space — a figure at odds with the **90%** scale settled on and reported in 2010 (above); the record does not explain the discrepancy, left here as recorded rather than resolved.[34]

## Three accounts, one builder

Three separate `From:` addresses carry this builder's own first-person build narrative at different points: the standing `rscockerham@...`; `okiebob@...`, first appearing mid-thread in August 2010 continuing, in the first person, the very resorcinol order announced days earlier from `rscockerham@...`, and signed with the same standing "Bobby, Oklahoma, USA";[9] and `okiebobby@...`, appearing in November 2011, its own `X-Yahoo-Profile` again simply mirroring the e-mail address rather than carrying a distinct handle, signed "Bobby, Okc, USA" and giving the same Oklahoma City location.[14] None of the three carries a distinct profile handle of its own — each mirrors the account's e-mail address — so identity here rests on content continuity and the unvarying signature, per this project's own standard for occasional secondary accounts. Flagged here so a future batch does not misread any of the three as a separate person.
