# Bobby (Oklahoma City)

**Bobby** is a first-time boatbuilder from **Oklahoma City** who began building a scaled-down Paradox in 2010, naming her "**[Paradox 357](/boats/paradox-357.md)**" for the arithmetic behind her: 85 percent of the stock 420 cm hull comes to 357 cm.[1] In 2015 he built and kept a community-maintained registry of Paradox builders and owners, handing routine upkeep to its own moderators in 2018.[2][3] In January 2017 he bought a second, already-built Paradox from **[Michael Beebe](/people/michael-beebe.md)** and named her "**[Oogway](/boats/oogway.md)**" after the *Kung Fu Panda* films.[4]

## Building "Paradox 357"

Pete Martin's own "[Johanna](/boats/johanna.md)," then under construction, drew him to the design in 2010.[5] Never having built a boat before, he committed himself that June: "Well mates, I'm in!"[6] He began cutting wood for the hull that July and that same month started a public build blog to pay forward the trail he had followed from other builders' own sites.[7]

He built at a reduced scale from the start, the opposite direction from builders drawn to scaling the design up: by September 2010 he had settled, for the time, on 90 percent of the stock size, a decision made after the bottom panel had already been cut to the plans' full thickness rather than a proportionately thinner one; rather than start over, he treated the extra material as ballast, reasoning the weight sat low in the hull exactly where ballast is wanted.[8] The same economy held at the stem: a first piece cut from scrap wood gave way to one in Douglas fir, unwilling to risk a cheaper timber failing years down the line to save a few dollars.[9] The scale later settled at 85 percent, the arithmetic that gave the boat her name; the build itself and where its record here ends are told in full at [boats/paradox-357.md](/boats/paradox-357.md).

## The Paradox registry

The design's published counts had gone stale by then, each depending on one person's own site staying current rather than on builders and owners keeping it up themselves; Pete Martin's build blog was informative but had never been meant for that job.[2] In July 2015 he proposed, then built within two days, a registry recording each boat's completion year, builder, current name and location, open to being kept current by anyone; he called the first version "a starting place" and invited corrections.[2] It persisted for years, with him its de facto keeper, until he handed routine upkeep to its own moderators in June 2018.[3] Its fuller history, including the corrections it drew, is at [references/builders-and-owners-census-2014.md](/references/builders-and-owners-census-2014.md#a-2015-proposal-for-a-group-maintained-update-and-a-short-lived-successor-registry).

## Buying and sailing Oogway

He met [Michael Beebe](/people/michael-beebe.md) in person for the first time at the 2012 Texas 200, his first close look at a finished Paradox; thick mud at the anchorage kept him from boarding her, though Beebe offered.[10] He had already tried, unsuccessfully, to buy Beebe's first Paradox.[4] In January 2017 Beebe agreed to sell him the second, already-built boat and he named her "[Oogway](/boats/oogway.md)" after the *Kung Fu Panda* films.[4] Her first season under his ownership and the modifications he made to her are told in full at [boats/oogway.md](/boats/oogway.md).
