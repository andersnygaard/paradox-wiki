# Benjamin Bloss

**Benjamin Bloss** is a builder **just north of Seattle, Washington**, on **Puget Sound**, constructing the [Paradox](/paradox.md) **"[Pathfinder](/boats/pathfinder.md)"** since his first cuts in **December 2021**.[1] He bought his plans from **Dave Bolduc** in September 2021, after an earlier email bounced, and the boat is his second self-built sailboat, following a Stevenson Weekender from his college years.[2][3] He lays the plans out in **CAD** before cutting wood and documents the build in a **[video build log](/references/sailing-pathfinder-videos.md)** begun before his first cut.[4][5] Full build account: [boats/pathfinder.md](/boats/pathfinder.md).

## From the sea to the workshop

Bloss came to the design from a maritime career rather than from woodworking. He began as a deck officer with the US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, standing navigation watches aboard the exploration ship **Okeanos Explorer** as a commissioning-crew member, the posting that first brought him to Seattle.[6] A sabbatical from a later career in electrical engineering turned into a return to the water by a different route: sailing lessons, volunteer work at Seattle's **Center for Wooden Boats** and a winter spent repairing sails in a Seattle sail loft.[6][7] By 2025 he had built up enough sea time crewing a local tour-boat fleet to work toward a **US Coast Guard captain's licence**, and expected to begin captaining that season.[8]

## Practice over preparation

His approach to building follows the same conviction that shaped his return to the water: skill comes from doing, not from reading about doing. "There's a point where there's diminishing returns on your reading unless you're actively practicing," as he puts it, and he treats his own mistakes as part of the method rather than something to hide.[9] He is explicit that Pathfinder is not a museum piece: during a discouraged break from the build he built a small plywood sharpie instead, and concluded that what he wants from Pathfinder is a boat sound enough "to beat the crap out of on beaches."[10] Part of the original inspiration for choosing Paradox was Alex Zimmerman's **Becoming Coastal**, whose account of cruising Puget Sound and the British Columbia coast showed him how much sailing lay within reach of his own backyard.[11]

## A table of contents for Don Elliott's manual

Bloss's most-used contribution to the community is not on his own boat. Working through [Don Elliott](/people/don-elliott.md)'s [*Building Paradox*](/references/building-paradox-series.md) series, he found the important material scattered across the parts and hard to reach while actually building, so he compiled and published a table of contents for the whole manual, paginated to the printed pages rather than the PDF reader's numbering, with the original spelling left intact so headings stay searchable.[12]

## The build on video

The video log was begun before Bloss's first cut, in conscious imitation of **[Sven Yrvind](/people/yrvind.md)**'s habit of short daily workshop videos, a model he names in his own first minute on camera.[5] He shares the work in progress, mistakes included, because he has himself got value from photographs other builders took of their own.[13] A ham radio operator, he also built a self-contained portable navigation system into a droid-head enclosure, combining GPS, an AIS receiver, a satellite modem and a software-defined radio, so as not to have to build a fixed navigation computer into every boat he sails.[8]
