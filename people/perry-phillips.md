# Perry Phillips

**Perry Phillips** (`perrywphillips`), of **Monroeville, Alabama**, first wrote to the mailing list in March 2007 — already an experienced small-boat sailor, owner of a junk-rigged, early C-Type **West Wight Potter** he named **"Bobber,"** and hoping to see a finished Paradox in person before committing to build one himself. Two years of pre-build questions and a junk-rig sail-plan experiment followed before he began construction to the stock design in May 2009: lead ballast cast, the bottom scarfed, bulkheads and transom done, side panels ready to scarf. No boat name is yet on record for his Paradox.

## Before starting: a build census, a Cedar Key invitation, and a junk-rig experiment

Introducing himself, having never taken on a project this size, he asked whether anyone had assembled a true list of Paradoxes built to date, hoping to see a finished one in person somewhere along the northern Gulf Coast before starting his own.[1] Glen Maxwell told him he was welcome to visit and see "Zoë," and invited him to that year's Cedar Key gathering.[2] The same thread led to an exchange with David Beard, arranging for Beard to detour past Monroeville with "Whisper" en route to Cedar Key, the two comparing driving distances and days off.[3]

Working out the rig for his own boat, he asked, unanswered on record, for a combined weight of the mast, spars, and sail.[4] He also worked up a **105 sq ft Hasler/McLeod junk rig** as an alternative to the stock lug, uploading his own sail-plan drawing to the group's Files area,[5] and, discussing a boomkin for it, cited G. Worcester's illustrations of tall, rectangular junk rigs used on Yangtze-river junks, where the wind often did not reach down to the water's surface.[6]

## Bobber's grounding, and the case against a thinner bottom

His own West Wight Potter, "Bobber," had grounded two years earlier at Palacios Bay, Texas: drawn up overnight on a sandy beach, he was woken about 2 a.m. by a grinding sound and gurgling water as the outgoing tide settled the hull onto three small pieces of gravel that ground through it with the motion. He judged a somewhat thicker bottom would have prevented the damage, and, given Paradox's greater displacement, said he would not consider building one with a thinner bottom than the plans specify.[7] Separately, he asked for a primer on **peel ply**, a technique new to him, hoping to match "Zoë"'s finish.[8]

## Starting construction (2009)

After five years of "lurking" and deliberation, he reported in May 2009 that construction was substantially under way to the stock design: lead ballast cast, the bottom scarfed, bulkheads #2 and #3 and the transom done, side panels cut and ready to scarf, and the rudder head halves cut. Unable to source Douglas fir locally, he bought clear, knot-free **cypress** — 1×12 and 2×12 boards, 16 ft long — from a sawmill near Milton, Florida, for all solid-wood parts and spars, and used **okoume plywood** from Chesapeake Light Craft ("really beautiful wood") with **MAS epoxy** and fiberglass throughout. Full construction detail: [construction/hull-planking.md](/construction/hull-planking.md#alder-and-hemlock-for-structural-and-joiner-work); materials sourcing: [references/materials-sourcing-links.md](/references/materials-sourcing-links.md).[9][10]
