# Perry Phillips

**Perry Phillips**, of **southern Alabama**, is an experienced small-boat sailor who owned a junk-rigged early C-Type West Wight Potter, **"Bobber,"** before building a [Paradox](/paradox.md) of his own to the stock design. Full building detail: [construction/hull-planking.md](/construction/hull-planking.md#alder-and-hemlock-for-structural-and-joiner-work).

## A grounding that set his terms

Bobber grounded at Palacios Bay, Texas, in 2005: drawn up on a sandy beach overnight, she settled onto three small pieces of gravel as the tide fell, and the motion ground them through her bottom, waking him near 2 a.m. to the sound of it.[1] Given a Paradox's greater weight, the grounding set his terms for the build to come: never a bottom thinner than the plans specify.[1]

Working out his own rig beforehand, he weighed a junk rig as an alternative to the stock lug, drawing up a 105 sq ft Hasler/McLeod sail plan and citing G. Worcester's illustrations of similar rigs on Yangtze-river junks, sailed where the wind often did not reach down to the water's surface.[2][3]

## Building in cypress and okoume

Construction began in May 2009, after five years' deliberation: the lead ballast cast, the bottom scarfed, the bulkheads and transom built, the side panels cut and ready to scarf, the rudder head halves cut.[4] Unable to source Douglas fir locally, he bought clear, knot-free cypress boards from a sawmill near Milton, Florida, for the solid-wood parts and spars, and used okoume plywood from Chesapeake Light Craft, in his own words "really beautiful wood," with MAS epoxy and fiberglass throughout.[5] Materials sourcing: [references/materials-sourcing-links.md](/references/materials-sourcing-links.md).
