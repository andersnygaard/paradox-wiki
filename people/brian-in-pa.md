# Brian ("Brian in PA")

**Brian** is a builder from **Clearfield County, Pennsylvania, USA**, whose own boating is confined to ponds, rivers and Curwensville Lake.[1] He bought the Paradox plans and Don Elliott's build manual in 2013, then spent two years weighing Paradox against several of Matt Layden's smaller designs on grounds of weight and ease of build, drawn most to a feature common to several of them: sailing a boat from inside its cabin.[1][2] In August 2015 he cut the first plywood for his own "**[Gorfnik](/similar-boat-designs/gorfnik-paraduck.md)**," a Puddle Duck Racer hull fitted with a Paradox-style cabin, becoming the design's second builder; by that November, with the build nearing completion, she was known as "**Kinfrog**."[3][4] He also built a working cabin heater from copper pipe and drew a 1:10 scale model of the Paradox plans.[5][6] Full build account: [similar-boat-designs/gorfnik-paraduck.md](/similar-boat-designs/gorfnik-paraduck.md).

## Choosing among Matt Layden's designs

Two small boats came before Paradox: one built to David Beede's "Summer Breeze" design and a one-sheet-plywood design of his own.[1] In his own words, "I guess I like starting things better than finishing them, a lifelong student at the school of hard knocks."[7] The same economy shaped his plan for Paradox itself: in an informal build-method poll he was the sole scratch-build vote, preferring to spread the cost of materials out over probably years rather than buy them all at once.[8]

To judge whether Paradox's cabin would actually suit him, he set aside an old, half-remembered account of a cardboard mockup and ran his own test instead: capping his pickup truck's bed and measuring it, finding it close enough in length and height to Paradox's main section to want the boat even more than before.[9][10]

## Building "Kinfrog"

Working from [André-François Bourbeau](/people/andre-francois-bourbeau.md)'s own published building record alone, Brian drew up a complete set of plans for Bourbeau's "Gorfnik" and worked out a leeboard shape from Bourbeau's own photographs and dimensions.[11][12] He cut the first plywood for his own copy in August 2015, deliberately using cheap non-marine ply to prove the plans out and generate the photographs still needed to finish drafting them, becoming the design's second builder after Bourbeau himself.[3] By that November she was known as "Kinfrog."[4] Full build account: [Gorfnik](/similar-boat-designs/gorfnik-paraduck.md).

## A cabin heater, twice built

Brian built a cabin heater from copper pipe twice over. After seeing a fellow builder's homemade charcoal heater in photographs, he drew a CAD version of his own in 2013 and test-built a sheet-metal prototype from scrap flashing and duct tape, fuelled with vaseline-soaked cotton and softwood scraps and judged copper pipe the better material for an eventual real build despite its higher cost.[13][14][15] He returned to the idea in 2015, as his own Gorfnik copy neared completion, and built and test-fired a working copper-pipe heater, hot enough that a fireproof base and a mounted fire extinguisher were flagged as still needed.[5] Full account: [operations/cabin-heating.md](/operations/cabin-heating.md).

## Drafting and small ideas

The Paradox plans are already mostly to scale, bulkheads at 1:5 and the hull at 1:10. In 2016 he drew his own 1:10 scale hull-cutting pattern, printable across six sheets, and offered it for a stitch-and-glue model too.[6] Two years later he took up the plans' recurring mast-drawing confusion, fixed its stray drafting-error arrows and published the annotated copy on his own Flickr page since the usual upload channel would not take it.[16] In 2020 he sketched a lighting technique for a future build: single LED bulbs salvaged from solar yard lights, set into a small drilled hole with a dab of clear epoxy or silicone rather than housed in a fixture.[17]
