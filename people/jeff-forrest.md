# Jeff Forrest

**Jeff Forrest** is a builder in **Southern Oregon**, USA, who began constructing his own [Paradox](/paradox.md) from scratch in December 2001, working from purchased plans and Don Elliott's [Building Paradox](/references/building-the-paradox-articles.md) construction articles.[1] He chose stitch-and-glue construction for the hull rather than the plans' bent-single-sheet method, and before starting had also weighed enlarging the boat by 10 to 15 percent.

## Building stitch-and-glue

His own plans already gave stitch-and-glue dimensions for the side panels.[2] He set out the case for building the rest of the hull the same way: less bulkhead framing, no precise beveling and looser fitting tolerances than the plans' single bent panel, with any weight lost by skipping chine logs made up in ballast, citing published stitch-and-glue designs as proof that bending 1/2 in plywood is manageable and summing the hull up as "basically a box."[3] He used Sam Devlin's published fillet-sizing rule for the joints, depth equal to the thickest ply and width one and a half to two times that thickness.[4]

## A larger boat

Before he began building, he weighed enlarging the hull by 10 to 15 percent, uncertain whether the increase should apply to beam as well as length; see [stretched Paradox](/design/ideas/stretched-paradox.md) for how the design handles a stretch in full.[5][6] He also tried to buy [Paramour](/similar-boat-designs/paramour-design.md), a from-scratch 16 ft redesign offering more room on the same rig, once its drawings were finished, and returned to the question nearly two years later.[7][8]
