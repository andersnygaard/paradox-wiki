# Lezlie Henson

**Lezlie Henson** is the builder of **"Tardis,"** a [Paradox](/paradox.md) she built to a high standard in the **Houston, Texas** area (Pasadena, then Kemah) between 2011 and 2012, as her first from-scratch boatbuilding project. She documented the whole build on a personal blog, `lezliesworld.blogspot.com`, preserved as a first-class source at [references/lezlie-henson-blog.md](/references/lezlie-henson-blog.md); this page draws the builder and her build out of it. She sold the boat **almost finished and never sailed her**; it went on to be well-known under its new owner **[Sean Mulligan](/people/sean-mulligan.md)** as **"[Scout](/boats/scout.md)"** — where the boat's launch, sailing, and Texas 200 record are documented in full.

She signs her blog only "Lezlie." The surname **"Henson"** used here follows the community record — Bill Serjeant's Paradox blog and owner list — though it is spelled inconsistently across sources ("Hensen," and "Hansen" in *Messing About In Boats*), and the blog itself does not give it.[1]

## The build

Henson came to the Paradox as a relative newcomer to both sailing and boatbuilding: she had learned to sail about three and a half years earlier on a **41′ Irwin ketch**, and — after her husband **Dale** found Matt Layden's design and she asked for plywood for Christmas — built two **PDRacers** (Puddle Duck Racers) first, to learn epoxy, fibreglass, rudders, leeboards, and tools, before taking on the Paradox.[2][3] She built to the plans plus **Don Elliott's** construction manual, working questions out on the **[Paradox Yahoo group](/references/mailing-list.md)**.[4]

The build ran roughly two years in a garage and driveway: first dry-fit of the bulkheads in **January 2011**, the bottom on by **March**, fit-out through 2011–12, and the boat all but complete by early 2013 — the **only Paradox she is known to have built**.[4][5]

The mailing list carries an earlier start than the blog does. In **May 2010**, some eight months before the blog's first dry-fit, she announced there that she had the plans and the building manual in hand and had begun laying out the bulkheads that day, introducing herself as a novice builder, asking the group's advice, and undertaking to post progress and photographs as she went.[11] Days later she reported already cutting out the bulkheads and sides, planning to build the rudder and mast next — taking the advice she had been given to make the smaller parts first while she still had the most room to work in.[12] By August 2010 she had reached the mast itself, asking the group for masthead photos and hints, "a picture is worth a thousand words"; see [components/rig/mast.md — sourcing a sheave and rejecting a masthead Windex](/components/rig/mast.md#sourcing-a-sheave-and-rejecting-a-masthead-windex) for the masthead-sheave answers this drew.[13] Once answered, she thanked the group for its help and looked forward to starting a build blog once she had something to show — partly, she said, to help "prod me along," knowing someone might be watching for her progress.[14] Still building that November, she asked how to wire running lights atop the mast and route the cable down to the cabin, then followed up asking how a fellow builder's own low-profile masthead light was made; see [electrical wiring — a further November 2010 recurrence](/construction/electrical-wiring.md#a-further-november-2010-recurrence-and-a-caution-against-an-exposed-mast-base-connector) for the answers this drew.[15][16] Into December she asked how other builders had attached the masthead lightning rod, and separately weighed the plans' built-in water tanks against simple storage and ballast; both were answered by fellow builders rather than herself — see [lightning protection — mounting the rod so it can be removed or swiveled down](/design/lightning-protection.md#mounting-the-rod-so-it-can-be-removed-or-swiveled-down) and [water tank construction and lining — a 2010 resolution](/construction/water-tank-lining.md#a-2010-resolution-watertight-hatches-found-and-a-bilge-pump-emptying-method) for the answers these drew.[17][18] In January 2011, once she had something to show, she shared the promised link with the group — the blog's opening post, published that same day.[19] She tracked the build's total cost as she went, finding it manageable spread out over time rather than paid all at once.[20] The following month, she asked the list how much trouble other builders had installing the chine logs, drawing two independent firsthand accounts of the job's difficulty; see [chine log vs. taped-seam construction — a 2011 recurrence](/construction/chine-log-vs-taped-seam.md#a-2011-recurrence-two-more-builders-firsthand-installation-accounts).[21] Days later she closed her own question out, reporting she had installed her own chine logs in two pieces without incident.[22] Two months after that, she reported the water tanks glassed and awaiting 3-way valves for plumbing, lead ballast collection under way with half the "pigs" already made, again pointing the group to her blog.[23] Asked about her standard of finish, she said that whenever she caught herself thinking "that's good enough," remembering that other good builders might be looking at her pictures pushed her to put in a little more effort — it helped, she said, just to know that people were watching.[24] Some three months later, having done everything else on the boat herself, she asked the group how others had bent the bronze rod for the roller-furling system — themselves, or through a professional shop; see [furling system — bending the furling rod: two working methods](/components/rig/furling.md#bending-the-furling-rod-two-working-methods) for the two techniques this drew.[25] Some six months further on, unable to locate the group's own earlier discussion of it, she raised a related material question for the boom's own swivel rod; see [tack strop — a 2012 recurrence: brass rejected, bronze and stainless favored](/components/rig/tack-strop.md#a-2012-recurrence-brass-rejected-bronze-and-stainless-favored-and-a-further-bending-method) for the debate this drew.[26]

## What she built, and how

The construction is documented in detail on the boat's page; in brief, hers was a **stock Paradox built carefully and a little beyond the minimum**. She **insulated** the hull (not all Paradoxes are) and covered the insulation with thin ply on epoxied dowel pegs; built the plans' **400 lb of lead "pig" ballast** into bolt-down "pens" alongside water-tank ballast that Dale — an electrical engineer — plumbed with 3-way valves for ballast use only; made her own **gimbaled stove** rather than buy one, modelling it on **[Glen Maxwell's](/people/glen-maxwell.md)** stove for "[Zoë](/boats/zoe.md)"; and finished her with off-white deck paint, a navy stripe, bright-varnished spars and trim, and non-skid decks.[5][6][7] The full construction record is at [Scout](/boats/scout.md#the-build-a-stock-paradox-named-tardis).

## Sold unlaunched, and a candid record of why

As late as February 2012 she still hoped to have "Tardis" finished in time for that year's Texas 200, replying to a fellow builder's own question about it — though a grandchild due that same week meant she doubted she could attend even so, and she meant to be at the finish line with the boat regardless, "that is for certain."[27] She made it to the event that June after all, without Tardis, and shared video there of a fellow owner's Paradox under sail.[28]

What sets her account apart is its candour about **why the boat was sold before it ever sailed**. Henson had begun the build as a dream shared with her husband; after they separated, the boat became "a reminder of a dead dream," she lost her motivation to finish and launch, and in **February 2013** she sold "Tardis" almost complete to a buyer in Arizona — writing, "I do wish I had been able to sail her... at least once."[8] Her January 2013 note of sudden interest from the United Kingdom is the seller's-side record of the same episode as the UK import-cost estimate documented on [Scout](/boats/scout.md).[9] When the boat was launched that September as "Scout" by Sean Mulligan, she followed it from his blog, closing her build series on the boat in someone else's hands.[10]

She had already announced the coming sale on the mailing list itself two months earlier, in December 2012, framing it around an opportunity rather than the separation: having the chance to go sailing for an extended period of time, she had decided to sell "Tardis" rather than let her sit gathering dust, planning to leave the first week of January but with someone able to show the boat if she had not sold by then.[29] A reply the next day asked whether she was in Houston — her own known base; she confirmed instead it was Kemah, where she had built the boat.[30][31] The 5 January departure date held, though with much still left to arrange she was unsure she would manage it all in time.[32] The listing drew interest from as far afield as Connecticut and, a few days later, Australia — the Australian reply pricing shipping a boat from the US at $3,000–4,000 and counting only about two Paradoxes then in the country, before concluding "I'll just have to build it" instead — though neither correspondent was close enough to follow through.[33][34] Asked directly what she wanted for the boat, she named **$3,000**, noting "Tardis" needed only to be rigged and that the trailer had been modified to fit her perfectly, and pointed to her blog's "paradox" category for the full build record.[35][36] Unsure the figure was right, she put the pricing question to the list itself in a new thread — see [secondhand Paradox market — a fuller valuation framework](/operations/secondhand-market.md#tardis-priced-at-3000-and-a-fuller-valuation-framework-january-2013) for the discussion this drew.[37]

## A sailmaking offer, a year into "Scout"'s ownership

Answering a fan's question about "Scout"'s sail a year after the sale, Sean Mulligan confirmed it was a Sailrite kit sail Henson had sewn herself, recommending her work highly and noting she might be willing to make sails for other owners, pointing to her blog at `lezliesworld.blogspot.com`.[38]
