# Stephen Yahn

**Stephen Yahn** was a builder on **Puget Sound**, Washington. Before Paradox he sailed a Picaroon, which in his own words was "not a trailer kindly boat," and reasoned that a smaller, trailerable design would let him travel far and wide behind a car instead.[1] He started building **"[Ironsides](/boats/ironsides.md)"** in July 2006, launched her on the Sound in October 2008 and cruised her there for four years.[2][3] He sold Ironsides in October 2012 and died in 2013.[4][5]

## Ironsides

Ironsides carried a steel-reinforced third bulkhead for a wider berth, a side-mounted outboard Stephen fabricated himself to spare his hands and a mast glued up from 2x4 lumber with an internal conduit run for the wiring and a lightning ground. Full build and cruising account, including her four Puget Sound seasons and her later voyages under a new owner: [boats/ironsides.md](/boats/ironsides.md).

## Technique and Puget Sound

He fiberglassed with a paintbrush rather than a squeegee, applying a liberal amount of resin and letting it soak in properly,[6] and used crushed walnut shells, sold industrially for sandblasting delicate machine parts and later repurposed as pet-lizard bedding, as non-skid decking.[7] At anchor one summer night near the Tacoma Narrows south of Gig Harbor, rocking Ironsides by hand, he watched her chine runners churn the water into a whorl of pale blue light streaming away aft, unprompted and unlooked for.[8]

He lived on Puget Sound, with a boat launch not far from his own house and Seattle's skyline visible to the east, glass towers that sometimes caught the summer sunset and threw back scattered flashes of light, a view he mostly saw from his own kayak since a neighbor's trees screened it from home, the same trees that spared his property the worst of the region's winter storms.[9] He sailed at night by preference, finding the Sound emptier and easier to navigate by lighthouse than in the hazy daytime view, and found moonlight sailing almost otherworldly.[10]

## A restless, generative mind

Yahn kept starting things beyond Ironsides herself. He was first to suggest modeling Paradox parts in Google Sketchup, an idea other builders later took up.[11] He floated fitting a Paradox deck, cabin, chine runners and rig onto a Bolger Brick hull instead of scaling the Paradox hull itself,[12] and separately proposed starting from a Phil Bolger sharpie with chine runners fitted in place of Bolger's own leeway devices.[13] He fused Tom Yost's folding-kayak construction method, an aluminum-tubing skeleton under a vinyl-canvas skin, with Matt Layden's Elusion design at her own roughly 9 by 3 ft dimensions, imagining a boat foldable enough to fly with and keep in a car trunk, "smaller, lighter, thrifty and effective."[14] A recurring daydream of a human-pedaled propeller for Ironsides drew on Philip Thiel's "dorycycle" designs,[15] and he took it far enough to hang a salvaged bronze propeller from a rope off Ironsides' own transom, gauging the idea's proportions against her tiny stern.[16]

Studying the design patiently rather than taking it at face value, he judged Paradox's rig, hull, cabin, chine runners, rudder, roller reefing and control lines too interconnected to change piecemeal, a conclusion he thought only patient study could reveal. Small as she looks from outside, drawing comment from everyone he sailed with, Ironsides proved surprisingly big and roomy within.[17] In April 2010 he began a second, unrelated small-boat project, "Victoria Patagonica."[18]

## Fastening, rot and rigging

Yahn's technical range covered nearly every part of the boat. A long personal essay weighed bronze, hot-dip galvanized steel and copper fasteners by era and application, favoring screws over nails mechanically and steering builders away from brass in saltwater.[19] For checking an old or secondhand Paradox's glassed-plywood hull for rot, his practice was the tap test: a light hammer or a screwdriver's plastic handle, tapping and listening for the tone, since a hull built well and never wetted through can last well beyond 30 years.[20] A vent-trunk drain question about "[Johanna](/boats/johanna.md)" drew out the difference between US pipe and tube sizing and the Schedule system that sets a pipe's actual wall thickness.[21] His roller-reefing tack-strop fitting, reworked through several iterations of pin and pivot material, corrected a common assumption that luff tension is set by halyard tension alone: on this rig it comes from the furling line, a fix recorded at [components/rig/tack-strop.md](/components/rig/tack-strop.md).[22]
