# Stephen Yahn

**Stephen Yahn** (Yahoo handles `yahns` and `yahn101a`, signing "Steve Yahn" or "Steve") is a Paradox builder from **Manchester, Washington**, on Puget Sound, and was one of the mailing list's most active and technically detailed contributors between 2006 and 2009. Before Paradox he sailed a **Picaroon**, a boat he found too large and awkward to trailer — his own stated reason for choosing a small, trailerable design he could tow widely across the western United States.[1] He built and launched "**Ironsides**" and went on to cruise Puget Sound in her, remaining active on the list afterward encouraging newcomers and answering technical questions.

## Building "Ironsides"

Stephen started "**Ironsides**" in Manchester in July 2006, after a long run of detailed pre-build questions covering rigging, ballast, and wiring, and launched her on Puget Sound on 15 October 2008.[2] The full build and cruising record — a steel-reinforced bulkhead 3, a side-mounted outboard motor mount, a glued-2x4 mast, and her first cruise — is told in full on [boats/ironsides.md](/boats/ironsides.md). Citing his own illness, Stephen offered Ironsides for sale in October 2012, placing himself in Port Orchard, Washington, and reporting he was in remission at the time; see [boats/ironsides.md](/boats/ironsides.md#sold-in-2012-citing-his-own-illness) for the listing in full.[62][63] The sale closed that December.[64]

## Contributions and stories

- **Hull condition:** recommended the **tap test** for checking an old or used Paradox's glassed-plywood hull for rot — tapping lightly all over with a small hammer or a screwdriver's plastic handle and listening for the tone — noting that a hull built well and never wetted through can last well beyond 30 years; see [checking a used Paradox for rot](/construction/rot-inspection-ice-pick.md#an-alternative-tool-free-technique-the-tap-test).[3]
- **Fasteners:** wrote a detailed essay on fastener-material choice by era and application — bronze, hot-dip galvanized steel, and copper — and judged screws mechanically superior to nails, with ring-shank nails' only real advantage being cost, and brass best avoided in saltwater; see [construction/fastening-and-gluing.md](/construction/fastening-and-gluing.md#fastener-material-by-era-and-application-bronze-galvanized-and-copper).[4][5]
- **Materials and figures:** gave concrete sizing figures for builders weighing alternatives to plywood — steel plate at about 5.1 lb per square foot for 1/8 in stock, a later minimum of 3.5 mm low-carbon steel, an aluminum mast tube around 73–88 mm with a 5 mm wall, and tungsten's very high density (19.25 g/cm³ against lead's 11.34 g/cm³) as a ballast curiosity; see [aluminum and steel Paradox (speculative)](/design/ideas/aluminum-construction-idea.md#gauge-weight-and-the-case-for-steel-over-wood-generally), [components/rig/mast.md](/components/rig/mast.md#aluminum-as-a-mast-material-engineering-fit-not-just-substitution), and [design/weight-and-ballast.md](/design/weight-and-ballast.md#improvised-and-exotic-ballast-materials-gym-weights-sandbags-and-tungsten).[6][7][8][9]
- **Vent trunk drain:** explained the difference between US pipe and tube sizing and the "Schedule" system that sets a pipe's actual wall thickness and outside diameter, and later added that the drain tube's water-contact section must stay bare copper to carry the mast's electrical ground; see [boats/johanna.md](/boats/johanna.md#vent-trunk-drain-pipe-sizing-metric-tube-against-the-plans-1-in-nominal-pipe).[10][11]
- **Self-steering and rowing:** recommended flexible fiberglass rods and tubes made for stunt-kite construction as a windvane-blade material resilient to boom collisions, and floated a "north woods" style pinned rowlock, curved to clear the cabin top and stow where the yuloh normally sits, for rowing Paradox with conventional oars; see [operations/self-steering.md](/operations/self-steering.md#boom-interference-vane-blade-materials-and-a-commercial-precedent-the-qme) and [operations/sculling-and-yuloh-technique.md](/operations/sculling-and-yuloh-technique.md#whether-paradox-can-be-rowed-with-conventional-oars).[12][13]
- **Non-skid decking:** recalled crushed-walnut-shell non-skid decking from his father's boatyard and set out a full masking-and-paint method for laying it down, noting the shells are now sold cheaply as pet-lizard bedding; see [construction/non-skid-deck.md](/construction/non-skid-deck.md#a-full-masking-and-paint-method-and-the-techniques-professional-origin).[14]
- **Fiberglassing:** learned the technique from his father, head painter at "Built Right Boats" in Santa Monica in the 1950s–60s — favored a paintbrush over a squeegee, applying resin liberally and letting it soak in, and always worked downward when glassing a hull rolled onto its side; see [construction/fiberglassing-technique.md](/construction/fiberglassing-technique.md#a-2009-wet-out-problem-and-a-brush-versus-squeegee-debate).[15]
- **Capsize and self-righting:** set out Matt Layden's design intent behind the deck's high arch — meant to make a fully inverted hull markedly unstable — and located and uploaded the plans' plotted stability chart to the group's files; see [self-righting and capsize recovery](/design/self-righting-and-capsize-recovery.md#design-intent-the-deck-arch-and-a-stability-chart-on-file).[16][17][18]
- **Sail geometry:** measured the newer sail plan's forward top corner at about 800 mm from the mast centerline (900 mm to the halyard attachment point) — a figure the plan itself doesn't give — and, running both sail plans through Sailrite's own calculator, found a 91.3° tack angle for the old sail against 95.65° and about 95.8 sq ft of area for the new one; see [Mk2 sail](/components/rig/mk2-sail.md#locating-the-sails-forward-top-corner-and-tuning-balance-through-the-sleeve-gap).[19][20] For his own sail, ordered from Sailrite in 2008, confirmed years later he had kept to the original, plans-matching listing rather than the newer one — **100.29 sq. ft.**, "or as Matt puts it 9.3m squared."[61]
- **Furling:** proposed building up the furling spool's diameter beyond the boom's own with a fiberglass-reinforced wooden doughnut, split in half and glued or screwed on, to reduce the line tension needed to furl, and later built his own version of the forward roller-reefing fitting a little differently from the stock design, sharing photographs of the result; see [Furling system](/components/rig/furling.md#a-proposed-larger-diameter-furling-spool) and [a built variant documented in photographs](/components/rig/furling.md#a-further-reply-a-built-variant-documented-in-photographs).[21][22][50]
- **A sea story:** passed on an account of a couple whose stern cleats survived a severe storm off Madagascar intact while the surrounding deck tore away instead, and fitted broad washers under his own cleat bolts and stem U-bolt accordingly; see [ground tackle and anchoring](/operations/ground-tackle.md#attaching-ground-tackle-at-the-bow-cleats-a-stem-u-bolt-and-chafe-protection).[23]
- **Own build and practice:** built Ironsides' mast from two glued 2x4s with a router-cut internal conduit for the wiring and lightning ground,[24] fitted torch-bent stainless rudder pins set into grooves in the stock's plywood halves as a cheaper alternative to the plans' hardware,[25] and learned — fairing a cabin-top beam by eye — that polycarbonate glazing can still buckle once screwed down, after which he recommended a dry-fit test first.[26] He kept a simple epoxy routine of West System bought by the gallon, sawdust saved as a cheap filler, and surgical gloves swapped out rather than solvent-cleaned,[27][28] plus small tips along the way — a 4 in C-clamp as a handle for moving a plywood sheet alone,[29] a Canadian COLD-CURE epoxy that worked well in an unheated garage,[30] and Gorilla Glue as a faster-curing, less skin-sensitizing alternative to standard epoxy.[31] For the built-in water-tank space he sourced a **Plastimo #PL18031** flexible bladder, 27 in square, one for each side; see [construction/water-tank-lining.md](/construction/water-tank-lining.md#flexible-bladders-and-insertable-tanks).[32] Full detail on [boats/ironsides.md](/boats/ironsides.md#building-the-mast-glued-2x4s-and-a-router-groove-wire-conduit), [construction/epoxy-brands-and-chemistry.md](/construction/epoxy-brands-and-chemistry.md#disposable-brushes-a-sawdust-based-filler-tip-and-a-further-no-complaints-mas-report), and [construction/epoxy-safety-and-ppe.md](/construction/epoxy-safety-and-ppe.md#surgical-gloves-as-an-alternative-to-solvent-hand-cleaning).
- **Registration:** registered "Ironsides" in Washington State and set out the general process for a home-built boat — keeping materials receipts, declaring the boat built for personal use, and receiving a permanent hull ID number; see [operations/registration-and-hull-numbering.md](/operations/registration-and-hull-numbering.md).[33]
- **Scaling ideas:** floated fitting a Paradox deck, cabin, chine runners, and rig onto a Bolger Brick hull instead of scaling the Paradox hull itself, and separately suggested starting from a Phil Bolger sharpie hull with chine runners fitted in place of Bolger's usual leeway devices; see [a scaled-down "mini" Paradox (speculative)](/design/ideas/scaled-down-idea.md) and [design/ideas/stretched-paradox.md](/design/ideas/stretched-paradox.md#bolger-hulls-plus-chine-runners-as-a-scaling-alternative).[34][35]
- **A pedal-drive daydream:** pursued a recurring "Silly Gadgets" idea of a human-pedaled propeller for Ironsides, drawing on Philip Thiel's "dorycycle" designs and mocking up a salvaged bronze propeller against the transom to gauge the idea's proportions; see [auxiliary outboard power](/operations/auxiliary-outboard-power.md#a-pedal-drive-outboard-conceived-for-ironsides-and-the-alternatives-it-drew).[36][37][38]
- **Encouraging other builders:** reassured a builder attempting a doubled-scale, 28 ft live-aboard Paradox that the stock 3½-to-1 length-to-beam ratio holds "sweet and slippery" at any uniform scale, and that ballast can correct whatever needs correcting once a boat has had some time in the water; see [design/larger-paradox.md](/design/larger-paradox.md#a-far-larger-unsanctioned-build-doubling-every-stock-dimension-for-a-live-aboard).[39] He also counseled a newcomer planning to sail to Hawaii to first crew an ocean passage under an experienced skipper; see [design/blue-water-suitability.md](/design/blue-water-suitability.md#a-2008-marina-del-rey-to-honolulu-plan-and-the-answer-it-drew).[40]
- **Puget Sound and voyages:** sailed regularly at night, finding Puget Sound emptier and easier to navigate by lighthouse than in the hazy daytime view.[41] His home village of Manchester has its own boat launch a half-mile from his house, with Seattle's skyline visible to the east.[42] Ironsides' first cruise, four days in south Puget Sound in July 2009, tested her ground tackle and yuloh and included a night watching bioluminescence stirred up by the chine runners — a display that recalled boyhood memories of glowing waves at a Santa Monica beach; see [boats/ironsides.md — a first cruise](/boats/ironsides.md#a-first-cruise-four-days-in-south-puget-sound-july-2009) and [boats/ironsides.md — a bioluminescent night at anchor](/boats/ironsides.md#a-bioluminescent-night-at-anchor).[43][44][45][46]
- **An outboard-mainsheet fix:** worked on the recurring problem of the mainsheet tangling with Ironsides' outboard, sketching a metal "line fender" rod fastened to the motor and a junk-rig doubled-mainsheet alternative; see [components/mainsheet-outboard-fouling.md](/components/mainsheet-outboard-fouling.md#a-2009-recurrence-across-four-boats-and-two-new-fix-ideas).[47][48][49]
- **A folding-boat daydream, and a second project of his own.** Fused Tom Yost's folding-kayak construction method — an aluminum-tubing skeleton under a vinyl-canvas skin — with Matt Layden's "Elusion" design (referred to here, per the recurring misspelling, as "Illusion") at her own roughly 9 ft by 3 ft dimensions, imagining a boat foldable enough to fly with and keep in a car trunk; see [matt-layden/elusion.md](/matt-layden/elusion.md#a-proposed-folding-kayak-construction-variant-april-2010) for the idea in full.[54] In the same week, began a second, separate small-boat project of his own, named "Victoria Patagonica," with his wife's active interest.[55]
- **On what makes Paradox itself:** judged the sail rig, hull, cabin, chine runners, rudder, roller reefing, and control lines so interdependent that changing any one of them would be a mistake, a conclusion reached only by studying the design patiently; the same design also has a peculiarity not obvious at first — she looks quite small from the outside, drawing comment from onlookers, yet proves surprisingly big and roomy within.[51] Also replaced a stolen Honda 2 hp outboard with a Suzuki 2.5 hp, finding it quieter and easier-starting on the same home-made transom mount; see [operations/auxiliary-outboard-power.md](/operations/auxiliary-outboard-power.md#a-stolen-outboard-and-a-firsthand-suzuki-25-hp-report-bearing-out-the-magazine-verdict-2010).[52][53]
- **Trailering:** adapted a stock trailer built for lighter fourteen-foot aluminum fishing boats with load-rated tires and a lower-geared, higher-capacity winch, launching by imitating other boaters' technique at a home ramp a quarter-mile away; praised Al Law's purpose-built "Little Jim" trailer as the most excellent design on record, if pricy; see [operations/trailering.md](/operations/trailering.md#a-pennsylvania-newcomers-question-and-an-underrated-trailers-real-world-fix-october-2010).[56]
- **Bending the furling rod.** Cuts a lathed groove for the securing screw before bending, clamps the rod in a bench vice with soft copper jaws to avoid marking it, heats only the bend point red hot with an oxy-acetylene torch, and bends it using a length of pipe as a lever — hot bronze moves easily, "no drama at all"; see [furling system — bending the furling rod: two working methods](/components/rig/furling.md#bending-the-furling-rod-two-working-methods).[57]
- **Why a foam hull would undercut the design.** Answering a newcomer's question about building in foam instead of plywood, argued the plywood hull's weight is part of what keeps the chine runners well submerged, so a lighter foam hull would upset the design's whole "gestalt" — and would in effect make the builder a designer in their own right rather than a builder of Matt Layden's exact design; see [chine runners — how they work](/chine-runners/how-they-work.md#why-heavy-displacement-and-heel-are-essential).[58]
- **A 2012 butt-joint and blush question answered.** Advised working a taped plywood butt joint and a chine fillet straight through in one continuous pass rather than curing and flipping, and confirmed that with West System epoxy specifically, amine blush comes off with plain clean water; see [construction/scarf-joints.md](/construction/scarf-joints.md#a-2012-recurrence-sequencing-a-taped-butt-joint-and-cleaning-blush-off-a-bead-before-taping).[59]
- **3D CAD and visualization.** Credited with the original 2008 suggestion to model Paradox parts in Google Sketchup; separately observed that translating a 2D plan into a 3D mental image is an acquired skill, historically eased by hand-drawn isometric or "phantom" (semi-transparent) plan views that took many hours to produce, and now easy to offer routinely alongside an ordinary 2D view with CAD; pointed to "Stereo Lithography" (3D printing, via the RepRap wiki) as a further, now DIY-affordable, visualization tool; see [references/building-plans.md — a March 2012 proposal](/references/building-plans.md#a-march-2012-proposal-3d-cad-models-to-supplement-the-paper-plans).[60]
- **A rudder-raise limiter.** After decreasing the pivot bolt's tightness alone did not reliably let the rudder blade drop, fitted a rubber-padded lag screw into the side of the rudder stock to cap how far the blade could be raised, keeping enough weight on the pivot's aft side at all times to want to swing back down; see [components/rudder.md — keeping the blade down under way](/components/rudder.md#keeping-the-blade-down-under-way-gravity-a-weighted-uphaul-and-a-raised-height-stop).[65]

## Death (2013)

News of Stephen Yahn's death reached the mailing list in July 2013, reported by the buyer of Ironsides; another builder who had exchanged many construction e-mails with him while he built her recalled him as an innovative, excellent craftsman in both metal and wood.[66][67] Learning of the death only in 2021, a further correspondent recalled once congratulating him on his own tack-strop craftsmanship — "a work of art."[68]
