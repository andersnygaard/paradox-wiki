# Alberto Callejon

**Alberto Callejon** is a journalist and online translator from **Rawson, Chubut**, on Argentina's Patagonian coast, a stretch of open South Atlantic with almost no local tradition of sailing beyond rowing and occasional windsurfing. Writing in Spanish and working through machine translation, he was active among Paradox builders from 2008 to 2021 and built **"[Cachivache](/boats/cachivache.md)"** (Spanish slang for a piece of junk or a knick-knack), with plywood itself hauled some 2,000 km to reach him.[1] In his own words she was **"el Paradox mas austral,"** the most southerly Paradox on record, a claim an independent 2014 web census of Paradox builders and owners later echoed.[2][3] He founded the Paradox builders' Facebook group in August 2011, a role he was still publicly claiming as of October 2019.[4][5][6] Full build account: [boats/cachivache.md](/boats/cachivache.md).

## Building far from the supply chain

Rawson sits on Argentina's Patagonian coast, a broad river mouth opening onto the open South Atlantic, a region of strong wind and sea with almost no local tradition of small-boat sailing beyond rowing and occasional windsurfing.[7] Whether the design could handle those conditions was an open question at the outset, with nothing local to measure it against; see [blue-water suitability](/design/blue-water-suitability.md#a-high-latitude-query-from-argentine-patagonia) for the reassurance this drew. Early on he weighed building the hull in sheet steel rather than plywood, a question that came back to a single practical fact: plywood itself had to be hauled some 2,000 km to reach him at considerable freight cost, leaving only pine and the local beech, lenga, readily available nearby.[1][8] The answer he drew favored a minimum 3.5 mm low-carbon steel plate, sandblasted and epoxy-coated against rust, though he built in plywood in the end.[9] Impregnating the wood with a diluted coat of polyester resin under epoxy proved not to work; he settled on laminating with epoxy just above the waterline and painting the rest.[10][11] Nearing launch, he obtained sections of railroad track at 20 kg per metre for ballast, installed close to bulkhead 3 and left unfixed since some pieces would need repositioning once test-sailing began; see [boats/cachivache.md](/boats/cachivache.md#ballast-railroad-track-on-hand).[12] Insulating against heat between the stove's burners and the aft deck above them was a further concern, resolved in full at [operations/cabin-heating.md](/operations/cabin-heating.md#insulating-the-aft-deck-above-the-stove-burners).[13] Construction reached a real milestone with the Patagonian spring: the hull, deck, sides, transom and cabin were finished in fiberglass by September 2009.[14]

The clearest measure of building this far from a supply chain is the tape-measure story. A cheap imported tape measure, marked "Made in China," turned out to read short on its centimetre scale, "99" where it should have read 100, a fault that went uncaught until every bulkhead already cut with it had to be checked, found wrong and redone.[15] He recovered by setting the faulty tool aside for a corrected one and, in his own words, after "many hours of meditation," destroyed the offending measure with a hammer before continuing.[16]

Distance shows up again as a pattern of equipment that went unreplaced, not for want of trying. A towed water-driven generator for onboard power was one option raised early on and never resolved;[7] after the launch, an echo sounder's display failed outright with no replacement part obtainable anywhere in Argentina;[17] a VHF radio's channel-16 and dual-watch keys went unexplained for want of a manual he could not obtain;[18] and, later still, the manual for a second, different sounder unit met the same silence.[19] The same pattern touched his own materials, not just his instruments: phenolic glue and sawdust as a fillet substitute for epoxy,[20] a polyethylene bubble-wrap film as a substitute for foam insulation,[21] and, as epoxy's price rose sharply, polyester as a substitute for it in laminating,[22] all raised and none settled. None of it reads as a man being ignored. Across thirteen years he was, on the fuller record, a welcomed and answered correspondent; the handful of equipment and materials questions that drew nothing back share one real cause rather than a pattern of being overlooked: nobody nearby held the parts, or the firsthand experience, to answer from.

## Mast and rig

For Cachivache's own mast, a roughly 63 mm aluminum tube he had on hand, with a 3 mm wall, was weighed twice in late 2008 as a substitute for the wooden mast in the plans; he abandoned the idea once he priced a suitable tube and built the mast in wood instead.[23][24] Placed beside that same decision, and left unresolved on its own account, is a dimension from that January: the tiller's length to bulkhead 4, given as 700 mm, and the rudder blade's turning angle.[25] The design's lack of stays or shrouds was a further concern, given the strong, unpredictable gusts on his home coast; the design relies on reefing and on the hull's own narrow-rolling behavior instead of standing rigging.[26] For his own sail, the choice between cutting the cloth flat or with some bag or curve was resolved by the broadseaming technique documented in full at [components/rig/sail.md](/components/rig/sail.md#broadseaming-how-the-sails-three-dimensional-shape-is-sewn-in).[27]

The aluminum idea did not stay abandoned. Nearing launch in December 2009, hinging the mast to clear a series of low bridges on the nearby Chubut river came up, and drew a suggested hinged-tabernacle arrangement, sourced from a separate junk-rig group's own files and requiring some custom metalwork, with a copy then added directly to the Paradox group's own files for easier reference.[28][29][30] Four years later, a different builder's own aluminum-mast question drew the report that the same tube had been fitted after all: 63.5 mm in diameter with a 3 mm wall, much lighter than wood and roomy enough to step all the wiring, lights and the radio antenna inside it, the tube's stamped end at the base covered by a shaped piece of wood under a hard rubber strip, the main-halyard sheave carried on a riveted omega clamp.[31] Its actual weight, given in January 2015, came to about 6 kg.[32] In 2017, still sailing her, the halyard's fixing point along the yard came up once more.[33] The full engineering, including the internal-sleeve reinforcement technique and the opinions favoring wood offered along the way, is at [components/rig/mast.md](/components/rig/mast.md#aluminum-as-a-mast-material-engineering-fit-not-just-substitution).

## Working in translation

Alberto is a journalist and does online translation for a living, work that explains why his own English occasionally reads more polished than the machine-translated Spanish carrying most of his correspondence. In March 2012 a compliment on one particularly fluent post drew the reveal in his own words: "It must be for my work, I am a journalist. And I do online translations."[34][35] The same identity accounts for the page's own recurring translation puzzles, phrases that resist confident decoding on a first read (a tape measure "made in China by hammer of 20 kgs," a sail's Spanish "vela" rendered as "the candle") but whose underlying sense survives once worked through; the strangeness sits in the translation, not in him.

## Community

He offered his own instant-messenger contact to other Spanish-speaking builders wanting to share the construction experience, signing off that same message "Alberto, Cachivache (el Paradox mas austral)."[2] He tried unsuccessfully to reach Don Elliott's own site for his out-of-print Paradox construction articles,[36] then subscribed to Don's own companion group and e-mailed him directly but got nothing back,[37] and weeks later raised the idea of an Argentine contact with US connections helping him obtain a copy.[38] He later credited Don's guidance and support in his own words: "Never forget the care and aid received from Don to be able to build my boat."[39]

He announced the Facebook group's creation directly that same month, inviting fellow builders to share experiences, information and images there; see [references/mailing-list.md](/references/mailing-list.md#sibling-and-successor-groups) and [references/paradox-facebook-group.md](/references/paradox-facebook-group.md) for its place in the wider archive.[5] The group he founded in 2011 is distinct from a newer, unrelated "Paradox Sailboat" group that formed in 2019; eight years after founding his own, a public claim to the administrator role still stood: "Hello friends I am the administrator of the page, you are all welcome. Greetings Alberto Paradox Cachivache."[6] Around the same period, an e-mail attempt to reach [Bengt Genell](/people/bengt-genell.md), known among builders as "the builder Ladikan" after his boat's own name, drew nothing back after several days,[40] and, the same week, an economical or secondhand echo sounder was sought on a friend's behalf, ahead of a trip to Miami.[41]

Junk-rigging a Paradox came up as a topic the same year;[42] a fellow owner, having already converted a different boat to the rig and planning to fit one to his own still-unbuilt Paradox, gave an encouraging response,[43] met in turn by a junk sail of his own already under way.[44] The following year, a fellow builder's outboard-sizing question drew Cachivache's own 3.3 hp Evinrude as the answer, judged enough power to overcome the Chubut river's current,[45] with a pointer again to the photographed mainsheet-clew fix already recorded on her own page.[46] That same month brought a report that Cachivache's own chine runners had been remade shorter and wider, following the shape of the hull with a downward tilt; once requested, since they were hard to make out on video, photographs followed directly by e-mail.[47][48] A trailer design of his own was wanted once more in 2017, and three different approaches came back independently;[49] the following year, his own practice for thickening epoxy for fillets came out as talc, used throughout the build.[50]

As late as March 2021, thirteen years after he first wrote in, the record shows him still at it: mooring Cachivache in the Río Chubut at Rawson and using an Evinrude 3.3 hp outboard to reach the sea and back, signing off simply "Alberto, Paradox 'Cachivache.'"[51]
