# Alberto Callejon

**Alberto Callejon**, who posted to the mailing list as **`laciudad2004`** and later administered the Paradox builders' Facebook group,[1] is a builder from **Rawson, Chubut**, on Argentina's Patagonian coast — a broad river mouth opening onto the open South Atlantic (43°21′S, 65°03′W), a region of strong wind and sea with almost no local sailing tradition beyond rowing and occasional windsurfing. Writing in Spanish and relying on machine translation for the mailing list, he began building a Paradox in 2008 and named her **"Cachivache"** — Spanish slang for a piece of junk or a knick-knack — styled on the list as "el Paradox mas austral," the most southerly Paradox on record. Full build account: [boats/cachivache.md](/boats/cachivache.md).

## Building Cachivache

Before starting to build, he asked whether Paradox could handle his home coast's strong wind and sea, given the lack of any local small-boat tradition to compare against; the reassurance he received — that Paradox is, for its size, stronger and safer than most other boats — is recorded in full at [blue-water suitability — a high-latitude query from Argentine Patagonia](/design/blue-water-suitability.md#a-high-latitude-query-from-argentine-patagonia).[2][3][4] A cheap imported tape measure used to mark out Cachivache's bulkheads later proved to read short on its centimetre scale, forcing every bulkhead already cut to be redone; he recovered by discarding the faulty tool and continuing construction with a corrected measure — full account at [boats/cachivache.md — a cheap imported tape measure, and a full bulkhead redo](/boats/cachivache.md#a-cheap-imported-tape-measure-and-a-full-bulkhead-redo).[5][6] With the return of the Patagonian spring he finished fiberglassing the hull, deck, sides, transom, and cabin in September 2009,[7] and, nearing launch, fitted ballast from sections of railroad track weighing 20 kg per metre.[8] By January 2010 he reported the launch itself only days away.[28] After the launch, an echo sounder aboard Cachivache failed outright with no replacement part obtainable anywhere in Argentina — a further, more remote case of the marine-electronics parts-scarcity problem documented at [GPS and navigation instruments](/operations/gps-navigation.md#a-remote-region-echo-sounder-failure-no-local-parts-market).[29]

## Materials and fit-out

Early in construction he raised building Cachivache's bottom and sides in sheet steel rather than plywood — a recurring idea among Paradox builders — drawing an unusually direct answer: a minimum 3.5 mm low-carbon steel plate, sandblasted and epoxy-coated to stop rust. Full exchange: [aluminum and steel Paradox (speculative) — a 2008 recurrence, from a builder in Patagonia](/design/ideas/aluminum-construction-idea.md#a-2008-recurrence-from-a-builder-in-patagonia).[9][10] Weighing how to impregnate the wood, he asked whether a diluted coat of polyester resin applied first would still let epoxy be laminated over it; see [epoxy brands, chemistry, and cure behavior — epoxy over polyester](/construction/epoxy-brands-and-chemistry.md#epoxy-over-polyester-wax-impregnation-and-a-professionals-caution) for why it would not.[11] He settled on laminating just above the waterline with epoxy, painting the rest.[12] He also raised the question of insulating the aft deck above the stove's burners; see [cabin heating — insulating the aft deck above the stove burners](/operations/cabin-heating.md#insulating-the-aft-deck-above-the-stove-burners) for the technique this drew.[13]

## Mast and rig

For Cachivache's own mast, he twice asked about substituting a roughly 63 mm aluminum tube he had on hand — its wall proving to be 3 mm — for the wooden mast in the plans, but abandoned the idea once he priced a suitable tube and built the mast in wood instead. Full exchange, including the reinforcement technique and the opinions favoring wood offered in reply: [components/rig/mast.md — aluminum as a mast material](/components/rig/mast.md#aluminum-as-a-mast-material-engineering-fit-not-just-substitution).[14][15][16][17] He also asked why the design carries no stays or shrouds, given the strong, unpredictable gusts on his home coast; see [components/rig/mast.md](/components/rig/mast.md#why-the-mast-carries-no-stays-or-shrouds) for the design's real defense against a gust.[18] Sewing his own sail, he asked whether the cloth should be cut flat or given some bag or curve; see [boats/cachivache.md](/boats/cachivache.md#building-the-sail-a-broadseaming-question-answered) and [components/rig/sail.md](/components/rig/sail.md#broadseaming-how-the-sails-three-dimensional-shape-is-sewn-in) for the broadseaming technique this drew.[19] Nearing launch in December 2009, he asked whether the mast could be made to hinge to clear a series of low bridges on the nearby Chubut river, drawing a suggested hinged-tabernacle answer sourced from a separate junk-rig group's files and requiring some custom metalwork, with a copy then added directly to the Paradox group's own files for easier reference; see [components/rig/mast.md](/components/rig/mast.md#a-2009-question-hinging-the-mast-to-clear-low-river-bridges) for Paradox's own lift-off arrangement and the fuller exchange.[25][26][27]

## Community

He offered his own instant-messenger contact to other Spanish-speaking builders wanting to share the construction experience.[20] He tried unsuccessfully to reach Don Elliott's own site for his out-of-print Paradox construction articles,[21] then subscribed to Don's own companion group and e-mailed him directly but got no answer;[22] weeks later he asked whether an Argentine contact with US connections might help him obtain a copy.[23] See [people/don-elliott.md](/people/don-elliott.md#contributions-and-technical-positions) for the companion group's own moderation policy. He later credited Don's guidance and support with having helped him complete his own build.[24]
