# Alberto Callejon

**Alberto Callejon**, who posted to the mailing list as **`laciudad2004`** and later administered the Paradox builders' Facebook group,[1] is a builder from **Rawson, Chubut**, on Argentina's Patagonian coast — a broad river mouth opening onto the open South Atlantic (43°21′S, 65°03′W), a region of strong wind and sea with almost no local sailing tradition beyond rowing and occasional windsurfing. Writing in Spanish and relying on machine translation for the mailing list — work he does professionally too, as a journalist doing online translation for a living[32][33] — he began building a Paradox in 2008 and named her **"Cachivache"** — Spanish slang for a piece of junk or a knick-knack — styled on the list as "el Paradox mas austral," the most southerly Paradox on record. Full build account: [boats/cachivache.md](/boats/cachivache.md).

## Building Cachivache

Before starting to build, he asked whether Paradox could handle his home coast's strong wind and sea, given the lack of any local small-boat tradition to compare against; the reassurance he received — that Paradox is, for its size, stronger and safer than most other boats — is recorded in full at [blue-water suitability — a high-latitude query from Argentine Patagonia](/design/blue-water-suitability.md#a-high-latitude-query-from-argentine-patagonia).[2][3][4] A cheap imported tape measure used to mark out Cachivache's bulkheads later proved to read short on its centimetre scale, forcing every bulkhead already cut to be redone; he recovered by discarding the faulty tool and continuing construction with a corrected measure — full account at [boats/cachivache.md — a cheap imported tape measure, and a full bulkhead redo](/boats/cachivache.md#a-cheap-imported-tape-measure-and-a-full-bulkhead-redo).[5][6] With the return of the Patagonian spring he finished fiberglassing the hull, deck, sides, transom, and cabin in September 2009,[7] and, nearing launch, fitted ballast from sections of railroad track weighing 20 kg per metre.[8] By January 2010 he reported the launch itself only days away.[28] After the launch, an echo sounder aboard Cachivache failed outright with no replacement part obtainable anywhere in Argentina — a further, more remote case of the marine-electronics parts-scarcity problem documented at [GPS and navigation instruments](/operations/gps-navigation.md#a-remote-region-echo-sounder-failure-no-local-parts-market).[29]

## Materials and fit-out

Early in construction he raised building Cachivache's bottom and sides in sheet steel rather than plywood — a recurring idea among Paradox builders — drawing an unusually direct answer: a minimum 3.5 mm low-carbon steel plate, sandblasted and epoxy-coated to stop rust. Full exchange: [aluminum and steel Paradox (speculative) — a 2008 recurrence, from a builder in Patagonia](/design/ideas/aluminum-construction-idea.md#a-2008-recurrence-from-a-builder-in-patagonia).[9][10] Weighing how to impregnate the wood, he asked whether a diluted coat of polyester resin applied first would still let epoxy be laminated over it; see [epoxy brands, chemistry, and cure behavior — epoxy over polyester](/construction/epoxy-brands-and-chemistry.md#epoxy-over-polyester-wax-impregnation-and-a-professionals-caution) for why it would not.[11] He settled on laminating just above the waterline with epoxy, painting the rest.[12] He also raised the question of insulating the aft deck above the stove's burners; see [cabin heating — insulating the aft deck above the stove burners](/operations/cabin-heating.md#insulating-the-aft-deck-above-the-stove-burners) for the technique this drew.[13]

## Mast and rig

For Cachivache's own mast, he twice asked about substituting a roughly 63 mm aluminum tube he had on hand — its wall proving to be 3 mm — for the wooden mast in the plans, but abandoned the idea once he priced a suitable tube and built the mast in wood instead. Full exchange, including the reinforcement technique and the opinions favoring wood offered in reply: [components/rig/mast.md — aluminum as a mast material](/components/rig/mast.md#aluminum-as-a-mast-material-engineering-fit-not-just-substitution).[14][15][16][17] The idea resurfaced in 2013: answering a fellow builder's own aluminum-mast question, he reported having fitted that same tube after all — see [boats/cachivache.md — a 2013 return to the aluminum mast](/boats/cachivache.md#a-2013-return-to-the-aluminum-mast) for the installation technique in full.[43] He also asked why the design carries no stays or shrouds, given the strong, unpredictable gusts on his home coast; see [components/rig/mast.md](/components/rig/mast.md#why-the-mast-carries-no-stays-or-shrouds) for the design's real defense against a gust.[18] Sewing his own sail, he asked whether the cloth should be cut flat or given some bag or curve; see [boats/cachivache.md](/boats/cachivache.md#building-the-sail-a-broadseaming-question-answered) and [components/rig/sail.md](/components/rig/sail.md#broadseaming-how-the-sails-three-dimensional-shape-is-sewn-in) for the broadseaming technique this drew.[19] Nearing launch in December 2009, he asked whether the mast could be made to hinge to clear a series of low bridges on the nearby Chubut river, drawing a suggested hinged-tabernacle answer sourced from a separate junk-rig group's files and requiring some custom metalwork, with a copy then added directly to the Paradox group's own files for easier reference; see [components/rig/mast.md](/components/rig/mast.md#a-2009-question-hinging-the-mast-to-clear-low-river-bridges) for Paradox's own lift-off arrangement and the fuller exchange.[25][26][27]

## A 2013 chine-runner reshaping

Years after completion, he reported having remade Cachivache's own chine runners shorter and wider, following the shape of the hull with a downward tilt, and found the change held direction better and turned more easily, with a more sensitive rudder — and, on request, sent photographs of the modified runners directly by e-mail. Full account: [boats/cachivache.md](/boats/cachivache.md#a-2013-chine-runner-reshaping-and-photographs-shared).[41][42]

## Community

He offered his own instant-messenger contact to other Spanish-speaking builders wanting to share the construction experience.[20] He announced the Facebook group's creation directly to the mailing list the same month it was founded, inviting fellow builders to share experiences, information, and images there — see [references/mailing-list.md](/references/mailing-list.md#sibling-and-successor-groups) and [references/paradox-facebook-group.md](/references/paradox-facebook-group.md) for its place in the wider archive.[30] He tried unsuccessfully to reach Don Elliott's own site for his out-of-print Paradox construction articles,[21] then subscribed to Don's own companion group and e-mailed him directly but got no answer;[22] weeks later he asked whether an Argentine contact with US connections might help him obtain a copy.[23] See [people/don-elliott.md](/people/don-elliott.md#contributions-and-technical-positions) for the companion group's own moderation policy. He later credited Don's guidance and support with having helped him complete his own build.[24] A later, unrelated equipment question — how to work a VHF radio's channel-16 and dual-watch keys, its manual unobtainable — went unanswered on record.[31] A further, separate question the same year asked whether anyone had sailed a Paradox under a [junk rig](/design/ideas/junk-rig.md), drawing an encouraging answer from a fellow owner who had already converted a different boat to the rig and planned to fit one to his own, still-unbuilt Paradox; Alberto answered in turn that he had begun sewing a junk sail of his own — full exchange at [boats/cachivache.md](/boats/cachivache.md#junk-rig-interest-and-a-sail-begun-september-2012) and [people/roselt-croeser.md](/people/roselt-croeser.md#a-2012-mailing-list-exchange-months-before-the-build).[34][35][36] In March 2013 he asked, on a friend's behalf, where an economical or secondhand echo sounder might be found ahead of a trip to Miami — see [operations/gps-navigation.md](/operations/gps-navigation.md#a-remote-region-echo-sounder-failure-no-local-parts-market) — and, the same week, tried repeatedly by e-mail to reach [Bengt Genell](/people/bengt-genell.md), addressed on the list as "the builder Ladikan" after his boat's own name, with no reply after several days; both went unanswered on record.[37][38] The following month he answered a fellow builder's outboard-sizing question elsewhere on the list with Cachivache's own 3.3 hp Evinrude, judged enough power to overcome the Chubut river's current, and pointed again to the same photographed mainsheet-clew fix already recorded on her own page; see [operations/auxiliary-outboard-power.md](/operations/auxiliary-outboard-power.md#a-2013-recurrence-three-owners-outboard-choices-compared-and-a-further-hybrid-power-proposal) and [components/mainsheet-outboard-fouling.md](/components/mainsheet-outboard-fouling.md#a-2013-recurrence-a-deck-led-sheet-still-tangling-a-photographed-fix-pointed-to-again-and-a-junk-rig-question-renewed).[39][40]
