# Matt (Ithaca, NY)

**Matt** (Yahoo handle `twitch_816`) is a Paradox builder from the Ithaca, New York area, on Cayuga Lake, who introduced himself to the list in June 2006 as a "long-time lurker" finally starting to build, about two weeks before a fellow builder launched his own Paradox, ["Enuf"](/boats/enuf.md).[1][2] He had already completed three other small boats — two Yost-designed folding kayaks and a PDRacer — and judged Paradox by far the most complex of the four.[2] Moving to a house on Cayuga Lake that fall, with limited room to build at the new property, he treated the summer of 2006 as a race to get as much of the hull built as possible beforehand, with two brothers due to help in July.[2] He was in the area for graduate studies in mathematics at Cornell, in no particular rush to graduate, in a part of central New York shaped by retreating glaciers into gorges, waterfalls, and deep lakes, about two hours south of Lake Ontario and on the shore of the 40-mile-long Cayuga Lake.[3] He kept a public build log at `www.math.cornell.edu/~noonan/boats/paradox.html`, alongside a page describing his three prior boats at `www.math.cornell.edu/~noonan/boats`.[2]

## Building the hull

By late July 2006, with his two brothers' help, he had wired on and glued his hull's bottom panel — its scarf joint came out cleaner than the side panels' had, done earlier on a flat work surface — and moved on to fitting out the interior and the vent trunk.[4] A laminated, double-layer panel's own bonding area so far exceeds a scarf or butt joint's that the joint itself barely matters for strength; full technical detail at [a double-layer plywood technique for an enlarged Paradox](/construction/plywood-grades-and-foam-flotation.md#a-double-layer-plywood-technique-for-an-enlarged-paradox-and-testing-an-unconventional-mast-wood).[4] In early August 2006, with the chine runners underway, the placement of the copper lightning-grounding flashing in the hull's layup was resolved, exposed copper's antifouling benefit was confirmed, and turning the hull over was named as his next milestone — full detail at [lightning protection — where the copper flashing sits in the layup, and copper's bonus as an antifoulant](/design/lightning-protection.md#where-the-copper-flashing-sits-in-the-layup-and-coppers-bonus-as-an-antifoulant).[5][6][7]

## Contributions and stories

- **Pigment color and UV protection.** Asking whether the pigment in his hull's epoxy protected it from UV on its own, or whether paint or varnish was needed over it as well — and half in jest, why so many green-hulled, red-sailed Paradoxen came from the UK side of the Atlantic — he drew out durable Paradox lore: a supplier's pigment alone was said to prevent UV degradation with no paint needed over it (see [Enuf's paint scheme](/boats/enuf.md#paint-scheme-three-tone-green-with-a-yellow-bottom) for the fuller account and its later complication), "forest green" was corrected to **British Racing Green**, and the red sail was explained as a deliberate high-visibility choice — a boat easy to spot in genuine trouble at sea, but inconspicuous with the sail down while creeping up rivers.[3][8]
- **A PDRacer as a quicker alternative.** He suggested building a PDRacer as a quicker alternative to a proposed scaled-down "mini" Paradox. See [a scaled-down "mini" Paradox (speculative)](/design/ideas/scaled-down-idea.md#reception-a-comparison-to-the-bolger-brick-and-a-cheaper-and-quicker-alternative).[9]
- **Sail sleeves versus lashings.** He relayed a question about why the Paradox sail uses sleeves rather than lashings on the yard and boom. See [why the sail uses sleeves rather than lashings, and a first sail of the 2006 season](/boats/jill.md#why-the-sail-uses-sleeves-rather-than-lashings-and-a-first-sail-of-the-2006-season).[10]
