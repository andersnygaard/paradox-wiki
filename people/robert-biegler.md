# Robert Biegler

**Robert Biegler** bought [Derek Clark](/people/derek-clark.md)'s Paradox **"[Enuf](/boats/enuf.md)"** when Derek offered her for sale in September 2006, shipped her to **Norway**, re-fixed her ballast to his own satisfaction and sailed her out of **Trondheimsfjord** for several years. He worked without an outboard, propelling her with a yuloh he built with a bigger blade than the stock design and building a second after the first was stolen.[1][2] Full ownership record: [boats/enuf.md](/boats/enuf.md).

## Taking over "Enuf"

In his first season he sailed her only twice, for short system checks, before loading her for cruising; Trondheimsfjord offered few places to ground out safely in a sea.[2] On those early outings she carried lee helm and made about 25 degrees of leeway; sailing her lower in the water and further to leeward cut the leeway to about 10 degrees and cured the lee helm.[2] When the stock furling gear proved too stiff to reef by its drum alone, he added two blocks, one redirecting the reefing line aft off the drum and one above the deck hole where the line passes below, enough to make reefing workable.[2]

## A rudder repair

In 2009 water had worked into the lower rudder stock through its pintle, leaving several degrees of play. He dried the wood with hot water bottles and a hot air gun, then locked the repair with a wrap of unidirectional carbon fibre around the pintle, isolated from the steel by a layer of glass. Full account: [boats/enuf.md](/boats/enuf.md#a-lower-rudder-pintle-problem-2009).[3]

## Selling "Enuf"

At some point in his ownership the boat was renamed **"Minnow,"** the name she still carried when he sold her in 2013 to [Bill Serjeant](/people/bill-serjeant.md), already the builder and owner of the Paradox "[Faith](/boats/faith.md)." She went to Bill with an undiagnosed leak somewhere around the vent box where the mast is stepped; its eventual diagnosis is at [boats/enuf.md](/boats/enuf.md#diagnosing-and-curing-the-leak).[4] An inspection of the side ballast after the sale found the lead cemented in so well that it held secure even against a full capsize.[5]
