# Simon B

**Simon B** is a Paradox builder in **Vilnius, Lithuania**. He joined the mailing list in December 2019 while still weighing how to build in **Baltic birch plywood** — cheap and good quality locally, but noticeably heavier than Okoume — and by April 2020 was about a quarter of the way through his own build.[1]

## Choosing materials and scantlings

Rather than reduce every plywood thickness a step to offset Baltic birch's extra weight, he weighed the trade-off against [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)'s own thinner-skinned Enigma 460 and a Puddle Duck Racer built the same way, and settled on **9mm bottom and 6mm sides** — plus **Norway spruce**, steam-bent inside a length of PVC pipe, for the stringers and chine logs, judging Baltic birch itself too stiff to bend. He also floated external bottom stringers for beach protection, a hydrodynamic-drag worry he raised himself, and decided against fitting them. See [plywood grades and foam flotation](/construction/plywood-grades-and-foam-flotation.md#a-2019-recurrence-a-baltic-birch-thickness-reduction-plan-and-the-enigma-460-precedent) and [rudder and chine experiments](/components/rudder.md#rocky-bottom-and-beach-protection-ideas) for the fuller exchange.[2][3][4]

## A quarter into the build, and a birdsmouth-mast lesson

By April 2020 he was a quarter of the way through construction and had already reworked his own mast plan: rather than sourcing straight, clear lumber, he had bought cheaper, knotty spruce and spent weeks resawing it, scarfing it, and designing a round, tapered birdsmouth section — tapered in both diameter and wall thickness — reasoning the result would come out lighter and stronger than a solid stock mast. In hindsight he judged the whole detour "not worth it" against simply buying better wood from the outset. See [mast — a birdsmouth mast weighed against buying better lumber](/components/rig/mast.md#a-birdsmouth-mast-weighed-against-buying-better-lumber) for the general lesson.[5]

## Building the vent trunk, and adopting others' advice (April 2020)

A few days later he was building the vent trunk — convenient work on the table — with the rudder next and the spars already waiting on the shelf for sanding and finishing, in a shop with room enough to move around. He singled out the advice to build the hull last as the best boat-building advice he had received. Prompted by other builders' own second-build lists, he set out plans for better and bigger vent-trunk pulleys, closer attention to watertightness, and embedded LED strip lighting from leftover stock; he also raised a water-tank design of his own and a lightning-conductor routing concern, both carried further on the relevant topic pages — see [construction/water-tank-lining.md](/construction/water-tank-lining.md#flexible-bladders-and-insertable-tanks) and [design/lightning-protection.md](/design/lightning-protection.md#an-external-conductor-considered-against-the-standard-through-mast-wire).[6]

## Shaping the rudder blade

By July 2022 he had reached the rudder: cut a pair of plywood guide fences offset from a NACA 0009 profile by the router bit's own protruding length, then ran the bit against them to shape the blade directly. General technique: [rudder — foil section](/components/rudder.md#foil-section-not-a-standard-naca-airfoil).[7]
