# Simon B

**Simon B** is a Paradox builder in **Vilnius, Lithuania**, who began building in the winter of 2019, choosing **Baltic birch plywood**: cheap and good quality locally, but noticeably heavier than okoume.[1] By April 2020 he was about a quarter of the way through construction.[2]

## Choosing materials and scantlings

Rather than reduce every plywood thickness a step to offset Baltic birch's extra weight, he weighed the trade-off against [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)'s own thinner-skinned Enigma 460 and a Puddle Duck Racer he had built the same way. He settled on **9mm bottom and 6mm sides**, using Norway spruce, steam-bent inside a length of PVC pipe, for the stringers and chine logs, since he judged Baltic birch itself too stiff to bend.[3][4] He also considered external bottom stringers for beach protection but decided against them, concerned about the added hydrodynamic drag, preferring to beach the boat when needed, as the plans allow; see [rudder: rocky-bottom and beach-protection ideas](/components/rudder.md#rocky-bottom-and-beach-protection-ideas) for the general question.[5]

## A birdsmouth mast, and a lesson learned

Rather than sourcing straight, clear lumber for his mast, he bought cheaper, knotty spruce and spent weeks resawing it, scarfing it and shaping a round, tapered birdsmouth section, tapered in both diameter and wall thickness, reasoning the result would come out lighter and stronger than a solid stock mast.[2] By his own later judgement the detour was "not worth it" against simply buying better wood from the start.[2] The general lesson is set out at [mast: a birdsmouth mast weighed against buying better lumber](/components/rig/mast.md#a-birdsmouth-mast-weighed-against-buying-better-lumber).

## Fitting out

Following [Alastair Law](/people/alastair-law.md)'s own practice of building the hull last, he built the vent trunk, the rudder and the spars first, working in a shop with room enough to move around.[6] For a second build he would fit bigger vent-trunk pulleys, pay closer attention to watertightness and embed LED strip lighting from leftover stock, an idea covered generally at [running lights: off-the-shelf LED sources for a boat-tent light](/operations/running-lights.md#off-the-shelf-led-sources-for-a-boat-tent-light); a water-tank design of his own and a lightning-conductor routing question are covered at [water-tank lining: flexible bladders and insertable tanks](/construction/water-tank-lining.md#flexible-bladders-and-insertable-tanks) and [lightning protection: an external conductor considered](/design/lightning-protection.md#an-external-conductor-considered-against-the-standard-through-mast-wire).[6]

## Shaping the rudder blade

By July 2022 he had reached the rudder: he cut a pair of plywood guide fences offset from a NACA 0009 profile by the router bit's own protruding length, then ran the bit against the fences to shape the blade directly.[7] General technique: [rudder: foil section, not a standard NACA airfoil](/components/rudder.md#foil-section-not-a-standard-naca-airfoil).
