# Tim McKinney

**Tim McKinney** is a sailor from **Texas** who acquired an already-built [Paradox](/paradox.md) in the fall of 2018 and founded the "**Paradox Sailboat**" Facebook group the following February: "A group interested in the Paradox Sailboat designed by Matt Layden. A little cruiser full of surprises."[1] The group is distinct from the earlier one founded by [Alberto Callejon](/people/alberto-callejon.md) in 2011.

## The boat

His Paradox was built by **[Michael Beebe](/people/michael-beebe.md)** of Rockport, Texas, Beebe's second Paradox and possibly the same hull later named "**[Oogway](/boats/oogway.md)**," though this remains unconfirmed.[2] Beebe had built her as a convertible, with a removable hardtop and conventional tiller steering in place of the design's rope-and-yoke loop, and had enjoyed sailing her himself with the top off and a short tiller in fair weather.[2]

## A steering redesign and the 2019 Texas 200

McKinney found the inherited, shortened tiller uncomfortable once the aft window was refitted, and built a replacement in square tubing with round tubing inside, sized to clear the window or pass through it for minor course changes, weighing that push-pull design against simply restoring the rope-and-yoke loop before settling on the push-pull.[3] He sailed her that June on the Texas 200, a roughly 200-mile, mostly downwind coastal raid, having had the newly acquired boat out on the water only once before; he had run the event the previous year in a Compac 16 and had sailed the same coast earlier still in a Balboa 16.[4] A snake bite to his leg cut the passage short, and boarding from the muddy shores along the way proved awkward.[5] Under sail she handled well and felt relatively fast, adjusting sail area on the fly to keep pace with a companion, and left to herself she would stall head to wind and sit there while he attended to other things; a wind scoop, he judged, would have much improved sleeping comfort on warm nights.[6] The steering redesign fared worse: it did not work at all, and he concluded the boat needed to be reengineered back to the original rope-and-yoke system, a case told in full at [components/steering.md](/components/steering.md#a-2019-tiller-conversion-reconsidered).[5][6]
