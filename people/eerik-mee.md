# Eerik Mee

**Eerik Mee** (byline **"Eerik"** on his blog) is a boatbuilder in **Tartu, Estonia**,[1] and the builder of **"[THU](/boats/thu.md),"** a [Paradox](/paradox.md) built **20% larger than the plans** and rigged as a **Chinese junk**, launched in 2017. He documented the whole build on a personal blog, `paradoxbuild.blogspot.com`, which is preserved as a first-class source at [references/eerik-mee-blog.md](/references/eerik-mee-blog.md); this page draws the builder and the build out of it.

He is notable in the archive for having actually built two things it had otherwise only argued about. THU is the **only scaled-up Paradox on record built and sailed** — the recurring "[larger Paradox](/design/larger-paradox.md)" realised, at almost exactly the 20% figures the mailing list had worked out years earlier — and it carries the **only purpose-designed cambered junk rig** on the design, as against the stock lug *converted* to junk on [Planktos](/boats/planktos.md).

## The build

Mee built THU from **July 2015**, scaling every measurement on the plans by **1.2** — "I multiplied just all measurements with 1.2, so yes, it is also wider and higher. LOA turned out about 5.05 m and width about 1.5 m."[2] The hull, chine runners, rudder, and a 6 m birdmouth mast were built through 2015–16; the cabin, deck, and fit-out followed, and the boat was **launched and christened "THU"** on an inland lake on **5 July 2017**.[3][4] Fitting-out continued for two more years — the ballast cast as lead pigs, a full electrical system and an electric outboard added — with more lake sailing in 2019.[5]

The build was **repeatedly paused for overseas work**: his projects took him away for long stretches — a Thailand project that "took about half a year longer than initially planned," later work on Bali and elsewhere in Indonesia, and a spell in Norway — each of which cost him a building or sailing season.[5] He built at home in **Estonia**, doing much of the work outdoors in a garden and garage and waiting on warm-enough weather for epoxy; the blog's own references fit an EU builder, citing EU rules on handling lead and the difficulty of sourcing the right aluminium alloy "in this country."[5]

## What he changed, and why

Mee's departures from the plans are the substance of his contribution, and he explained his reasoning in the blog's comment threads:

- **The 20% scale-up.** He sized the boat up uniformly to fit himself, arriving at **LOA ≈ 5.05 m, beam ≈ 1.5 m** — within centimetres of the worked 20% scale-up the list had carried since 2008.[2] The stock scantlings were kept; the spars scaled with the hull.
- **The cambered junk rig.** Rather than convert the stock lug, he designed a **six-panel cambered junk sail** from scratch, following **Arne Kverneland**'s method and drawing on the **Junk Rig Association**.[6] He first tried a flat-cut sample plan, "but I didn't like the looks of the boat; so I modified it towards Reddish type at roach. The HML is actually flat cut sail with not so good winward performance. It's the AK's camber that improves it."[6]
- **The mast moved forward.** To carry the taller (6 m vs ~4.8 m) junk rig in balance, he moved the mast **from aft of bulkhead 2 to as far forward of it as possible**, wanting more mast below deck and clearance between the transom and the upper sheet.[6] He intended the boat to be **moored, not trailered**, accepting the heavier mast's rig-up time in exchange.[6]
- **A worked-out ballast and systems boat.** THU carries **~240 kg of ballast** (twelve cast-lead pigs plus water ballast) in a compartment aft of bulkhead 3, and — unlike most Paradoxes — a **full electrical system and an electric outboard**.[7][5]

His full account of how she was built, equipped, and sails is at [THU](/boats/thu.md); the source itself at [Eerik Mee's blog](/references/eerik-mee-blog.md).

## The record's limits

Mee posted to the mailing list eleven times on record: once before his own build began and ten more times once it was under way. In November 2014 — nearly a year before starting THU — he cautioned a fellow member that a magnetic aquarium-glass cleaner, floated as a way to wipe salt off a Paradox's windows underway, would scratch its acrylic or polycarbonate glazing immediately, since such cleaners are made for glass fish tanks.[10] In August 2015, with the hull already a season old, he asked the list's short-lived registry thread whether a photographed boat — an unfamiliar rig, and on a second look no visible chine runners at all — still counted as a Paradox; see [people/john-starkie.md](/people/john-starkie.md#a-modified-paradox-with-no-chine-runners-and-a-keel-theory-of-tacking) for the fuller technical exchange it drew.[11][12] Days later, on the "Paradox tacking difficulties" thread the registry exchange had spun off, floated a further theory of his own — that heavy reefing might move the sail's centre of effort forward rather than aft, since the head is shorter than the foot, adding lee helm and making tacking harder; see [operations/sailing-characteristics.md](/operations/sailing-characteristics.md#reefed-sail-tacking-trouble-and-a-chine-runner-as-keel-theory-2015) for the fuller thread.[13] Refining the theory the next day, argued the sail's own forward tilt means reefing furls away its lower, more-aft part while the more-forward part stays set, adding lee helm masked in normal sailing by the large rudder but showing up once heavily reefed — offered as the likely reason Petite Lady's chine runners were cut down aft, and noted a second Paradox, his own, nearing the chine-runner stage and being considered for runners set a little further forward than stock.[14] A further message, three days later, finally tied that second Paradox to the blog build by name: having "locked" her name and posted his own "going 3D" photo — a tradition he noted every Paradox seems to observe — he confirmed on the list that the boat under way in Estonia was a **20% larger version**, and described checking and fixing the diagonals separately at every section while assembling bulkheads to sides, since sections can each be out of true differently; full detail on [THU](/boats/thu.md#citations) and at [construction/hull-assembly-sequence.md](/construction/hull-assembly-sequence.md#truing-the-hull-the-diagonal-comparison-method).[15] The same week he twice gave general cutting technique to a second builder constructing an unrelated Puddle-Duck-based design ("Gorfnik") — a router-and-bearing trick for trimming identical panels off a first-shaped one, and a circular-saw method producing smoother curved cuts than a jigsaw — and mentioned in passing treating THU's own okoume plywood with a monoethylene-glycol solution before epoxy sealing; full detail at [Gorfnik's own page](/similar-boat-designs/gorfnik-paraduck.md#building-the-cabin-panels) and [THU's build notes](/boats/thu.md#build-details-worth-noting).[16][17] Answering a fellow correspondent's direct question three days later,[18] he confirmed the 20% enlargement ran through every dimension of the boat, not just her length — the earliest on-the-record confirmation of that point, years before the 2019 blog comment above restated the same figures.[19] Later the same day he reported THU's bottom now fitted: a straight aluminium bar sighted along the chine logs confirmed they ran level to each other and to the bulkheads over the full length, corrected with a block plane and finished with a sanding block, and a pair of witness-dowel holes drilled through the dry-fitted bottom into bulkhead #3 let it be relocated in exactly the same position for the permanent glue-up; full detail at [THU](/boats/thu.md#build-details-worth-noting) and [construction/hull-assembly-sequence.md](/construction/hull-assembly-sequence.md#truing-the-hull-the-diagonal-comparison-method).[20] A further message the next day added a bulkhead-fitting refinement: when dry-fitting bulkheads and sides, never took the box fully apart again, instead pressing the sides a little way apart to free a single bulkhead at a time for a bevel adjustment and reinstalling it immediately with thickened epoxy, found easier that way; full detail at [THU](/boats/thu.md#build-details-worth-noting) and [construction/hull-assembly-sequence.md](/construction/hull-assembly-sequence.md#truing-the-hull-the-diagonal-comparison-method).[21] Otherwise his build was reported only on his own blog. The blog answers a few readers' questions and links out to the Junk Rig Association, but it is a single builder's record of a single, heavily modified boat, and the sailing it documents is a handful of day-sails on a lake rather than a cruise.[3][4] What survives is a completed, launched, and repeatedly sailed boat, tuned but not finished tuning when the record ends in 2019.[5]

He was, however, an active member of the [Facebook group](/references/paradox-facebook-group.md) from soon after it started, posting sailing videos and the first-hand sailing experience the blog's 2019 endpoint doesn't reach, and helping other builders — for instance advising a builder on rudder-gudgeon geometry — with that material folded into [THU](/boats/thu.md).[8][9]
