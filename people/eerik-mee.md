# Eerik Mee

**Eerik Mee** is a boatbuilder in **Tartu, Estonia**.[1] From **2015** he built **"[THU](/boats/thu.md)"**, a [Paradox](/paradox.md) enlarged **20%** on every dimension of the plans, launching her on an inland Estonian lake over the weekend of **1-2 July 2017** and tacking cleanly on her first trials.[2][3] She carries a six-panel cambered junk sail designed from scratch to the **Junk Rig Association**'s method, the only sail of its kind built for the design, and has been sailed, and reefed down in a real breeze, across several seasons since.[4] Full build account: [THU](/boats/thu.md); the source itself at [Eerik Mee's blog](/references/eerik-mee-blog.md).

## Building around other work

Mee scaled every measurement on the plans by **1.2** to fit himself, arriving at an LOA of about **5.05 m** and a beam of about **1.5 m**, and kept the stock scantlings while the spars grew with the hull.[2] The build stretched across several years, repeatedly interrupted by work abroad: a Thailand project that ran about half a year longer than planned, later work on Bali and elsewhere in Indonesia and a spell in Norway each cost him a building or sailing season.[5] He built at home in Estonia, much of the work done outdoors in a garden and garage while he waited on warm-enough weather for epoxy, working under EU rules on handling lead and finding it hard to source the right aluminium alloy locally.[5]

## The junk rig

Rather than convert the stock lug to a junk rig, as was done on [Planktos](/boats/planktos.md), Mee designed his own six-panel cambered sail from scratch, following **Arne Kverneland**'s method and drawing on the Junk Rig Association.[4] He set aside a flat-cut sample plan on both looks and performance, since "it's the AK's camber that improves it," and moved the taller rig's mast **forward of bulkhead 2** to keep the boat in balance.[6] THU also carries about **240 kg** of lead-and-water ballast and, unlike most Paradoxes, a full electrical system and an electric outboard.[7] The full technical account, including the balance and sheeting problems the rig has not yet fully solved on the water, is at [THU](/boats/thu.md).

## A careful hand with materials

Mee plans his materials precisely enough to know in advance which piece of wood becomes which part of the boat, a habit shown in a plain line he gave a builder starting out: "I ordered my wood, and the only plank that was left over was the one meant for yuloh that I didn't make."[8]
