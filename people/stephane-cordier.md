# Stéphane Cordier

**Stéphane Cordier** is a Belgian correspondent who introduced himself to the [Paradox](/paradox.md) mailing list in December 2017, already partway into building his own boat.[1] A professional woodworker, he already owned a 9-metre stainless-steel cruising sailboat of the "Esquilo" type built for long-distance voyaging and associated with French navigator Nicole van de Kerkoven, sailed mainly in Scandinavian waters, but had long wanted to build himself a much smaller boat to get away into places a normal sailboat could not reach.[2][5]

## Drawn to Paradox by Lezlie Henson's "Tardis"

Cordier had followed [Lezlie Henson](/people/lezlie-henson.md)'s build blog since its very start, and it was seeing her Paradox, "[Tardis](/boats/scout.md)," that made him want to build one himself; he wrote to her directly to say so, reading her blog daily for construction detail as well as to follow the small events of her life.[2]

## Construction under way (November-December 2017)

By his own account he began building about a month before introducing himself to the list, with the bulkheads finished or very nearly so within that time — reported done in his first message and "almost finished" five days later.[1][3] Woodworking being his trade, he found the manual side of the build no trouble, but was still unsure of some of the less straightforward parts, naming the stem and the chine runners specifically.[3]

## Joining the list's French-speaking builders

Cordier introduced himself on the same "Des constructeurs de Paradox français?" thread that had brought a Québécois correspondent, [Sylvain Caro](/people/andre-francois-bourbeau.md#a-francophone-correspondent-welcomed-december-2017), to the list a few weeks earlier, and asked Caro directly how his own construction was coming along.[3] Weeks later, on the same thread, he turned to [André-François Bourbeau](/people/andre-francois-bourbeau.md), the list's veteran two-Paradox owner, for a firsthand read on the design before pressing on with his own build: whether the boat had genuinely been sailed offshore in real waves or only on sheltered water, how she performed honestly, what he would improve, whether the sheet really does catch on everything the way it looks like it might, whether her windward pointing is good, whether the sail furls easily, and whether any Paradox has yet made a major open-water passage such as a transatlantic crossing.[4]
