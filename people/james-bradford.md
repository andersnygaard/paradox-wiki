# James (Bradford)

**James**, of Bradford, Yorkshire, UK, designed a lighter, car-topping [Paradox](/paradox.md)/[Enigma](/matt-layden/enigma.md) hybrid in October 2007, with the cabin sides and roof bolting off the hull and inverting to stow inside it for towing, and began cutting the bottom plywood for his own version in January 2008.[1][2] His own introduction to small, enclosed-cabin sailing came aboard a "sea tramp," a Hartley ferro-cement hull with a deckhouse moored at Hamble, which he judged "rather similar to paradox except for scale."[3] He already owned a 15 ft "Summer Breeze"-type boat of the same name, decked over with higher freeboard, and tried an early flexible-window pop-up roof on her before adapting the idea to his car-topping hybrid.[4] Full account of the design: [car-topping conversion ideas](/design/ideas/car-topping-conversion-idea.md).

## Building a car-topping Paradox/Enigma

Cutting began within days of buying the plywood. The chine runners were planned in 3/4 in plywood, sized for a higher aspect ratio on the designer's own suggestion, with a soft cabin top in the style of Enigma. A companion question, whether a 65 sq ft sail or a shorter boom would suit strong summer winds better, is answered on [Little Jim's own page](/boats/little-jim.md#a-boom-trim-question-a-proposed-poole-meet-and-2008-cruise-plans-with-bill-serjeant).[5] The hull depth was set at 2 ft, closer to Enigma's than to the stock Paradox, the aim being to combine Paradox's cabin comfort with the sailing performance and light weight of a Michael Storer "Goat Island Skiff."[6] By June 2008, with a workshop extension going up fast, building was expected to resume soon on what had become an Enigma-type hull with chine runners, a lug sail and an open cockpit like Enigma's.[7] Full technical account, including the worked weight estimate: [car-topping conversion ideas](/design/ideas/car-topping-conversion-idea.md#the-build-begins-cutting-plywood-and-a-hull-depth-set-nearer-enigmas).

## Ballast and heating

He favored Paradox's fixed heavy ballast over water ballast for an all-weather micro-cruiser, though he thought Enigma the better choice for some builders.[8] For a cabin heater he had a narrower-flued, more pot-bellied barbecue of his own, convertible to top-loading with a flue adapter.[9]
