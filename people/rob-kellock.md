# Rob Kellock

**Rob Kellock** is a Paradox builder and owner from **Canterbury, New Zealand**, near Christchurch, who began building a Paradox in **2014**.[1] He moved from an open, slot-topped Michalak "Philsboat" to a closed cabin he could sleep and sail offshore in.[2] He spent three and a half years building his own hull, "[Mini](/boats/mini.md)," and put her through her first sea trial in December 2017.[3] In the years since he has sailed and tested her with unusual method and precision, changing her rig after sailing and comparing both versions and building a GPS-tracked course on Lake Ellesmere specifically to check exaggerated hull-speed claims against measurement. Full build and sailing record: [boats/mini.md](/boats/mini.md). His own description of the boat, unromantic and exact, is the throughline: "not even close to gold plated, definitely work grade, but still a very solid build."[4] She was still under sail in February 2025.[5]

## Choosing Paradox over an open boat

He had looked at a built Paradox near Auckland about four years before he started his own and passed on the design at the time, put off by how dark the cabin was forward of the ventilation box.[6] He came back to it by 2014: he was the only one who ever sailed his Philsboat and he wanted to go offshore. In the colder waters he frequents he also wanted to sleep in comfort at night without the drafts a slot top lets through.[2] The Philsboat sold in mid-2015, leaving him boatless until "Mini" could be launched, which he found helped keep him motivated through the build.[7] Before starting from scratch he had hoped to buy an existing Paradox, "[Petite Lady](/boats/petite-lady.md)," directly from her builder, and inspected her not long after a fellow enthusiast bought her instead; building his own boat was the result.[8]

## Build order

He built the mast, spars, yuloh, tiller and rudder before his hull, on the view that a finished hull's small workspace tempts a builder to shortcut the appendages once a boat-shaped object already exists.[9] He rates the vent box as the cleverest single detail in the design, and solved moving the finished hull around his own shop with a low-tech answer, a square of carpet dragged across the floor and conduit pipe used as rollers.[10] Full build account: [boats/mini.md](/boats/mini.md).

## Hull speed, and what she does in a seaway

He sails by measurement rather than by impression. When hull-speed claims for the Paradox began to sound more like folklore than record, he laid out a GPS-tracked one-mile triangle on Lake Ellesmere and sailed it, preferring, in his own phrase, "the facts rather than the fantasy."[11] The same habit decided his rig: below about 10 knots his standing lug made little progress to windward, so he sewed a four-sided sprit sail to match the lug's own centre of effort, sailed both and adopted the sprit outright once a systematic comparison across a full season showed it ahead on every point of sail but one.[12] Full account: [boats/mini.md](/boats/mini.md#the-rig-from-standing-lug-to-sprit) and [boats/mini.md](/boats/mini.md#how-she-sails).

## On taking one offshore

The blue-water question interests him more than it settles for him: he has openly doubted a Paradox's chances falling down the face of a breaking sea and has never volunteered himself for an ocean crossing.[13] His own answer came from a deliberate overnight trial off Banks Peninsula in 2019, scaled back from an initial hundred-mile plan once the real time and exposure involved sank in: a drogue only earns its keep together with active sailing, and the open sea itself proved simply boring, never once unsafe from the boat, only from other vessels' traffic.[14] Full account: [boats/mini.md](/boats/mini.md#an-offshore-trial-september-2019).

## Cruising the South Island, and a keeler reconsidered

He sails year-round and has cruised widely from his home waters on Lake Ellesmere and Lyttelton Harbour, with week-long trips to Nelson and Tasman Bay, Abel Tasman National Park, Tauranga Harbour and Queen Charlotte Sound. Full cruising record: [boats/mini.md](/boats/mini.md). Mooring alongside a friend's steel ketch during the Abel Tasman cruise left him newly certain of the Paradox's own stability by comparison and increasingly disenchanted with the idea of a keeler and the maintenance it demands sitting idle between sails.[15]

## Why he would build one again

He would build a Paradox again without hesitation: the build taught him a real amount about boat construction, she is safe enough to sail offshore without fear, she is versatile enough for shallow lakes and open water alike and her low-windage cabin and yuloh have meant she has never needed an outboard. He has weighed only one real alternative, Chesapeake Light Craft's "Autumn Leaves" canoe yawl, working through the changes building one the Paradox way would need before settling on the boat he already has.[16]
