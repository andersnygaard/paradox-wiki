# Steve (Kansas City)

**Steve**, of Kansas City, Missouri, began building a [Paradox](/paradox.md) in January 2004 with plans, plywood and fir lumber already in hand, "a mere 15 hour drive from the nearest salt water cruising grounds."[1] He built the appendages, mast, yard, boom, rudder, yuloh and beams, before turning to the hull itself, planning to sail her only after an expected retirement several years off and preferring, in his own words, to "enjoy the trip as well as the destination."[1] Two working names, "Windependent" and "Cette Assez" (French for "It's Enough"), were floated for her early on; an independent 2014 census later recorded the boat, still under construction, as **"Telestai."**[1][2]

## Building the appendages first

The box mast's dimensions, given on page 12 of the plans, became legible with a fellow builder's explanation of the drawing's plan views, since kept as a "Rosetta Stone" for reading them; full account at [mast, reading the mast drawing](/components/rig/mast.md#reading-the-mast-drawing-a-recurring-point-of-confusion).[3] With three of the mast's four pieces already epoxied together, he preferred to keep the masthead light's wiring inside the mast rather than bring it out below the sheave, if that proved practical; full account at [electrical wiring, routing masthead-light wiring](/construction/electrical-wiring.md#routing-masthead-light-wiring-outside-the-mast-below-the-sheave).[4] His yuloh was laminated from two Douglas fir two-by-fours, thinned on a radial arm saw, its handle narrowed with a saber saw and the blade finish-shaped with a portable grinder and sanding disc; full account at [yuloh, building the yuloh itself](/components/yuloh.md#building-the-yuloh-itself-blade-curvature-and-the-pivot-boltsocket).[5][6] Once his own deck beams were laminated, he used the finished pieces themselves as a template for the bulkheads, tracing their curvature directly rather than working from the plans and trimming the beams to fit afterward; full account at [deck and cabin beam lamination, drawing bulkhead curvature from the finished beams](/construction/deck-and-cabin-beams.md#drawing-bulkhead-curvature-from-the-finished-beams-rather-than-the-plans).[7] That same closeness to the plans left the mast's lightning-dissipation system an open question rather than a settled departure: fitting it meant routing a strike down a hollow wooden mast and through the boat's interior, rather than overboard through a stayed rig's own standing rigging.[8]

## The build left unfinished

Roughly seven years after he began, in 2011, he offered the boat's unfinished parts and materials for sale rather than complete her himself: her beams and yards, a Douglas fir mast already wired for a masthead light, several plywood pieces already cut, framing stock, hardware and about 100 lb of lead ballast he had cast from wheel weights. Full listing at [Secondhand Paradox market, an unfinished hull for sale](/operations/secondhand-market.md#an-unfinished-hull-for-sale-telestai-march-2011).[9]
