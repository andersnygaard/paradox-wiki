# Chris Curtis

**Chris Curtis**, of **Sandpoint, Idaho**, was the third owner of the [Paradox](/paradox.md) **"[Scout](/boats/scout.md)"** — the hull [Lezlie Henson](/people/lezlie-henson.md) built in Texas as "Tardis" and [Sean Mulligan](/people/sean-mulligan.md) finished and launched. He bought her from Mulligan, sailed her for many years in north Idaho and the Puget Sound, and sold her on in August 2024. He is a lifelong shoal-draft sailor, and "Scout" was one of a small succession of **[Matt Layden](/matt-layden/index.md) and Layden-influenced boats** he has owned. The boat's own build-and-sailing record, across all four owners, is kept at [Scout](/boats/scout.md); this page covers Curtis himself.

## Owner of "Scout"

Curtis bought the boat from **[Sean Mulligan](/people/sean-mulligan.md)**, making him her third owner in a chain of **Henson → Mulligan → Curtis → Lee**, and he rated her built to a very high standard.[1][2] The purchase itself is dated by his own words on the mailing list to **March 2015**, replying to André-François Bourbeau's St-Johns River cruise report with a first-time signature of "S/V Scout" alongside his Enigma 460 and a note that he had "recently purchased a Paradox" (see [Disambiguation](#disambiguation) below).[10] Under his ownership she was **shown several times at the Port Townsend Wooden Boat Show**, pictured there around 2017, and in 2019 he called her a great boat in almost every way.[1] He day-sailed her hard on **Priest Lake** in north Idaho — once knocked down by a gust, though not past about 90° — cruised the **Puget Sound**, and camped aboard many nights.[2] Her 2014 **[Texas 200](/merits/texas-200.md#the-texas-200-and-a-port-townsend-wooden-boat-show-showing)** run was made under Mulligan, before Curtis owned her.

A refit he began — deck hardware to re-bed, windows to reinstall, a repaint, and a slow leak at the vent-hole end-grain — stalled when he moved on to a much larger boat, and he offered her for sale from 2022.[2] In **August 2024** he sold her to **Lee, of Maine**, who trailered her some 3,500 miles home.[3]

## A fleet of Layden boats

Curtis also owns an **[Enigma](/matt-layden/enigma.md) 460** named **"Fantasma"** — a Layden design slightly larger than a Paradox but lighter in displacement, built in the same style as the smaller Enigma, with an interior he reckons roughly 42 inches by 7 feet — which he has offered for sale over several years and exhibited at the Port Townsend Wooden Boat Festival.[4] An Enigma of his was inspected in depth by **Matt Layden** at Sail Oklahoma in 2014; by Curtis's account Layden "quietly gave me the OK, saying it was well built."[5]

By the mid-2020s he had, in his own phrase, graduated to a slightly bigger boat: a roughly 30,000 lb **[Hogfish Maximus](/similar-boat-designs/hogfish-maximus.md)** — **[Chris Morejohn](/people/chris-morejohn.md)'s** big shoal-draft sharpie, which Curtis calls basically a gigantic Paradox and beaches the same way. He lives aboard her, cruising from the Pacific Northwest down to Southern California and Mexico.[6][7] It was that move to a larger boat that finally freed "Scout" for sale.[3]

## On the design

Curtis set out his design loyalties plainly in the community: that the Paradox's windward performance is doubted only by people who do not own one, that her roller-furling lug is remarkable, and that ordinary keelboat sailors will never understand how much simpler and less stressful life is without a keel, grounding out being no worry at all.[8]

## Disambiguation

Distinct from a Phoenix, Arizona correspondent who also used the name "Chris Curtis" (see [references/paradox-uk-website.md](/references/paradox-uk-website.md#a-uk-and-worldwide-build-count)), and from [Chris Becker](/people/chris-becker.md) and [Chris Morejohn](/people/chris-morejohn.md), who share only a first name.

"Chris Curtis's Paradox" and "[Scout](/boats/scout.md)" are **one hull under successive owners**, not two Henson-built boats: Henson's blog records her building only one Paradox, and Curtis's own account has this boat built by Henson and bought from Mulligan. (His source spells the builder "Hensen.")[1]

**Identity confirmed, March 2015.** This page's subject and the long-tracked mailing-list correspondent `joe_mapango`/`ccurtis` (Sandpoint/Lake Pend Oreille, N. Idaho) are the same person. That correspondent had been carried for nine years, batches 384-812, as a below-threshold namesake — a 2006 newcomer who spent years scaling up an unnamed "maxi-Paradox," then a Jim Michalak "Philsboat," then an unnamed two-hulled Florida boat — explicitly kept separate from this page for lack of a shared boat name or a self-ID (see the project watch-list for the full, now-resolved history). A February 2015 message had already narrowed the gap to a name-and-type match on the Enigma 460 "Fantasma."[9] The following month, replying to André-François Bourbeau's own St-Johns River cruise report, the same account signed off **"Chris Curtis, S/V Fantasma, S/V Scout"** and reported having "recently purchased a Paradox" — naming, for the first time, the very boat this page is written around.[10] A shared boat name is the trigger the watch-list had always set for this case, and it is now met: the correspondent is this page's Chris Curtis, and March 2015 is accordingly the best-attested date for his purchase of "Scout" from Sean Mulligan (see [boats/scout.md](/boats/scout.md#later-ownership-chris-curtis-then-lee-maine)). Folding his mailing-list footprint (11+ files, 20+ messages, largely de-attributed per the register-shift rules) into this page's own citations remains a separate, deferred task for a dedicated consolidation pass or the next holistic review — resolved here is the identity question itself, not that consolidation.
