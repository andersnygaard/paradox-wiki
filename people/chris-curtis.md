# Chris Curtis

**Chris Curtis**, of **Sandpoint, Idaho**, was the third owner of the [Paradox](/paradox.md) **"[Scout](/boats/scout.md)"** — the hull [Lezlie Henson](/people/lezlie-henson.md) built in Texas as "Tardis" and [Sean Mulligan](/people/sean-mulligan.md) finished and launched. He bought her from Mulligan, sailed her for many years in north Idaho and the Puget Sound, and sold her on in August 2024. He is a lifelong shoal-draft sailor, and "Scout" was one of a small succession of **[Matt Layden](/matt-layden/index.md) and Layden-influenced boats** he has owned. The boat's own build-and-sailing record, across all four owners, is kept at [Scout](/boats/scout.md); this page covers Curtis himself.

## Owner of "Scout"

Curtis bought the boat from **[Sean Mulligan](/people/sean-mulligan.md)**, making him her third owner in a chain of **Henson → Mulligan → Curtis → Lee**, and he rated her built to a very high standard.[1][2] The purchase itself is dated by his own words on the mailing list to **March 2015**, replying to André-François Bourbeau's St-Johns River cruise report with a first-time signature of "S/V Scout" alongside his Enigma 460 and a note that he had "recently purchased a Paradox" (see [Disambiguation](#disambiguation) below).[10] Under his ownership she was **shown at the Port Townsend Wooden Boat Show at least as early as September 2015**, and several times since — pictured there around 2017 — and in 2019 he called her a great boat in almost every way.[1][11] He day-sailed her hard on **Priest Lake** in north Idaho — once knocked down by a gust, though not past about 90° — cruised the **Puget Sound**, and camped aboard many nights.[2] Her 2014 **[Texas 200](/merits/texas-200.md#the-texas-200-and-a-port-townsend-wooden-boat-show-showing)** run was made under Mulligan, before Curtis owned her.

A refit he began — deck hardware to re-bed, windows to reinstall, a repaint, and a slow leak at the vent-hole end-grain — stalled when he moved on to a much larger boat, and he offered her for sale from 2022, in July that year posting a dedicated notice offering her alongside his Enigma 460 "Fantasma" as he prepared to move aboard the larger boat and leave for Mexico that September; full account at [boats/scout.md — later ownership](/boats/scout.md#later-ownership-chris-curtis-then-lee-maine).[2][20] In **August 2024** he sold her to **Lee, of Maine**, who trailered her some 3,500 miles home.[3]

## A fleet of Layden boats

Curtis also owns an **[Enigma](/matt-layden/enigma.md) 460** named **"Fantasma"** — a Layden design slightly larger than a Paradox but lighter in displacement, built in the same style as the smaller Enigma, with an interior he reckons roughly 42 inches by 7 feet — which he has offered for sale over several years, as early as January 2021, and exhibited at the Port Townsend Wooden Boat Festival.[4][19] An Enigma of his was inspected in depth by **Matt Layden** at Sail Oklahoma in 2014; by Curtis's account Layden "quietly gave me the OK, saying it was well built."[5]

By the mid-2020s he had, in his own phrase, graduated to a slightly bigger boat: a roughly 30,000 lb **[Hogfish Maximus](/similar-boat-designs/hogfish-maximus.md)** — **[Chris Morejohn](/people/chris-morejohn.md)'s** big shoal-draft sharpie, which Curtis calls basically a gigantic Paradox and beaches the same way. He lives aboard her, cruising from the Pacific Northwest down to Southern California and Mexico.[6][7] It was that move to a larger boat that finally freed "Scout" for sale.[3]

## A 2017 meeting recalled, and a size comparison

Congratulating OkieBob on finally taking delivery of a Paradox in January 2017, Curtis recalled meeting him years earlier on the shore of a Lake Eufaula, Oklahoma gathering and watching him work for years toward a boat of his own.[13] The exchange drew a size comparison from OkieBob himself: with only about a one-foot difference in waterline length, Curtis's Enigma 460 appeared to carry roughly twice a stock Paradox's interior volume — full figures at [matt-layden/enigma.md — Paradox vs. Enigma 360 vs. Enigma 460](/matt-layden/enigma.md#paradox-vs-enigma-360-vs-enigma-460) — though OkieBob guessed Curtis would still favor the Paradox solo and leave the Enigma at home when sailing with company.[14]

The same month, Curtis showed interest in joining a planned March 2017 loop cruise around North Carolina's Albemarle Sound aboard "Scout," proposed by André-François Bourbeau; full account at [boats/ironsides.md — a planned 2017 Albemarle Sound cruise](/boats/ironsides.md#a-planned-2017-albemarle-sound-cruise-and-its-march-start).[15]

## On the design

Curtis set out his design loyalties plainly in the community: that the Paradox's windward performance is doubted only by people who do not own one, that her roller-furling lug is remarkable, and that ordinary keelboat sailors will never understand how much simpler and less stressful life is without a keel, grounding out being no worry at all.[8]

## A stretched-Paradox skepticism, and a pre-purchase test against Tardis (2017)

Asked to weigh in on a February 2017 "Stretched Paradoxes Sailing?" thread, Curtis judged the [Lil Cruiser](/matt-layden/little-cruiser.md) and his own Enigma 460 both really just stretched Paradoxes already, and reckoned any sufficiently narrow, relatively heavy sharpie under about 20 ft would sail somewhat like one — but doubted the value of building one bigger for a single sailor, since the stock size seemed to him already close to perfect for one person, and enlarging "seems to me to distract more than add." The same exchange recalled the mystery of Gunther Battista's "Manatee," an enlarged Paradox that came close to finishing years earlier before its builder vanished, and supplied a further, years-earlier data point of his own: a pre-purchase test sail of his Enigma 460 against "Tardis" — the boat he would later buy and rename "Scout" — while Sean Mulligan still owned and piloted her; full account at [boats/scout.md — a pre-purchase test sail against an Enigma 460](/boats/scout.md#a-pre-purchase-test-sail-against-an-enigma-460).[16][17] Further discussion of the exchange, including its technical detail on ballast and sail-area scaling, is at [design/larger-paradox.md](/design/larger-paradox.md#a-february-2017-recurrence-the-cube-law-objection-restated-and-a-question-about-completed-stretched-paradoxes).

## Community involvement

Curtis also moderates a separate mailing list dedicated to Jim Michalak's designs, and in September 2015 invited a builder of a Paradox-inspired hull to also share it there.[12] He recalled having been banned himself, years earlier, from an early, stricter Paradox group under [Don Elliott](/people/don-elliott.md)'s moderation after proposing changes to the design — judging that earlier group "certainly controlled," but lacking the soul and diversity of the present, more open list.[12]

For an unprotected rudder that keeps finding the bottom, he recommends gluing rope into the leading edge with epoxy as a sacrificial edge that can always be ground off and replaced. See [components/rudder.md](/components/rudder.md#a-proven-copper-pipe-leading-edge-and-a-dacron-rope-alternative).[18]

## Disambiguation

Distinct from a Phoenix, Arizona correspondent who also used the name "Chris Curtis" (see [references/paradox-uk-website.md](/references/paradox-uk-website.md#a-uk-and-worldwide-build-count)), and from [Chris Becker](/people/chris-becker.md) and [Chris Morejohn](/people/chris-morejohn.md), who share only a first name.

"Chris Curtis's Paradox" and "[Scout](/boats/scout.md)" are **one hull under successive owners**, not two Henson-built boats: Henson's blog records her building only one Paradox, and Curtis's own account has this boat built by Henson and bought from Mulligan. (His source spells the builder "Hensen.")[1]

**Identity confirmed, March 2015.** This page's subject and the long-tracked mailing-list correspondent `joe_mapango`/`ccurtis` (Sandpoint/Lake Pend Oreille, N. Idaho) are the same person. That correspondent had been carried for nine years, batches 384-812, as a below-threshold namesake — a 2006 newcomer who spent years scaling up an unnamed "maxi-Paradox," then a Jim Michalak "Philsboat," then an unnamed two-hulled Florida boat — explicitly kept separate from this page for lack of a shared boat name or a self-ID (see the project watch-list for the full, now-resolved history). A February 2015 message had already narrowed the gap to a name-and-type match on the Enigma 460 "Fantasma."[9] The following month, replying to André-François Bourbeau's own St-Johns River cruise report, the same account signed off **"Chris Curtis, S/V Fantasma, S/V Scout"** and reported having "recently purchased a Paradox" — naming, for the first time, the very boat this page is written around.[10] A shared boat name is the trigger the watch-list had always set for this case, and it is now met: the correspondent is this page's Chris Curtis, and March 2015 is accordingly the best-attested date for his purchase of "Scout" from Sean Mulligan (see [boats/scout.md](/boats/scout.md#later-ownership-chris-curtis-then-lee-maine)). Folding his mailing-list footprint (11+ files, 20+ messages, largely de-attributed per the register-shift rules) into this page's own citations remains a separate, deferred task for a dedicated consolidation pass or the next holistic review — resolved here is the identity question itself, not that consolidation.
