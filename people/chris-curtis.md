# Chris Curtis

**Chris Curtis**, of **Sandpoint, Idaho**, is a lifelong shoal-draft sailor who has owned a run of **[Matt Layden](/matt-layden/index.md) and Layden-influenced boats**. He bought the Paradox **"[Scout](/boats/scout.md)"** from **[Sean Mulligan](/people/sean-mulligan.md)** in 2015, becoming her third owner after **[Lezlie Henson](/people/lezlie-henson.md)** built her in Texas as "Tardis." He sailed her for years out of north Idaho and the Puget Sound before selling her on to Lee, of Maine, in 2024. He also owns an Enigma 460 named "Fantasma" and, since 2022, has lived and cruised aboard a much larger **[Hogfish Maximus](/similar-boat-designs/hogfish-maximus.md)** sharpie. "Scout"'s own build and ownership record is kept at [boats/scout.md](/boats/scout.md); this page covers Curtis himself. He is not to be confused with Curtis Clark, the Scottsdale, Arizona owner of "[Raffles](/boats/raffles.md)," whose first and last names invert his own.

## Owner of "Scout"

Curtis bought "Scout" from Mulligan in **March 2015**, first signing himself "S/V Scout" that same month, noting he had "recently purchased a Paradox," alongside the Enigma 460 he already sailed.[1] Under his ownership she was shown several times at the Port Townsend Wooden Boat Show, and in 2019 he judged her "a great boat in almost every way."[2] He rated her built to a very high standard, day-sailed her hard on **Priest Lake** in north Idaho, once knocked down by a gust though not past about 90 degrees, cruised the **Puget Sound** and camped aboard many nights; a refit he began later stalled when he moved aboard a much larger boat.[3] He sold her on to **Lee, of Maine**, in **August 2024**.[4]

## A fleet of Layden boats

Curtis also owns an **[Enigma](/matt-layden/enigma.md) 460** named **"Fantasma,"** a Layden design slightly larger than a Paradox but lighter in displacement, built in the same style as the smaller Enigma, with an interior he reckons roughly 42 inches by 7 feet; he has offered her for sale over several years and shown her at the Port Townsend Wooden Boat Festival.[5] Matt Layden himself inspected the Enigma in depth at Sail Oklahoma in 2014 and approved the build, in Curtis's own words, "quietly gave me the OK, saying it was well built."[6] By 2022 Curtis had, in his own phrase, graduated to a slightly bigger boat still: a roughly 30,000 lb **[Hogfish Maximus](/similar-boat-designs/hogfish-maximus.md)**, **[Chris Morejohn](/people/chris-morejohn.md)'s** big shoal-draft sharpie, which he calls basically a gigantic Paradox that beaches the same way.[7] He has lived aboard her since 2022, cruising by 2024 from the Pacific Northwest down to Southern California and Mexico.[8]
