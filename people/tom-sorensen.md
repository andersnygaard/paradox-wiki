# Tom Sorensen

**Tom Sorensen** built Jim Michalak's small **"Robbsboat"** design (by his own account possibly the first one completed) and a cambered-junk-rigged Marples 3m Sea Clipper trimaran he had also built and sailed for years.[1][2] He compared Robbsboat to Paradox as "essentially a poor (or lazy?) man's Paradox" with "many of the same virtues" and considered an actual Paradox for a later build.[1]

## Robbsboat as a comparison point

Robbsboat's build offered two running comparisons with Paradox: steam-bending and laminating her chine logs and gunnels,[3] and her ballast and hull shape set against Paradox's chine runners.[4] Full accounts: [construction/chine-log-vs-taped-seam.md](/construction/chine-log-vs-taped-seam.md#citations) and [chine-runners/how-they-work.md](/chine-runners/how-they-work.md#jim-michalaks-robbsboat-and-a-critique-of-his-view-of-chine-runners).

## Materials, systems and rig knowledge

His own boats gave him a practical grounding in wood choice (Douglas fir over hemlock),[5] a trawler's heater flue cleared through the hull with a ventilated thimble,[6] ballast tank plumbing (a deck-level fill valve, a breather tube against temperature swings and a diaphragm pump doubling as a bilge pump),[7] and acrylic windows bedded in plain latex caulk.[8] A cambered junk rig he built and sailed for years on his own trimaran gave him a basis to compare a junk conversion's sheet-twist control, load spread and reefing ease against the simplicity of lowering Paradox's stock roller-furling sail from the mast.[2][9] Full account: [design/ideas/junk-rig.md](/design/ideas/junk-rig.md).
