# Rodney Cannon

**Rodney Cannon** (Yahoo handle `rdnycannon`, signing "Rod") is a Paradox builder from **Venice, California**, a few blocks from the ocean, who grew up in **Kansas City** and spent his teenage years in Seattle.[1][43] A former professional magician who later ran a window-cleaning business and took up short-order cooking work, he introduced himself to the list on 23 June 2008, new to both boatbuilding and sailing itself, having just ordered Paradox plans over a competing "Black Skimmer" design.[2][3] Of three designs he weighed — Black Skimmer, Robbsboat, and Paradox — Paradox won out "because of a fluke": a fellow builder's account of Matt Layden's own story left him "all teary eyed," and, as a beginner, it offered far more information and a more supportive online builder community than Robbsboat did at the time.[4] He began construction on 7 August 2008, three months ahead of his own stated schedule.[5] His boat is not named anywhere in the archive itself; a later, external source — [Bill Serjeant's 2014 worldwide builders' census](/references/builders-and-owners-census-2014.md#the-table) — gives her name as **"Wizard Spawn,"** still listed as "in build" six years on.[6] By February 2012 he reported that build had come close to completion in California before a personal move to **New Orleans, Louisiana** forced him to abandon her unfinished, and that he had started building again there, finding the process easier the second time; see [boats/wizard-spawn.md — abandoned in California, and a fresh start in Louisiana](/boats/wizard-spawn.md#abandoned-in-california-and-a-fresh-start-in-louisiana-2012).[48]

## Building Wizard Spawn

New to sailing as well as building, Rodney prepared with ASA sailing classes, *Chapman Seamanship and Small Boat Handling*, *Learn to Sail in a Weekend*, and a paid copy of the *Virtual Sailor 7.01* simulator.[7] Building from a small one-car apartment in Venice Beach — a finished bulkhead in every closet, the spar hung from ceiling hooks, sanding done in the bathtub — he worked through every task he could complete before joining the hull, so that the day it was finished he could go straight sailing, and said he would not regret the process even if he never got to sail her.[8][9] A personal relocation reported at the end of 2008 would have taken him from Venice to Florida — first to the Tampa area, then narrowed to Fort Lauderdale — but by October 2009 the move had still not gone through, and he was continuing the build from the same apartment, encouraging a newcomer weighing a one-car-garage build with his own account of working every snag out via the plans and the archive.[10][11][12] Full construction account: [boats/wizard-spawn.md](/boats/wizard-spawn.md).

## Contributions and stories

- **Auxiliary power and cruising plans.** He took part in the list's early debate over Paradox's auxiliary-power options (see [operations/auxiliary-outboard-power.md](/operations/auxiliary-outboard-power.md#a-newcomers-hybrid-electric-proposal-and-the-aquasub-precedent)); his own cruising plans — coastal cruising down to Mexico, and later fresh-water lake and stream cruising inland — shaped a preference for keeping the built-in water tanks free of routine salt water, and he later pointed the list to a review of the Torqeedo electric outboard as a possible backup when the yuloh proves difficult to use. See also [construction/water-tank-lining.md](/construction/water-tank-lining.md).[13][14]
- **A ballast idea.** He floated old gym weight-lifting plates as a possible ballast source, unsure whether older plates were lead or cast iron and wondering whether high-school gyms or pawn shops might turn up some cheaply, amid concern that environmental restrictions were making lead an increasingly expensive component. See [design/weight-and-ballast.md](/design/weight-and-ballast.md#improvised-and-exotic-ballast-materials-gym-weights-sandbags-and-tungsten).[15]
- **A masthead-light idea.** He proposed making the masthead light plug into a removable socket, with a lightning rod similarly sleeved and secured by a butterfly screw for quick removal. See [construction/mast-sealing-and-fit.md](/construction/mast-sealing-and-fit.md#further-anchor-light-and-lightning-rod-length-fixes).[16]
- **Build techniques.** He built his own beam-laminating jigs after seeing another builder's design — a technique credited in turn on [boats/cachivache.md](/boats/cachivache.md#continued-progress-into-the-patagonian-winter) — fixed a missed bulkhead stiffening curve by extending the bulkhead's cleats over the panel's edge and filling the gap with epoxy rather than re-cutting the panel, and built the yard, boom, and yuloh with hand tools alone, planning several yulohs of differing blade curvature to compare in the community's debate over the right shape. See [components/yuloh.md](/components/yuloh.md#building-the-yuloh-itself-blade-curvature-and-the-pivot-boltsocket).[17][18][19][20][21][22]
- **Choosing a sail.** From Sailrite's two 2008 listings he chose the original Paradox sail pattern over the newer, taller-peaked one, favoring its easier furling and lower cost since he did not plan to race, and sewed the kit himself. See [components/rig/sail.md](/components/rig/sail.md#choosing-between-the-two-2008-sailrite-listings).[23][24][25]
- **A plans-revision discovery.** Investigating why his own plans showed the tack strop's spacer tube tied off with a plain knot while completed boats' photographs showed a forked top, he found that his paper and digital copies of the plans simply disagreed — the fork was a dated Sheet 9 revision, not a builder modification. See [components/rig/tack-strop.md](/components/rig/tack-strop.md#a-may-1994-plans-revision-explains-the-forked-vs-plain-spacer-tube).[26][27]
- **A proposed Paradox gathering, and a Hawaii passage.** He proposed a large, informal Paradox gathering — a "Paracon" — pairing the idea first with his own Hawaii sailing daydream and then with a time-share scheme offering his own boat and an arranged camping stay to builders who could not bring one of their own, and later formally proposed sailing his finished Paradox from Marina del Rey to Honolulu, resolving after the list's reply to build the boat and build his seamanship alongside it. Two years later he returned to the same Hawaii idea, by then declining to predict a finish date at all and describing a growing love for the craft of building itself. Answering a newcomer's own Atlantic-crossing hypothetical in 2011, judged Paradox could very likely make an ocean crossing given the right sailor, weather, and route, though "totally un-fun." See [merits/gatherings.md](/merits/gatherings.md#a-proposed-all-paradox-gathering-2010) and [design/blue-water-suitability.md](/design/blue-water-suitability.md#a-2008-marina-del-rey-to-honolulu-plan-and-the-answer-it-drew) (with its [2010 return](/design/blue-water-suitability.md#a-2010-return-to-the-marina-del-rey-to-honolulu-question) and [2011 recurrence](/design/blue-water-suitability.md#a-2011-recurrence-an-atlantic-crossing-hypothetical-self-righting-restated-to-120-degrees-and-a-former-faith-owners-own-appraisal)) for the fuller threads.[28][29][30][31][32][38][46][47]
- **A pedal-powered watermaker-and-battery idea.** In the same 2011 exchange, asked whether the pedal effort proposed for driving a watermaker could instead be switched, via a lever, to charge a battery. See [operations/auxiliary-outboard-power.md](/operations/auxiliary-outboard-power.md#a-watermaker-as-an-alternative-use-for-pedal-power).[45]
- **A second, unrelated boat.** While raising the funds needed to finish Wizard Spawn's hull glassing and finishing, delaying a hoped-for May 2010 launch, he began building a Yost-designed folding kayak, the "Sea Bee 13," from scrap wood. See [boats/wizard-spawn.md](/boats/wizard-spawn.md#a-funding-gap-delays-glassing-and-a-second-unrelated-boat-begins-april-2010).[37]
- **Faith in the design.** Nearing the end of his build, found a fellow owner's trust-the-designer testimonial reassuring given the controversy that surrounds the design, and reflected that he had chosen Paradox "on faith" — the word of actual owners rather than others' untested experience — finding the build easier than the folding kayak he built alongside it. See [boats/wizard-spawn.md](/boats/wizard-spawn.md#nearing-completion-and-a-reflection-on-building-on-faith-october-2010).[39]
- **A tribute to "Zoë."** When Glen Maxwell put "Zoë" up for sale in November 2010, he called her and Glen's own adventures in her "the driving force" behind his own choice to build a Paradox, judging the asking price about half what the boat was worth. See [boats/zoe.md](/boats/zoe.md#the-2010-sale).[41]
- **A research method for newcomers, and Glen Maxwell's personal website named.** Asked in the same thread for the "very informative website" behind that praise, set out in detail how he himself had prepared before starting his own build: downloaded the whole forum's own photographs and build information into folders on his hard drive, then searched the wider web for other Paradox builders' sites and downloaded what he found there too. Named Glen's own personal site directly, crediting it less for technical build detail than for the inspiration and stories linked from it, and pointed instead to the forum's own pic-by-pic construction logs as the real technical resource — the working method that got him unstuck being to keyword-search the archive first, then post and be helped despite, in his own words, "my crazy ramblings." See [boats/zoe.md — the 2010 sale](/boats/zoe.md#the-2010-sale).[42]
- **Design comparisons, and settling on Paradox for good.** He pointed a prospective South African builder to John Welsford's "Fafnir" as an alternative worth checking before deciding on Paradox — heavier, wider, and easier to build, but designed for genuine blue-water voyages. See [similar-boat-designs/comparisons.md](/similar-boat-designs/comparisons.md#john-welsfords-fafnir).[44] He compared Paradox to the Swaggie design as a bigger, distantly related alternative he might build himself in a few years, while sticking with Paradox for now; in a 2009 headroom debate he pointed out that Paradox's sliding hatch lets a sailor stand; and he separately guessed — correctly — that "Hogfish," seen only on a builder's website at the time, was a larger design sharing many of Paradox's own ideas. See [similar-boat-designs/comparisons.md](/similar-boat-designs/comparisons.md#john-welsfords-swaggie) and [similar-boat-designs/hogfish-maximus.md](/similar-boat-designs/hogfish-maximus.md).[33][34][35] Satisfied by a fuller explanation of why Paradox is self-righting where ordinary sharpies and dories are not, he summed up his choice of the design for a first build: "I knew I loved this boat, I just did not know why."[36] In an October 2010 exchange he named the Bolger Micro his favorite-looking boat in the whole category, having also considered and ruled out the larger Black Skimmer, but held that Paradox's build process better suited his own needs and that, while right for him, the design may not be the best boat for just anybody. See [Alternatives for a larger sailor — Bolger's Micro, Birdwatcher, and the "Adelie"](/similar-boat-designs/alternatives-for-a-larger-sailor.md#bolgers-micro-birdwatcher-and-the-adelie-alternatives-for-a-larger-sailor).[40]
