# Rodney Cannon

**Rodney Cannon**, who goes by "Rod," is a builder from **Los Angeles**. A former professional magician, he took up sailing and boatbuilding together in the summer of 2008.[1][2] He weighed Black Skimmer and Robbsboat against Paradox and chose Paradox after another builder's account of [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)'s own story drew him to the design.[3] He began construction that August, working from a one-car apartment near the beach with no workshop of his own, and kept at the boat through moves and setbacks for the next three and a half years, until a 2012 move to New Orleans ended the build there; starting over, he found the second [build](/boats/wizard-spawn.md) came easier than the first.[4] He never named the boat himself: an outside registry recorded her in 2008 as "Magic-Hat," and a 2014 census as "Wizard Spawn."[5][6] Full build account: [boats/wizard-spawn.md](/boats/wizard-spawn.md).

## From Kansas City to the Pacific coast

He grew up in **Kansas City** and spent his teenage years in **Seattle**, where he busked sleight of hand and took magic classes at a shop by **Pike Place Market**. He had gone there once before, as a boy leaving home for the first time, "to be a wizard," and by 2008 was weighing a return, having reconnected with old friends.[7][8]

As a beginner to both boatbuilding and sailing, he found Paradox came with far more information and a more supportive community than Robbsboat did at the time. He also weighed a Swaggie, John Welsford's larger, distantly related design, before settling on Paradox, and later judged Welsford's Fafnir, heavier and easier to build but meant for genuine blue-water voyages, a reasonable alternative for a newcomer to weigh; full comparison: [similar-boat-designs/comparisons.md](/similar-boat-designs/comparisons.md).[9][10]

## Building from a one-car apartment

New to sailing, Rodney enrolled that summer in ASA sailing classes, working through *Chapman Seamanship and Small Boat Handling* and *Learn to Sail in a Weekend* and a paid copy of the *Virtual Sailor 7.01* simulator to learn the basics.[11] His method for getting unstuck was to search first: he gathered a whole community's worth of photographs and build information from other builders' sites early on, and worked out most snags himself by studying the plans before seeking outside help.[12] He also planned his build in sequence, finishing every sub-assembly he could before joining the hull together, so that the day the hull itself was complete he could go straight sailing.[13]

The apartment shaped the build as much as any plan did. A finished bulkhead occupied every closet, the spar hung from hooks screwed into the ceiling, hand-sanding went on in the bathtub or bathroom at night and power-sanding and cutting were done out back by day. Whatever the boat's own eventual fate, he judged the project already worthwhile for everything it had taught him unrelated to sailing or boatbuilding themselves.[14] A funding gap in 2010 that delayed the hull's glassing sent him briefly to a parallel project, a Yost-designed folding kayak built from scrap wood while the Paradox waited on funds.[15] Life intervened more than once: a relocation to Florida planned at the end of 2008 never came through, and he was still building from the same Los Angeles apartment a year later.[16][17] Full build account, including the beam-laminating jigs, the bulkhead-curve fix and the mast and rudder questions worked through along the way: [boats/wizard-spawn.md](/boats/wizard-spawn.md).

## Starting over in New Orleans

Even before the hull was finished he pictured sailing her to Hawaii, and imagined a Paradox gathering there or in the Bahamas once she was built; full account: [merits/gatherings.md](/merits/gatherings.md).[18] By February 2012 the California hull had come close to completion when a move to New Orleans ended the build there. Starting over, he found the process easier the second time: he weighed a Fafnir again but chose Paradox once more, judging her shallow draft still the better fit for his own sailing conditions.[4]

Neither name given to his boat came from him. A "Magic-Hat" in 2008, and six years later a "Wizard Spawn": both fit a former professional magician who had once, as a boy, set off "to be a wizard."[5][6][7]
