# Darren (Victoria, BC)

**Darren** (Yahoo handle `dlesper`) is a prospective Paradox builder and sailing instructor in **Victoria, British Columbia**, active on the mailing list from September 2013. By then his build was in its late structural stages — about to install the sheer clamps — with a launch hoped for before the following summer. No boat name is on record.

## Build and rig

Replying to a fellow owner's canvass of the fleet's locations across North America, he reported his build's only real deviation from the plans: a **split lug sail**, drawn from Slieve McGalliard's AYRS "Catalyst 37" design, chosen for better pointing and more drive than the stock lug. He also planned beefier scantlings and spars than stock, for extended offshore work, and was keen to compare the split lug's performance directly against a "stock" rig.[1]

## A planned Vancouver Island circumnavigation

He set out a two-summer plan: launch and test the boat's limits the first summer, then circumnavigate **Vancouver Island** — roughly 1,000 nautical miles — the summer after, recommending some offshore experience for that attempt. As a sailing instructor himself, he offered to teach along the way to anyone willing to crew.[1]

## Community connections

One of his sailing partners is francophone and an experienced sailor, offered as a point of connection to a fellow correspondent building far away in Québec.[1]

## A height increase to the hull and cabin (September 2014)

A year on, the build had progressed enough to test raising the whole structure for extra headroom: 50 mm added to the hull height throughout with no structural issues, and a further 50 mm planned for the cabin top specifically — the only real compromise a slightly narrower top, since the new pieces are sized from the work already in place. See [crew size and fit — a 2014 exchange](/operations/crew-size-and-fit.md#a-2014-exchange-the-boom-mast-and-hatch-consequences-of-raising-the-cabin) for the wider interdependency discussion this data point informs.[2]

## A 2015 tack-furler assembly question

By April 2015 he was fabricating the tack/furler assembly and asked the wider group why the plans' swivel rod is split where it meets the spacer pipe, weighing whether to skip the split and bolt the rod straight through instead. Full technical exchange at [components/rig/tack-strop.md](/components/rig/tack-strop.md#a-2015-exchange-why-the-fork-helps-pivot-bolt-orientation-and-a-caution-against-over-modifying-it).[3]
