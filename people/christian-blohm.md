# Christian Blohm

**Christian Blohm** is a builder in **north-east Ohio**, USA. He weighed a Wharram Tiki 21 against a [Paradox](/paradox.md), judging two hulls impractical to take on at once, and settled on the single hull; a set of plans ordered about seven years earlier had gone missing, so he reordered one from Dave Bolduc together with Don Elliott's building guide and cleared his garage for a start in January 2023.[1] His hull, later named **"Rebel,"**[2] progressed through the deck beams, bulkheads and side panels in a careful, methodical first build that has run in fits and starts and remained half-finished as of 2026.[3]

## Preparing before the first cut

He gathered marine plywood and epoxy to the plans' own materials list[4] and spent three months quietly preparing before making his first cuts on 26 February 2023, setting up a workshop and reading earlier builders' discussions and general wooden-boat-building references; a jig cut all 42 strips needed for the six laminated deck beams, bought alongside his first tablesaw, a SawStop CTS.[5] The first beam was laminated with the whole sequence rehearsed in advance from a Don Casey book and the strips marked in order beforehand, a level of preparation that held even when an old, still-sealed box of nitrile gloves fell apart mid-glue-up and the lamination had to be finished bare-handed (see [hand protection](/construction/epoxy-safety-and-ppe.md#hand-protection) for the same lesson from another builder's own gloves).[6] Within two weeks all six beams were complete and the first two bulkheads cut, the beams fitting both bulkhead curves "spot on" on a first attempt at transferring plans to plywood.[7] The pace was set by a seven-days-on, seven-days-off work schedule that kept him away from home on his weeks on, giving him about 24 days of building at five hours a session by late April.[8] Sail-cloth durability was weighed the same way, against a plan to sail the Great Lakes and, eventually, the Florida Keys and Bahamas for about 40 days a season; see [sail cloth weight and cost](/components/rig/sail.md#sail-pricing) for how the option he priced compares with other builders' choices.[9]

## Side panels and scarfing

Rather than laying out the second side panel from scratch as the build manual directs, he traced it from the first: the cut panel screwed to the next sheet of plywood and copied with a router, a small jig transferring the bulkhead locations across to the second side; the full technique is at [panel layout: copying one side panel to its mate](/construction/panel-layout.md#layout-tips).[10] The same discipline of practicing before committing carried into the scarf joints: waste stock took the first cuts with a new hand planer,[11] and once freehand planing proved not accurate enough, two side jigs built from a near-perfect practice piece guided the planer for the real panels, starting with the port forward side; full account at [scarf joints: a router jig for repeated scarf cuts](/construction/scarf-joints.md#a-router-jig-for-repeated-scarf-cuts).[12]

## Building in fits and starts

Progress ran in fits and starts. Resuming after roughly a year's hiatus in August 2024, he finished the stem and aligned the panels for glue-up, and gave the hull her name, **"Rebel," as in resisting generally accepted convention.**[2] As of an April 2026 comment on an unrelated post, the hull was still half-built, and he was looking for the motivation to finish it.[3]
