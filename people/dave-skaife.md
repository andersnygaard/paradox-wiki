# Dave Skaife

**Dave Skaife**, an American builder, built a [Paradox](/paradox.md) from scratch in Hawaii and sailed her engine-free across Hawaii, the Gulf Intracoastal Waterway, the Bahamas twice and Cuba between 2014 and 2018, selling her from Pensacola, Florida at the end of that run. He never named her, calling her simply his Paradox; under a later owner she became **"[Paw Paw](/boats/paw-paw.md)."** He documented the whole build and voyage on a personal blog, `daveskaife.wordpress.com`, preserved as a source at [references/dave-skaife-blog.md](/references/dave-skaife-blog.md). Full build and ownership record: [boats/paw-paw.md](/boats/paw-paw.md).

## Why he built a Paradox

Dave came to the design moving down, not up. He had a 36 ft monohull that he almost always sailed solo, and found himself maintaining and pushing around far more boat than he needed; he wanted something small, simple and trailerable. Looking through many designs, he found a widely circulated sketch by [Don Elliott](/people/don-elliott.md) that caught him at once: it was the kind of sailing he wanted, which he learned was called microcruising. Not wanting to build from scratch but finding it the only way to get one, he ordered the plans through [microcruising.com](/references/microcruising-website.md).[1]

He judged the plans packed with detail, almost overwhelming, and warned that a Paradox takes much longer to build than a normal fourteen-footer because of everything built into it, worth the extra effort in the end. He called [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md) "an evil genius when it comes to boat designs, he thinks so far outside the box that you forget there was a box." The boat, soon after launch, had already exceeded his expectations.[1]

## Building in Hawaii

He built the boat from scratch, blogging the tail end of construction from December 2013. His first entry set the tone for the outfitting that followed: a tiny bamboo drip-guide fitted under the cabin windows to catch condensation and lead it to a sponge in the corner, an easy upgrade to make before the windows and paint went on, and, in his own words, "probably one of those times when I over think something."[2] Through the build and first season he worked out his electrical system, fender and anchor storage, dual-purpose running lights, a hatch lock and multipurpose water-storage buckets, small, considered choices that carry through the rest of the blog. She was built trailerable and with fishing in mind, a plan settled on once Hawaii changed its mooring-permit fee structure; a cheap trailer and hardware were sourced early on eBay to spread the build's cost.[3]

He weighed a scavenged length of mast tubing against the plans' wooden mast and gave an aluminum tube a try, an experiment engineered in full at [components/rig/mast.md](/components/rig/mast.md#aluminum-as-a-mast-material-engineering-fit-not-just-substitution).[4] For ground tackle he leaned toward a 5 kg Bruce anchor over a 2 kg one and loaded up on chain in place of some of the lead ballast, reasoning that chain, unlike dead lead, still earns its keep; the fuller anchor discussion his and other builders' choices fed into is at [operations/ground-tackle.md](/operations/ground-tackle.md#anchors-builders-favor).[5] He leaned on Don Elliott's construction guide at every stage of the build, alongside pictures from other builders' blogs, and found Don's own drawings of the boat in use inspiring, an odd thing for him to admit.[6] Sailing in warmer water than most Paradox owners, he fitted a fan inside the vent box for when the hatch had to stay closed and a lip along the vent baffle to keep spray out of the stern compartment, a lesson carried over from an earlier small boat of his own; full account: [components/vent-box.md](/components/vent-box.md) and [components/vent-baffle.md](/components/vent-baffle.md).[7]

## Four seasons under sail and oar

The boat was motorless throughout: Dave sculled and sailed her through locks and calms as well as open water, across four seasons from 2014 to 2018 that took her through Hawaii, the Gulf Intracoastal Waterway, the Bahamas twice and Cuba, an unusually wide-ranging record of engine-free Paradox cruising.[8] Full account, season by season: [boats/paw-paw.md](/boats/paw-paw.md#sailing-record).

## Comfortable microcruising

Between seasons he published a deliberate twelve-post "comfortable microcruising" series, his own thinking on how to make a fourteen-foot Paradox genuinely liveable: ventilation, a padded interior, dressing for the conditions, window covers, a personal environment management system and the balance of sentiment against minimalism aboard a very small boat. Alongside it he ran a review series covering the gear he favored, from an anchor light to a camp stove.[9]

## The boat's later life

He offered the boat for sale from Pensacola in 2017 as he moved on to overland travel in Southeast Asia, and she sold in March 2018, the proceeds earmarked for further adventures.[10][11] The only name he gave her in his own hands survives from a direct 2017 inquiry: on paperwork alone, he entered her as Viking funeral.[12] Once the sale went through, he began looking for a lighter, secondhand [Elusion](/matt-layden/elusion.md) or [Enigma](/matt-layden/enigma.md) to replace her.[13] Full record of her later ownership and name: [boats/paw-paw.md](/boats/paw-paw.md).
