# Kjell Karlsson

**Kjell Karlsson**, of Sweden's Stockholm archipelago, is the only person on record to build a [Paradox](/paradox.md) to Sven Yrvind's own "BRIS" composite method rather than plywood, a project under way since 2003.[1] A personal acquaintance of [Sven Yrvind](/people/yrvind.md) since the mid-1970s, and one of only two people in this record known to have corresponded with Yrvind directly about his building philosophy (the other being [William H. Longyard](/people/william-longyard.md), author of *A Speck on the Sea*), he had already spent a decade on a stalled "BRIS"-method dinghy project when he learned of [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)'s designs through Yrvind in 1998, and obtained the Paradox plans directly from Matt around that time.[1] An introduction arranged for the following year, when his family holidayed in Florida, fell through when wildfires sent them north instead, and the meeting he still regrets never took place.[1] He had also sketched his own two-person, scaled-up version of the boat; shown to Yrvind in person, it drew a gentle warning that the result would be "a 50% heavier and quite another boat," so he built to Matt Layden's stock plans instead, while keeping the BRIS composite material rather than switching to plywood, a choice "blessed by Sven and through Sven also by Matt."[1] The boat, nicknamed **"Paraplastic"** and documented in full at [the "BRIS Paradox"](/boats/bris-paradox.md), is now owned and sailed by [Johan Bloom](/people/johan-bloom.md) on Sweden's west coast as **"Tetris."**

## A few laws of nature

Kjell frames his approach to building with five half-joking maxims: big boats bring big trouble and small boats small trouble; wood and water don't mix; aluminum and human flesh don't mix; plastic is fantastic; and water should stay outside the boat.[1] He describes himself as having "no intentions to be a constructor nor designer," and treats Yrvind, trained in chemistry, as a "guru."[2] What drew him to Paradox in the first place was less a wish to sail than a shared interest in Layden and Yrvind as free-thinking designers who publish open, non-proprietary solutions rather than patenting or selling their work.[3] During one shop visit, Yrvind inspected Kjell's completed bulkheads, pronounced them too heavy, and left the verdict in coarse ink on the wall: "TÄNK PÅ VIKTEN, Yrvind" ("Think of the weight").[1]

Worried at one point that his own posts had taken on "a slight Sweden-chauvinistic odour," he traced the feeling to the BRIS method's exacting, hands-on standards.[4] While puzzling over the plans he preferred an outdated office chair and, having found music distracting, worked instead to two CDs of Swedish birdsong.[5] On seeing [Gunther Battista](/people/gunther-battista.md)'s cost-minimizing project, he suggested comparing the two boats later for functional differences, since his own considerably more resource-intensive composite build was pursuing the opposite approach.[6] At 56, with the boat expected ready to sail that summer, he offered his own build as a case for starting a Paradox later in life.[7]

## Translating a friend's book

Answering a standing wish that someone translate Yrvind's new Swedish-language book into English, Kjell translated its Matt Layden/Paradox chapter and released it as "chapter9.doc," with the author's own permission and good wishes.[8] He flagged himself a "highly unauthorized" translator and invited corrections to any factual slips.[8] After a further conversation with Yrvind he circulated a revised translation, "Chapter9rev," correcting and clarifying his own earlier work.[9] A wider community push followed to crowd-translate the whole book, but it closed without a volunteer effort: Yrvind was willing to see the already-translated Paradox chapter offered to a publisher as a sample, but wanted a professional translator, not volunteers, for the rest.[10][11][12] Full saga at [Sven Yrvind's book](/references/yrvind-book.md).

## Sailing again, after twenty years

Kjell's first actual sail in the BRIS Paradox came after **21 years** without having sailed anything at all, single-handed on a nearby lake: "such a long time since I sailed last... so I don't really know what I expected."[13]

He held a long-standing, half-serious ambition to sail to the Baltic states, particularly Estonia, tracing it to a failed attempt some thirty years earlier to reach Leningrad that Soviet authorities refused him permission for; before World War II, he recalled, the Estonian coast had been a popular Swedish beach-holiday destination, cut off entirely once the Iron Curtain fell.[14] A short sail one gusty weekend went worse than expected, drifting the boat into reeds and buckling a trailer fender on the way out; Kjell closed the account wryly, joking that he might "start collecting stamps" instead.[15]

## Later ownership

The Paradox Karlsson built is now owned and sailed year-round out of Sweden's Bohuslän fjords by [Johan Bloom](/people/johan-bloom.md) as **"Tetris,"** identified as the same hull by matching Divinycell scantlings and the shared "Paraplastic" nickname.[16][17] Full account at [the "BRIS Paradox"](/boats/bris-paradox.md#later-ownership-johan-blooms-tetris).
