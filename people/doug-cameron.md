# Doug Cameron

**Doug Cameron** is a sailor, paddler and small-boat writer from **Sewanee, Tennessee**, who races WaterTribe events under the name **Ridgerunner** and has written for *Small Craft Advisor* and *Messing About in Boats*. He has sailed eleven Everglades Challenges and several other WaterTribe races by 2017, mostly in a **Core Sound 20** cat ketch he built for the 2007 Everglades Challenge to a design by Graham Byrnes, and keeps a blog, [Sailing and Paddling with Doug](/references/capt-doug-blog.md).[1][2] He is not a Paradox builder, owner or sailor. He is the author of the *Small Craft Advisor* profile of [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md), and racing the same events put him on beaches and at finish lines where he saw Layden's boats firsthand.

## Racing under WaterTribe

His regular racing teammate is **Michael Collins**, tribe name Greybeard; the two won the 2004 Everglades Challenge outright and set a course record, sailing a Kruger Cruiser tandem canoe under a schooner rig.[1] The Core Sound 20 was in her fourth Challenge by 2014, Cameron's eighth, with a knockdown and dismasting in 2012 behind her.[2] A Core Sound 20 Mark III prototype followed in 2014 to 2015, and in 2017 a pedal-and-paddle **Waterlust sailing canoe**, a Dillon Majoros design built from a CNC-cut kit, chosen after years of trying Kruger canoes, Core Sounds, a Gig Harbor Melonseed and a Hobie Adventure Island in search of a boat that would both sail and paddle well.[1] His best finish in the Core Sound is the 2010 Everglades Challenge, in 2 days 20 hours and 43 minutes, fourth overall on a late penalty against an earlier finisher and second in class, a personal best that WaterTribe marked that year with its Ancient Mariner award for long membership.[3] He withdrew from the inaugural North Carolina Challenge in 2009 on its second morning, judging the conditions around Harkers Island beyond what an open, unsheltered boat should take on.[4]

## Writing about Matt Layden

Cameron met Layden repeatedly at the small-boat gathering held every first weekend of May on **Atsena Otie**, the island across the channel from Cedar Key, and over supper at Key Largo during a WaterTribe race. His profile of Layden ran in *Small Craft Advisor*'s May/June 2009 issue and on his own blog, recording an evening in conversation with trimaran designer Jim Brown and closing with his verdict on Layden: **"At worst, Matt Layden is an eccentric; at best he is a genius."**[5] Full account: [Matt Layden's biography](/matt-layden/biography.md).

## An outside eyewitness

Because he raced the same events, his blog and his writing on the [messing-about.com forums](/references/messing-about-forum.md) preserve sightings of Layden's boats found nowhere else. [Dave Bolduc](/people/bolduc/dave.md), sailing [Enigma](/matt-layden/enigma.md), passed Doug's anchored boat during the 2009 North Carolina Challenge and was seen beating the beaches the next morning;[4] the 2010 Everglades Challenge starting beach held four of Layden's craft at once, including his new [Elusion](/matt-layden/elusion.md), by Cameron's count;[3] and his captioned finish-line photographs supply the placing, Wizard third, that closes [the 2010 Ultimate Florida Challenge](/merits/ultimate-florida-challenge-2010.md#the-finish-manitou-cruiser-whitecaps-and-wizard-third).[6] Layden's own EC2007 announcement of [Sand Flea](/matt-layden/sand-flea.md#matt-laydens-own-announcement-of-sand-flea-february-2007), forwarded verbatim from the WaterTribe discussion board, survives in his writing on messing-about.com;[7] so does a description of Enigma's portaging axle;[8] and so does a head-to-head between Layden sculling a Paradox on a yuloh and another boat under oars, with Cameron at the oars of a longer, heavier Sea Pearl he could not keep pace with.[9]

He was present for everything described here, but his blog is a participant's recollection rather than a results record and is loose in a couple of identifiable places. The full assessment of it as a source is at [its limits, on the capt-doug-blog.md reference page](/references/capt-doug-blog.md#its-limits).
