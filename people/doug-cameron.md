# Doug Cameron

**Doug Cameron** is a sailor and paddler from **Sewanee, Tennessee**, who races under the WaterTribe name **Ridgerunner** and writes about small boats for *Small Craft Advisor* and *Messing About in Boats*. He is not a Paradox builder, owner, or sailor. He appears in this archive because he wrote the **profile of [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)** that ran alongside Matt's *Small Craft Advisor* interview in May/June 2009 — the single densest biographical account of the designer the bundle holds — and because his own blog, [Sailing and Paddling with Doug](/references/capt-doug-blog.md), records what he saw of Matt and his boats on beaches the mailing list could only watch from a distance.

He is, in other words, one of the archive's few **eyewitness outsiders**: close enough to the WaterTribe world to be on the same start line, independent enough of the Paradox community to have no stake in what the boats are supposed to do.

## His own boats and racing

Doug's long-serving race boat is a **Core Sound 20** cat ketch that he built himself, to a design by **Graham Byrnes** — a designer with his own WaterTribe record, described by Doug as a multiple Everglades Challenge and North Carolina Challenge winner. He built her for the 2007 Everglades Challenge and was still racing her in 2014, by which time she had four Challenges of her own behind her.[7] He went on to build a **Core Sound 20 Mark III** prototype (2014–2015) and, in 2017, a **CLC Waterlust sailing canoe** from a CNC-cut kit — a Dillon Majoros design, pedal-driven by a Hobie drive, chosen after years of trying Kruger canoes, Core Sounds, a Gig Harbor Melonseed and a Hobie Adventure Island in search of a boat that would both sail and paddle.[8] His regular racing partner is **Michael Collins**, tribe name Greybeard.

He had sailed his first Everglades Challenge by **2003**, counted his eighth in **2014**, and by 2017 put the total at **eleven**, alongside several other WaterTribe events.[1][7][8] He and Collins **won the 2004 Everglades Challenge outright, setting a course record**, sailing a Kruger Cruiser tandem canoe under a schooner rig.[8] His best result in the Core Sound is the **2010 Everglades Challenge**: 2 days 20 hours 43 minutes, fifth overall — later corrected to fourth when a penalty was assessed against an earlier finisher — and second in class, which he called a personal best.[1] The pair were given that year's "Ancient Mariner / Old Man and the Sea Award," for having been tribe members long enough that newer entrants saw them as dinosaurs.[1] The 2012 Challenge cost him a knockdown and a dismasting.[7] He withdrew from the inaugural **North Carolina Challenge** in September 2009 on the second morning, judging the conditions around Harkers Island beyond what he wanted to take on in an open-cockpit boat with no shelter on that side of the island.[2]

## The Matt Layden profile

The profile is treated in full at [Matt Layden — biography](/matt-layden/biography.md), where its content is drawn on throughout. What matters here is how it came to be written. Doug had met Matt repeatedly at the annual first-weekend-of-May small-boat gathering on **Atsena Otie**, the uninhabited island across the channel from Cedar Key, Florida, and had talked with him over supper at Key Largo during a break in a WaterTribe race around Florida.[3] The profile's method is observational rather than interrogative: it watches Matt strike up a conversation about design with **Jim Brown**, the WindRider trimaran designer, work through short-canoe-paddle propulsion, leg-powered boats and an improved tacking yuloh with him at an evening cookout, and concludes that both men were "informed by that knowledge rather than limited by it."[3]

Its most-quoted line is Doug's own verdict: **"At worse, Matt Layden is an eccentric; at best he is a genius."**[3] The blog copy — the version that survives — breaks off mid-sentence at "but amazed," and no complete text has been found.

## What he witnessed

Four separate sightings, each of them the archive's only outside record of its subject.

- **The 2009 North Carolina Challenge.** Anchored for the night in Taylor Creek, Doug recorded that "Freebyrd's Matt Layden-designed Enigma passed us during the night," and next morning saw [Dave Bolduc](/people/bolduc/dave.md) "beating close to the beaches" in 20–25 knots around Harkers Island.[2] Bolduc won his class in that race; his own account of it is the bundle's other source, and this is the independent one.
- **The 2010 Everglades Challenge starting beach.** Surveying the fleet at Fort DeSoto, Doug catalogued **four Layden-designed craft on the same sand**: [Elusion](/matt-layden/elusion.md) ("his new Elusion, a 9 foot cruiser"), [Enigma](/matt-layden/enigma.md) under her new owner Bolduc, the **Tridarka Raider** under a new owner with a bigger sail, and a Layden-designed **expedition sailboard** sailed by Windwalker (Trey Flemer).[4]
- **The finish of the 2010 Ultimate Florida Challenge.** His captioned photographs give the placings — Manitou Cruiser first, Whitecaps second, **Wizard (Matt Layden) third** — that the mailing list, which followed that race hour by hour for eight days, never reported.[5] See [The 2010 Ultimate Florida Challenge](/merits/ultimate-florida-challenge-2010.md#the-finish-manitou-cruiser-whitecaps-and-wizard-third).
- **Robb White.** His account of the boatbuilder and author Robb White's last visit to Sewanee, shortly before White's death, closes with a line that places Matt in the community rather than above it: "(Matt Layden suggested that I share this tale.)"[6]

## On the messing-about.com forums (2007–2009)

Beyond the blog and the magazine profile, Doug is a recurring poster on the [messing-about.com forums](/references/messing-about-forum.md) — the B&B Yacht Designs builder community, where he was building and racing a Graham Byrnes design. The identification is secure on several agreeing signals: the name, the self-built Core Sound 20 for the Everglades Challenge, the WaterTribe racing, and the same designer's shop. **It is a distinct venue from the magazine he writes for**; see [messing-about.com forums — not to be confused with *Messing About In Boats*](/references/messing-about-forum.md#not-to-be-confused-with-messing-about-in-boats).

Three of his forum posts are the archive's source for material found nowhere else:

- **He relayed Matt Layden's own EC2007 announcement**, forwarded verbatim from the WaterTribe discussion board in February 2007 — the passage in which Layden announces [Sand Flea](/matt-layden/sand-flea.md#matt-laydens-own-announcement-of-sand-flea-february-2007) and gives her particulars. Doug is therefore the conduit for one of the very few pieces of direct Layden text in this bundle.[9]
- **His summary of Layden as a competitor**, in the same thread, supplies both the West Marine inflatable story and the honest limit — that downwind or reaching in strong wind Layden could not stay with Byrnes's boats. See [the Everglades Challenge](/merits/everglades-challenge.md#how-the-win-read-to-the-rest-of-the-fleet).[9]
- **The yuloh-versus-oars comparison** in which he could not keep up with Layden sculling a Paradox while rowing a longer, heavier Sea Pearl — the only head-to-head of its kind on record.[10] See [sculling and yuloh technique](/operations/sculling-and-yuloh-technique.md#a-yuloh-against-oars-comparison-from-the-boat-that-lost-it).

He also supplied the concrete detail of **[Enigma](/matt-layden/enigma.md#racing-history-and-change-of-ownership)'s portaging axle** — mounted through the chine wings, carrying pram wheels — while working out how to portage his own boat over the same forty-mile stretch.[11]

## How reliable he is

Doug was present for everything he reports here, which is more than can be said of any other source on these events, and the bundle treats him accordingly. But his blog is a participant's recollection written up afterwards, not a record of results, and it is loose in two documented places: he calls the Tridarka Raider a *catamaran* where the archive otherwise has it as a **trimaran** designed in collaboration with WaterTribe founder Steve Isaac,[4] and the finishing-photograph post is titled "at Cedar Key Checkpoint" while its own captions place the pictures at the finish at Fort DeSoto.[5] Details of rig type, placing, and location taken from him are worth a second source where one exists. The full assessment is at [references/capt-doug-blog.md — its limits](/references/capt-doug-blog.md#its-limits).
