# Jack Brubaker

**Jack Brubaker** is a builder from **Bloomington, Indiana**, who began building a Paradox in 2015 and enlarged her from the stock 14 ft to **18 ft**. The extra length and a reshaped hull made room forward of the mast for a composting head and general storage, and for a combined foredeck hatch and anchor locker of his own design.

## Building an 18 ft enlarged Paradox

Stretching the hull gave him room forward of the mast for a composting head and general storage. Into that space he built a foredeck hatch combined with an in-deck anchor locker, meant to let anchoring and bow-line handling be done standing in safety rather than crouched on deck; splash rails around the hatch and rubber seals against which it closes keep most water out, and he expects the hatch to help ventilation in dry weather.[1] Full account of the debate this answered, including a contrasting built solution from another builder: [components/hatch.md: a foredeck hatch, revisited (2016)](/components/hatch.md#a-foredeck-hatch-revisited-2016-access-and-ventilation-against-dryness-and-a-solar-panel). The same combination had been proposed, unbuilt, over a decade earlier as a way to keep ground tackle out of the accommodations without losing visibility forward; see [ground tackle and anchoring: a flush anchor well](/operations/ground-tackle.md#a-flush-anchor-well-weight-distribution-and-drainage).

Rather than a uniform scale-up of the stock lines, the hull was reshaped a bit wider, fuller at the transom and deeper from deck to bottom, fitting ballast tanks into the sides with foot room left between them for a more comfortable sitting position. He drew the enlarged plan himself and lofted it to work out his own bulkheads, building the hull on a strongback, fitting the plywood panel by panel straight off the frame rather than from a pre-drawn cutting layout.[2] Full account of the fit-up technique: [construction/panel-layout.md](/construction/panel-layout.md#layout-tips). Her build reached about a year and a half along by November 2016, with launch expected the following spring and a blog under way to record it. Full account of the enlargement, including the wider debate over scaling Paradox at all: [design/larger-paradox.md](/design/larger-paradox.md).

## Approach

Building in the American Midwest, where summer winds run light, he chose to maximize sail area with the Mk2 rig rather than the smaller sail a stronger-wind sailor might prefer, expecting to reef early much of the time but wanting all available power when the wind and water go flat; he found an intellectual challenge in nursing headway out of light air.[3] On his own rudder he protected the leading edge with a salvaged piece of half-round bronze from a former dinghy's bow fitting, screwed on with the voids filled in thickened epoxy.[4]
