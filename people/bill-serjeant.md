# Bill Serjeant

**Bill Serjeant** is a small-boat sailor from **Essex, England**, on the **River Crouch**. He received the Paradox plans in October 2003 and spent the next five and a half years running a UK Paradox web presence and its closest thing to a build registry, until the site closed in 2009.[1] He built his own Paradox, **"[Faith](/boats/faith.md)"**, to the stock plans over about two years, launched her at Burnham-on-Crouch in June 2007, cruised her along England's south coast to the Isles of Scilly and sold her in 2009. He later took on a second Paradox, kept the name **"[Minnow](/boats/enuf.md)"** she already carried, refitted her and sold her on in turn. He also kept the build-and-cruise blog **Bill's Log**. Full build account: [boats/faith.md](/boats/faith.md).

## The UK web presence and registry

After receiving the plans in October 2003, Bill added a Paradox section to his existing site, `www.smallsailboats.co.uk`, then split it into a dedicated `www.paradox-uk.co.uk`, giving the design's UK builders a home-country counterpart to microcruising.com, with a companion discussion group alongside it.[1] For roughly five and a half years he kept the site's closest thing to a build registry, a running list of UK and overseas Paradoxes kept up to date as each builder's boat became known, with a standing invitation to visit and see Faith.[2] The site closed at the end of March 2009, after three months' notice; the community fell back on the companion discussion group, and Pete Martin kept a personal copy of its content.[3] Full detail, including the site's sailmaker recommendations and its role relaying the designer's own contact address, is at [references/paradox-uk-website.md](/references/paradox-uk-website.md).

## Building and launching Faith

Already an experienced small-boat sailor with a boat he called "Bumper," aboard which he crossed paths with Alastair Law watching the start of the OSTAR at Plymouth in 2005, Bill ordered his Paradox timber from Robbins of Bristol that August[4][5] and spent about two years building her, needing neither a table saw nor a bandsaw: an ordinary rip saw, hand planing and an electric jigsaw did the work, with the clamp the one truly indispensable tool.[6] Weighing about eighty candidate names, from Braveheart and Crusader to Mudlark and Xanadu, he settled in March 2006 on "Faith": "I require faith more than anything to make my dream of ownership a reality."[7] She was launched at Burnham-on-Crouch in June 2007.[8] Bill credited the loan of Alastair Law's own "Little Jim" for an early sail and Matt Layden's designs and research with making the build possible, and expected Faith to be exactly the right retirement boat.[9] Full build record: [boats/faith.md](/boats/faith.md).

## Cruising Faith

A member of the Dinghy Cruising Association, Bill cruised Faith along England's south coast to the Isles of Scilly in 2008, an account that appeared in the DCA's own bulletin that October; the full text is at [merits/faith-scilly-cruise.md](/merits/faith-scilly-cruise.md).[10] Falling overboard from Faith's foredeck during a 2007 Dinghy Cruising Association meeting at Cobnor, he reboarded using the boarding step built into the rudder stock and wore a buoyancy aid rather than a lifejacket ever after, judging it easier to swim in even though a lifejacket would right an unconscious wearer that a buoyancy aid will not.[11][12] After two full seasons he found nothing he would change: sailing from inside, protected from the weather, suited him best.[13] Selling her still weighed on him: years later, weighing whether to part with a second Paradox too, he judged Faith "the only boat I've regretted selling."[14] Her later ownership is recorded at [boats/faith.md](/boats/faith.md#sold-in-2009-the-forepeak-reworked-a-higher-peaked-sail-and-the-yuloh-reshaped).

## Close to the stock plan

Bill held that Paradox suits a sailor built close to Matt Layden's own frame, giving his own figures, 5 ft 8 in and 149 lb, as about right for the boat, and that a considerably taller, heavier or less agile sailor is better served by a different design than by modifying this one.[15] He built and sailed Faith close to the stock plans, considering a sufficiently altered boat a Paradox derivative rather than a modified Paradox.[16] His own Wharram Hina catamaran, once knocked flat by a sudden, freak gust on an otherwise calm afternoon, was behind that caution even for waters he judged sheltered.[17]

## A lifetime of small boats

Self-steering interested Bill across several boats and many years, not only Faith. He ran a Hasler SP90 windvane in 1974 that worked well downwind with only slight meandering[18] and later fitted a QME vane, cheap and popular though reliable only on an exceptionally well-balanced boat, to his own Hunter 19.[19] For Faith herself he settled on a simple electronic autopilot, judging himself unlikely ever to attempt long ocean passages.[20]

Bill did not stop sailing small boats after Faith. Within three years he had taken on a "C" Type West Wight Potter and logged a 66-day cruise along England's south coast in 2013.[21] An outside weblog covering his travels that year noted a long-standing habit: taking particular pleasure in changing boats regularly, often building the smaller ones himself.[22]

## A second Paradox, Minnow

In August 2013 Bill took delivery of Derek Clark's old hull, **"[Minnow](/boats/enuf.md)"** (built and first sailed as "Enuf"), renamed by an intervening Norwegian owner. He kept the name through his own ownership rather than reverting it and worked through a leak at the mast step, a rough cabin top and a polystyrene buoyancy fill he found dangerously flammable and removed entirely.[23] He put her up for sale in 2014 and sold her on the following year. Full record of the acquisition, the leak and the refit: [boats/enuf.md](/boats/enuf.md#second-owner-in-norway-and-delivery-to-bill-serjeant-2013).

Days after taking her on, he passed his own chine-log bending technique to a newcomer starting a build in Sweden, recalling his own difficulty bending and clamping Faith's chine logs years before.[24]
