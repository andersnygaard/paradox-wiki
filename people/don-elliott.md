# Don Elliott

**Don Elliott** (also spelled "Elliot"), of Tomah, Wisconsin, was one of the earliest Paradox builders and — as of mid-2001 — one of only two people known to have a sailing Paradox, the other being [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)'s own boat.[1] He built his own heavily built Paradox, authored the **"Building the Paradox"** construction guide, and served as a primary technical resource for builders working from Matt Layden's plans, insisting on strict fidelity to the plans as drawn. He died on 2 December 2008, after a lengthy battle with cancer.[2][3]

## What he built and authored

Don's **"Building the Paradox"** series ran as a **sixteen-installment feature** in *Messing About In Boats* (vol. 14 nos. 9-24, September 1996 – May 1997) — Parts 1-15 plus a Conclusion — and included a valued jig for building the coach roof.[1] One early list post referenced only the series' first five installments (issues 9-13).[1b] For years the guide was sold only directly from Don, mailed from his Tomah, Wisconsin address on payment.[1c][4] A 2003 print run of the booklet had some copies missing a chapter; Don offered replacement pages directly to affected buyers.[5][6] See [Building the Paradox articles](/references/building-the-paradox-articles.md) and [the series' construction content](/references/building-paradox-series.md).

Beyond the technical articles, Don wrote a two-part essay, **"A Story, a True One,"** analyzing why Matt Layden's Paradox — the smallest, stubbiest, and only fully enclosed boat in the fleet — beat lighter, faster kayaks, dinghies, and multihulls over the 304-mile 2003 Everglades Challenge, crediting a weatherproof, sail-from-within cabin (letting its skipper rest and hold a course in any weather without exposure to cold, spray, or rain) as the decisive advantage over open, human- or wind-powered competitors, and concluding that the design's unconventional concept had been proven sound. He allowed a fellow builder to republish the essay years later on condition it not be edited.[7]

His own boat went formally unnamed on the list for roughly two years, despite references to "Elliott's Paradox," until a UK builder's website photos led to the name **The Edge** — see [The Edge](/boats/edge.md#the-name) for the full record.

## Contributions and technical positions

- **Plans fidelity.** Don was a firm advocate of building to Matt Layden's plans to the letter, citing Sheet 1's specification of bronze ring nails as a permanent backup to the glue joint and urging that any deviation be cleared with Matt first.[8] See [fastening and gluing](/construction/fastening-and-gluing.md).
- **Weight and ballast.** He supplied the definitive loaded-weight breakdown (≈1,365 lb; ≈930 lb in the bottom 10 in; 400 lb lead), the waterplane sensitivity (~1 inch per 200 lb), and his lead-installation technique, later expanded into a dedicated illustrated article on the ballast "pig" layout, sizing, and floor-barrier detail.[9][10][11] See [weight and ballast](/design/weight-and-ballast.md#the-ballast-pig-layout-in-full-don-elliott).
- **Panel layout.** He coached builders through the tight bulkhead-#2 / transom / side-panel nesting.[12] See [panel layout](/construction/panel-layout.md).
- **Hull assembly sequence.** He detailed pulling a hull together after the bulkheads are up — stem first, tank top/floor as one early-positioned piece, a hot wet blanket to soften side panels, dry-fitting to bevel joints.[13] See [hull assembly sequence](/construction/hull-assembly-sequence.md).
- **Build schedule.** He estimated a Paradox should take about six months to build.[12]
- **Tacking technique.** He gave a detailed tacking-technique checklist — close-hauled tacking only, timing to wave crests, gentle rudder easing (no more than 30 degrees either side), downhaul and perpendicularity checks, and a paddle in very light wind — holding that there was nothing wrong with Paradox's lateral plane or rudder size.[4b][4c] See [rudder and chine experiments](/components/rudder.md).
- **Against enlarging Paradox.** He argued against scaling the boat up: her width is fixed by the standard 4 ft plywood sheet; lengthening alone alters the displacement distribution; a true enlargement demands a complete redesign with fresh calculations, model testing, and revised scantlings, concluding that a substantially enlarged boat would no longer be a Paradox in the designer's sense.[4a] See [stretched Paradox](/design/ideas/stretched-paradox.md). A later builder independently reached the same verdict under the name "Paradox derivative" for any boat substantially altered from the plans — see [paradox.md](/paradox.md#citations).
- **Little Cruiser cutaway offer.** He offered to produce a free complete cutaway drawing of [Little Cruiser](/matt-layden/little-cruiser.md)'s interior for a planned book, given enough photos, sketches, and dimensions, noting his understanding that no plans or drawings were ever made for Little Cruiser.[14]
- **Exterior-vs-marine plywood advice.** Answering a new builder's plywood question, he confirmed a good-quality Douglas fir exterior ply (not white pine) will serve structurally, holding the same glue as marine grade, but recommended marine grade for the side hull panels and cautioned against any deck plywood with thin outer veneers.[15] See [plywood grade and foam flotation debate](/construction/plywood-grades-and-foam-flotation.md#a-new-builders-exterior-vs-marine-choice-and-why-marine-usually-wins-on-labor).
- **Foam flotation over plywood.** He held that it was safe to bond foam flotation over epoxy-coated interior fir plywood, citing reassurances from System Three and West System, and planned a follow-up installation article.[16] See [plywood grade and foam flotation debate](/construction/plywood-grades-and-foam-flotation.md).
- **Epoxy mixing.** Sharply disputed a round-vs-square mixing-container argument, holding that the great majority of bad epoxy mixes trace to measurement, temperature, and attention lapses rather than container shape, and recommends a square plastic tub.[17] See [mixing container shape and stir time](/construction/epoxy-coating-technique.md#mixing-container-shape-and-stir-time).
- **Water tank storage alternative.** For a builder settling on fresh water rather than self-flooding ballast, suggested building the tanks as drawn but with open, oval-shaped hatch lids in place of the sealed covers, so the space can double as dry stowage.[18][19] See [provisioning — water](/operations/provisioning-and-water.md#self-flooding-sea-water-ballast-tanks-feasible-but-risky).
- **A homemade hand sander.** A 15 in sander fitted with a 3 in (75 mm) wide grit pad made from the abrasive belt of a handheld belt sander, described in his "Building the Paradox" booklet and later used by another UK builder shaping a boom. See [builder's toolkit](/construction/builders-toolkit.md).[20]
- **Moderating a companion group.** Beyond the main mailing list, Don ran his own companion group restricting discussion strictly to the Paradox as designed by Matt Layden, with no changes discussed — a policy strict enough that other correspondents warned newcomers about it before they joined. See [people/alberto-callejon.md](/people/alberto-callejon.md).[21] A 2011 recollection, offered in the course of an unrelated debate over starting a rival Facebook group, held that group as the very first Yahoo Paradox group, strict enough to see a good many members expelled for disagreeing with Don, and credited it with prompting `paradoxbuilders` itself to form as a freer environment for dissenters — a claim not otherwise corroborated by the archive's own founding record, which credits `paradoxbuilders`' July 2001 founding to Glen Maxwell with Don already an active early contributor. See [references/mailing-list.md](/references/mailing-list.md#sibling-and-successor-groups).[50] A further, independent, firsthand account from 2015 lends support to the expulsion claim specifically, distinct from the founding-timeline question above: a different correspondent recalled being banned from that companion group himself after proposing design changes, met with a "thou shall not deviate from the designers wishes" response, and judged the group certainly controlled, if lacking the soul and diversity of the present, more open list.[72]

## A separate, unbuilt design: "Illusion"

Before his death Don was working on a design of his own, entirely separate from Paradox: a 3 metre micro-sailboat he named **"Illusion"** — rectilinear in style, resembling a Bolger "Brick" dinghy or a Puddle Duck Racer, and unrelated to the one-design miniature keelboats of the same name raced at several English sailing clubs. Don's "Illusion" is easily confused with [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)'s similarly named **"Elusion"** — a different boat by a different designer — and at least one secondary account mixes the two up. Complete plans for Don's "Illusion" were never published.[22]

Illusion's development played out in extended discussion on the separate Yahoo "Smallboats" group, and grew out of an earlier design of Don's own, **"Squeezebox,"** originally conceived as a lifeboat; the Smallboats correspondence survives online, though the group itself receives only spam traffic today. An open web directory of Don's own illustrations remains publicly accessible, preserving most of his work on Illusion, needing only a builder to pick the design up and carry it forward.[42] A further suggestion asked whether Wanda Elliott, already patiently fulfilling Building Manual orders, might also be willing to furnish some of the wider body of Don's drawings and designs beyond Illusion alone, reasoning that the effort behind them deserved to have its legacy preserved.[43]

## After his death

Don's death was announced directly to the mailing list within days, in his widow Wanda Elliott's own words: he had continued to run his instructional-materials business almost to the end, and she intended to keep making his construction articles available for purchase, though her own limited boat-building knowledge meant she could not answer technical questions in his place; ordering procedures would continue as normal, with some initial delay, and she asked that orders be withheld until after 1 January 2009. The family planned to keep Don's own Paradox and sail her with the grandchildren, closing with the thought that Don had "sailed off into a new adventure."[3] The news drew a personal note of condolence from [Alberto Callejon](/people/alberto-callejon.md), the Patagonian builder of "[Cachivache](/boats/cachivache.md)," who credited "the care and aid received from Don" with having helped him build his own boat — Alberto's own earlier account records a companion-group subscription rather than personal correspondence with Don, so the debt he acknowledged here likely runs through Don's published construction articles themselves rather than a direct exchange.[23] Word of the death also reached at least one Paradox owner independently, over a month later, remembering him simply as "a gifted artist" who "helped many builders understand the building process for the Paradox."[24] By early March 2009, at least one buyer who had already paid for the Building Manual by PayPal reported no acknowledgement or delivery in return — a real-world data point on how the transition to Wanda Elliott's own handling of orders was actually working out, consistent with her own warning above that initial delays should be expected.[25] The same difficulty recurred four months later: a further buyer, having tried Don's own e-mail/web address without success, asked the list how to obtain a copy.[26] When no one else answered, a fellow builder offered his own personal copy instead, conditional on the newcomer first getting clearance from Don's touchy-about-copyright family.[27] That offer proved unnecessary: within about a month the same buyer had found a working route after all, obtaining the Manual directly from Wanda Elliott as a PDF for $14 by PayPal.[28] The pattern of mixed outcomes continued regardless — a further newcomer trying the same address a day later got only a bounce, "no mailbox here by that name."[29] The bounce proved to be a one-letter typo rather than a dead address: the correct address doubles the "t" (`donelliott@`, not `donelliot@`) — a mistake made by the earlier buyer[28] himself before he finally reached Wanda Elliott.[30] See [Paradox building plans — a July 2009 recurrence](/references/building-plans.md#a-july-2009-recurrence-still-unreachable-through-don-elliotts-own-address) and [a further August 2009 recurrence](/references/building-plans.md#a-further-august-2009-recurrence-a-working-route-through-don-elliotts-widow-and-a-bounced-address) for the fuller pattern this continues.

The same difficulty recurred once more that October: a new correspondent gave up waiting after two weeks and began buying materials regardless, drawing both a further paid-copy offer and, for the first time, an entirely free copy-for-a-beer offer from a fellow builder — alongside a competing view that the Manual, being available nowhere near so reliably as [Al Law](/people/alastair-law.md)'s own build website, was not strictly necessary to start building at all.[31][32][33][34] See [Paradox building plans — an October 2009 recurrence](/references/building-plans.md#an-october-2009-recurrence-an-unmet-request-and-an-informal-alternative-to-the-manual) for the fuller account.

The correct address needed one further clarification beyond the single-T slip already caught above — it takes **two Ls as well as two Ts** in "Elliott."[35] Buying a copy through another channel in the meantime, the same newcomer found the mast-layout sheet only "ALMOST" making sense even with the Manual open beside the plans (see [components/rig/mast.md — reading the mast drawing](/components/rig/mast.md#reading-the-mast-drawing-a-recurring-point-of-confusion) for the technique that eventually resolved it).[36] Doubt was then raised over whether Wanda Elliott could accept PayPal at all, most buyers by then having mailed a check or money order instead[37] — but the newcomer's own experience overturned that doubt directly: having already obtained a copy elsewhere, he separately PayPal'd Wanda regardless and received a further copy by e-mail the next day, so her ordering process does after all take PayPal.[38]

## The closure of Don's own Yahoo group (2010)

In June 2010, word reached the mailing list that Don's own Yahoo group, "paradoxboatbuilding," was closing outright. Spam had accumulated there over the years, since partly cleaned up by Don's son; Wanda Elliott had sought volunteer moderators for it but, lacking the time or heart to keep the search going, gave notice the group would close regardless, while reaffirming — as she had at his death — that Don's articles and build manuals would remain available for purchase, and that the family was considering setting up a website of its own for his material.[44] The closing group held photographs, messages, a member database, and files and links built up over Don's years running it.[45] Before the shutdown, a suggestion went round the mailing list to download and repost its files and photographs there instead, so the material would not be lost.[46] Fully preserving a closing Yahoo group's content, however, was understood to take more than an ordinary member's own downloads: only a moderator could capture and mirror everything across — every photo album under its original creator's name, plus files, links, membership records, and the archive itself — where an individual member could only save bits and pieces; one correspondent had offered to moderate the group himself but still could not have mirrored its contents over this way.[47] The same discussion separately named two further related Yahoo groups — "Smallboats," where books had been serialized across message installments, and "enigmabuildersasdesigned."[48]

[microcruising.com](/references/microcruising-website.md) refers to Don as deceased in a 3/20/14 blog entry directing buyers to his Paradox Builders Booklet, placing his death before that date.[39] The site credits his 16-part *Messing About in Boats* construction article and reproduces its sketches with Don's permission, and a later update (5-11-18) points readers to Don's own how-to-build-books site, **smallboatmanuals.weebly.com**, as the current source for the building manual.[40][41] See [Building the Paradox articles](/references/building-the-paradox-articles.md).

## A 2011 retrospective from the plans' distributor

Confirming a new buyer's payment for the Paradox plans had arrived and would be mailed out the same day, [Dave Bolduc](/people/bolduc/dave.md) offered his own assessment of Don's guides: their building techniques were mostly standard rather than unconventional, their real value lying in Don's illustrations and detailed instructions; he called Don's death a real loss to the Paradox design and to small boats generally, and regretted that Don had spent some of his energy arguing online with people who saw things differently — while pointing the buyer to Don's old posts on the companion group, before Wanda Elliott closed it down for good.[49]

## A further November 2011 recurrence: a renewed FAQ question, and a preservation worry restated

A newcomer asking whether the mailing list kept any FAQ file — covering junk rig, scantlings (ply weight), stitch-and-glue construction, and water ballast — drew the reply that Yahoo Groups has no dedicated FAQ feature, but that a large amount of the same information already sits in the group's own Files and Links sections.[51] A further amount, the reply continued, still survives in the old, Don-founded "Paradoxboatbuilders" group, worth checking in its own right, not least for Don's own drawings — though, more than a year after that group's closure had first been announced, how much longer it would stay online was itself still uncertain.[52] A separate reply proposed downloading and storing all of Don's files somewhere before they were lost for good — the same preservation worry raised when the closure was first announced, restated once more.[53]

## A further August 2013 recurrence: encouragement, and confirmation the manual remains available

A prospective builder who had owned the plans for years without starting, wary of the size of the undertaking, asked whether Don's manual could still be had.[54] The reply likened building a Paradox to eating an elephant — one bite at a time, rather than looking at the whole meal — with the project bound to be finished eventually if approached that way; confirmed **Don's book remained available from his widow** and was worth the read.[55] A further reply confirmed buying the book and finding it a terrific value even setting the Paradox itself aside, presenting several methods and techniques usable on other projects around the shop, having read it through at least twice.[56] Asking how to actually place an order, the correspondent was pointed to a historic price list preserved in the microcruisers group's files — $10 plus $2 shipping within the US (or $4 for faster air shipping), $10 plus $4 shipping overseas (or $8 airmail), payable by cash, check, money order, or PayPal to Don Elliott's own Tomah, Wisconsin address.[57][58] That notice was corrected as outdated: current orders instead go by e-mail to Wanda, who can send the manual as a PDF or on CD.[59] The Files-section document giving the old address had already gone unanswered when written to directly, prompting a request to have it updated;[60] within a day the file itself was replaced, the booklet's own description uploaded afresh to the group's Files section under its Frequently Asked Questions/"Don Elliot's Book" folder.[61] A further reply on the same thread reported leaning on the manual at every phase of a multi-year build under way in Hawaii, its drawings of the boat in use a continuing source of inspiration; full build account at [people/dave-skaife.md](/people/dave-skaife.md#building-the-boat-in-hawaii).[62]

The corrected notice was filed specifically in the group's FAQ folder within the Files section.[63] Reaching Wanda directly did work, at least at first: e-mailing her drew a reply within about a day, and a PayPal payment went through — though the PDF itself had not yet arrived by the time this exchange breaks off in the archive as consulted here.[64] A second buyer's own e-mail to the same address went unanswered over the same days, read by him as the address likely having been flooded with requests once it was posted to the list; he asked the going price.[65] The answer came back at **$18 US**, with payment already sent and a follow-up note dropped when no reply came — the first buyer's own PDF, like the second's, had still not arrived by the point the exchange closes on record.[66] The second buyer took the silence as more of the same flood of requests rather than any special trouble, and looked forward to hearing how long the first buyer's own order actually took.[67]

Both waits ended within days. The first buyer's PDF arrived — Wanda had simply been preoccupied with her grandchildren and had missed the earlier PayPal notice — and he told the second buyer he hoped his own order was right behind.[68] The second buyer still had no reply of his own at that point, weighing whether to try again that autumn if no other project had displaced this one by then, and had held off ordering the printed plans, preferring to read the manual first for a better sense of what a build actually involved.[69] Wanda then wrote back to him directly with her standard order notice in full — the PDF at **$18**, payable by PayPal or by mail to her Tomah, Wisconsin address — closing, as she always did, with Don's own promotional line touting the original Paradox's outright win in the 2003 Everglades Challenge (see [the 2003 Everglades Challenge](/merits/everglades-challenge.md)).[70] He ordered on the strength of it and had his own PDF within a day, going on to separately order the printed plans from Dave Bolduc for $40 — still unsure himself whether he would ever build a Paradox, but judging Don's manual worth having regardless for shop techniques transferable to any project.[71]

See [Paradox building plans — a further August 2013 recurrence](/references/building-plans.md#a-further-august-2013-recurrence-encouragement-and-confirmation-the-manual-remains-available) for the same exchange.

## "The Edge" offered for sale (2017)

Eight years after Don's death, and against the family's own stated plan to keep his boat and sail her with the grandchildren, "The Edge" was offered for sale in 2017 — sound overall, needing only minor toe-rail and rudder attention, priced at $2,800 OBO with a trailer and some cruising gear included — and sold within about two weeks to **[Deric](/people/frederic-tallman.md)**, a longtime list-lurker in New York State who had bought Don's plans some five years earlier without ever starting to build, choosing instead to keep sailing an existing Herreshoff-designed cat ketch — he towed the finished boat home across five states from Wisconsin, and recalled Don as prolific well beyond Paradox: a catamaran, a houseboat, a wooden kayak kept by his son, and an unbuilt interest in foiling boats besides his cartoons and other guides.[73][74] Full record of the sale and Deric's restoration work at [boats/edge.md — sold to Deric, and restoration begins (2017)](/boats/edge.md#sold-to-deric-and-restoration-begins-2017).
