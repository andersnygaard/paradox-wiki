# Don Elliott

**Don Elliott**, of Tomah, Wisconsin, was one of the earliest Paradox builders and became a primary technical resource for the plans, authoring the sixteen-part **"Building Paradox"** construction guide that builders still buy today.[1] He built his own Paradox, "[The Edge](/boats/edge.md)," heavily built to Matt Layden's plans,[2] and separately drew an unbuilt design of his own, the micro-sailboat "[Illusion](/similar-boat-designs/don-elliotts-illusion.md)." He died in 2008.[3]

## Building Paradox

Don's **"Building Paradox"** ran as a sixteen-part feature in *Messing About In Boats* from September 1996 to May 1997, Parts 1 through 15 plus a Conclusion, including a valued jig for the coach roof, and was sold only directly from Don for years.[4]

Beyond the series, Don authored a two-part essay, **"A Story, a True One,"** on why Matt Layden's Paradox, the smallest, stubbiest and only fully enclosed boat in the fleet, beat lighter, faster kayaks, dinghies and multihulls over the 304-mile 2003 Everglades Challenge. The essay credits a weatherproof, sail-from-within cabin, letting its skipper rest and hold a course in any weather without exposure to cold, spray or rain, as the decisive advantage, and closes that "Matt has proven that his unusual concept works and very well at that."[5]

## Building strictly to plan

Strict fidelity to the plans was his standard for other builders too: Sheet 1's bronze ring nails, specified as a permanent backup to the glue joint, were not optional, and any departure was to be cleared with Matt Layden first. Full account: [fastening and gluing](/construction/fastening-and-gluing.md).[2] His own boat carried a full loaded-weight and ballast breakdown, with her lead installed in epoxy-bonded hardwood blocks, tapped for threaded fastenings only once the epoxy had cured, forward trim lead counterbalancing the battery and every piece sealed in plastic with rubber padding. Full technique: [weight and ballast](/design/weight-and-ballast.md#installing-the-lead-ballast).[6] His coaching covered the tight nesting of bulkhead 2, the transom and the side panels, and pulling a hull together stem first, a hot wet blanket softening the side panels into shape before dry-fitting and beveling the joints. Full account: [hull assembly sequence](/construction/hull-assembly-sequence.md).[7]

The same fidelity extended to the design itself. Paradox's width is fixed by the standard plywood sheet, and enlarging her calls for a full redesign, with fresh model testing, rather than a simple stretch, an argument he reached independently of Layden's own case against scaling up. Full account: [stretched Paradox](/design/ideas/stretched-paradox.md#don-elliotts-case-against-simple-enlargement).[8]

## The Edge

[The Edge](/boats/edge.md) was heavily built to Matt Layden's plans[2] and was, by mid-2001, one of only two Paradoxes known to be sailing, the other being Matt Layden's own prototype.[1] She went formally unnamed for roughly two years before a UK builder's launch photographs supplied her name. Full build record, the name's own story and her later restoration: [boats/edge.md](/boats/edge.md).

## A separate design: Illusion

Before his death Don was working on a design entirely apart from Paradox: **"Illusion,"** a 3 metre micro-sailboat, never completed.[3] See [Don Elliott's "Illusion"](/similar-boat-designs/don-elliotts-illusion.md) for the design and its reception.

## After his death

His construction guide remained in demand for years afterward, filled by his family.[9] "The Edge" was sold in 2017 to [Frederic Tallman](/people/frederic-tallman.md), who restored her and has sailed her since. Full account: [boats/edge.md](/boats/edge.md#sold-to-deric-and-restoration-begins-2017).[10]
