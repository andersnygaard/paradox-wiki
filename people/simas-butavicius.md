# Simas Butavičius

**Simas Butavičius** is an amateur [Paradox](/paradox.md) builder in **Lithuania**, in the Baltics, who began building in the winter of 2019.[1] He builds in **exterior-grade Baltic birch plywood**, the cheapest good option available to him locally. He thinned the panel schedule to **12mm bottom, 9mm sides and 6mm deck** to offset the wood's extra weight, a scheme fellow Baltic-birch builder [Anders Nygaard](/people/anders-nygaard.md) later credited as an inspiration for [Miramis](/boats/miramis.md).[2][3] After repeated pauses, he glued up the bulkheads and sides in November 2024.[4]

## Building in Baltic birch

He chose exterior-grade Baltic birch plywood because it was the cheapest good option available to him, at roughly 680 kg per cubic metre, about 1.3 times the density of okoume, the plywood the plans assume.[2] For the floorboards he used English oak (*Quercus robur*), chosen for being heavy, good below the waterline and very rot-resistant; one board came from table-leg stock a neighbour gave him, ripped in half and glued to make wider boards.[2] For deck beams and the transom's above-waterline bottom cleat he used Norway spruce (*Picea abies*), about half the weight of the oak.[2] For beams and some bulkhead-to-beam joints he used polyurethane glue, waterproof to the European D4 standard and, by his own account, the European equivalent of Gorilla glue: convenient to apply and easy to clean up, since the squeeze-out cures to a foam.[2]

## Build details

He built the vent box separately on a table rather than in place in the hull, since it involves awkward drain-pipe angles; after ruining two wood blocks trying to drill a long 35mm hole for the drain pipe, he cut a groove for it with a table saw instead and bedded the pipe in thickened epoxy, roughening the PVC pipe first with 40-grit sandpaper to help the epoxy grip it.[5]

He built the stem from the plans' line drawings rather than measuring off the pre-assembled hull, having no confident way to take measurements from the assembly directly; it came out accurate to about plus or minus 1mm by laser level once the sides were pulled to meet it.[6]

His mast is a tapered birdmouth spar.[7][8] Half-joking about the result of his choices, he called his build the **"Sport Edition."**[9]

The build paused more than once after starting in the 2019 to 2020 winter; in November 2024 he glued up the bulkheads and sides using a two-stage dry-assembly method: screwing the bulkheads in place to cut sheer-clamp notches, then redoing the dry assembly with the screws' threads ground down to leave gaps for thickened epoxy, a technique that let him glue the hull together solo, in alignment, without the usual scramble.[4]
