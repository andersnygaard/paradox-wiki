# Dave Raftery

**Dave Raftery** is a Paradox and Enigma enthusiast from **eastern Massachusetts** and the compiler of **[Dave's Wiki](/references/daves-wiki.md)**, a technical reference on Matt Layden's designs that this bundle cites for its Paradox/Enigma comparison and its working notebook on the Enigma family.[1][2] He bought **Paradox plans** without building, having no fiberglass or epoxy experience of his own, and set his building intent on Matt Layden's **[Enigma 460](/matt-layden/enigma.md)** instead; by 2013 he was building one of his own.[1][3] Where a builder wanted more boat than stock Paradox, his answer was the Enigma 460 rather than a scaled-up Paradox, and he also founded an Enigma builders' group of his own.

## Dave's Wiki

Raftery compiled two pages that this bundle draws on: a feature-by-feature comparison of Paradox and the Enigma 460 called "Advantages of Matt Layden mini cruisers," and a working builder's notebook on the Enigma family covering design particulars, a Paradox/360/460 comparison table, construction schedules and a ballast-materials analysis.[1] Its standing as a source and how it is cited are set out at [references/daves-wiki.md](/references/daves-wiki.md#the-author-on-list-2009): it collects primary quotations, notably from Matt Layden himself and from the builder of the Enigma 460 prototype, more than it offers original measurement of its own. For warming a cabin without a stove, he assembled a small file of outside sources: a candle heater built from stacked clay flower pots; a version built from tin cans, photographed for his own wiki; and a Mother Earth News treatment of the same idea.[4] A photograph from the wiki showing two people fitted inside a Paradox hull before the deck went on was still being linked by other builders years after it first appeared there.[5]

## The Enigma 460 as the alternative to enlarging Paradox

Raftery's alternative to a scaled-up Paradox was Matt Layden's Enigma 460: a little over a foot longer than stock Paradox; easier and quicker to build; and plenty strong especially with a layer of biax cloth, with its prototype builder's own construction blog as the working record.[6][7] He supplied the family's numbers as they became known: the 4.6 m Enigma then under construction was expected to weigh about 260 lb empty and need a minimum of 110 lb of removable ballast, against the original 3.6 m Enigma's 180 lb;[8] a 12' 3" LOA figure for the smaller boat came from the EnigmaBuilders site itself, left unreconciled against a separate 360 cm figure.[9] Paradox itself is much heavier in his comparison, with thicker plywood, more bulkheads and essentially a double bottom, making the 460 much easier to build.[10]

The same comparison recurred in December 2019: a thickness reduction under consideration was weighed against the Enigma 460's own thinner scantlings, 9mm bottom and 6mm sides, and its builder's blog, the identical case restated on the identical evidence.[11] By February 2021 he still held the Paradox plans and construction book bought years earlier and still hoped to build something like the Enigma 460, though a cabin-building project of his own then took priority; plywood he had bought for an Enigma went instead toward a Gorfnik. His own wiki, in his own words, held "a lot of info on Enigma which I gathered over the years... No plans, though!"[12] In April 2025 the same builder's blog seemed to have gone offline; the PDF copy he had personally saved in 2012 was offered in its place, though the original proved still live.[13]

## His own build

Before he turned to Enigma, Raftery had crewed LNG tankers for four years and cruised small boats and canoes on his own. That background shaped his approach to provisioning aboard a small boat: Paradox's own rain-catchment system plus a few flexible water containers cover the need for a watermaker, and a handheld VHF radio is the safety item worth the space.[14]

The forward reefing fitting on the roller-furling gear does two jobs: it provides the mechanical means to reef and unreef the sail and a clear lead for the reefing line from deck to drum. Matt Layden's own plans changed here too: an original drawing tying the bottom tube's rope directly to the next piece of structure was replaced, in a revision dated to May 1996, by the split "Y" fitting still used.[15][16] For clamping a scarph joint, he pointed builders to a fellow Enigma 460 builder's own construction blog.[17]

On the stock Paradox cabin, the box shape gives the roof and cabin their strength; cutting away the rear roof and window for a removable rear panel needs both rear corners reinforced, a point he had worked out for his own intended boat.[18] Without Paradox's internal hull stringers and floors, Enigma's stitch-and-glue construction takes much less time to build, in his comparison of the two.[19]

By February 2012 he was sequencing a fiberglass butt joint between two plywood panels on his own hull and cleaning amine blush off a cured epoxy bead before taping over it, hands-on work far from his 2009 description of himself as having no fiberglass or epoxy experience.[20] The following year, building his own Enigma about two years behind a Paradox owner finishing "Tardis," he had a fellow Enigma 460 builder's photographed yuloh-making to follow for the same dimensions.[3]

## Keeping the Enigma group going

When the original EnigmaBuilders group closed in 2011, Raftery founded its replacement, `enigma_sailboat`, keeping the design's builders connected.[21]
