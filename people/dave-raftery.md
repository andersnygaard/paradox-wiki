# Dave Raftery

**Dave Raftery**, posting under the handle **`aikidave`** with the profile `daraffa`, is a Paradox and Enigma enthusiast from **Walpole, Massachusetts**, and the author of the `aiki.pbworks.com` wiki that this bundle cites as an outside source in its own right — see [references/daves-wiki.md](/references/daves-wiki.md).[1][2] He had followed Matt Layden's designs for about five years by 2009 and had purchased **Paradox plans** several years before that without building, having no fiberglass or epoxy experience of his own; his own building intent settled instead on Matt Layden's **[Enigma 460](/matt-layden/enigma.md)**.[1][2] Active on the list from 2007 to 2010, he contributed across more topics than almost any other non-builder in the archive — the Enigma family, roller reefing, build effort, cabin heating, provisioning, the yuloh, and the sharpie lineage.

## The wiki, and its standing as a source

Raftery compiled two pages that the archive treats as citable: a feature-by-feature **Paradox/Enigma-460 comparison** ("Advantages of Matt Layden mini cruisers") and a working builder's notebook on the **Enigma family** with design particulars, a Paradox/360/460 comparison table, construction schedules, and a ballast-materials analysis. He identified himself as their author on the list in October 2009.[1] The wiki's nature, reliability, and citing convention are set out at [references/daves-wiki.md](/references/daves-wiki.md#the-author-on-list-2009) — it is a compiling source, valued chiefly for the primary quotations it collects.

## The Enigma 460 as the alternative to enlarging Paradox

His most sustained contribution to the archive is a single, consistently argued position: where a builder wants more boat than stock Paradox, the answer is the Enigma 460 rather than a scaled-up Paradox. He put the case as a matter of both size and labour — the 460 is a little over a foot longer than stock Paradox, easier and quicker to build, and plenty strong especially with a layer of biax cloth — and pointed repeatedly to its prototype builder's own construction blog as the working record.[6][7] See [design/larger-paradox.md — a 2009 coda](/design/larger-paradox.md#a-2009-coda-two-further-declared-attempts-a-towing-weight-fix-and-the-enigma-460-alternative). Ten years on, having said he wanted to follow the mailing list's own move to groups.io, he made the same point once more on the new platform — a newcomer weighing a thickness reduction was pointed to the Enigma 460's own thinner scantlings (9mm bottom, 6mm sides) and its builder's blog, the identical case restated on the identical evidence.[24] Four years later still, in April 2025, he pointed a further correspondent to the same blog once more — reporting it appeared to have gone offline and offering the copy he had personally saved as a PDF back in 2012 — only for the correspondent to find it was in fact still live.[26] See [matt-layden/enigma.md — a 2025 continuation](/matt-layden/enigma.md#a-2025-continuation-reverse-engineering-from-photographs-and-the-george-van-sickle-blog-reconfirmed-live) for the fuller exchange.

He also supplied the family's numbers and their availability status. On weight, the 4.6 m Enigma under construction was expected to come in at approximately **260 lb empty** and to need a minimum of **110 lb of removable ballast**, against the original 3.6 m Enigma's 180 lb empty — the larger boat trading 80 lb of hull weight for its extra size.[5] On the smaller boat's length, he located a **12' 3" LOA** figure on the EnigmaBuilders group's own front page, without judging whether it or the 360 cm figure was the more accurate.[4] On plans, he confirmed in March 2009 that the Enigma 460's were not yet available — the prototype was in the water and had been test sailed, but its builder was still tweaking her, with plans expected once she was proven.[7] See [matt-layden/enigma.md — the Enigma family: the 360 and the plywood-cabin 460](/matt-layden/enigma.md#the-enigma-family-the-360-and-the-plywood-cabin-460). Comparing the two designs directly, he judged Paradox much heavier — thicker plywood, more bulkheads, and essentially a double bottom — so that the 460 should be much easier to build.[3]

## Roller reefing: the fitting's functions, and a May 1996 plans revision

Raftery gave the clearest functional breakdown on record of what the forward reefing fitting has to do: provide the mechanical means to reef and unreef the sail via the roller drum and reefing line, and provide a lead for the reefing line to pass unobstructed from the deck to the drum.[8] He then contributed the one genuinely new historical fact in that whole discussion — that **Matt Layden's own plans evolved on this fitting**: the original drawing tied the bottom tube's rope directly to the next piece of structure, and a revision he recalled as **May 1996** replaced it with the split "Y" fitting instead.[9] See [components/rig/furling.md — the "Y" fitting's purpose confirmed, and the plans' own May 1996 revision](/components/rig/furling.md#the-y-fittings-purpose-confirmed-and-the-plans-own-may-1996-revision), where that recollection is set against an already-documented, better-dated May 1994 account of what is very likely the same revision.

## Construction: build time, scarphs, and a structural caution

On how long a Paradox takes, he made the comparison that runs through his other advice: Enigma, being stitch-and-glue construction without Paradox's internal hull stringers and floors, would likely take much less time to build, especially without spending as much time on exterior finishing.[10] See [construction/build-effort.md — time](/construction/build-effort.md#time).

Asked in 2010 how to clamp a hull scarph joint, he pointed to the section of the Enigma 460 blog showing exactly that operation on a related design — the same source he had been citing for years, put to a builder's immediate practical use.[11] See [construction/scarf-joints.md — further published tutorials, and a related design's own clamping account](/construction/scarf-joints.md#further-published-tutorials-and-a-related-designs-own-clamping-account).

By February 2012 he was himself asking hands-on lamination questions — how to sequence a plain, backing-free fiberglass butt joint between two plywood panels, and how to clean amine blush off a cured epoxy bead at the inside chines before taping over it — a marked shift from his 2009 self-description as having no fiberglass or epoxy experience of his own.[21] See [construction/scarf-joints.md — a 2012 recurrence](/construction/scarf-joints.md#a-2012-recurrence-sequencing-a-taped-butt-joint-and-cleaning-blush-off-a-bead-before-taping).

His one structural caution is worth keeping: on a proposal to make the cabin's rear panel removable, he warned from the design's own geometry that the stock cabin's box shape is what gives the roof and cabin their strength, so cutting away the rear roof and window calls for reinforcing both rear corners — a modification he had considered for his own intended boat and thought through on that basis.[12] See [matt-layden/little-cruiser.md — the removable back window](/matt-layden/little-cruiser.md#the-removable-back-window-design-rationale-and-paradox-builders-adaptation-ideas).

## Provisioning and water, from four years crewing LNG tankers

Answering a newcomer worried about carrying a watermaker, Raftery drew on four years crewing LNG tankers plus his own small-boat and canoe cruising: Paradox's own rain-catchment system, plus a few flexible 5-gallon water containers, covers the problem without one. The same reply pointed to a handheld VHF radio as the piece of safety gear that actually earns its space.[13] See [operations/provisioning-and-water.md — water: cube tankage, rain catchment, and a manual watermaker](/operations/provisioning-and-water.md#water-cube-tankage-rain-catchment-and-a-manual-watermaker).

## Cabin heating: candle heaters

To the recurring question of how to warm a Paradox cabin without a stove, he contributed the candle-heater family — a heater built from stacked clay flower pots, a version built from tin cans (photographed on his own wiki), and a *Mother Earth News* treatment of the same idea.[14] See [operations/cabin-heating.md — a charcoal or kerosene can heater](/operations/cabin-heating.md#a-charcoal-or-kerosene-can-heater-and-the-transom-cook-stove-alternative). The carbon monoxide risk these carry is treated at [the same page's CO caution](/operations/cabin-heating.md#a-carbon-monoxide-caution-for-candle-and-diffuser-heaters).

## A 2021 restatement: still no plans, and plywood bought for an Enigma redirected toward a Gorfnik

Answering the archive's last recorded recurrence of the Enigma-plans question, in February 2021 pointed again to his own `aiki.pbworks.com` wiki page as holding "a lot of info on Enigma... No plans, though" — still had the Paradox plans and construction book bought years earlier, still hoped to build something like the Enigma 460 someday, though a cabin-building project of his own then had first call on his time. Named a fellow builder's Enigma 460 construction blog once more, and reported plywood he had bought for an Enigma several years earlier might instead go toward a Gorfnik build.[25] See [matt-layden/enigma.md — a January 2021 answer](/matt-layden/enigma.md#a-january-2021-answer-no-official-plans-but-a-builders-own-notes-online) for the fuller thread this closed.

## The yuloh, and other contributions

- **The yuloh's pivot.** Having found the yuloh drawing on plan sheet #12, he asked whether the builder intended the "ball joint" pivot or a regular oarlock of the kind Matt Layden uses for sculling in a video, and linked a hosted clip of Matt sculling — opening the ball-joint-versus-oarlock question that the archive later worked out in full.[15] See [components/yuloh.md — ball joint versus oarlock](/components/yuloh.md#ball-joint-versus-oarlock-and-pivot-socket-wear-over-time).
- **A further yuloh pointer, and his own build progress (February 2013).** Confirmed the yuloh's dimensions live on plans sheet 12 for a new owner finishing a nearly complete Paradox, pointed to a fellow Enigma 460 builder's own photographed yuloh-making, and mentioned his own Enigma build was then about two years behind the addressee's Paradox.[22] See [boats/scout.md — finishing "Tardis"](/boats/scout.md#finishing-tardis-and-her-first-launch-as-scout-2013).
- **Phil Bolger's death, May 2009.** He relayed a fuller, personal account of Bolger's death and the health decline that preceded it, given by Bolger's wife and business partner, the boat designer **Susanne Altenburger**, who confirmed that Phil Bolger & Friends, Inc. continues under her direction.[16] See [design/sharpie-lineage.md — Phil Bolger's death](/design/sharpie-lineage.md#phil-bolgers-death-may-2009).
- **The air-flow study files.** He reported the group's newly uploaded computational air-flow-study PDFs corrupted and unopenable — the reason the study itself is only partly recoverable.[17] See [components/vent-box.md — a computational airflow study of the vent trunk](/components/vent-box.md#a-computational-airflow-study-of-the-vent-trunk-and-where-it-falls-short).
- **Plans routing.** He pointed prospective builders toward Enigma and the separate EnigmaBuilders Yahoo group, noting a 15 ft version of Enigma then being built.[18] See [references/building-plans.md](/references/building-plans.md#a-second-september-2007-recurrence-and-a-pointer-to-enigma-for-a-larger-sailor).
- **A successor Enigma group, 2011, and its own migration in 2019.** When the original EnigmaBuilders Yahoo group closed, he started a replacement, `enigma_sailboat`, and moved most of the old group's information — including a set of unofficial DXF hull-layout files a fellow builder had used to build an Enigma to hull-and-deck stage without official plans — into its file section.[20] See [matt-layden/enigma.md — an unofficial DXF hull layout, and a 2011 update on both Yahoo groups](/matt-layden/enigma.md#an-unofficial-dxf-hull-layout-and-a-2011-update-on-both-yahoo-groups). When Yahoo announced Groups' shutdown in October 2019, he reported the `enigma_sailboat` group would need migrating too, and asked to subscribe to the Paradox list's own successor once one existed.[23]
- **Biax cloth.** Interested in biaxial cloth for his own intended Enigma build, he asked a New England Paradox owner whether biax had been used on his boat's bottom.[2] See [boats/jill.md — helping a prospective New England builder](/boats/jill.md#helping-a-prospective-new-england-builder-and-a-sandbar-story).
- **A cabin-fit photograph.** In reply to a newcomer's request, linked a photograph from his own wiki showing two people lying stretched out in a Paradox hull before the deck was installed.[19] See [operations/crew-size-and-fit.md — a photograph of two people fitted before the deck was installed](/operations/crew-size-and-fit.md#a-photograph-of-two-people-fitted-before-the-deck-was-installed-2011).
