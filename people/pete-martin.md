# Pete Martin

**Pete Martin** is a builder in **Cheltenham, England**, originally from **Northern Ireland**. He ordered the plans for a stock [Paradox](/paradox.md) in October 2006[1], started construction on **1 December 2006**[2] and launched her as **[JoHanna](/boats/johanna.md)** on the evening of **11 June 2011**[3], a build of about four and a half years. He has sailed her since, including a 22-night, 140-mile river cruise made mostly under yuloh.[4][5] Full build account: [boats/johanna.md](/boats/johanna.md).

## From dinghies to a first boat

Long before he built anything, Pete Martin sailed dinghies from the age of nine, a 13 ft "Voyager" microcruiser round the north coast of Northern Ireland and, a few years later, a Caprice through the Inner Hebrides. He crewed offshore on larger boats throughout the same years, took up a Laser dinghy in the late 1980s and then became a committed windsurfer. He found the Paradox design in October 2006, a first-time boatbuilder.[1]

He is also a licensed radio amateur, callsign G4ISJ, with some thirty years of low-power QRP operating behind him on a station that is almost entirely homebrew.[6] His cruising ambitions for JoHanna reached past her early sailing to a hoped-for crossing of the Irish Sea, to show her off at Carrickfergus and nearby Strangford Lough, and on to the Clyde and the Inner Hebrides.[7]

## Building JoHanna

He worked largely alone, most of it inside a one-car garage barely large enough to open a parked car's doors.[8] A few days before Alastair Law measured "Little Jim"'s righting moment at Beale Park in June 2009, seeing her in person gave him fresh incentive to get moving on his own build.[9] As a first-time builder, he found scarfing the side panels felt like "impending doom" beforehand, and proved far less intimidating once begun.[10] One of the few departures he made from the stock plans was a removable panel at the aft end of the cabin roof, modelled on Little Cruiser's own; see [boats/johanna.md](/boats/johanna.md#a-removable-aft-cabin-top-panel-tried-may-2010) for how it was built and sealed.[11]

He worked out a paper-masking method for reading the mast-layout drawing on Sheet 12, a point of the plans that had defeated him for two years, and set out the full walkthrough (see [components/rig/mast.md](/components/rig/mast.md#reading-the-mast-drawing-a-recurring-point-of-confusion)).[12] Shaping the stem's curve, cardboard templates cut to each station gave him a target to plane toward, turning a job other builders found daunting into one he enjoyed (see [construction/stem-notching.md](/construction/stem-notching.md#a-cardboard-template-method-and-reassurance-the-fit-is-more-forgiving-than-the-drawings-suggest)).[13] He filmed his own technique for turning the hull, and years later built a simple frame that let it be turned over single-handed (see [construction/hull-assembly-sequence.md](/construction/hull-assembly-sequence.md#turning-the-hull-over)).[14][15] That spring he entered the Amateur Boat Building Association's competition at Beale Park, not to compete for the award but as a fixed target to build toward.[16]

## A 140-mile river cruise under yuloh

In July 2013 JoHanna joined [Little Jim](/boats/little-jim.md) and [Faith](/boats/faith.md) at a Dinghy Cruising Association rally at Poole Harbour, probably the first time three Paradoxes had gathered together in one place.[17]

His best-known voyage came five years later, in his own words "a bit experimental": 22 nights aboard, 140 miles covered, 90 of them under yuloh power alone.[4] A composting toilet, a bucket with sawdust and a urine-separating bottle, went along as a deliberate trial for long-term cruising, and a 100 W solar array and an 80 Ah battery ran a 40 lb-thrust electric trolling motor for the first 65 miles downstream without coming close to depleting half the battery. Torrential rain then flooded the river for the return leg; a backup outboard sent ahead failed to start, so a borrowed Tohatsu took over while he still yulohed wherever the current allowed. He judged the electric-and-yuloh combination well suited to a long canal cruise, "but it's still hard to beat a small IC engine which is easily refuelled instantly too."[5] Full figures: [operations/sculling-and-yuloh-technique.md](/operations/sculling-and-yuloh-technique.md#performance), [operations/head-and-sanitation.md](/operations/head-and-sanitation.md#a-2018-recurrence-a-composting-toilet-field-trial-and-a-doggy-bag-comparison), [operations/auxiliary-outboard-power.md](/operations/auxiliary-outboard-power.md#a-2018-field-test-solar-and-an-electric-trolling-motor-prove-adequate-until-a-flood-does-not).
