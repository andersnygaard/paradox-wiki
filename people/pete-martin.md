# Pete Martin

**Pete Martin** (Yahoo handle `randoneur`, e-mail `pdmartin@…`, u=25214471) is a UK builder from **Cheltenham, England**, originally from Carrickfergus, Northern Ireland. He started construction of his Paradox on **1 December 2006**, launching her as **[JoHanna](/boats/johanna.md)** on the evening of **11 June 2011** after a near-five-year build.[1][2]

## JoHanna

The full build and launch narrative lives on **[boats/johanna.md](/boats/johanna.md)**; the hull was sheathed and turned on a home-built rolling frame through 2008–09, the rudder leaded and the decks laid, before a 2010 painting campaign repeatedly lost to weather pushed the launch past a hoped-for September 2010 date.[3] After launch, Pete moved his sailing writing to a separate "Paradoxically Speaking" blog.[2] He settled early on leaving out the plans' built-in water-ballast tanks in favor of a flexible bladder tank or old soft-drink bottles topped up with lead and stores, working out a periodic bleach flush to keep a bladder tank's water fresh between uses.[4][5] By 2013 JoHanna carried a De Graaff SB 730/2 ADJ road trailer fitted with flat rubber rollers, and an outboard cage to cut down mainsheet snagging, with the sheet leads later moved from the transom to the aft deck.[6][7]

## Contributions and stories

- Relayed the list's first report of Matt Layden's newly designed **Enigma 460** — then under prototype construction — via the separate EnigmaBuilders group, see [Enigma — the Enigma family](/matt-layden/enigma.md#the-enigma-family-the-360-and-the-plywood-cabin-460), and later flagged a new Taiwan-based builder's rapid, professional-looking progress to the list.[8][9]
- Independently spotted a May 1994 revision to the tack strop's spacer tube, posting a photograph of the earlier, unrevised fitting, and separately confirmed a related May 1996 revision to the reefing drum's Y-fitting; see [tack strop — a May 1994 plans revision](/components/rig/tack-strop.md#a-may-1994-plans-revision-explains-the-forked-vs-plain-spacer-tube) and [furling — the Y-fitting's purpose confirmed](/components/rig/furling.md#the-y-fittings-purpose-confirmed-and-the-plans-own-may-1996-revision).[10][11]
- Worked out the paper-masking method for reading Sheet 12's mast-layout drawing and wrote it up in full for a fellow builder still finding it unclear; see [reading the mast drawing](/components/rig/mast.md#reading-the-mast-drawing-a-recurring-point-of-confusion).[12]
- Clarified how luff tension actually works on Paradox's rig — maintained by the furling line's own tension once the halyard is belayed, the tack strop's only job being to set the boom's height — recalling Matt Layden's own instruction for it: "make fast to cleat and forget it." See [tack strop — what the tack strop does, and doesn't do](/components/rig/tack-strop.md#what-the-tack-strop-does-and-doesnt-do-luff-tension-comes-from-the-furling-line).[13]
- Gave a fellow builder a worked deck-and-cabin-beam example — dimensions, lamination counts, and glue-up technique — see [construction/deck-and-cabin-beams.md](/construction/deck-and-cabin-beams.md), and gave Phil Biggs the actual, well-under-plans bronze-ring-nail quantities used on JoHanna; see [actual bronze-nail quantities used](/construction/fastening-and-gluing.md#actual-bronze-nail-quantities-used-well-under-the-plans-recommended-amount).[14][15]
- Traced a stubborn topsides wet-out problem to a tight-weave cloth meant for vacuum-bagging rather than hand lay-up, and resolved it by cutting out and redoing the affected patch; see [construction/fiberglassing-technique.md](/construction/fiberglassing-technique.md#a-2009-wet-out-problem-and-a-brush-versus-squeegee-debate).[16][17]
- Filmed and posted a video demonstrating his own hull-rollover technique; see [turning the hull over](/construction/hull-assembly-sequence.md#turning-the-hull-over).[18][19]
- Found the foredeck's tight curve near the stem stubbornly stiff to shape after leaving no "wings" of his own for leverage, needing extra grinding, with steaming considered as a next step.[20]
- Asking after his own future trailer drew out [Alastair Law](/people/alastair-law.md)'s actual weighed trailering figures for "[Little Jim](/boats/little-jim.md)" — 526 kg loaded on a purpose-built trailer of just over 400 kg — while Pete himself weighed building a trailer with a neighbour from a donor caravan chassis.[21]
- Built JoHanna almost entirely — about 95% of the work — inside a roughly 16 ft by 7 ft one-car garage too small even to open a parked car's doors, working from a wheeled trolley for all-around access; see [operations/first-boat-suitability.md](/operations/first-boat-suitability.md#a-one-car-garage-and-a-first-timers-difficulty-question-october-2009).[22]
- Recalled his own first scarf joint as a moment of "impending doom" that proved unfounded, setting it against Matt Layden's own verdict on scarfing over taped butt joints — "glued scarfs are faster, easier, cheaper and bend fairer"; see [construction/scarf-joints.md](/construction/scarf-joints.md#choosing-between-a-scarf-a-butt-block-and-a-taped-butt-joint).[23]
- Told a correspondent who called JoHanna's build photographs "not a boat... more like a work of art" that it was his first build, that he was no craftsman, and that "epoxy is a wonderful filler" for the bodges that don't show.[24]
- Credited an eyewitness account of "[Zoë](/boats/zoe.md)"'s sea trials, [Little Jim](/boats/little-jim.md)'s construction-photo website, and both [Little Jim](/boats/little-jim.md)'s and [Faith](/boats/faith.md)'s cruise reports with keeping his own project's motivation alive through a long build.[25]
- Keeps up an active cycling hobby around Cheltenham; his cruising ambitions extend past JoHanna's shakedown sailing to eventually crossing the Irish Sea to show the boat off at his native Carrickfergus and nearby Strangford Lough, and on to the Clyde and Inner Hebrides.[26]
- In summer 2012 he sailed JoHanna to **Seafair Haven**, the biennial festival on the Milford Haven Waterway, where he filmed [Alastair Law](/people/alastair-law.md)'s "[Little Jim](/boats/little-jim.md)" tacking down the estuary against a force 5 — footage later used to show how little a Paradox's pitching is actually felt from the helm; see [Little Jim — Seafair Haven, Milford Haven](/boats/little-jim.md#seafair-haven-milford-haven-filmed-under-way-2012).[27]
