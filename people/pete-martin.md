# Pete Martin

**Pete Martin** (Yahoo handle `randoneur`, e-mail `pdmartin@…`, later also `paradoxpete.pdm@…`, u=25214471) is a UK builder from **Cheltenham, England**, originally from Carrickfergus, Northern Ireland. He started construction of his Paradox on **1 December 2006**, launching her as **[JoHanna](/boats/johanna.md)** on the evening of **11 June 2011** after a near-five-year build.[1][2]

## JoHanna

The full build and launch narrative lives on **[boats/johanna.md](/boats/johanna.md)**; the hull was sheathed and turned on a home-built rolling frame through 2008–09, the rudder leaded and the decks laid, before a 2010 painting campaign repeatedly lost to weather pushed the launch past a hoped-for September 2010 date.[3] After launch, Pete moved his sailing writing to a separate "Paradoxically Speaking" blog.[2] He settled early on leaving out the plans' built-in water-ballast tanks in favor of a flexible bladder tank or old soft-drink bottles topped up with lead and stores, working out a periodic bleach flush to keep a bladder tank's water fresh between uses.[4][5] By 2013 JoHanna carried a De Graaff SB 730/2 ADJ road trailer fitted with flat rubber rollers, and an outboard cage to cut down mainsheet snagging, with the sheet leads later moved from the transom to the aft deck.[6][7]

## A weight-and-displacement breakdown, answering a 2019 payload question

Broke the widely quoted 1,410 lb design figure down concretely — reached only once lead, water, bodies, gear, and stores are all added to the bare hull, not a fixed constant — and, as a lighter-than-average sailor who needs more lead of his own to bring JoHanna down to her marks, put his own empty-hull guess at under 1,000 lb, never having actually weighed it. Full detail: [design/weight-and-ballast.md — independent weight and displacement estimates](/design/weight-and-ballast.md#independent-weight-and-displacement-estimates).[63]

By February 2020 he expected to put JoHanna up for sale later that year, still in the UK; full account at [boats/johanna.md — weighing a sale](/boats/johanna.md#weighing-a-sale).[64]

**Explained the masthead sheave's 60-degree offset, and a furling-friction tip.** Answering a fellow builder's question in 2021, traced the angle to where the halyard actually runs off the sheave — from the mast's starboard, aft side, while the yard raises to port — squaring it to the centreline instead would add friction and make the sail harder to raise and furl; recommended as thick a halyard as practical, split into two gauges if needed, and leather gloves regardless, since a thin line under load will cut the hands. See [mast — why the masthead sheave sits at 60 degrees](/components/rig/mast.md#why-the-masthead-sheave-sits-at-60-degrees-and-the-halyard-sheet-and-reef-line-diameters).[65]
