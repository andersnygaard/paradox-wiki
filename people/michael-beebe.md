# Michael Beebe

**Michael Beebe** is a builder on the **Texas Gulf coast** near Corpus Christi. He built his first [Paradox](/paradox.md), launched at Rockport in February 2012.[1] A second, built a few years later, was sold in January 2017 to **[Bobby (Oklahoma City)](/people/okiebob.md)**, who named her "**[Oogway](/boats/oogway.md)**."[2] He published a recurring "Meanderings" column in *[Messing About In Boats](/references/messing-about-in-boats.md)*, documenting his own build of a Matt-Layden-style lug rig onto a 12 ft Lehman dinghy.[3] Full build accounts: [boats/michael-beebes-first-paradox.md](/boats/michael-beebes-first-paradox.md) and [boats/oogway.md](/boats/oogway.md).

## Two Paradoxes on the Texas coast

She launched at Rockport still unfinished in places, her cabin top left deliberately removable; in his own words, "she sails nice."[1] That first Paradox ran the 2012 Texas 200, filmed making a difficult tack under sail alone, in close quarters, at the Padre Island Yacht Club in Corpus Christi.[4] Beebe pushed her side decks out to the sheer line and lengthened her tiller so he could sit sideways and steer as on a conventional sailboat, a change his second Paradox, built to the stock cabin plan, did not repeat.[5] Full account of the first boat, including her departures from the plans and her eventual sale: [boats/michael-beebes-first-paradox.md](/boats/michael-beebes-first-paradox.md).

A Paradox Tim McKinney acquired in the fall of 2018, built as a convertible with a removable hardtop and conventional tiller steering, may be the same hull as Oogway, since Beebe built only two Paradoxes, though this remains unconfirmed.[6] Beebe had enjoyed sailing her himself with the top off and a short tiller in fair weather. Full account: [people/tim-mckinney.md](/people/tim-mckinney.md#the-boat).

## Meanderings, and a cabin fix

His column ran across several 2016 issues of *Messing About In Boats* and repeatedly referenced Matt Layden's lug rig; it describes his own build of a Matt-Layden-style lug rig onto a 12 ft Lehman dinghy, sailing among a small circle of fellow Paradox owners, with a solo trip toward Cedar Bayou.[3] A cam-cleat fix credited to him turns the downward pull of the halyard and furling-line cleats into a horizontal one, so both can be cleated and uncleated from the seat, easing reefing.[7]
