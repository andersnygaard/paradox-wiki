# Michael Beebe

**Michael Beebe** is a **Rockport, Texas** builder who launched his first [Paradox](/paradox.md) on 25 February 2012, having started construction that July.[1] He went on to build a **second** Paradox, sold in January 2017 to fellow correspondent **[OkieBob](/people/okiebob.md)**, who named her "**[Oogway](/boats/oogway.md)**" — possibly the same hull **[Tim McKinney](/people/tim-mckinney.md)** later owned — and became a recurring "Meanderings" columnist for *[Messing About In Boats](/references/messing-about-in-boats.md)*, where he wrote about building a Matt-Layden-style lug rig onto a 12 ft Lehman dinghy and sailing among a small circle of fellow Paradox owners.[2]

## The first Paradox (2012)

Beebe announced her launch himself: begun that July, she went in the water at Rockport still unfinished in places — the interior not done, the cabin top left unfinished and deliberately built removable — "it's not pristine to be sure but she sails nice."[1] Asked for photographs, he had no easy way to post them from the device he was replying on.[5] That June she ran the **2012 Texas 200**, filmed executing a particularly difficult tack into a strong wind, in close quarters, at the **Padre Island Yacht Club** in Corpus Christi — a maneuver few other boats managed under sail alone, without incident — with the footage posted to YouTube.[6][7]

That first Paradox was itself modified from the stock plan: her side decks were pushed out to the sheer line and her tiller lengthened so Beebe could sit sideways and steer as on a conventional sailboat — a change reported, secondhand, to have cost the side decks' usual storage for the yuloh and mast, and made climbing on and off the cabin top harder; rather than reverting the modification, Beebe sold the boat and built a second one with the cabin to the stock plan, read as a sign the widening had not paid off as hoped, though no complaint of poor visibility from the wider cabin was ever reported, even after several days' sailing together in wet weather.[23]

## A second Paradox, later "Oogway"

By 2016 Beebe had sold that first boat and built a second, built to the stock cabin plan and referenced in his own MAIB "Meanderings" columns alongside the Matt-Layden-style lug rig he built onto a 12 ft Lehman dinghy, repeated mentions of Layden's rig, a fellow Paradox owner, and a solo Paradox trip toward Cedar Bayou.[2] He sold that second Paradox in January 2017 to fellow correspondent [OkieBob](/people/okiebob.md) — the same OkieBob who had tried, unsuccessfully, to buy Beebe's first Paradox back in 2012 — making OkieBob a Paradox owner without ever finishing the one he had spent years building himself, and OkieBob went on to name her "Oogway." Full account of the boat, its modifications, and its sale: [boats/oogway.md](/boats/oogway.md).[25]

That same hull may also surface independently in the archive from a later owner's side: Tim McKinney acquired a Paradox in the fall of 2018 he described as "the second Paradox [Beebe] built," built as a **convertible** with a **removable hardtop** and **conventional tiller steering** in place of the stock rope-and-yoke arrangement — the same removable-cabin-top approach Beebe had already used on his first boat six years earlier. Beebe had enjoyed sailing her himself with the top off and a short tiller in fair weather. Full account of that hull's ownership and McKinney's own modifications: [people/tim-mckinney.md](/people/tim-mckinney.md#the-boat).[3]

## Considering a sale, and the December 2012 listing

In July 2012 Beebe considered selling this first Paradox, noting only that she could be seen sailing in that year's Texas 200.[8] [Dave Bolduc](/people/bolduc/dave.md) posted the tacking video already on record above to save others the trouble of searching for it, remarking that Beebe looked to have had a fun time in the Texas 200.[9] That November, replying to a correspondent's search for a west-coast Paradox to see in person, Beebe advertised his own boat as open to visitors, giving his location as just above Corpus Christi.[10] By December he had listed her formally for $2,000, again pointing to the same Texas 200 footage as evidence of how she sailed;[11] asked to clarify the figure, he confirmed the price and the video's location on the Texas 200 site.[12][13] Asked why he was selling, he explained he and his wife were planning an extended trip to Panama — driving there while pulling a trailerable boat large enough to live and sail in, and bringing a Bolger sailing canoe instead — so the Paradox, "an excellent boat," simply did not fit those plans; he said he would take her himself if he could, and that if she didn't sell, she would wait for him "out back."[14][15] Direct links to the Texas 200 tacking video already on record above were posted for anyone who wanted them, drawing his thanks.[16][17] A Dallas–Fort Worth correspondent asked her location;[18] and a longtime West Coast correspondent — whose own Paradox, "Zoë," had been destroyed by fire in transit the year before (see [boats/zoe.md](/boats/zoe.md#destroyed-by-fire-in-transit-2011)) — offered to buy above the asking price if needed, prompting Beebe to recognize him at once as "the fellow with the burned up in transit" and pass along a phone number, repeated two days later with one digit changed.[19][20][21]

A year later, still unsold, the boat's listing was shared again — sails from Duckworks, marine plywood construction, and "lots of fiberglass" — pitched as a bargain: a completed Paradox selling for less than the materials alone would cost to build.[22]

## A 2015 dockside look at the second Paradox's modifications

A visit to the 2015 Port Aransas PlyWooden Boat Festival gave a fellow builder a guided look at the second Paradox in the flesh, cataloguing several modifications from the stock plan beyond the removable coachroof and lengthened tiller already recorded above: hatches cut into bulkheads #2 and #4, the bulkhead-#4 hatch doubling as the cockpit seat; the portside shelf widened forward to meet the vent trunk, making it triangular rather than rectangular; a tiller run above the aft deck; a metal mast (the first boat's was a repurposed flagpole, possibly this one's too); a simplified tack-strop/spooling arrangement; an all-locker subfloor in place of water ballast tanks; seat cushions arranged to convert between a recliner and a bed; and a leather-look chafe guard for the boom and sail improvised from an ordinary boot fitted over the mast.[24]

## A cam-cleat fix credited to him (2018)

Reorienting the cam cleats where the halyard and furling line enter the cabin — from a downward to a horizontal pull, so both can be cleated and uncleated from the seat, easing reefing — was passed on to the mailing list years later as a trick shown by Beebe himself. Full technique: [components/rig/internal-rigging.md](/components/rig/internal-rigging.md#cam-cleat-orientation-cleating-uncleating-and-reefing-from-the-seat-2018).[26]

## On the Paradox Sailboat Facebook group

Beebe remained active in the Paradox community into the 2020s: a photograph from one of his Facebook group posts was later chosen to represent the group itself — one which fellow owner Johan Bloom noted, with pride, was actually of his own boat "Tetris."[4]
