# Michael Beebe

**Michael Beebe** is a **Rockport, Texas** builder who launched his first [Paradox](/paradox.md) on 25 February 2012, having started construction that July.[1] He went on to build a **second** Paradox, later sold to fellow Texan **[Tim McKinney](/people/tim-mckinney.md)**, and became a recurring "Meanderings" columnist for *[Messing About In Boats](/references/messing-about-in-boats.md)*, where he wrote about building a Matt-Layden-style lug rig onto a 12 ft Lehman dinghy and sailing among a small circle of fellow Paradox owners.[2]

## The first Paradox (2012)

Beebe announced her launch himself: begun that July, she went in the water at Rockport still unfinished in places — the interior not done, the cabin top left unfinished and deliberately built removable — "it's not pristine to be sure but she sails nice."[1] Asked for photographs, he had no easy way to post them from the device he was replying on.[5] That June she ran the **2012 Texas 200**, filmed executing a particularly difficult tack into a strong wind, in close quarters, at the **Padre Island Yacht Club** in Corpus Christi — a maneuver few other boats managed under sail alone, without incident — with the footage posted to YouTube.[6][7]

## A second Paradox, later McKinney's "convertible"

By 2016 Beebe had sold that first boat and built a second, referenced in his own MAIB "Meanderings" columns alongside the Matt-Layden-style lug rig he built onto a 12 ft Lehman dinghy, repeated mentions of Layden's rig, a fellow Paradox owner, and a solo Paradox trip toward Cedar Bayou.[2] That second hull surfaces independently in the archive from its later owner's side: Tim McKinney acquired her in the fall of 2018, describing her as "the second Paradox he built," built as a **convertible** with a **removable hardtop** and **conventional tiller steering** in place of the stock rope-and-yoke arrangement — the same removable-cabin-top approach Beebe had already used on his first boat six years earlier. Beebe had enjoyed sailing her himself with the top off and a short tiller in fair weather. Full account of that hull's ownership and McKinney's own modifications: [people/tim-mckinney.md](/people/tim-mckinney.md#the-boat).[3]

## Considering a sale, and the December 2012 listing

In July 2012 Beebe considered selling this first Paradox, noting only that she could be seen sailing in that year's Texas 200.[8] [Dave Bolduc](/people/bolduc/dave.md) posted the tacking video already on record above to save others the trouble of searching for it, remarking that Beebe looked to have had a fun time in the Texas 200.[9] That November, replying to a correspondent's search for a west-coast Paradox to see in person, Beebe advertised his own boat as open to visitors, giving his location as just above Corpus Christi.[10] By December he had listed her formally for $2,000, again pointing to the same Texas 200 footage as evidence of how she sailed;[11] asked to clarify the figure, he confirmed the price and the video's location on the Texas 200 site.[12][13] Asked why he was selling, he explained he and his wife were planning an extended trip to Panama — driving there while pulling a trailerable boat large enough to live and sail in, and bringing a Bolger sailing canoe instead — so the Paradox, "an excellent boat," simply did not fit those plans; he said he would take her himself if he could, and that if she didn't sell, she would wait for him "out back."[14][15] Direct links to the Texas 200 tacking video already on record above were posted for anyone who wanted them, drawing his thanks.[16][17] A Dallas–Fort Worth correspondent asked her location;[18] and a longtime West Coast correspondent — whose own Paradox, "Zoë," had been destroyed by fire in transit the year before (see [boats/zoe.md](/boats/zoe.md#destroyed-by-fire-in-transit-2011)) — offered to buy above the asking price if needed, prompting Beebe to recognize him at once as "the fellow with the burned up in transit" and pass along a phone number, repeated two days later with one digit changed.[19][20][21]

A year later, still unsold, the boat's listing was shared again — sails from Duckworks, marine plywood construction, and "lots of fiberglass" — pitched as a bargain: a completed Paradox selling for less than the materials alone would cost to build.[22]

## On the Paradox Sailboat Facebook group

Beebe remained active in the Paradox community into the 2020s: a photograph from one of his Facebook group posts was later chosen to represent the group itself — one which fellow owner Johan Bloom noted, with pride, was actually of his own boat "Tetris."[4]
