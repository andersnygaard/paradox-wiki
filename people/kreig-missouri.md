# Kreig (Missouri)

**Kreig**, who signs the mailing list only by that first name, is a builder in **Blue Springs, Missouri**, active on the list from 2020. He finished his own [Paradox](/paradox.md) and sailed her for the first time in the **fall of 2022** — no boat name is on record. Laminating the sheer and chine logs in two staggered-scarf pieces of Douglas fir, seeing no way to bend a single piece into the hull's curve, was among his own build techniques; full detail at [sheer strakes — laminated versus solid sheer and chine logs](/components/sheer-strakes.md#laminated-versus-solid-sheer-and-chine-logs).[1]

## Opening the lifelines question

Once sailing, he found the boat itself gave a strong sense of security, but going out on deck — an occasion he expected to be rare — felt like a different matter entirely, and docking and loading onto the trailer already felt somewhat perilous even before the boat had properly gone to sea. Asking whether anyone had fitted lifelines or a pulpit of any kind opened the archive's first dedicated thread on the question, drawing on-deck safety practice from two established owners. Full account: [personal safety gear and man-overboard preparedness — lifelines and pulpits](/operations/personal-safety-gear.md#lifelines-and-pulpits-a-permanent-bow-to-transom-line-instead).[2]

## Ballast, an outboard, and tacking

Answering back a week later, he reported himself very pleased with the boat and finding no difficulty tacking so long as some momentum is carried into the turn. His own ballast layout: full water ballast plus close to 400 lb of lead, with 32 lb of it forward of bulkhead #1; a Suzuki 2.5 outboard mounted on the port stern lets him carry one fewer 32 lb block of lead on that side; and four further blocks, two per side, sit forward of bulkhead #3 — a layout he judges balances the boat well. This page and [design/weight-and-ballast.md](/design/weight-and-ballast.md#ballast-placement-outboard-vs-centered) are the sole record of a hull that has not been given a name on the mailing list.[3]

## Materials: Douglas fir throughout

Built almost entirely of Douglas fir, including the deck beams, sourced from local lumber yards that had to special-order in the marine-grade Douglas fir plywood — consistent with the Douglas fir he had already used to laminate the sheer and chine logs. He judges the finished boat "extremely strong." Two photographs attached to this message show a beam clamped up in lamination, dated by EXIF to 2017, mid-build, and the finished hull painted white with a teal-and-navy stripe, mast stepped, strapped to a trailer under trees — the only photographic record of this hull on the mailing list, and independent confirmation that a real, completed Paradox stands behind the account.[4]
