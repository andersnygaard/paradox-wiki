# Sean Mulligan

Sean Mulligan owns the Paradox **[Scout](/boats/scout.md)**, sailed out of **Lake Havasu City, Arizona**, originally built and named **"Tardis"** by **[Lezlie Henson](/people/lezlie-henson.md)**. He bought the hull almost finished in 2013, completed the remaining work that summer and launched her that September, sailing her for several seasons before selling her on to **[Chris Curtis](/people/chris-curtis.md)**. He founded the **Havasu Pocket Cruisers Convention**. Full build and ownership record: [boats/scout.md](/boats/scout.md).

## A firefighter's approach to risk

Mulligan is a career firefighter. The discipline of the job carried into his sailing: his department's own risk-management model, recitable by every rank from the newest recruit to the top officers, became the same habit of mind he brought aboard "Scout."[1] Before he settled into small-boat sailing he had already flown for a living, holding commercial pilot, sailplane and hot-air-balloon ratings.[2]

## Finishing what another builder began

Henson had built "Tardis" to a high standard in Texas and sold her almost complete, never having sailed her. Mulligan finished the remaining work that summer and launched her that September as "Scout," the fastest and easiest boat he had ever rigged.[3] He went on to sail her hard: an engine-less fifty-mile night sail named the Bosunator Challenge, after a dog, Bosun, who had accompanied the convention for years,[4] and the 2014 Texas 200.[5] He put the same engine-less sailing into print for a national readership, as "The Upside of Downsizing" in *[Small Craft Advisor](/references/small-craft-advisor.md)*, making the general case for small accessible boats rather than a report of the sail itself.[6]

He credited Henson publicly as Scout's builder and called himself only her custodian.[7] The two later presented the boat together, Henson on the build and Mulligan on the sailing, at Sail Oklahoma in October 2014; see [boats/scout.md](/boats/scout.md#the-builder-and-the-sailor-in-public). He continued recommending Henson's sailmaking to other owners taking on their own boats.[8]

## The Havasu Pocket Cruisers Convention

He founded the **Havasu Pocket Cruisers Convention**, an annual trailer-sailer gathering each February at Lake Havasu City that grew into the largest US trailer-sailer meet of its day and, by his own account, a six-to-seven-month-a-year undertaking. He paused it after the 2013 event, hoping to revive it later.[9] Full record of the convention's growth: [merits/gatherings.md](/merits/gatherings.md#the-havasu-pocket-cruisers-convention-lake-havasu-city-arizona). He credited **[Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)**'s design and **[Dave and Mindy Bolduc](/people/bolduc.md)**'s bringing it to a wider audience online as the reason the design reached builders like himself at all.[10]

## Blog and video work

He documented his sailing on the Blogger blog **[Full n By](/references/full-n-by-blog.md)** (`fullnby.blogspot.com`), later moved to a self-hosted site, with a YouTube channel carrying further Paradox sailing footage and trip accounts.[11]
