# Sean Mulligan

**Sean Mulligan** was the owner of the Paradox **[Scout](/boats/scout.md)** (originally built and named **"Tardis"** by [Lezlie Henson](/people/lezlie-henson.md)), sailed out of **Lake Havasu, Arizona**. He founded the **Havasu Pocket Cruiser's Convention** and, with his wife Jo, also owns and sails a trailer sailer named **"Dauntless."** His purchase of Tardis, his completion of her, her launch as "Scout" on 12 September 2013, and her subsequent launch routine, heavy-wind sailing, and 2014 Texas 200 cruise are recorded in full on [Scout](/boats/scout.md); he later sold her on to **[Chris Curtis](/people/chris-curtis.md)** of Sandpoint, Idaho.

## Background

Away from sailing, Mulligan is a **career firefighter** — he works shift "tours" on a department whose risk-management model he later mapped onto his sailing — and a **licensed commercial pilot** who also holds sailplane, aerobatic commercial-sailplane, and commercial hot-air-balloon ratings, having given aerobatic glider rides to paying passengers and flown corporate advertising balloons before he took up small-boat sailing more seriously.[1][2] The **Havasu Pocket Cruiser's Convention** he founded grew into the largest US trailer-sailer gathering of its day and a six-to-seven-month-a-year undertaking; he paused it after the 2013 event, hoping to revive it in 2015.[3] The event was already well established three years earlier: an August 2010 mailing-list post advertising the 2011 convention (14–21 February, Lake Havasu City) recorded 70 boats the year before and 40 boats and crews from seven Western states and Canada already signed up six months out, toward a goal of 100 or more — posted from the account `nebwest2@…`, matching the YouTube handle already on record below and confirming his own contemporaneous, first-person promotion of the event on the Paradox mailing list; full text at [merits/gatherings.md](/merits/gatherings.md#the-havasu-pocket-cruisers-convention-lake-havasu-city-arizona).[6] By late October, three months before the start, registration had grown to **102 boats and crews from 14 US states and 2 countries**, averaging 535 miles of driving distance.[7] In July 2013, still finishing "Scout" (then still "Tardis"), he reported fitting new trailer plates for her and for his wife's Montgomery 23 "Dauntless," judging himself close enough to finished "to taste it" but expecting the summer heat to push completion into September — a forecast the boat's actual 12 September launch bore out almost exactly; full text at [boats/scout.md](/boats/scout.md#finishing-tardis-and-her-first-launch-as-scout-2013).[8] He sails with his wife **Jo**; their Golden Doodle **Ensign** crews aboard, taken on after the loss of an earlier dog, **Bosun**, for whom Mulligan's Lake Havasu night-sail event, the "Bosunator Challenge," is named.[3]

## Online presence

Mulligan documents his sailing on the Blogger blog **[Full n By](/references/full-n-by-blog.md)** (`fullnby.blogspot.com`) — a first-hand primary source on finishing and sailing "Scout" — a newer self-hosted site (`fullnby.com`, which he moved to in January 2014), and a YouTube channel (`youtube.com/user/nebwest2`) carrying Paradox sailing footage, photos, and trip accounts.[4][5]

## Regard for the design's originators

Encouraging a fellow owner about to start his own Paradox, Mulligan credited [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)'s design and adventures, and Dave and Mindy Bolduc for bringing it forward to the world on the internet — without them, in his view, hardly anyone would ever have known of Matt's designs at all — and called the three of them "pioneers" and "truly in a league of their own."[9]

## Disambiguation

Sean Mulligan is a different person from [Sean Herron](/boats/defiant.md), an unrelated, already-documented Paradox correspondent; the two share only a first name.
