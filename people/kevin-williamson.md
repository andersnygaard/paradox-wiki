# Kevin Williamson

**Kevin Williamson** is a Paradox owner in **Perth, Western Australia**. He had gone to see **[Roselt Croeser](/people/roselt-croeser.md)**'s junk-rigged **"[Planktos](/boats/planktos.md)"** meaning to order plans and build his own, and went back for a second look; a further visit and "a delightful couple of hours sailing" ended instead with him buying Croeser's boat, in his own words becoming "the new custodian of his Paradox."[1] He renamed her "**Skye**" and keeps her sailing out of Perth. Full build and sailing record: [boats/planktos.md](/boats/planktos.md).

## Adapting the rig for trailering

Williamson finds the junk rig easy to use, but it costs more to strike than the lug rig it replaced: the lowered sail bunches up bulkier, and unstepping the mast for a bridge meant untying enough running rigging that doing it afloat looked risky.[2] His fix was a folding mast that lowers the whole rig onto the cabin top and stands it back up again without disconnecting anything, needing only the aft lazy jacks slacked so the sail stack can settle. Left permanently rigged, the boat now goes from trailer to sailing in a few minutes, faster than most conventionally rigged boats, turning the usual objection to a junk rig on a trailer-sailer on its head.[3] He later extended the mast for more sail area and a clearer view forward over the cabin top, worked out with John Hyndman and Graeme Kenyon; the engineering and measurements are at [Junk rig](/design/ideas/junk-rig.md#planktos-the-stock-lug-converted-outcome-not-method).[4]

## Cruising the Swan River

In May 2024 he cruised Skye overnight from Guildford, upriver of Perth, down the Swan River to Fremantle and back, mooring for the night between fallen tree limbs and cooking aboard on a metho burner. Persistent westerlies turned the return short of Fremantle, but he judged the trip worthwhile for what it taught him about camping aboard. Full account: [boats/planktos.md](/boats/planktos.md).[5]

## Practical fixes

A deck hatch forward of the mast, paired with a small screw-in inspection port ahead of the first bulkhead, is his planned fix for the forward compartment's otherwise poor access from inside; detailed at [components/hatch.md](/components/hatch.md#a-foredeck-hatch-and-inspection-port-planned-by-a-later-owner-2024).[6] He treats the deck's non-skid the same way: on a boat this small and this ready to heel underfoot without tipping, the whole topside needs a grippy surface, not just the area around the hatch; see [Non-skid deck finish](/construction/non-skid-deck.md).[7]
