# Roselt Croeser

**Roselt Croeser** is a Paradox builder in **Western Australia** and a member of the **Amateur Boat Builders' Association**, the Perth home-boatbuilding club.[1] He built the Paradox "**[Planktos](/boats/planktos.md)**" from **early 2013 to the end of 2016**, and presented the build and its shakedown cruise to Shark Bay at the association's **April 2021** meeting, the write-up of which is the archive's sole source on him.[1][2]

He appears on the mailing list itself three times: twice before or right at the start of the build — replying to a fellow owner's September 2012 question about junk-rigging a Paradox, he revealed he had already converted a different boat to junk rig and planned to fit one to his own, still-unbuilt Paradox too — a plan carried out four months later, when construction of what became Planktos got under way (see [below](#a-2012-mailing-list-exchange-months-before-the-build)); and again the following March, offering a fellow newcomer a stretching idea and general encouragement (see [below](#a-march-2013-mailing-list-exchange-a-stretching-idea-and-general-encouragement)) — and once more, mid-build, in August 2014, confirming a fellow Western Australian owner's coastal voyages (see [below](#an-august-2014-exchange-confirming-a-fellow-owners-voyages)).[7][8] Otherwise his build ran years after the list era the archive is mostly drawn from, worked up from the plans and — on his own assessment — from the builders' group behind them, which he credited alongside the quality of the plans in judging Paradox "the easiest type of boat to build."[3]

## Approach

The build was run **alongside owner-building a house**, moved through **four different locations**, and was finished deliberately to a **working boat** standard: "very strong, very simple," using whatever was already to hand.[2] That frugality is what makes the build notable — the mast was **repurposed from a sunken boat** and the sail came from an **SS18**, holding the whole build to about **$2500** in materials.[2][3]

He also **converted the boat from the stock lug rig to a junk rig**, making Planktos the only junk-rigged Paradox in the archive built by *converting* the stock lug rather than designing the rig from scratch — the other built-and-sailed junk-rig Paradox on record, [Eerik Mee](/people/eerik-mee.md)'s [THU](/boats/thu.md), was purpose-designed.[1] His verdict on the conversion, and his full account of the build, costs, and how she sails, are recorded at [Planktos](/boats/planktos.md).

## On the group: a sailing life and a builder's counsel

Croeser later joined the [Paradox Sailboat Facebook group](/references/paradox-facebook-group.md) as "Waldo Cruiser," where his posts round out both the sailing of "[Planktos](/boats/planktos.md)" and his settled view of how to build one. He sailed her out of Perth — overnight rendezvous at the north end of **Garden Island**, day-sails from **Mangles Bay** — and reported her real numbers, sustained speeds to 5.5 knots and 80-nautical-mile overnight runs, all drawn out at [Planktos](/boats/planktos.md).[4]

His building advice is consistently that of a pragmatist who has finished a boat and sailed it hard: the Paradox is "one of the easiest boats you can build," best approached without anxiety about a perfect finish because "epoxy can fill the gaps"; construction-grade plywood is fine (and bends more easily than marine grade); he used **12 mm** for the bottom because 18 mm "was too heavy to carry," judging it "bullet proof" once glassed to the plans; and he skips foam insulation entirely, having had moisture build up and rot behind it before, lashing in empty plastic containers for buoyancy instead.[5] He also gave prospective builders a plain cost breakdown, the sail and trailer being the big items and the plywood the least of it.[6] He sold Planktos in July 2023 to **Kevin Williamson**, who kept her junk rig and later extended her mast, renaming her **"Skye"**; Croeser has stayed in the group cheering her on under her new name.[4]

## A 2012 mailing-list exchange, months before the build

Replying to [Alberto Callejon](/people/alberto-callejon.md)'s question whether anyone had sailed a Paradox under a junk rig, Croeser answered no, but that he had already converted an eighteen-foot fibreglass production boat to junk rig himself and planned to fit one to his own Paradox too, confident there are "many different ways to rig a junk rig" and that it could be adapted to suit. The exchange predates his own build by about four months, and the plan stated here is exactly what Planktos became: the archive's only Paradox converted from the stock lug to a junk rig.[7]

## A March 2013 mailing-list exchange: a stretching idea and general encouragement

Replying to a newcomer's plan to enlarge his own Paradox by roughly 20%, Croeser offered a distinct scaling idea from the ones already canvassed on the thread — keep the existing frame spacing and simply stretch the side panels longer, rather than widen the bays between them — alongside general encouragement: experimenting is what boatbuilding is all about, and Matt Layden himself only arrived at the Paradox design after quite a few earlier boats, so there was no reason not to continue that journey. The exchange came either just before or just as his own Planktos build got under way (see [Approach](#approach)).[8]

## An August 2014 exchange: confirming a fellow owner's voyages

Replying on a "Longer Cruises" thread, Croeser confirmed a fellow Western Australian Paradox owner's coastal voyages were genuine, adding that the owner had made a further round trip to the Abrolhos Islands and back to Perth the previous year. Full account at [Small Fry](/boats/small-fry.md#a-distance-and-duration-recorded-for-the-perth-to-shark-bay-passage-and-an-abrolhos-round-trip-dated-to-2013).[9]

## A November 2014 exchange: the bulkhead side curve

Replying on a "Bulkhead side curvature" thread, Croeser gave the plainest answer on record for why a bulkhead's sides carry a slight convex curve: the hull's side panels are themselves curved in the third dimension, and the bulkhead's sides simply follow that shape — "no other reason." Fuller exchange at [hull assembly — why the bulkhead-side curve exists](/construction/hull-assembly-sequence.md#why-the-bulkhead-side-curve-exists-and-fixing-one-already-cut-straight).[10]

## Earlier projects

Planktos was not his first boat. The association's newsletter refers readers to its **June–July 2014** and **April–May 2015** issues for his past projects.[1] Neither issue is held in the archive, so nothing further about those boats is on record.
