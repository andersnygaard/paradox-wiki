# Roselt Croeser

**Roselt Croeser** is a Paradox builder in **Perth, Western Australia**, and a member of the **Amateur Boat Builders' Association**, the Perth home-boatbuilding club. He built the Paradox "**[Planktos](/boats/planktos.md)**" from **early 2013 to the end of 2016**, working through **four different locations** while owner-building a house at the same time, and converted her from the stock lug rig to a **junk rig**.[1][2] The build and Planktos's shakedown cruise to Shark Bay were the subject of a presentation he gave to the association's **April 2021** meeting, following features on two earlier boatbuilding projects in the same newsletter.[1] Planktos was sold in **July 2023** to [Kevin Williamson](/people/kevin-williamson.md), who renamed her "**Skye**" and keeps her sailing in Western Australia under her new name.[3]

## Building "Planktos"

The build ran from early 2013 to the end of 2016, competing for time with a house under construction at the same stretch, and moved through four different locations before finishing to a deliberately frugal, working standard: the mast came from a sunken boat and the sail from another boat, an SS18.[2] A longtime friend's Paradox, "Small Fry," sailed up and down the Western Australian coast by Geoff Davis, was an inspiration to him during the build.[4] Assessed afterward as a building proposition, Paradox was, in his own verdict, "the easiest type of boat to build," credited to the plans' quality and the community of builders behind them.[5] Full build record: [Planktos](/boats/planktos.md).

Planktos was launched in November 2016 in a building 15-knot breeze, still light on ballast and without windows fitted; full account at [Planktos's launch](/boats/planktos.md#launch-november-2016).[6] Two earlier boatbuilding projects, before Planktos, were the subject of separate features in the association's own newsletter.[1]

## The junk rig conversion

An eighteen-foot fibreglass boat had already been converted from its stock rig to junk rig before Planktos was ever laid down, and the same conversion was planned for the still-unbuilt Paradox four months before construction began.[7] The reasoning, restated years later against firsthand experience of a friend's stock-lug Paradox, came down to two frustrations a junk rig removes: a sail that is hard to raise, and one that cannot be reefed fast enough when caught out.[8] Some lug sails twist so badly that only part of the sail is doing any work at all, a fault the earlier eighteen-foot conversion had already proved a junk rig cures.[9]

The conversion's own trade-off, worked out through years of sailing, is more rope and about fifteen minutes' extra rigging time at launch, against never again fighting to raise or lower the sail; the cost is as much social as mechanical, since the rig draws unfamiliar looks on this design, and a stowed sail bundle is bulkier in the lazyjacks than a neatly furled lug sail.[10] A Bermuda rig, weighed against the same conversion, was judged worse on every count for a boat this size: it needs a boom vang, a tight forestay and all the extra gear proper reefing demands, and running downwind the mainsail simply blankets the jib.[11] Full technical account of the conversion, the sheeting system and how she performs under it: [Planktos, the junk rig](/boats/planktos.md#the-junk-rig).

## Sailing "Planktos," and passing her on

Planktos was sailed mostly close to home around Perth, with a shakedown cruise north to Shark Bay; full sailing record at [Planktos, how she sails](/boats/planktos.md#how-she-sails).[1] In 2019 she was towed in after an eight-hour Force 6 ordeal; the lesson drawn from it was that a Paradox's real limit is her skipper's own endurance rather than any fixed number: "The Paradox certainly looks after its skipper." Full exchange at [design/blue-water-suitability.md](/design/blue-water-suitability.md#a-2019-test-case-a-force-6-rescue-and-skipper-endurance-as-the-real-limit) and [Planktos](/boats/planktos.md#a-force-6-rescue-2019).[12]

Planktos was sold in July 2023 to [Kevin Williamson](/people/kevin-williamson.md), who kept her junk rig, extended her mast for more sail area and renamed her "Skye." She remains a junk-rigged Paradox in Western Australia under her new name.[3]
