# David Beard

**David Beard** is a first-time boatbuilder from **Tupelo, Mississippi**, 90 miles south of Memphis.[1] He began building his own [Paradox](/paradox.md) around 2000 and completed the bulkheads before the build stalled; he was back on her within a few months.[2][3] In November 2001, still partway through his own hull, he bought a secondhand Paradox first built and sold out of Steinhatchee, Florida, meaning to sail her while he finished his own,[4][5] then set the scratch build aside again once he had a finished Paradox in hand to study.[6] He refitted the secondhand boat through 2002, naming her **"[Whisper](/boats/whisper.md)"**,[7] and went on to become one of the archive's most prolific and enthusiastic contributors, active from 2001 into 2016.

## Building "Whisper," and a Paradox begun from scratch

On his own hull, David planned from early on to leave the first bulkhead solid and watertight,[3] and settled two questions once he had "Whisper" in hand to study first: he skipped the plans' built-in water tanks in favor of small containers plus permanent ballast, and chose a plywood other than checking-prone fir for the sides and bottom.[6] Studying an already-finished Paradox in person, he found, answered many of his own building questions before he ever had to ask them.[8] Outfitting "Whisper" produced two small, durable fixes of his own: a discarded wheelchair cushion that eliminated helm-seat fatigue entirely, and, years later, a child's beanbag chair he judged the best seating solution he had found.[9][10]

## Chine runners, rudder work, and other hands-on fixes

David built his own chine runners early on, using templates, epoxy fill for the low spots, and a hand plane for the high.[11] Years later, once "Whisper"'s own runners had fallen far out of shape, a careful rebuild brought them within 1-2 mm of correct and transformed her performance, a persistence rewarded in full on [Whisper](/boats/whisper.md#chine-runners-rebuilt-after-the-shakedown-cruise).[12][13] He cast his own glassed-epoxy lower rudder fitting, working out how to get the heavy roving to lie down in the mold,[14] and separately raised the rudder's pivot point on the transom centerline.[15] One notable discovery, that "Whisper" seemed to sail better with her rudder left up, sent him testing a possible fifty-percent reduction in rudder size.[16] He built his own yuloh too, comparing its curved blade against Matt Layden's straighter, harder-on-the-wrist design.[17][18] Two recurring hardware failures on his own boat he chased down and solved for good: a tack tie-down strut that kept failing under compression, ended by switching to bronze Schedule 40 pipe,[19] and a stock mainsheet/topping-lift tang that bent under load, stopped by bolting three tangs together in place of the single stock part.[20]

## Community and mentorship

A self-taught first-time builder who credited the community with making the build possible,[21] David also credited [Don Elliott](/people/don-elliott.md)'s "Building the Paradox" articles as essential reading,[22] and, still learning himself, told a hesitant newcomer "Sure you can!"; Paradox had been his own first boat too.[23] He took a deliberate motor-free yuloh trip early on, crediting [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)'s own view that an engine is fine as a luxury but should never be depended on, and signed off "Thanks Matt !!"[24] He filmed "Whisper"'s own refit and shakedown cruise, sailing, cooking and sleeping aboard, to show newcomers what Paradox cruising actually looks like, and offered copies to anyone who wanted one; full account at [Whisper](/boats/whisper.md#history-and-refit).[25]

He was also the first to reply to designer Matt Layden's own first-ever message, addressing him directly as a fellow builder rather than in the third person,[26] and repeatedly opened his home waters near Tupelo to visiting builders over the years, once inviting a Dallas-area builder to come and see a finished Paradox for himself.[27] Ahead of the 2007 season he laid out a visit for a builder from South Alabama on his own way to Cedar Key, staying aboard "Whisper" the whole time.[28] When a community-written Build Manual opened for contributions he volunteered to write the yuloh section,[29] and later, having missed a magazine's own sea-trial article on "Zoë" in print, prompted a copy to be made available to other builders.[30] He urged builders to use proper respiratory protection when working with epoxy: "You can protect yourself !! Don't let it stop you from building."[31] He also opened a recurring design discussion comparing Paradox's coastal-cruiser classification against Sven Yrvind's blue-water Bris designs.[32]

## A cruising life on the Tenn-Tombigbee, and letting "Whisper" go

During a week's cruise on "Whisper" early on, tacking without motor assistance proved a genuine struggle, and a drunk jet-skier collided with her at anchor one night, doing only cosmetic damage, which David called proof "of the sound design of the hull."[33] In September 2006 he planned a roughly 300-mile winter delivery cruise for "Whisper" down the Tennessee-Tombigbee Waterway, running a 2 hp Honda outboard as main power the whole way; the full route and reliability record are at [Whisper — a winter Tenn-Tom Waterway delivery cruise](/boats/whisper.md#a-winter-tenn-tom-waterway-delivery-cruise).[34][35] That December he took "Whisper" on a solo, deliberately "electronic free" Christmas cruise down the same waterway, from Pickwick Lake, Tennessee, to Amory, Mississippi: "a Christmas present to myself." A burst of test messages from a new account was confirmed around the same time as his own: a computer crash and a change of internet provider had left him struggling to get set up again.[36] In 2013 he planned a further extended trip as an early-retirement present to himself, working out a route through the southern Louisiana bayous toward the Great Loop; full account at [Whisper](/boats/whisper.md#a-2013-location-check-and-two-unfulfilled-cruising-ideas).[37]

Twice, years apart, David considered converting "Whisper" into a motor-only "power sharpie" for local rivers rather than selling her, unable to bring himself to part with a boat that "motors great."[38][39] In 2007 he briefly considered trading her for a used Peep Hen, since he and his son had outgrown her, but decided against it, since a second Paradox of his own was still partly built and he was not ready to sell "Whisper" outright.[40] He renewed the same trade offer three years later, again without a taker.[41] By January 2016 he announced he was moving up from a Paradox to do some long-range cruising,[42] and in the years that followed he did sell "Whisper," a decision he came to regret: "Sold my Paradox, big mistake," he wrote in 2024, and later, "Any out there for sale? I miss mine."[43]

## Disambiguation

David Beard corresponded under two accounts over the years: one used from 2001 through mid-2008, and a second taken up after a computer crash and a change of internet provider in December 2008, confirmed as the same person by his own words and by the continuity of a specific, repeated trade offer for his boat.[36]

He is not [David LeBlanc](/people/david-leblanc.md), a different member entirely; the two are easily confused in the raw archive, since Beard's own Paradox is named "Whisper" and LeBlanc's own list account is confusable with that name for unrelated reasons of its own. Every citation on this page attributing "Whisper" to David Beard is confirmed from the source messages' own headers, not from the boat name alone. He is also not a third, similarly spelled member, David Beede, who separately asked about plans in the autumn of 2001 (see [building plans](/references/building-plans.md)).
