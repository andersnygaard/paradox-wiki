# David Beard

**David Beard** is a first-time boatbuilder from **Tupelo, Mississippi**, 90 miles south of Memphis.[1] He began building a Paradox from scratch around 2000 and had her bulkheads finished before the build paused; within a few months he was back on her, and in November 2001 he bought a secondhand Paradox as well, meaning to sail her while he carried on with his own hull.[2][3] The secondhand boat had been built and sold by **Bob Archibald** of Steinhatchee, Florida.[4] He refitted her through 2002 and named her **"[Whisper](/boats/whisper.md)"** that spring, then went on cruising and improving her for well over a decade while his own hull stayed a parallel, unfinished project. Full build and refit account: [boats/whisper.md](/boats/whisper.md).

## Building "Whisper," and a Paradox begun from scratch

Studying Whisper in person, once he had her, answered many of his own building questions before he had to ask them, and settled the only two changes he carried into his own hull: no built-in water tanks in favor of small containers and permanent ballast, and a plywood other than checking-prone fir for the sides and bottom.[5] Outfitting Whisper produced two small, lasting fixes of his own: a discarded wheelchair cushion that ended helm-seat fatigue outright, and, years later, a child's beanbag chair he judged the best seating solution he had found.[6][7] See [crew size and cabin fit](/operations/crew-size-and-fit.md) and [interior fit-out](/components/interior.md) for the fuller comfort record.

He built his own chine runners using templates, epoxy fill for the low spots and a hand plane for the high, and years later, once Whisper's own runners had fallen out of shape, a careful rebuild brought them within 1 to 2 mm of correct and transformed her performance; full account at [Whisper](/boats/whisper.md#chine-runners-rebuilt-after-the-shakedown-cruise).[8] He cast his own glassed-epoxy lower rudder fitting, raised the rudder's pivot point on the transom centerline and built his own yuloh, comparing its curved blade against Matt Layden's straighter, harder-on-the-wrist design.[9][10][11] Two recurring hardware failures on Whisper he chased down and solved for good: a tack tie-down strut that kept failing under compression, ended by switching to bronze pipe, and a stock mainsheet tang that bent under load, stopped by bolting three tangs together in its place.[12][13] See [chine runner construction](/chine-runners/construction.md), [rudder bearing materials](/construction/rudder-bearing-materials.md) and [the yuloh](/components/yuloh.md) for the full technical accounts.

## Community and mentorship

A self-taught first-time builder, David relied on [Don Elliott](/people/don-elliott.md)'s "Building the Paradox" articles as essential reading during his own build.[14][15] He treated epoxy dust and fumes as a real hazard, in his own words: "You can protect yourself !! Don't let it stop you from building."[16] He contributed the yuloh section when fellow builders compiled a shared Build Manual, and repeatedly opened his home waters near Tupelo to visiting builders, hosting sails aboard Whisper at Pirates Cove, Alabama and welcoming a Dallas-area builder who wanted to see a finished Paradox for himself.[17][18][19] He also filmed Whisper's refit and shakedown cruise to show what Paradox cruising looks like; full account at [Whisper](/boats/whisper.md#history-and-refit).[20]

## A cruising life on the Tenn-Tombigbee, and letting "Whisper" go

Whisper carried David on years of cruising along the **Tennessee-Tombigbee Waterway**. In September 2006 he ran a roughly 300-mile winter delivery cruise for her down the Tenn-Tom, using a 2 hp Honda outboard as main power the whole way; full route and reliability record at [Whisper](/boats/whisper.md#a-winter-tenn-tom-waterway-delivery-cruise).[21] That December he took her on a solo, deliberately electronic-free Christmas cruise down the same waterway, from Pickwick Lake, Tennessee, to Amory, Mississippi, in his own words "a Christmas present to myself."[22] A drunk jet-skier collided with Whisper at anchor one night on an earlier cruise, doing only cosmetic damage that he took as proof of the hull's soundness.[23] In 2013 he planned a further extended trip as an early-retirement present to himself, working out a route through the southern Louisiana bayous toward the Great Loop; full account at [Whisper](/boats/whisper.md#a-2013-location-check-and-two-unfulfilled-cruising-ideas).[24] By 2016 he had moved on from Paradox toward longer-range cruising, and Whisper was eventually sold; he came to regret it, saying afterward that he missed having a Paradox of his own.[25]
