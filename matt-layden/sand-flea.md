# Sand Flea

**Sand Flea** is an **8-foot cruising dinghy** built by [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md), the seventh boat in his documented build order — after [Enigma](/matt-layden/enigma.md) and before [Elusion](/matt-layden/elusion.md), his two other post-hiatus micro-cruiser designs. She is not a Paradox hull.[1]

## Design particulars

An illustrated survey gives the fullest design account on record: Sand Flea is **8 ft by 3 ft 9 in**, a **double-chined pram dinghy** with a deck, entered in the **2007/8 WaterTribe Everglades Challenge**.[2]

| Particular | Value |
|---|---|
| LOA | 8 ft |
| Beam | 3 ft 9 in |
| Empty weight | 85 lb |
| Sail area | 45 sq ft |

Notable details:[2]

- A **single leeboard on her starboard side**, in place of the [chine runners](/chine-runners/how-they-work.md) used on most of Matt's other designs — independently corroborated by a fellow WaterTribe racer comparing Sand Flea against Elusion's own runners.[15]
- A **sliding hatch** that can completely cover the rectangular cockpit, making the boat lockable and possibly watertight; a later photo shows her fitted with a **dodger** in place of the sliding hatch.
- A **furling, high-peaked lugsail**, 45 sq ft, on a mast, yard, and boom each about **8 ft** long, stowable on the port side deck "without extending beyond the bow and stern."
- Cut with a noticeably **larger roach** than the stock Paradox or Little Cruiser lug — a deliberately different-looking sail, visible in the group's photo section.[7]
- Like [Paradox](/paradox.md), a **raisable rudder** with the tiller passing through a sealed port in the transom.
- Secondary propulsion is a **pair of oars**, stowed on the starboard side deck.
- Like [Enigma](/matt-layden/enigma.md), a pair of **attachable wheels for portaging**.

## Matt Layden's own announcement of Sand Flea (February 2007)

Sand Flea is one of the very few boats in this archive whose launch is recorded **in the designer's own words**. On 23 February 2007, days after the boat's first sail, Matt Layden posted an announcement to the WaterTribe discussion board that was relayed the same day to the [messing-about.com forums](/references/messing-about-forum.md), where it survives verbatim:[11]

> Class 4 beware; this is to announce that I'm changing my entry for EC2007 from Enigma, the 12' boat I sailed in last year's UFC (and current single division record holder for the EC), to a new and even more advanced super-go-fast racer that I'm finishing up as I write (well the epoxy is hardening on the last parts anyway). On the theory that the less boat you have to drag around the faster you can go, I'm continuing my trend of several years in bringing out a shorter boat than last year's. Sand Flea is an 8' decked pram, 45" beam, 85 lbs empty weight, 45 sqft sail area. Sand Flea hit the water for her trial sail yesterday and let me tell you, she is blazing fast! Still, in spite of her obvious superior speed capability I plan on reining her in and letting a few other competitors finish ahead of her this year, just out of the kindness of my heart... Will be leaving tomorrow or Sunday to sail in to Ft DeSoto; see you all in a week! Matt

Three things in it are worth separating from the joke. It **independently corroborates three of the four particulars above** from the designer himself — 8 ft, 85 lb empty, 45 sq ft — and gives the beam as **45 inches**, which is the same figure as the 3 ft 9 in recorded above, stated in inches. It **dates her first sail to 22 February 2007**, a fortnight before that year's Everglades Challenge. And it states, in one sentence, the design reasoning behind the whole late sequence of Matt's boats: **"the less boat you have to drag around the faster you can go"** — a portaging argument rather than a sailing one, and an explicit statement that the shrinking length of his race boats was a deliberate multi-year trend rather than a series of one-offs. The same post is the archive's only record that he **sailed the boat to the start**, from his home in Stuart round to Fort De Soto.[11]

A fellow competitor's mid-race description confirms the layout from outside: a **multichine pram almost totally decked over, with a single leeboard** — consistent with the illustrated survey above, and adding that at that point in the race she was crossing Florida Bay in company with **seven other boats, all at least twice her size**.[12]

## Race record

Matt built Sand Flea after Enigma and — remarkably for an 8-foot boat — raced her competitively:

- Entered in **two 300-mile Watertribe Everglades Challenges**, in **2007** and **2008**.
- Entered in the **2012 Ultra Marathon**.
- In each of these races she finished **"near the top,"** consistent with the strong race results recorded for Matt's other small boats — see [Enigma](/matt-layden/enigma.md) (1st in class, 2006 Watertribe Ultimate Challenge) and [Elusion](/matt-layden/elusion.md) (1st in class, 2010 Watertribe Ultimate Challenge).

In the 2007 Everglades Challenge specifically, racing under the WaterTribe name "Wizard," Matt sailed Sand Flea solo, paused mid-race to rescue a capsized kayaker, and still finished less than half a day behind a crewed boat.[3]

An eyewitness at that same race adds the fullest picture on record of how an 8-foot boat is actually campaigned over 300 miles. Offered a bunk in another competitor's camper the night before the start, Matt **declined and slept aboard**, in a cove near the beach; the next morning he was seen **wheeling the boat through the parking lot** to the beach rather than sail it round in the dark — the observer guessing, correctly as it turned out, that the wheels were being carried in preparation for the following year's Ultimate Challenge. At the Chokoloskee checkpoint he crossed the mud flats by **poling**, keeping at it "right along on the mud" down to **8–10 inches** of water, and did it **barefoot** rather than in technical boots. He spent 30–40 minutes at the checkpoint, and shared a few changes he had in mind for the boat.[13] Over the race he **slept at anchor every night**, one night to the extent of four and a half hours — which his wife characterized as oversleeping.[14]

Photographs of the 2012 race circulating afterward on a WoodenBoat Forum thread show Matt smiling at the start — rare enough in the record to be worth noting.[8] Firsthand observation from the same race weekend confirmed Sand Flea sailed that year unchanged except for a newly added dodger, distinct from the 1,200-mile Ultimate Florida Challenge circumnavigation Matt had sailed in Elusion two years before — and noted Matt was, by then, occupied with a demanding new job.[9][10]

Set alongside Matt's other designs, Sand Flea reads as "similar, but shorter and wider" than Paradox — "an audacious little cruiser — one that stretches the imagination, not the budget" — and in Matt's hands she sailed **hundreds of miles on the shallow waters of the Everglades**.[4]

## Whether plans will ever be released

As of late 2009, whether Sand Flea's plans might ever be released to the public remained an open question: one newcomer recalled seeing something suggesting Matt intended to make them available eventually, but nothing further had surfaced since, and by then he was thought likely occupied with Enigma instead.[5]

## Sized against other beginner dinghies

Sand Flea's own scale invites comparison against the many stepping-stone dinghies newcomers build before attempting something Paradox-sized: she is judged **broadly similar in size to the numerous 8-foot pram dinghies** for which plans are widely available, with such a pram itself a much quicker project than either Sand Flea or Paradox — useful for gauging whether a bigger project is worth pursuing at all before committing to one. The **PD Racer** (`pdracer.com`) is one specific pram-style design recommended on this basis: simple, finishable in many different ways, and backed by an active builder community and its own Yahoo group.[6]
