# Elusion

**Elusion** is [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)'s newest micro-cruiser[1] — the most recent boat in the documented Layden build order, following [Terrapin](/matt-layden/terrapin.md), [Swamp Thing](/matt-layden/swamp-thing.md), [GJAC](/matt-layden/gjac.md), [Little Cruiser](/matt-layden/little-cruiser.md), the [Paradox](/paradox.md), [Enigma](/matt-layden/enigma.md), and [Sand Flea](/matt-layden/sand-flea.md). She is not a Paradox hull but shares much of Enigma's construction and design — itself a boat that blends [Little Cruiser](/matt-layden/little-cruiser.md)'s hull shape with [Paradox](/paradox.md)'s furling rig and [chine runners](/chine-runners/how-they-work.md).

## A lighter, smaller Enigma

Elusion is similar to Enigma in construction and design, but smaller and lighter still — about **120 lbs**, light enough to be realistically transported on the roof of a vehicle, making her even easier to cartop than Enigma.[2]

## Design particulars

| Particular | Imperial |
|---|---|
| LOA | 9 ft |
| Beam | 38" |
| Draft | ~6" |
| Sail area | not given |
| Displacement | ~360 lbs |

Sail area is not given.[3] The ~360 lb displacement and the ~120 lb "roof-transportable" weight most likely represent laden/design displacement versus a lighter unladen hull weight, following the same empty-vs-laden pattern documented for [Enigma](/matt-layden/enigma.md).

Interesting details, matching the same set used for Paradox and Enigma: **chine runners**; **stand-up paddle auxiliary**; **self righting**; **positive buoyancy**; **all lines lead into cabin**.[3]

## An unusual auxiliary: a stand-up paddle

Where the rest of the Layden lineage relies on a **sculling oar** or yuloh for auxiliary propulsion (see [yuloh and stowed-rig retention](/components/yuloh.md)), Elusion is the exception: her auxiliary is not the usual sculling oar but instead a **stand-up paddle**.[4]

## Racing history

In **2010**, Matt entered Elusion in the **1,200-mile Watertribe Ultimate Florida Challenge** — the demanding circumnavigation race around Florida in which Enigma had won her class back in 2006 — and **placed first in his class** once again, at one point during the race leading Class 4 (Small Sailboats).[4][5] She finished **third overall** among the race's three leaders, behind two purpose-built Kruger paddling canoes, and was photographed with them at the finish back at Fort DeSoto Park — the one record that confirms a 9-foot boat completed the full circumnavigation, portage included.[25] Word of the new boat's debut travelled beyond microcruising.com while the race was still under way: a UK Paradox owner following along on the mailing list was already declaring his own interest in building an Elusion next, once he parted with the boat he currently sailed.[16] Five days after the mailing list's own account of the race closed, the same UK owner pointed the list to a fuller retrospective on the new boat, published that day and already "causing quite a stir."[17] Recalled two years afterward, one competitor's impression from the day before the start was that Elusion had had little if any test sailing before the race began.[30]

## Paradox, Enigma, and Elusion compared: three sisters, three ballasting strategies

Paradox, Enigma, and Elusion are described as **"sisters"** — Paradox the eldest of the three designs, Elusion the youngest and by far the smallest, at **9 ft by 38 in** against Paradox's 13 ft 10 in.[5] The three share a family resemblance but ballast themselves three different ways: **Paradox** carries fixed lead plus water ballast; **Enigma** carries bagged sand plus cruising gear; **Elusion** carries cruising gear, water, and crew weight, with no fixed lead at all.[5] Like Paradox and Enigma, Elusion is **epoxy-and-GRP-sheathed plywood** construction — a builder comparing all three lug-rigged Layden designs against an unsheathed roof-rack cruiser found this the one construction feature all three shared.[6]

The three designs are also read as one continuous expression of a single design philosophy, carried furthest in the smallest and newest boat: Matt is understood to have "concentrated on being super-efficient at reducing friction" rather than relying on waterline length for speed, so his boats carry **no standing rigging and few external halyards, downhauls, and the like**, and he avoids fitting a topping lift at all.[5]

Set against Paradox specifically, in a comparative appraisal by an owner who had sailed one of Matt's designs for years, Elusion is judged **"a little easier to manage"**: she is transportable on the roof of a car, and her removable wheels slot into the sides of the hull for portaging — though a road trailer would still be preferred where practical. The same appraisal judges Elusion **"not as foolproof when at sea as 'Paradox', on account of being more dependent on the ability of the crew for keeping her upright when the going gets tough."**[7] A fellow WaterTribe racer who compared all three hulls directly judged Elusion's own chine runners rather short, but proportionately about the same as Paradox's, since Elusion is a heavy boat for her size once her own crew and gear are stowed.[31]

## Not to be confused with "Illusion"

A separately built, similarly named hull — "Illusion," owned and later offered for sale by another builder, missing Elusion's cabin top — is "very similar" to Elusion but is not a Matt Layden design; it is a homage built by an admirer, not a sister boat.[9] Elusion's real family resemblance to Paradox has been described directly, in Matt's own boat-family terms, as a "baby sister Paradox."[10] Adding a further wrinkle, mailing-list correspondents following the 2010 Ultimate Florida Challenge itself sometimes spelled Elusion's own name as "Illusion" — a simple misspelling of the same boat, not a reference to either of the other two distinct hulls above.[14] The same misspelling persisted after the race, surfacing in a proposal for a folding version of the boat whose stated dimensions track Elusion's own published spec sheet rather than Don Elliott's differently shaped design.[18]

## On the starting beach beside Enigma (March 2010)

A competitor's survey of the Everglades Challenge starting beach at Fort DeSoto that same March catches Elusion at her public debut, sitting a few yards from the boat she succeeded: "Wizard (Matt Layden), known for his very small cruising boat designs, is here with his new Elusion, a 9 foot cruiser, and his older design, Enigma, is also on the beach for the Everglades Challenge, skippered by its new owner, Freebyrd (David Bolduc)."[26] The two boats being drawn up on the same sand is what let a side-by-side comparison of their chine runners be made at all — Dave Bolduc's own reading of that comparison, that Elusion's runners run about as long as Paradox's and so needed no mast rake to balance her, is at [chine runners — how they work](/chine-runners/how-they-work.md). The same beach held two further Layden-designed craft that morning: the **Tridarka Raider** under a new owner with a bigger sail, and an **expedition sailboard** sailed by Windwalker (Trey Flemer).[26]

## First archive sighting (February 2010)

Before her Ultimate Florida Challenge debut, Elusion's construction and early sea trials were already circulating outside microcruising.com: Dave Bolduc's own description of the new boat, cross-posted to the Paradox builders mailing list in February 2010, judged she looked much like a Paradox/Enigma 460 crossed with long chine runners, a wide stern, and a long rudder blade, expecting excellent directional stability from the combination. At that stage she was being trial-sailed with Sand Flea's own sail while a new sail, to use a roller furling drum, was still being made; the trial photographs also appeared to show a spray skirt rather than a hatch closing the cockpit, though that detail was a guess from the photographs rather than a confirmed specification.[11] The same photographs drew a closer look at the rudder itself: a small end plate on the cheek plate, visible only on the port side opposite the swing-up rudder mechanism, was at first taken for a trick of the light but confirmed on close inspection of an enlarged image — the same [boarding step](/components/rudder.md#sailing-to-windward-with-the-rudder-raised-and-boarding-from-a-raised-step) Paradox herself carries on her own rudder, for climbing back aboard after a swim.[12][13]

## The 2010 Ultimate Florida Challenge: where the race record lives

The list followed the race itself hour by hour for eight days — a dockside inspection of the boat on the eve of the start, the three-way lead battle with Whitecaps and Manitou Cruiser, tracker-measured speeds of 4.5 to 4.8 knots with bursts above 5, the shift from sailing to paddling up the St. Marys River, and the forty-mile portage where the archive's account ends. That day-by-day record is an account of the race and its whole fleet rather than of this boat's design, and lives at [merits/ultimate-florida-challenge-2010.md](/merits/ultimate-florida-challenge-2010.md).

## A final word-play footnote, and a firsthand transom-lettering memory

The word-play speculation drew one more reply: recalling having watched Matt letter the boat's name onto her transom in person, one correspondent found it brought to mind the late Don Elliott's own cartoons and his particular love for this kind of boat, judging Elliott would have been pleased to see the idea realized — a personal reflection offered without resolving whether the name itself was ever intended as a nod to Elliott's own unfinished "Illusion" design, which remains, as before, an unconfirmed guess rather than a stated fact.[15]

## A proposed folding, kayak-construction variant (April 2010)

A folding, kayak-inspired variant was proposed in early April 2010: fuse Tom Yost's folding-kayak construction method — an aluminum-tubing skeleton under a vinyl-canvas skin, extended to a cabin fitted with clear vinyl windows — onto Elusion's own hull, at dimensions of roughly 9 ft by 3 ft closely tracking her actual 9 ft LOA and 38 in beam.[18] Folded down, such a boat could travel in the trunk of a car, or be flown to a destination and assembled on arrival. The idea drew immediate encouragement as one worth attempting by a correspondent judged to have the skills for it, though no build of a folding Elusion is recorded.[19]

The name itself caused confusion for at least one reader searching the designer's own reference site later that month: unable to find "Illusion" there, only "Enigma,"[20] he corrected himself a few hours later to the alternative spelling "Elusion,"[21] which a further correspondent confirmed the same day was accurate — a new Matt Layden design not yet posted to the [microcruising.com](/references/microcruising-website.md) web page as of mid-April 2010.[22]

## Continued interest, and a first outside hybrid design (May 2010)

A month later, a question about whether anyone had gotten serious about building an Elusion, with plans possibly sketched up and posted to the group's files section, drew word of a first such project: a 10 ft boat of its own design, arrived at after reading and studying as much material on Elusion, Enigma, and Paradox as could be found, and judged strongly influenced by all three — no boat name given, and whether the result would work out as well as hoped left for time to tell.[23][24]

## A 2018 renewal of interest, and a Freeship pointer

Interest in building an Elusion instead of a Paradox recurred again in 2018, asked directly of the list: had anyone tried using the Paradox plans to attempt an Elusion?[27] The answer, from a builder who had ordered and received his own Paradox plans a decade earlier: "virtually yes," pointing to **Freeship** — a free naval-architecture hull-design program — as what would be needed to open the file for such an adaptation.[28] Unable to run Freeship himself, the questioner reported being glad others were pursuing an Elusion regardless, and — having just received his own Paradox plans — hoped to work out a smaller design of his own from them.[29]
