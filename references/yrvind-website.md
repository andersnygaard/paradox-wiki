# yrvind.com (Sven Yrvind's website)

**yrvind.com** (`https://www.yrvind.com/`) is the personal website and blog of **[Sven Yrvind](/people/yrvind.md)** — a Swedish designer and ocean voyager known for building and sailing extraordinarily small blue-water boats. It is an **authored, first-person primary source**, and an unusually authoritative one on the [chine runner](/chine-runners/index.md): its author personally knew [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md), sailed [Paradox](/paradox.md) for a month in 1997 (and again in 2003, 2006, 2007), and corresponded with Matt weekly for years. Where it covers Matt's boats, their dimensions, or the runner's history, it is treated as high-quality primary evidence; where it advances a personal theory (e.g. the "deflector, not hydrofoil" mechanism), it is recorded as **hypothesis**, clearly attributed.

A bare link to `www.yrvind.com` was twice passed along for photos of the boats and building work shown there.[1]

## Provenance and dating

Unlike the effectively-frozen [microcruising.com](/references/microcruising-website.md), yrvind.com is a **live, actively-updated blog** — new boat projects and essays continued to be posted well beyond the pages cited here. The four pages cited for this bundle span a wide date range: a **2008** essay and photo gallery on Paradox and Enigma, a **2008** post on Matt's chine-runner build, a **2009** posting of an extract from Matt's *Small Craft Advisor* interview, and a **2021** post on near-vertical ballasted runners.

## Continuing interest, and confirming the blog runs the whole way back

The list continued to notice fresh posts on the site over the years. One update, spotted in early 2009, drew a wish that its past history were kept in a separate archive, since only the newest entries seemed visible at first glance,[2] alongside admiration for the boat as a kind of "top-of-the-line" Paradox and anticipation for its coming sea trials and chine-runner verdict.[3] Checked directly, that wish proved mistaken: the blog was confirmed to already run the whole way back to the very start of the build being followed, not only its newest entries — an impression corrected once the site was revisited.[4][5]

## The pages cited

Together, the four pages cited below carry some 101 photographs of Matt Layden's and Sven Yrvind's chine-runner boats.[6]

- **"The near-vertical ballasted chinerunners"** (2021) — `https://www.yrvind.com/the-near-verticalballasted-chinerunners/`: a deflector hypothesis and the seven reasons behind the ballasted, near-vertical runners of the YRVIND ½.
- **"Matt Laydens chinerunners based on Swamp Thing, Paradox & Enigma, etc."** (2008) — `https://www.yrvind.com/matt-laydens-chinerunners-based-on-swamp-thing-paradox-enigma-etc/`: Swamp Thing as the first chine-runner boat, "the most important innovation for small boats during the 20th century," photos of Matt's Paradox and Enigma runners, and a composite build method.
- **"Paradox & Enigma"** (2008) — `https://www.yrvind.com/videophoto/outher-boats/enigma/`: a long essay on shallow-draft cruising, voyaging history, Matt's boats and their dimensions, the 1997 month sailing Paradox, and the "greatest innovation of the century" verdict, with an extensive Enigma photo gallery.
- **"Present project"** (2009) — `https://www.yrvind.com/present_project.html`: hosts an extract of Matt Layden's May/June 2009 *Small Craft Advisor* interview, describing the chine-runner system's function in his own words.[7]
