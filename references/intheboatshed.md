# intheboatshed.net (Gavin Atkin's weblog)

**intheboatshed.net** is a long-running UK weblog written by **Gavin Atkin**, a boat-design writer, describing itself as a weblog "for the sort of people who like looking inside boat sheds… about old boats, traditional boats, boat building, restoration, the sea and the North Kent Coast." It covered the British small-boat scene closely through the years the UK [Paradox](/paradox.md) fleet was being built and sailed, and carries eleven posts of interest here.

Its value is that it is **outside coverage**. Almost everything the archive holds on the UK boats comes from the sailors themselves — the mailing list, [Bill's Log](/references/bills-log.md), [Alastair Law's own site](/references/little-jim-website.md), [Pete Martin's build blog](/references/pete-martin-blog.md). Atkin has no stake in any of it. Where he reports the same events, he does so contemporaneously and independently, and he occasionally records things the participants never wrote down.

Its limits follow from the same fact. Atkin is **secondary on everything he reports about the boats**: he is reading Bill's Log and relaying it, or recalling a show he attended. He gets names wrong — "Matt Leyden" throughout the 2014 post, "Alistair Law" in 2010, "Bilol Serjeant" in a 2013 tag. Where he and a participant's own account differ, the participant's account governs, per the bundle's [source-priority order](/paradox.md). His photographs and his own show-floor observations, by contrast, are primary.

## Third-party attestation of the 2008 Scilly cruise

Three posts follow [Faith](/boats/faith.md)'s 2008 cruise as it happened, and together they are the only account of it by someone who was not on it.

Writing **6 April 2008**, mid-cruise, Atkin describes Bill Serjeant sailing his "remarkable little heavyweight 14-ft *Paradox* cruiser *Faith*" from **Burnham on Crouch** along the South Coast to the West Country, and states the purpose plainly: **to meet and sail in company with another *Paradox* sailor, Al Law**. This is contemporaneous outside evidence that the rendezvous with [Little Jim](/boats/little-jim.md) was the plan from the outset rather than something arranged en route. He adds the boat's progress to that date, read off Bill's Log — an anchorage in the **Swale**, then out along the **North Kent Coast**, then a couple of days weatherbound in **Ramsgate Harbour**.[1]

On **7 May 2008** he records the outward arrival at **Plymouth**, calling it an "epic 14ft boat voyage" and Bill "brave."[2] On **19 May 2008**, two days after the event, he records the arrival at the **Scilly Isles** — "at the end of his epic cruise starting from the coast of Essex, near his home" — in "a **Matt Layden**-designed 14ft *Paradox*," and notes that **Al Law, "another British *Paradox* builder-owner," accompanied him for the last leg**.[3] See [merits/faith-scilly-cruise.md](/merits/faith-scilly-cruise.md) for how this sits alongside the participants' own accounts.

## Five photographs of Little Jim, and the Beale Park record

The post of **8 May 2009**, "A Paradox, up close and personal," is the richest Paradox page on the site. Atkin writes about meeting **Al Law** and seeing **Little Jim** close up at the **Beale Park Boat Show** — "last year," so the **2008** show — and publishes **five photographs**, the archive's best close-up documentation of a UK-built Paradox's cabin and deck: the DCA fleet with Little Jim in the foreground; her cabin looking forward, showing the hinged table, GPS and compass on the forward bulkhead and the side bins; her sliding hatch and deck detail from aft; her foredeck and cabin from forward with the sail furled in its cover; and Al Law himself in the hatch in a Dinghy Cruising Association sweatshirt.[4] A sailing video from the show, "Paradox sailing at the Beale Park Thames Boat Show," remains online.[4]

The site adds three further Beale Park data points. In **May 2010** Atkin previews the Dinghy Cruising Association's attendance and writes that the association means to show that a range of small craft "(including, hopefully, **Alistair Law's *Paradox***)" can be used for cruising in coastal waters — the DCA putting a Paradox forward as an exemplar coastal cruiser, though the wording makes it an expectation rather than a confirmed attendance.[5] In **June 2012** he reports the DCA's boats moored on their jetty "including **two built to Matt Layden's famou[s] *Paradox* design, of which I only know of a couple in the UK**," and his gallery photographs them: a green hull matching Little Jim's colouring alongside a blue-hulled boat carrying the name **"Johanna"** — see [boats/johanna.md](/boats/johanna.md).[6] That parenthesis is also an outside observer's estimate of the **UK fleet size in 2012**, consistent with the mailing list's own picture that the great majority of Paradoxes were US-built.[6]

## The furling system's line sizes, from two owners

The post of **31 January 2014** is three sentences pointing at a video, but its comment thread is substantive. The video is "Sailing Paradox Scout — What's it like?", uploaded by **[Sean Mulligan](/people/sean-mulligan.md)** — so the boat is "[Scout](/boats/scout.md)."[7] Atkin's own impression of the boat from it: "surprisingly steady for a 14ft boat," and, like a go-kart, close enough to the water that the sense of speed is remarkable.[7]

In the comments, two owners on different continents converge on the same operational fact. **[Pete Martin](/people/pete-martin.md)** notes that the raw footage shows a great deal of furling and unfurling, that the system is very simple but that repeated use takes a toll on the hands, and gives his own practice — **gardening gloves, slipped on and off every time he uses the furling gear**, the alternative being "blisters and raw flesh at the end of the day."[8] Sean Mulligan, identifying himself as the sailor in the video, supplies the numbers: **the lines are 1/4″ and 3/16″**, and **the reefing system "takes a hefty haul to roller reef the main"** — it works well but needs "a slightly excessive force to get it going," and without gloves "You'll do it once… then you'll be wearing bandages."[9] Atkin's outside suggestion is a **thicker, softer sheet** instead of gloves.[8] See [components/rig/furling.md — hand loads, line diameter, and gloves](/components/rig/furling.md#hand-loads-line-diameter-and-gloves-two-owners-2014).

## An outside account of why Matt Layden guards his designs

A post of **29 February 2008** on the boat [Sven Yrvind](/references/yrvind-website.md) designed in collaboration with **[Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)** contains the site's clearest general writing on the type. Atkin describes the new boat as having a Layden-style form — a **deep and heavy sharpie hull in which the runners along the chine work like a keel, holding the boat up to the wind without the draft a conventional keel would require** — and makes the observation that the design "seems to complete a circle": **Yrvind's influence is visible in several of Layden's earlier designs, while Layden's influence on Yrvind's new boat "could not be more obvious."**[10] See [chine-runners/how-they-work.md](/chine-runners/how-they-work.md) and [chine-runners/origin-and-evolution.md](/chine-runners/origin-and-evolution.md).

Asked in the comments whether plans for the collaboration might become available, Atkin replies that **Layden is "quite cagey" about his designs**, and gives the reason as he understands it: **Layden worries that people will not understand the designs and the importance of each of their details, will change them, and may get into trouble as a result.**[11] This is second-hand — Atkin's understanding, not a statement from Layden — and should be cited as such. It matches the design-integrity argument the mailing list makes in its own words, from a disinterested observer outside the community.

## Two dated details the participants' own records do not carry

A comment left on the 2008 Plymouth post on **6 June 2010** records a sighting: "just met faith on the yealm today 06 06 10" — **Faith on the River Yealm, Devon, in June 2010**, under her later ownership.[12] It sits alongside the Google Street View and BBC News sightings of the same year at [boats/faith.md](/boats/faith.md#a-further-media-sighting-bbc-news-video-and-a-second-satellite-image-november-2010).

A comment left on the 2009 Little Jim post on **18 May 2011** is **[Greg Lockhart](/people/greg-lockhart.md)**'s, and predates his first mailing-list post by about a month: after three previous builds he has "discovered PARADOX," she is going to be his retirement boat, "perfect for a sailing bachelor like myself."[13] It is the earliest record of him finding the design. See [boats/endeavour.md](/boats/endeavour.md).

## The site's own Bill Serjeant record

Two later posts have no Paradox content but bear on the person. In **May 2013** Atkin, reading Bill's Log, places Serjeant at **Oare Creek and Faversham** on a *West Wight Potter*, travelled over from Burnham and Leigh and hoping to make Ramsgate the next day, and characterises him: **"for many years he has taken a particular pleasure in changing boats regularly, often building the smaller ones himself."**[14] In **August 2013**, announcing the Potter's eBay auction, he records that **"Bill has recently completed a 66-day cruise along the South Coast in a variety of winds"** — a concrete duration for that cruise, which the archive otherwise holds only as an in-progress narrative.[15] See [people/bill-serjeant.md](/people/bill-serjeant.md).

## Citing this source

Cite in the form:

> [intheboatshed.net](/references/intheboatshed.md) — "[Post title]," Gavin Atkin, YYYY-MM-DD — [detail].

Comments are cited to their own author and date, distinguished from Atkin's post text. Posts are dated and the site is live, so citations are in the past tense and to publication date, consistent with the bundle's historical-archive convention.

A note for any later pass: two link targets from these posts are **dead and not yet recovered** — the YouTube video of Faith making way (`UwTJ_58K5pA`) and its host page, Bill Serjeant's own Paradox video page at `smallsailboats.co.uk/paradox/videos/videos.htm`, that domain now being a "domain for sale" parking page consistent with the site's closure at the end of March 2009 (see [references/paradox-uk-website.md](/references/paradox-uk-website.md#closure-and-where-the-content-survives-2009)). The Internet Archive was globally offline when this source was captured on 2026-08-04, so neither could be revived on that pass.
