# messing-about.com forums

**messing-about.com** is an American amateur-boatbuilding discussion forum. It hosts, among its sections, the official builder community for **B&B Yacht Designs** (Graham and Carla Byrnes, Vandemere, North Carolina) and for Tom Lathrop's Bluejacket Boats, and has been running since the early 2000s. It is **not a Paradox forum**, and nothing on it is written by a Paradox owner: its Paradox and [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md) content is incidental, arising almost entirely because the same people race the **WaterTribe Everglades Challenge** that Layden raced, and because a working boat designer and his shop took a professional interest in [chine runners](/chine-runners/index.md).

That outsider position is exactly what makes it worth having. Every other substantial source in this archive is written from inside the design's own community — the [mailing list](/references/mailing-list.md), the [Bolducs' site](/references/microcruising-website.md), owners' blogs. The forum records how Layden's boats looked to competent small-boat people who had no stake in them: builders choosing between designs, racers sizing up a rival, and a naval architect testing whether the chine runner idea transfers to his own hulls.

## Not to be confused with *Messing About In Boats*

**messing-about.com is a different entity from [*Messing About In Boats*](/references/messing-about-in-boats.md)**, Bob Hicks's print magazine at `messingaboutinboats.com`. The two share a name derived from the same *Wind in the Willows* line and nothing else — different owners, different medium, different decade. Citations must not be conflated: magazine citations carry a volume/number/date and a page, forum citations a topic number and a post date.

## What is on it

A full guest search of the forum in **August 2026** across the relevant terms — *paradox*, *layden*, *chine runner(s)*, *little cruiser*, *enigma*, *elusion*, *sand flea*, *microcruising*, *bolduc*, *wizard*, *yrvind* — returns roughly **twenty topics** with genuine Layden content, spanning **2004 to 2016**, out of 11.2k topics and 114.7k posts on the board. The earliest Paradox mention on record there is a passing 2004 remark that the "Paradox builder's yahoo groups" files "seem to have been removed" — an outsider's note on the mailing list's own accessibility.[1]

The material falls into five kinds, and the facts themselves live in the thematic tree rather than here:

**1. Matt Layden in his own words.** The single most valuable item is a **verbatim Layden announcement**, relayed to the forum from the WaterTribe discussion board in February 2007, declaring his change of entry for that year's Everglades Challenge from Enigma to the newly built **[Sand Flea](/matt-layden/sand-flea.md)** — with the boat's particulars, its trial sail, and his own stated design logic ("the less boat you have to drag around the faster you can go").[2] Direct Layden text is rare enough in this archive that the passage is quoted in full on the Sand Flea page.

**2. A working designer's firsthand evaluation of a Layden boat.** In March 2016 **Graham Byrnes** of B&B Yacht Designs — posting as "Designer" — sailed a chine-runner **[Enigma](/matt-layden/enigma.md)** brought to his shop by a WaterTribe competitor, estimated her tacking angle at about **120°**, capsized her in a bad jibe and came close to a complete inversion. He had already set out how he planned to test hot-glued chine runners on his own V-bottomed Core Sound 17 Mk3, and why a flat-bottomed, vertical-sided Layden hull carries them horizontally where a flared V-bottom would not.[3][4][5] The thread also carries an independent designer's cross-hull-form test results and B&B's own analysis of the runners' drag trade-off.[6][7] See [chine runners and windward performance](/chine-runners/windward-performance.md) and [how chine runners work](/chine-runners/how-they-work.md).

**3. A reasoned outside account of why Paradox is narrow.** A 2007 Design Forum thread, ["Rocker and beam in hull shape,"](https://messing-about.com/forums/topic/5454-rocker-and-beam-in-hull-shape/) opens with a newcomer asking why Layden makes an already cramped boat narrower still, and draws an argument from B&B's **Alan Stewart** about the maximum beam a ballasted boat can carry before acquiring a second, inverted point of stability.[8][9] It runs on to 2012, when a later arrival relays Layden's own *Small Craft Advisor* explanation of the chine runner as a fence turning the heeled hull into one large hydrofoil.[10] This is the thread that prompted the archive to look at the forum at all; see [beam and stability](/design/beam-and-stability.md).

**4. Everglades Challenge and Ultimate Florida Challenge detail from the people racing alongside him.** The 2006 UFC portage rig — an axle through Enigma's chine wings carrying pram wheels — the observation that Enigma was the only sailboat to finish that race, the winner's own account of Layden's passage up the upper St. Mary's, and eyewitness notes of how he handled checkpoints, mud flats and sleep in 2007.[11][12][13][14] See [Enigma](/matt-layden/enigma.md) and [the Everglades Challenge](/merits/everglades-challenge.md).

**5. Paradox as seen by people choosing a boat.** Recurring, and useful precisely because it is not advocacy: a builder who owns the plans reporting that Paradox is "basically for one person only"; a 2009 essay working through why the design cannot simply be scaled up for a bigger sailor; a note that several Layden designs "point quite respectably without centerboard, leeboard or keel"; the observation that the square-headed sail is *made necessary* by reefing from inside.[15][16][17][18] See [a larger Paradox](/design/larger-paradox.md) and [crew size and fit](/operations/crew-size-and-fit.md).

## Who the recurring voices are

None of them is a Paradox owner, and their standing varies:

- **Graham Byrnes** ("Designer") — professional naval architect, principal of B&B Yacht Designs, Everglades Challenge winner. His statements about hull behaviour carry professional weight; his figures are field estimates, and he says so.
- **Alan Stewart** — B&B Yacht Designs; sailed Sand Flea with Layden at the 2007 EC finish at Key Largo.
- **Tom Lathrop** — designer of the Bluejacket boats, a long-standing forum presence.
- **"PAR"** — a boat designer and repairer posting his own chine-runner test results across several hull forms.
- **Doug Cameron**, **Gordy Hill**, **JeffM**, **Dale Niemann**, **ScottWidmier** — WaterTribe racers and small-boat sailors reporting what they saw. Several are the source of the eyewitness Layden material.

Contributors post under handles, and the forum's own profile pages are the only identity evidence available; where a poster's full name is not given on the forum, this archive cites the handle as printed.

## Provenance and citing

Topics resolve at `https://messing-about.com/forums/topic/<id>-<slug>/`, and individual posts at that URL plus `?do=findComment&comment=<id>`. Both are publicly readable without an account, as is the forum's search. Topic ids are stable; the board has been migrated to Invision Community software at least once, and pre-migration posts carry a generic "Posted <date>" byline in place of the author's name where the account did not survive — two posts in the Sand Flea thread are affected, and are cited here as anonymous.

Cite in the form:

> [messing-about.com forums](/references/messing-about-forum.md) — "[Topic title]" (topic [id], [forum section]), [author], [date] — [detail].

Because posts are dated to the day and quote events days old, the forum is a **contemporaneous secondary source** for the races it describes: better than recollection, weaker than the racer's own log. Where it conflicts with [microcruising.com](/references/microcruising-website.md) or with Layden's own words, those are controlling.

## Its limits, and two specific cautions

The forum carries **no construction data for Paradox**, no plans discussion, no owner's build log, and no photographs of a Paradox. Its Layden threads are short — most run under twenty posts — and much of what they contain is second- or third-hand.

Two specific errors in it are worth naming, because both would propagate:

**Enigma's length is given inconsistently.** Layden's own 2007 text calls her "the 12' boat"; Graham Byrnes in 2016 calls the boat behind Vlad's car "a Matt Layden 14' Enigma."[2][5] The archive's own particulars give the Enigma 360 as 11' 10" and the later Enigma 460 as about 15' 1" — so the 2016 "14'" is either a loose eyeball figure or, more likely, a 460 rather than a 360. It is treated here as unresolved and is not used as a dimension.

**"Chine runner" appears on the forum in an unrelated older sense.** A Nova Scotia builder uses "chine runners" for the sacrificial hardwood wear strips fitted along an outboard boat's chines to survive beaching — nothing to do with Layden's leeway-resisting plates.[19] Keyword searches of general boatbuilding sources will return both.
