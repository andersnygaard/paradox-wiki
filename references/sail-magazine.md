# SAIL Magazine — "Go Small, Go Now"

**SAIL Magazine** (`sailmagazine.com`) is a long-running American sailing monthly whose relevance to this archive rests on a single article: **"Go Small, Go Now: A Cruising Couple and their Micro-Cruisers,"** a first-person retrospective by **[Dave Bolduc](/people/bolduc/dave.md)** covering the whole arc of the couple's micro-cruising life, from a leaky 13-foot dinghy on the Hudson River to a six-week solo Bahamas cruise in an eleven-foot boat.[1] It is written for a mainstream cruising readership rather than for boatbuilders, which shapes both what it is good for and what it leaves out.

The couple's own site records the piece as one they wrote in 2014, and the archive has long carried that bare fact without the article behind it.[2] The article itself is the thing this page adds.

## What the article is worth as evidence

Its value is that it is the **only single narrative in the archive that lays the Bolduc–Layden story out end to end, in order, with dates attached** — where every other first-party source gives one episode, one year, or one technique. Four things in it are firsthand and otherwise unrecorded or only half-recorded elsewhere.

**How they found Matt Layden.** Two black-and-white magazine photographs in **February 1991** showed them a fifteen-foot lug-rigged boat with a large pilothouse, captioned with its owner's name and the fact that he had worked his way up to Cape Cod for the winter; the couple contacted him and then drove **1,300 miles round trip** to meet him. That is the origin of the archive's single most productive relationship, and it is dated here and nowhere else.[1] See [the Bolducs' Matt Layden firsthand record](/people/bolduc/matt-layden-authority.md).

**What Matt Layden had already done by 1991.** He had been living aboard the flat-bottomed sharpie for **three years**, had sailed as far north as the **Bay of Fundy**, could cover **up to 50 miles a day** entirely under sail and oar, and — the detail that appears nowhere else in the archive — had **survived a capsize in the Gulf Stream on an earlier boat** while bound for the Bahamas. The boat he was living aboard was the **third such boat he had built**.[1] See [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md).

**Paradox's first long passage, dated and measured.** Matt spent **six weeks** sailing the new Paradox down the **Intracoastal Waterway from Connecticut** to join the Bolducs in Florida, cruised the Bahamas alongside Little Cruiser, and afterward crossed the Gulf Stream alone and **sailed back up the ICW to Connecticut** — a round trip under sail in the boat's first season. See [original Paradox](/boats/original-paradox.md).[1]

**Little Cruiser as inspected at purchase, and how she got her name.** The article describes the boat's construction as the couple found it in 1992 — the one-inch plywood bottom doubling as ballast, the bow centerboard, the two inches of foam, the water-ballast tanks fed from the toe-rail drains — and records that it was **the Bolducs who christened her "Little Cruiser,"** after refurbishing her. See [Little Cruiser](/matt-layden/little-cruiser.md).[1]

It is also a clean **year-by-year list of the seven Bahamas voyages** (1993, 1994, 1995, 1996, 1998, 2001, 2003) with destinations attached, and the fullest published itinerary of the 2013 solo *Enigma* cruise.[1] See [the Bolducs' Bahamas cruising](/merits/bahamas-and-exumas.md).

## Its limits

It is a **retrospective written twenty years after the earliest events it describes**, for a general audience, and it carries **no construction data at all** — no scantlings, no weights beyond two round figures, no method. Where it overlaps the contemporaneous mailing-list record it corroborates rather than corrects, and where the two differ on a date, the contemporaneous source should be preferred. Two specific cautions: the article's compressed account of the **1993–94 Intracoastal Waterway passages** does not obviously reconcile with the archive's separate 1993 and 1994 entries for the same boat, and its remark that the couple "made five more voyages" after the second trip yields **seven** Little Cruiser Bahamas trips, consistent with the rest of the record but not with a strict reading of the couple's own later "seventh trip overall" wording for 2013.[3] Neither is resolved here.

The published page also **carries no photographs**, though the article's own closing credit line shows the original was illustrated with the Bolducs' pictures.

## Provenance and dating

Published online **19 July 2014** under the section "Profiles," and in the print magazine the same year. The page's byline reads **"By Webmaster"** — a content-management artifact of a later site migration, not an author. The text is unambiguously **Dave Bolduc's**, written in the first person singular ("my wife, Mindy, and me"; "I bought one of Matt's older micro-cruisers"), and the photo credit names David and Mindy Bolduc. It is cited to Bolduc throughout the bundle.

Internal dating anchors: "28 years ago" from a 1986 courtship fixes the writing to 2014, and "I left Key Largo in March last year" places the solo *Enigma* cruise in **March 2013**.

Articles resolve at `https://sailmagazine.com/<section>/<article-slug>/` — this one at `https://sailmagazine.com/cruising/go-small-go-now-a-cruising-couple-and-their-micro-cruisers/`. Cite in the form:

Attribute to **David Bolduc**, never to the page's "Webmaster" byline. As a dated snapshot of a historical record, its content is cited in the past tense, consistent with the bundle's historical-archive convention.
