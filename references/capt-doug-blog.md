# Sailing and Paddling with Doug (Doug Cameron's blog)

**Sailing and Paddling with Doug** (`https://capt-doug.blogspot.com/`) is the personal Blogger site of **[Doug Cameron](/people/doug-cameron.md)**, a Sewanee, Tennessee sailor and paddler who races under the WaterTribe name **Ridgerunner**. It is not a Paradox blog — Doug sails a Core Sound 20 cat ketch he built himself, and most of its seventy-nine posts are canoeing, kayaking, and Core Sound content. Its standing in this archive rests on a narrower fact: Doug was repeatedly on the same beaches as [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md), he wrote the *Small Craft Advisor* profile of him, and he wrote down what he saw.

That makes the blog a **firsthand eyewitness source on Matt Layden and his boats**, distinct in kind from the [Paradox builders mailing list](/references/mailing-list.md), which followed the same events at a distance through trackers and relayed reports.

## What the blog is worth as evidence

Three things, in ascending order of what the archive could not otherwise get.

First, it **reprints in full the *Small Craft Advisor* profile of Matt Layden**, posted by its own author on 22 April 2009 alongside a photograph of Matt on the beach at Atsena Otie standing beside [Enigma](/matt-layden/enigma.md) and looking at [Paradox](/paradox.md).[1] That profile is the densest single biographical source on Matt in the bundle — the Connecticut oysterman grandfather, the cabinet-shop apprenticeship, the Balboa 20 refit, the design philosophy stated in his own terms — and it was previously cited from its URL alone. The blog post is the copy that survives; it is truncated at source mid-sentence, at "but amazed," and no fuller version has been found.

Second, it supplies **outside eyewitness corroboration of Layden boats being raced by other people**. Doug watched [Dave Bolduc](/people/bolduc/dave.md) sail Enigma past his own anchored boat in the night during the inaugural 2009 North Carolina Challenge, and saw him beating close along the beaches the next morning.[2] At Fort DeSoto in March 2010 he catalogued the starting beach and found **four** Layden-designed craft on it at once.[3]

Third — and this is what makes the blog worth a reference page of its own — it **records the finishing order of the 2010 Ultimate Florida Challenge**, which the mailing list never did. The list's day-by-day following of that race, the archive's most closely followed single event, simply stops at the forty-mile portage with no result reported for any of the three leaders. Doug's photographs, captioned with placings, close it.[4] See [The 2010 Ultimate Florida Challenge, followed from the list](/merits/ultimate-florida-challenge-2010.md).

The fifth and last of the Layden-related posts carries no Paradox material at all, but is worth recording for its closing line. Recalling the boatbuilder and author Robb White bringing his Feluccia to a Friends of the Library event at Sewanee, Tennessee shortly before his death — snow fell during the lecture, students set snowmen at the helm of both his boat and Doug's Joel White-designed Shellback dinghy, and White carried his home to Thomasville, Georgia for grandchildren who had never seen snow, dying about a month later of a brain aneurysm — the post ends "(Matt Layden suggested that I share this tale.)" In an archive whose subject is repeatedly described as shunning publicity, that is a rare direct trace of Layden prompting someone else to publish something.[5]

## Its limits

Doug is a sympathetic, well-informed observer, but an observer: he is not a Paradox builder or owner, and the blog carries no construction data, no plans discussion, and no sailing appraisal of any Layden design from the helm. His Layden material is incidental to a blog about his own boats, and it amounts to five posts out of seventy-nine.

The record is also **loose in its details in a way that matters when citing it**. Two examples are documented on the pages that use them: he calls the Tridarka Raider a *catamaran* where the rest of the archive has it as a trimaran, and the 2010 finishing-photo post is titled "at Cedar Key Checkpoint" while its own captions place the pictures at the finish at Fort DeSoto. Neither is fatal — he was there and the other sources were not — but placings, rig types, and locations taken from this blog should be read as a participant's recollection rather than an official record, and are worth a second source where one exists.

## Provenance and dating

A conventional dated-post Blogger site, running from **21 April 2009** to **6 February 2020**, seventy-nine posts, still live. Doug posts under "Capt. Doug" and signs the reprinted articles "Doug Cameron"; the Blogger profile name is **Doug Cameron**. The Layden-relevant material clusters tightly in **April 2009 – March 2010**.

## Citing this source

Each post resolves at `https://capt-doug.blogspot.com/YYYY/MM/<slug>.html`, where `YYYY/MM` is the post's publication year and month and `<slug>` is Blogger's own slugified form of its heading — note that Blogger's slug often preserves the post's *original* heading rather than its current title (the "Matt Layden" post lives at `.../2009/04/my-profile-of-matt-layden-accompanies.html`). Cite in the form:

> [Sailing and Paddling with Doug](/references/capt-doug-blog.md) — "Post title," Doug Cameron, YYYY-MM-DD — [detail].

Where the material being cited is the *Small Craft Advisor* profile rather than Doug's own blogging, name the article and its print venue as well, since the profile is a published magazine piece that the blog happens to carry: *Doug Cameron, "Matt Layden — A Profile," Small Craft Advisor, May/June 2009, © 2009 Doug Cameron; posted in full to [Sailing and Paddling with Doug](/references/capt-doug-blog.md), 2009-04-22.*

As a dated snapshot of a historical record, its content is cited in the past tense and dated to the post it comes from, consistent with the bundle's historical-archive convention.
