# Small Sailboats / Paradox-UK websites

A UK-focused Paradox web presence was built in two stages: after plans arrived, a Paradox section was added to an existing **Small Sailboats** site (`www.smallsailboats.co.uk`), angled toward builders and sailors in the UK, then that material was split out into its own dedicated site, **`www.paradox-uk.co.uk`**, kept under the Small Sailboats umbrella.[1] The pairing gave the design's growing UK builder community a home-country counterpart to the Bolducs' [microcruising.com](/references/microcruising-website.md). This UK web presence ran for nearly two years before its builder started his own Paradox build in August 2005 — see [people/bill-serjeant.md](/people/bill-serjeant.md) for the biography and the build itself.

Building from the plans surfaced two rudder questions that reached the wider design record: the rudder blade's pivot-bolt fixing (see [design/rudder.md — the pivot-bolt fixing](/components/rudder.md#the-pivot-bolt-fixing-and-its-record-under-grounding-loads)) and a twin-rudder arrangement proposed to free up the transom centerline for an outboard, judged not worth the added complexity (see [rudder and chine experiments — a twin-rudder proposal](/components/rudder.md#a-twin-rudder-proposal-for-a-centerline-outboard-judged-not-worth-the-complexity)).[2] The same exchange produced a fuller self-steering record: an auto-pilot-versus-wind-vane comparison;[3][4] a self-steering history spanning a rudder-mounted tab on a Folksong, a Hasler SP 90, and an Autohelm/Wasp-wind-generator combination; the specific problem Paradox's **folding** rudder poses for mounting a trim-tab wind vane, resolved with a Bowden-cable arrangement;[5] a wind-vane maker's own director, skeptical that any wind vane would be worthwhile on a boat this size, weighed against the case that a suitable vane could still benefit a Paradox coastal cruiser;[6][7] and a method for heaving to without deploying a sea anchor.[8] See [self-steering — auto-pilot versus wind-vane self-steering: a practical comparison](/operations/self-steering.md#auto-pilot-versus-wind-vane-self-steering-a-practical-comparison), [self-steering — a trim-tab wind vane and Paradox's folding rudder](/operations/self-steering.md#a-trim-tab-wind-vane-and-paradoxs-folding-rudder), [self-steering — a wind-vane maker's skeptical outside view](/operations/self-steering.md#a-wind-vane-makers-skeptical-outside-view), [self-steering — a forward-of-mast wind vane, and a case for self-steering's real value](/operations/self-steering.md#a-forward-of-mast-wind-vane-and-a-case-for-self-steerings-real-value), and [self-steering — heaving-to without deploying a sea anchor](/operations/self-steering.md#heaving-to-without-deploying-a-sea-anchor).

The clearest one-line classification on record of Paradox against a genuine ocean racer — measured against the Dix-designed Mini Transat 650 — is the verdict that the two are "as different as chalk and cheese."[9] See [design/blue-water-suitability.md — a racer/cruiser comparison](/design/blue-water-suitability.md#a-racercruiser-comparison-and-bill-serjeants-verdict).

The group's original "Tools" list was **compiled and posted** from what one builder had used to build his own Paradox. The list is a compilation rather than an endorsement or list from that builder himself, and is distinct from a separate, possibly copyrighted **Building Materials list** that came bundled with a bought plans package.[10] See [build effort and workspace — tools](/construction/build-effort.md#tools) for the underlying list and discussion.

## A third UK small-boat resource: the irbs.com Small Sailboats Usergroup

Beyond the Small Sailboats / Paradox-UK pairing, Paradox builders also have access to a **third-party** UK small-boat venue: the bulletin boards at `www.irbs.com` ("I'd rather be sailing"), and in particular its **Small Sailboats Usergroup**, set up alongside `www.smallsailboats.co.uk`. The Usergroup's scope is any sailing boat 26 ft LOA and under, monohull or multihull, covering building, cruising, handling technique, rig types, auxiliary engines, cruising grounds, rallies, and messabouts.[11]

## A companion Yahoo Group: "smallsailboats"

A dedicated **Yahoo Group** of the same name, `smallsailboats`, open to `paradoxbuilders` members, serves as a same-umbrella companion to the irbs.com bulletin board above, covering any small sailing craft, traditional or modern, "from messing about, to top class racing."[12]

The site's info page also serves as a **plans-contact relay**: it republishes Dave Bolduc's contact address at `www.btinternet.com/~w.serjeant/paradox/info.htm`, a third route to the plans distributor alongside e-mailing Dave and Mindy directly or posting to the `microcruising` Yahoo Group.[13] See [building plans — two more 2004 recurrences, and how to reach the Bolducs when e-mail fails](/references/building-plans.md#why-paradox-resists-easy-enlargement).

A *Messing About In Boats* sea-trials article was reproduced on paradox-uk.co.uk with permission (copyright remaining with the original author) as "Zoë's Sea Trials"[14][15][16] — see [Zoë — press coverage](/boats/zoe.md#press-coverage-two-2003-magazine-articles) for the article itself, and [furling system — a UK builder's topping-lift question](/components/rig/furling.md#a-uk-builders-topping-lift-question) for a rigging question the reproduction prompted.

## A temporary domain outage on smallsailboats.co.uk

In late May 2004, a web-hosting problem took the `www.smallsailboats.co.uk` domain temporarily out of service, with the older `www.btinternet.com/~w.serjeant` address serving as a stand-in.[17] The domain came back online about a week later, at which point the older BT Internet address was retired rather than kept running alongside it — leaving the `.co.uk` domain as the site's sole address going forward, aside from a few links still needing attention.[18]

## A second outage, June 2006, and an apparent domain-registrar issue

The site went down again in June 2006, this time presenting visitors with a registrar's holding page (`www.123-reg.co.uk`) rather than its own content — a symptom of a lapsed or transferring domain registration rather than a hosting fault. A below-threshold UK-based list member flagged the problem, suspecting his own computer at first, and separately noted the site's menu had briefly rendered as corrupted characters the day before, wondering if it reflected new ownership; a second, established below-threshold correspondent confirmed the same failure independently, ruling out a fault on the first member's own computer. The report was acknowledged with thanks, and work went into bringing the site back online, with a warning it might take hours or days; shortly after came confirmation that the registration itself was actively being sorted out, without a specific cause given.[19][20][21][22][23]

`www.smallsailboats.co.uk` was restored within a day, first re-uploading the site and then confirming it fully "up and running," with a request for visitors to report any errors.[24][25] The correspondent who first flagged the outage sent thanks, glad it had not been a fault on his own end, and volunteered that the site's real-world sailing links (UK Dinghy Cruising among them) and its owner's own sailing life made it one of his favorite places to browse.[26]

The site is also a source for UK sailmaker recommendations: established UK sailmakers Banks Sails, Crusader Sails, Jeckells Sails, Kemp Sails, and Lucas Sails are on record there, with Lucas recommended from firsthand experience having a junk sail made there — weighed as an alternative to a costly local quote against the US price already on record.[27] See [sail — sail pricing](/components/rig/sail.md#sail-pricing).

"Zoë's Sea Trials" settled at its current address under the recovered `smallsailboats.co.uk` domain — `www.smallsailboats.co.uk/paradox/trials.htm` — superseding the older BT Internet URL recorded above.

Absent a fresh trip report, a 2004 list query for recent sailing adventures ("has anyone gone sailing lately") drew two pointers back to existing written accounts rather than a new one: Glen Maxwell's article already posted in the group's Files section — [Zoë's sea trials](/boats/zoe.md#press-coverage-two-2003-magazine-articles), reproduced here as "Zoë's Sea Trials"[28] — and several pieces about "[Little Jim](/boats/little-jim.md)" on its own web site. The query's reply from "Little Jim"'s own owner reported no fresh open-water adventures that year, but several weekend outings sailing her in sheltered estuaries near home; full text and ballast-trim account at [boats/little-jim.md](/boats/little-jim.md#ballast-and-trim).[29][30][31]

## A UK and worldwide build count

No running total of completed Paradoxes worldwide, or of boats under construction specifically in England, exists on record. The closest thing to a census the community has assembled is the `www.smallsailboats.co.uk` site, holding "a fairly complete list" of completed boats. Builders specifically underway in the UK are harder to track: at one point only a single prospective builder in Cornwall was known to have expressed serious interest, with no confirmed progress on record.[32]

There is no reliable way to know the true build count, but at least **three** further completed boats could be added to those already listed on `www.smallsailboats.co.uk/paradox/parindex.htm`, the registry depending on builders reporting their own boats in to keep it current.[33] Within days, one Paradox was reported essentially complete and sail hoisted, one of the additional boats implied by that estimate.[34]

Two months later, the same registry stood at "at least 14 boats" either on the water or under construction, still dependent on builders volunteering amendments to keep `www.smallsailboats.co.uk/paradox/names.htm` current.[35] One registry entry recorded around this time: a still-unnamed boat, being built per plan, progressing slowly behind other priorities — the registry table itself, linked from `paradoxbuilders`, going unnoticed by at least one builder on a previous visit to the site.[36] The list's accuracy depends entirely on builders reporting in.[37]

The site owner's own boat history illustrates the churn behind these counts: an autumn-2005 plan to build a new Paradox was confirmed as "as well as" rather than instead of an existing boat, "Bumper," since a boat was seldom kept more than two seasons — and building a Paradox meant casting a fresh batch of lead ballast, with none left over from the previous boat.[38][39]

In August 2005, the registry site's first update since starting that build was posted — photos of deck beams, cabin beams, and hatch beams — again asking the list to help keep `names.htm` current with any builder not yet listed.[40]

## A 2006 database renewal, and a new Los Angeles builder

In February 2006, the registry appeal was renewed once more — asking builders to check their own entry at `www.smallsailboats.co.uk/paradox/names.htm` for accuracy, submit details if not yet listed, and, this time, specifically requesting photos of finished boats **on the water or on their trailer** rather than further building-stage pictures.[41]

The appeal drew the archive's introduction of a Los Angeles, California builder whose Paradox was by then 90% complete, built strictly to plans, with no name chosen yet and completion expected by summer 2006. A Selway-Fisher 50/50 sailing canoe had also been built, sharing that design lineage's "taste" with the registry site.[42] A query came in asking whether "L.A." meant the city proper or the wider county area, hoping to see the boat in person on a future visit.[43] Thanks followed, the boat was added to the registry, and a note that the registry-keeper's own build would likely miss finishing that year since he would be away during July and August — the best months for outdoor building in the UK — and had no indoor space to build in regardless.[44]

A separate proposal to add builders' e-mail addresses to the registry itself followed, with the registry-keeper's blessing secured to poll the list's opinion on the idea first.[45][46] No objection was raised to including that Los Angeles builder's own address,[47] and agreement came from another builder likewise.[48] See [people/mark-waters.md](/people/mark-waters.md) for that build.

A newcomer's January 2007 request to see a sailing or finished Paradox firsthand on the west coast of North America drew no reply pointing to any specific boat there — a real gap, since the closest documented build to that part of the continent is the Los Angeles builder above, and even that builder's own registry entry lists no way for a stranger to arrange a visit beyond the e-mail column.[49][50] Not aware of a nearer alternative, the newcomer noted Florida and the UK were both a considerable distance to travel just to see one in person.[50]

A dedicated e-mail-address column was added to the registry itself, listing the first four volunteers (David Beard, Mark Waters, Derek Clark, and the registry-keeper) and inviting further builders to opt in.[51] The proposal drew in a genuinely new prospective builder: a Phoenix, Arizona sailor who had already ordered the plans and book, hoping to use the registry's e-mail addresses to find other owners in his area to learn more about the design firsthand.[52] The same standing rule was said to apply to any newcomer — details would be added once building actually started, not merely on ordering the plans.[53] The registry's direct link (`www.smallsailboats.co.uk/paradox/names.htm`) was then posted to the whole list, repeating the open invitation to be added and separately flagging that the e-mail address on file for the Los Angeles builder appeared to be incorrect.[54] A belated request followed to add another e-mail to the column as well, having missed the earlier request.[55]

The same "Database" thread also turned up a **modified Paradox** absent from the registry: a boat nicknamed **"mini me,"** built by a UK-associated correspondent first named as "Dave Mason" but corrected in the same thread to **Wayne Mason**, with photos posted to the `microcruising` Yahoo Group's photo section rather than to `paradoxbuilders` itself.[56][57] Checking further, "mini me"'s hull, though similar to a Paradox's, was judged to be its builder's own design rather than a variant close enough to classify as one, given the number of differences between the two boats — so the registry was not updated to include her.[58] A question followed on whether "mini me" had chine runners, and whether any photos existed of her under sail even though she was not a true Paradox.[59] The photos in the `microcruising` Yahoo Group's own "Mini Me" album were pointed to, with a judgment that the boat was a sturdy little craft, but likely **more vulnerable than a Paradox** because of her cockpit arrangement and higher-aspect rig; a note also flagged what looked like a daggerboard case on the cabin's port side in one photo, raising the possibility that "mini me," unlike a stock Paradox, used a daggerboard rather than chine runners for lateral resistance.[60]

A January 2007 update to the registry page (`www.smallsailboats.co.uk/paradox/names.htm`) again flagged missing information — the new owner of "[Enuf](/boats/enuf.md)," whether [Bengt Genell](/boats/ladikan.md)'s Paradox was built to plan, and any detail on Jeff Forrest's boat beyond his living in the US, including whether his surname takes one or two "r"s — with a standing invitation for readers to forward corrections directly.[61] That "Enuf" gap closed several months later, once her new Norway-based owner surfaced on the list himself with reefing and sailing questions of his own — see [people/robert-biegler.md](/people/robert-biegler.md).[62]

A prospective South Alabama builder, hoping to see a completed Paradox in person along the northern Gulf Coast before starting his own build, asked in March 2007 whether anyone had ever assembled a true list of Paradoxes built to date.[63] The registry was pointed to as the closest thing to one,[64] but the registry-keeper was candid that it fell well short of a true account of every Paradox built or building anywhere: only Dave Bolduc, working from plans-sold records, could really know the number, and even he would be unlikely to release buyers' names and addresses for privacy reasons — the registry-keeper's own preference would be exactly such a list, so he could write to each recipient and ask whether they had gone on to actually build, the only way in his view to turn a plans-sold count into a genuinely useful registry of owners and builders.[65] The registry's evident international spread — boats built or building across the US, the UK, and continental Europe — drew a remark in reply, welcomed as encouraging news for a builder just starting out.[66]

The registry-keeper was still actively fielding "who's building" check-ins as late as September 2008: a returning correspondent's general query drew a pointer straight to `www.smallsailboats.co.uk/paradox/names.htm`, with a candid caveat, repeated from years earlier, that the list remains "far from comprehensive" and can only be updated as builders and owners volunteer their own information.[67] The same exchange caught a builder nearing completion — see [boats/ironsides.md — nearing completion, a September 2008 launch expected](/boats/ironsides.md#nearing-completion-a-september-2008-launch-expected-and-registry-congratulations) — confirmed as the registry's last-added entry, with a standing request to be told once the boat actually launched so her details could be amended and to hear how her sailing trials went.[68]

## A UK kit-built option: Jordan Boats

Beyond plans and self-build, a **kit** option for a UK builder emerged in January 2005: **Jordan Boats** (`www.jordanboats.co.uk`) agreed to supply the first "kit" Paradox, working directly with [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md) on the project.[69] Jordan Boats was understood to be open to manufacturing Paradox kits under license for builders in the USA or elsewhere — or kits of any other plywood boat design, including one's own.[70] The Jordan Boats option is also relevant to a newcomer weighing whether he was too old to take up a Paradox, alongside a list of UK and nearby European cruising grounds reachable by road and ferry; see [first boat suitability](/operations/first-boat-suitability.md#age-and-the-case-for-going-smaller-rather-than-larger) for the full account.

By mid-2005, the first Jordan Boats kit Paradox was reported on track to ship soon, despite acknowledged "mixed feelings" among some list members about a kit-built option. The decision to pursue the kit traced directly back to trying out [Little Jim](/boats/little-jim.md) firsthand at Plymouth, an experience credited with deepening the wish to own one.[71]

The kit venture ultimately fell through. Despite an effort to work out a way to make every kit part fit together, the design and cutting process for the panels and components needed more research than could be completed in any realistic timeframe, and word came that no kit would be delivered after all. The idea of eventually offering a foolproof, production-quality kit had not been abandoned, and the plywood that would have gone into the kit was supplied instead; building began from [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)'s original plans rather than waiting on the kit effort, with thanks extended for the collaboration on the attempt. The episode is recorded as the archive's clearest account of why a Paradox kit proved harder to bring to market than a one-off self-build from plans.[72]

## Closure, and where the content survives (2009)

The site closed for good at the end of March 2009, after three months' notice given the previous January.[73][74] Its companion Yahoo Group, `smallsailboats`, continued under new stewardship — Kellan Hatch took over running it — and remained the best place to ask after any of the site's former content, with the site owner also by then posting directly to the separate `microcruising` Yahoo Group.[75] A request to know whether a video of cruising aboard "Faith" remained available anywhere as a DVD went unanswered on record.[76] A personal-use copy of the whole site was kept by [Pete Martin](/people/pete-martin.md), with the owner's blessing; some of that material — including the owners/builders registry — could potentially be rehosted elsewhere, though only with the original owner's further permission, since the data remains his.[77][78] The Internet Archive's Wayback Machine was also suggested as a possible route to any pages no longer live.[79]

## The site's own pages, recovered and dated

The live site is gone, but the Internet Archive holds enough of it to read directly rather than only through the mailing-list record of what it said. **Sixty-two pages** were recovered and **61 of them survive in usable form** — 32 under `paradox/`, 17 under the wider `micro-sb/` section, and 12 under the retired BT Internet address — running to roughly 32,100 words; the odd one out, a fifth construction gallery, came back only as a domain-sale placeholder from after the domains lapsed.[80] Two limits apply to any use of them. The domains later lapsed and were picked up by a **domain-parking service**, so captures made after 2010 are sponsored-listing pages rather than the site; only captures dated 2010 or earlier are the site itself.[80] And the archive preserved the site's **text far more completely than its photographs**: of roughly 327 distinct images the pages reference, only about 63 were ever crawled, so the great majority of the galleries below survive as captions and layout without the pictures they were built around.[80]

The pages were authored in **Claris Home Page 2.0**, which writes each file's own save date into its first line. That gives every page a date of its own, independent of when the Internet Archive happened to crawl it — a rare thing for a static site with no post dates. It records when a page was **last saved**, not when it first went up, so it is an upper bound rather than a publication date: `why.htm` carries the sign-off date 24 October 2006 in its own text, matching the mailing-list record of the page being written that month, but was last saved on 29 August 2007.[81][7] Where a page's own sign-off and its file date agree — as on `parindex.htm`, signed 23 November 2004 and saved the same day — the page was not revised after it was written.[82]

Dated this way, the `paradox/` section spans **31 May 2004 to 16 September 2008**, with the load-bearing pages falling as follows: the section's front page and the gallery of [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)'s own boat, November 2004;[82][83] the plans-and-CAD page, May 2006;[84] the site's statement of purpose, September 2006;[85] the four surviving construction galleries, March–June 2007;[86] the build's summary page, July 2007;[87] the appraisal of "[Faith](/boats/faith.md)" and the boat-naming page, late August 2007;[88][89] the cost breakdown, September 2007;[90] the plans-relay Information page, January 2008;[91] and the builders and owners registry last of all, **16 September 2008**.[92]

That last date matters beyond this page: three of the documents this bundle already draws on — the cost breakdown, the appraisal of "Faith", and the builders/owners table — reached it through [Bill's Log](/references/bills-log.md)'s later verbatim republications of 2010 and 2014, and the originals were until now undated. See [the registry's own date](/references/builders-and-owners-census-2014.md#the-registrys-own-date-16-september-2008) for what the registry's date changes about how that census should be read.

## An earlier worldwide count: nine boats, in early 2004

The earliest dated worldwide Paradox count on record anywhere in this bundle sits on the front page of the site's BT Internet incarnation: only one of these coastal cruising sharpies had then been built in the United Kingdom — **one of nine in total throughout the world** — in a page whose own text is signed 3 March 2004.[93] It is a UK builder's outside estimate rather than a builder-by-builder tally, and it predates by eight months the "at least 14 boats either on the water or under construction" figure the registry itself reached that November (see [a UK and worldwide build count](#a-uk-and-worldwide-build-count) above).[35] The rewritten `smallsailboats.co.uk` front page of November 2004 drops the nine-boat figure entirely, reporting instead that a second UK boat had been started by [Derek Clark](/people/derek-clark.md) in Wolverhampton.[82]

The site's statement of purpose, revised in September 2006, gives a third such snapshot in the same informal register: [Alastair Law](/people/alastair-law.md) had built one and Derek Clark had just finished his that summer, a third UK build had started, most Paradoxes were still in the US, one had been finished in Australia, and two more were in the pipeline.[85]

## What the site set out to do, and a UK owners' group that never formed

The reasoning behind splitting off a dedicated `paradox-uk.co.uk` is set out on the site itself. Apart from Alastair Law's own "[Little Jim](/boats/little-jim.md)" site there was no UK rallying point for the design, and the intention was explicitly **not** to draw focus away from the design's American promoters — [microcruising.com](/references/microcruising-website.md) among them — but to complement them while letting builders and sailors around the British coast help one another.[94][85] Alastair Law is credited by name as the leading light of that UK presence, both for his own site and for his standing offer to let people come and see his boat in person.[94][85]

Two intentions stated there went unrealised on record. A formal **UK Builders and Owners' Group**, distinct from the registry table, was hoped for, with an online presence to let UK owners communicate and meet; nothing in the archive records one ever being constituted.[94][85] Owners' **rallies** around the British Isles were likewise raised as a hope as more Paradoxes appeared afloat.[94][85] The nearest either came to happening is the informal fleet meetings the boats' own pages record — the joint West Country cruises, and the gathering of three UK Paradoxes at Poole Harbour in 2013 (see [boats/faith.md](/boats/faith.md#three-uk-paradoxes-together-at-poole-harbour-july-2013)) — arranged boat to boat rather than through any group.

## The Information page as a plans, booklet, and study-material relay

The site's Information page is the fullest single statement of how a UK builder actually got hold of Paradox material, and it was maintained over four years, so its two surviving states show the prices moving. Plans were **$35** shipped to the UK in 2003, including [Dave Bolduc](/people/bolduc.md)'s build CD, and **$40** by January 2008.[95][91] [Don Elliott](/people/don-elliott.md)'s building booklet ran **$12** in 2003, described as sixteen chapters of tips, illustrations, and suggested tools for each process, and **$14** by 2008, with his contact address changed between the two;[95][91] the same page carried his Tomah, Wisconsin postal address as an alternative to e-mail throughout, and recommended the booklet as highly worth having while stating plainly that it is not necessary to build the boat.[95][91] See [building plans](/references/building-plans.md) and [the "Building the Paradox" articles](/references/building-the-paradox-articles.md).

The page also relayed [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)'s own postal address at Jensen Beach, Florida, alongside the observation that he tends to shun publicity, and pointed readers to a Guillemot Kayaks page as the one link then giving any detail about him.[95][91] Bolduc's [study plans](/references/study-plans.md) and study sketches were linked separately, and both Yahoo groups were named — `paradoxbuilders` itself, and the later `Paradoxboatbuilders`.[95][91] By its 2008 revision it had become a directory of individual boats too, splitting them into finished ones — "Little Jim", [the original Paradox](/boats/original-paradox.md), and "[Zoë](/boats/zoe.md)" — and boats still building, namely [Pete Martin](/people/pete-martin.md)'s "[JoHanna](/boats/johanna.md)" and [Matthew Noonan](/people/matthew-noonan.md)'s.[91]

## The plans described sheet by sheet, and a set of CAD renderings

The site carries a **sheet-by-sheet index of Matt Layden's twelve plan sheets**, the only such index in this bundle: sheet 1 the sail plan; 2 the lines; 3 the interior and deck layouts; 4 general construction; 5 the section at frame 3; 6 the side-panel measurements and the cut-out positions on plywood for frames, bottom, decks and transom, together with the building sequence; 7 and 8 further bulkhead details including the transom and stem dimensions; 9 the vent trunk and reefing arrangements; 10 cabin construction; 11 the rudder and chine runners layout; and 12 the spars and yuloh particulars.[84][96] This corroborates the separately reported twelve-sheet count at [building plans](/references/building-plans.md) and puts content against each sheet number — including the two sheets, 6 and 12, whose reading has given builders the most trouble elsewhere in the archive.

Two **CAD renderings** of the design accompanied that index, made by **Paul Raymond** and published with his permission, credited as having helped considerably in understanding how the parts fit together.[84][96] Raymond's own caution travelled with them: the simulations may contain inaccuracies and should be interpreted with care.[84][96] Neither rendering survives in the archived capture, and no other Paradox CAD work of this period is on record in this bundle.

## The boat galleries: nine Paradoxes

The Paradox gallery reached **seven boats** by 2004 and nine in total across the site's life, and its roster is a useful independent check on which boats were actually finished and photographed at the time. Each gallery is credited to the boat's own owner by name and published with permission.[97] The boats are the original Paradox — including photographs from the Exumas;[83] "Zoë";[98] "[Whisper](/boats/whisper.md)";[99] "[Petite Lady](/boats/petite-lady.md)", across two galleries;[100][101] "[Little Jim](/boats/little-jim.md)";[102] "Zero" ([Andrew Graham](/people/andrew-graham.md)'s Australian boat, whose gallery includes interior shots of the wind vent, food store, and under-floor stowage);[103] "[The Edge](/boats/edge.md)" ([Don Elliott](/people/don-elliott.md)'s, with his own interior sketch);[104] "[Jill Baby](/boats/jill.md)" ([Jack Gardiner](/people/jack-gardiner.md)'s, with her Gaspee Point launching site);[105] "[Enuf](/boats/enuf.md)";[106] and [Mark Waters](/people/mark-waters.md)' boat.[107] Photographs were solicited from owners by e-mail, with postal submission of original prints offered as an alternative.[97]

## The companion micro-sailboat section

`smallsailboats.co.uk` was a micro-sailboat site before it was a Paradox site, and its `micro-sb/` section is where its owner set out the standard he was measuring boats against when he came to Paradox. A **micro-sailboat**, as defined there, is wind-powered, built from environmentally sustainable or low-impact materials, virtually non-polluting in use, and equipped basically but sufficiently for safe cruising in the waters it was designed for.[110] A weight ceiling of **350 lb** covers hull, lateral-resistance surfaces, spars, sails, rudder, and tiller, with boats up to 550 lb still admitted, set primarily so one person can launch and recover the boat alone and secondarily to cap the materials a build consumes; displacement, which includes crew and gear, is explicitly distinguished from that weight.[110] Human effort — oars, yulohs, paddles, propellers, paddle wheels, water jets — is the intended secondary power, with electric auxiliaries accepted, more readily if charged from solar or wind.[110] Integral buoyancy sufficient to float boat and crew after a total capsize is called for, and boats should ideally be **self-righting**, with rudder steps and hand grips to let the crew get back aboard.[110]

Paradox meets that standard on every count but the weight ceiling, which she exceeds several times over — the fixed lead ballast alone runs to 397 lb (see [weight, ballast, and displacement](/design/weight-and-ballast.md)) — and she is the design the site's owner went on to build after writing it. Her enclosed, self-righting hull, yuloh auxiliary, engine-free operation, and foam buoyancy answer the rest of the definition point for point, which is much of what the [Why build a Paradox](/boats/little-jim.md#a-scilly-isles-rendezvous-and-a-why-build-paradox-web-page) page argues in its own terms.

The section also documents three boats that are not Paradoxes but that share the design's problem. **"Sleeper"**, by Derek Van Loan, is a decked, hatched sailing dinghy with sleeping accommodation for two, written up for the site by [Don Elliott](/people/don-elliott.md) — a further authored piece of his beyond the "Building the Paradox" series — and notable here for its **Maurice Griffith hatch**, which keeps green water out without seals or gaskets, needing only a hasp, while its drain holes and coaming clearance still admit air.[111] See [hatch](/components/hatch.md) for Paradox's own sliding-hatch arrangement and the recurring question of whether a hinged design would be safer. **"Flying Pig"**, Keith Holdsworth's own design, is built from five pieces of plywood and carries twin keels whose lower sections hinge down under gravity and are linked by rope to the kick-up rudder, so grounding the rudder lifts the keels and lets the boat retreat into deeper water.[112] **"Tread Lightly"**, a 13 ft John Welsford single-hander planned to come in just under the same 350 lb ceiling, is covered as a design the site's owner admired without building.[113]

In use she is virtually non-polluting and her equipment is basic, but sufficient for safe cruising in the locality for which she was designed." A micro-sailboat "should weigh no more than 350 lbs/160 Kgs, (being the complete weight of the integral hull, keel/centreboard/leeboards, spars, sails, rudder and tiller)"; "If your boat weighs up to 550 lbs, she could still be classified as a micro-sailboat"; "The prime reason for a weight restriction is ease of launching and recovery from the water by one person, but a maximum weight limit also controls the amount of materials used in her construction." Weight is explicitly distinguished from displacement, which includes crew and gear. Secondary power "should be physical effort on the part of the crew, who may use any of the following: oars, yulohs, paddles, propellers, paddle wheels, water jets"; auxiliary electric engines are acceptable, "but more so, if their batteries are charged by solar or wind generated electricity." Integral buoyancy sufficient "to keep her and her crew afloat" after a total capsize is called for, and boats "should be self-righting, with aids such as rudder steps and hand grips to enable the crew to regain access."

A design the site's owner admired without building.
