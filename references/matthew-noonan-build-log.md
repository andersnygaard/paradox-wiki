# Matthew E. Noonan's Paradox build log

**"Building a Paradox Sharpie"** is a daily build journal kept by **[Matthew E. Noonan](/people/matthew-noonan.md)**, then a graduate student in mathematics at **Cornell University**, on his university web space at **Ithaca, New York**. It runs **eight entries**, from the plans arriving on **6 June 2006** to **Day 7 on 16 June 2006**, and it stops there.

Its particular value is **granularity at the one stage most build records skip.** Finished builders write up a Paradox in phases — bulkheads, hull, deck, fit-out — and the first week disappears into a sentence. This log gives that week day by day: which tools were bought, which sheet would not lay out, which scarphing method was tried and abandoned, and what the first hour with epoxy was like. Four of its eight entries fall **before its author's first message to the [mailing list](/references/mailing-list.md)**, so for 6–11 June 2006 it is the only record of this build that exists.

## What the log is worth as evidence

It is **contemporaneous, builder-authored, and written the same day as the work.** Entries are dated and numbered by build day, illustrated with the writer's own photographs, and candid about failure — the first plywood scarph is reported as having "predictably" come out badly from a poorly-made jig, before the method that replaced it is described.[1][2]

Its limits are severe and should be stated plainly. It is **eight days of one build**, and it ends **before the hull is assembled** — no bottom, no chine runners, no deck, no rig, nothing about how a Paradox sails. The build's remaining recorded progress is on the mailing list, not here. And the log has **no ending**: it was never continued past Day 7, and no source in the archive records whether the boat was finished. The most recent fixed point is the [2014 census](/references/builders-and-owners-census-2014.md), which still lists the boat as "In build," with no name.

## Provenance, dating, and the moved address

The journal was written at `www.math.cornell.edu/~noonan/boats/paradox.html`, which is the address recorded in the builder's own 2006 mailing-list message and, from that message, in this archive.[3] **That address no longer resolves.** Cornell's mathematics department has since moved its personal web space, and the journal is now served at:

> `https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~noonan/boats/paradox.html`

with the parent page, "Messing About in Ithaca," at `https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~noonan/boats/`.

The page presents its entries in **reverse-chronological order**, newest first, each headed by a date and a build-day number. It carries no separate posting timestamps, so each entry is dated only by its own heading. Independent archive snapshots show the page had reached **four entries by 16 June 2006** and its full **eight by July 2012**, unchanged from then to the present — so Days 4 through 7 were written up within days of the work, and nothing was added or removed afterward.

## Relationship to the mailing-list record

The two records are **consecutive far more than they overlap.** The log runs 6–16 June 2006; the builder's mailing-list messages run 12 June – 16 August 2006. Only the five days of 12–16 June appear in both, and even there they carry different things — on 16 June the log records beams going into the jig while the list message describes Ithaca and asks [Derek Clark](/people/derek-clark.md) about pigment and UV.[4]

The division is clean:

- **The log alone** holds 6–11 June: the plans, the materials order, the tool buying, the first four bulkheads, and the panel-layout problem.
- **The list alone** holds 21 July – 16 August 2006: the bottom panel wired on and scarfed, the [lightning-protection](/design/lightning-protection.md) copper flashing, the chine runners, and the rig questions — all already recorded at [people/matthew-noonan.md](/people/matthew-noonan.md).

Where the two touch, neither corrects the other. The log is cited here for the first week and for the three technique findings it carries that the list does not: the plans CD's failed alternate sheet layout (see [panel layout](/construction/panel-layout.md#tight-spots-builders-hit)), the stepped-scarph method (see [scarf joints](/construction/scarf-joints.md#a-2006-stepped-scarph-cut-to-each-glue-line-and-chiselled-back)), and an early attestation of [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)'s scarf-versus-taped-butt verdict.

## Citing this source

The whole journal resolves at `https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~noonan/boats/paradox.html`; cite in the form:

> [Matthew E. Noonan's Paradox build log](/references/matthew-noonan-build-log.md) — entry of YYYY-MM-DD ("Day N") — [detail].

As a dated snapshot of a historical record, its content is cited in the past tense and dated to the entry it comes from, consistent with the bundle's historical-archive convention. The author signed his mailing-list messages "Matt" and publishes as **Matt Noonan**; the fuller form is used for the page title only.
