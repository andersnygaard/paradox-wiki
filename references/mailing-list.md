# Paradox builders mailing list

The primary community for Paradox builders and sailors, and the source archive for this bundle.

# History

| Era | Group | Notes |
|-----|-------|-------|
| 2001– | `paradoxbuilders` (Yahoo Groups) | Founded 7 July 2001 to exchange building and sailing information.[1] |
| later | `paradoxbuilders2` (Yahoo Groups) | Second Yahoo group; a dedicated file-upload group was first proposed in March 2004, once the original group's photo storage ran short, with an initial response of archiving old files and considering a separate website; actually founded on 10 May 2004, by the acting moderator during a founder's Atlantic crossing, once the storage limit was reached in earnest — see [the photo-storage limit, and the founding of paradoxbuilders2](#the-photo-storage-limit-and-the-founding-of-paradoxbuilders2) below. |
| 2019– | `paradoxsailboat` (groups.io) | Created 5 Nov 2019 by the list owner **"Mattsplatt"** (signing "Matt") to replace both Yahoo groups; content migrated over. |

The 2019 groups.io owner posted under the screen name **"Mattsplatt"** and signed "Matt".[2] He is **not** designer [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md) — see [the 2019 Yahoo Groups shutdown scare](#the-2019-yahoo-groups-shutdown-scare) below for the identification.

The group's **Files area** is organised into folders — e.g. **construction**, **stove**, and **Study Plans** — hosting construction photos and a guide/description of the study plans (uploaded early on).[3][4] The dedicated "stove" folder points to a cabin stove being a fitted item on these boats. See [Study Plans](/references/study-plans.md).

# Sibling and successor groups

Beyond the `paradoxbuilders`/`paradoxsailboat` lineage itself, two related but separate Yahoo groups came up as cross-references for Paradox builders:

- **[Micro Cruising](/references/micro-cruising-group.md)** — a broader micro-cruising discussion group started around September 2001, which Paradox builders were repeatedly encouraged to also join; still an active cross-reference as late as April 2002.
- **Smallboats** (`http://groups.yahoo.com/group/Smallboats`) — a separate Yahoo group Paradox questions were redirected to after a resignation from `paradoxbuilders` in October 2001, stating a refusal to "belong to any group that promotes unsafe practices."[5] See [plywood grade and foam flotation debate](/construction/plywood-grades-and-foam-flotation.md) for the exchange that preceded this. Smallboats' scope, membership, and activity are not otherwise documented in this archive.

A further, informal alternative surfaced in **August 2011**: [Alberto Callejon](/people/alberto-callejon.md) announced a new **"Paradox" group on Facebook**, inviting members to share experiences, information, and photographs.[24] The proposal drew a defense of the mailing list's own value — an established archive, searchable by subject, holding years of contributions from long-standing members, and judged less intrusive than Facebook — alongside doubt that a second forum was needed at all.[25] A reply took a middle view: Facebook suits real-time social contact better, while a Yahoo- or Google-group format remains the better resource for technical support and archived material.[26] Responding in turn, the Facebook group's own founder stressed no competing intent, crediting the help received on the mailing list with having let him complete his own build.[27] Further opinion split along the same lines — one member's own family Yahoo Group saw its traffic collapse from 200-400 messages a month to under 20 within a year of starting a Facebook page for the same family, which drew around 20 posts a day at its busiest, and judged Facebook's interface more powerful for anyone actively building, asking questions, and engaging rather than just reviewing a daily digest;[28] another held that Facebook offered no practical way to search or preserve older posts, unlike the mailing list's own archive.[29] Both groups continued afterward, members free to belong to either or both; see [references/paradox-facebook-group.md](/references/paradox-facebook-group.md) for the disambiguation from the unrelated, newer "Paradox Sailboat" Facebook group founded in 2019.

# The 2019 Yahoo Groups shutdown scare

On 16 October 2019 Yahoo announced it would delete Yahoo Groups' content outright, touching off the mailing list's most substantial migration debate on record. Rob Kellock (`creditscorenz`) opened the thread, proposing a move to the Facebook group [Alberto Callejon](/people/alberto-callejon.md) had founded in 2011 (see above), absent a better idea.[31] A new correspondent, "Matt" (`mattlippey`), asked instead whether anyone held a copy of the archive's roughly 9,500 messages: Yahoo's own export tool returned only a member's own posts, not the whole group, and he did not know who the current moderator was; he added that he did not use Facebook and, "if I do eventually build and launch a Paradox," would sign up temporarily to say so.[32] Eerik Mee argued for a self-hosted phpBB forum instead — subjects sorted and photos organized boat by boat, unlike Facebook, where an old post from a newer, still-building member is hard to find again — provided someone was willing to host it.[33] Frederic Tallman ("Deric," owner of [Edge](/boats/edge.md)) objected to Facebook over what he took to be its data mining, and said he would keep posting to the Yahoo group for as long as it remained possible.[34] The list's head moderator, [Glen C. Maxwell](/people/glen-maxwell.md), said he would look into archiving what could be saved, naming Facebook's own inability to search back through past discussions as its biggest drawback — the same reply's own quoted header shows the account it answers, `mattlippey`, posting as "paradox@mattsplatt.com."[35] Rob Kellock, closing the thread's substantive exchange, reported that his own, smaller Yahoo group's export tool had the identical limitation, and judged an open, self-hostable solution — Eerik's idea — the only one likely to last;[36] Tallman, agreeing, recalled a hydrofoil-trimaran forum's own past shutdown as a loss of otherwise-inaccessible record, and offered to help fund a hosted alternative despite having no technical means to build one himself.[37]

Later the same evening, `mattlippey` reported he had found a way to hit the Yahoo Groups API directly with a script, had already downloaded roughly 80% of the message history into a database format he could put to direct use, and offered to host a new archive and forum once he could arrange a new address before the shutdown — asking how many members were still around and would actually use one; realizing on a re-read that the message had come out "about half in geek-speak," he repeated the same offer more plainly a few minutes later.[38][39] `woodersonj` ("Woody"), a longtime non-building commentator who held the plans but had never had the time or resources to build, valued the list purely as a resource "for dreaming and scheming over the years" and pointed to groups.io's own published guide for transferring a Yahoo Group.[40] Rob Kellock, closing out the day's exchange, put a number on the list's own health — his own sense, from the pattern of a video link drawing a burst of replies before long silences, was that no more than 100 people were actively following the group and perhaps two builds were in progress — and judged self-hosting a genuinely awkward problem: the group's own format combines a bulletin board, a mailing list and a CMS, trading email-security exposure for the engagement only a mailing list brings, and bandwidth cost for the photos and videos members would expect to keep posting. "There's no free lunches, that's for sure."[41]

The following morning, a further correspondent, Larry Howell, reported that one of his own hobby Yahoo Groups had just switched to groups.io, with an easy transfer path from Yahoo Groups.[42] `mattlippey` followed with the specific tools he had found for extracting the archive himself — a wiki page on Yahoo Groups archiving from the Archive Team, and a GitHub-hosted "YahooGroups-Archiver" script, together with the cookie information a script needs to access a private group — judging the web side of a successor easy for him to build or host, but a proper email-list server a different undertaking he was not sure he would tackle.[43] Putting the platform choice to the list directly — a true email-list system, or a web-only archive with an ongoing forum — he noted Rob Kellock's own point that engagement would differ if the group stopped being an email list, and settled on groups.io as the way to go after both Woody and Howell had pointed to its easy migration path from Yahoo Groups; groups.io, he added, had itself been created by Mark Fletcher, who had originally written "ONElist/eGroups" before Yahoo acquired and renamed it Yahoo Groups in early 2000, this time because he wanted it "done right" and felt he had "unfinished business."[44]

Four days later, on 20 October, `mattlippey` confirmed the destination and turned to the mechanics of the handover — pointing to groups.io's own wiki page on transferring administrative access from a Yahoo Group, asking whether a sitting moderator wanted to own the new group, and offering, with their permission, to take on the ownership and set it up himself if no one else wanted the job.[46] On 24 October he reported he was about to create the group and put its name to a vote — keep `paradoxbuilders`, or something more general such as `paradoxsailboat`?[47] Three replies settled it unanimously for the broader name, one reasoning that a general name suited a list he read as much for owners' ongoing "trimming and fiddling" after a build as for builders still under construction.[48]

The mechanics took longer to settle than the name. Douglas Depperman, a returning correspondent, pointed to groups.io's own published process for transferring a Yahoo Group wholesale, having seen it mentioned on another Yahoo group facing the same shutdown.[49] Larry Howell, replying, warned that the same mass exodus from Yahoo might already have the formal path backed up, and that members might need to save and repost their own material by hand if a transfer could not be arranged in time; one of his own hobby groups, for aircraft, had been grandfathered into a $10-a-month, 10 GB groups.io plan since roughly doubled in price for new signups.[50] `mattlippey` found the formal Yahoo-to-groups.io transfer would cost $220 (AU$320) for a Premium account, balked at paying it, and created a free `paradoxsailboat` group himself instead, asking the list how to proceed.[51] A member named William then offered to fund the Premium upgrade outright; `mattlippey` thanked him and invited him into the new group to become an owner and upgrade it directly, or offered to take payment by PayPal instead.[52] The same evening he separately asked Glen Maxwell or another moderator to make him a moderator of both `paradoxbuilders` and the [paradoxbuilders2](#the-photo-storage-limit-and-the-founding-of-paradoxbuilders2) overflow group, so he could grant groups.io's own transfer tool the Yahoo-side access it needed to collect the data, linking its process page.[53] Howell, prompted by William's gift, noted groups.io built a running donation tally into a group's own page — his aircraft group had drawn enough in its first day or two to cover five years — and that the platform's price increase was itself meant to fund the extra servers needed for the hundreds or thousands of groups leaving Yahoo at once.[54] The following morning `mattlippey` announced the new group was now Premium and able to run the full transfer, asked everyone else to do nothing yet while he waited on a Yahoo-side moderator to grant him the access the move needed, and explained what it would do — copy every member and their existing settings across, and send each one a welcome email from the new system.[55] [David C. Smith](/people/david-c-smith.md) answered that request directly: he held only a moderator's rights on `paradoxbuilders` himself, with [Glen Maxwell](/people/glen-maxwell.md) the group's sole owner and the only person able to grant the access a migration needed, and pointed `mattlippey` to him — noting separately that he and Maxwell co-owned the group he called `Paradoxowners2` — the same `paradoxbuilders2` named above, as he confirmed himself in an almost identical message sent minutes apart, this time naming it correctly — on which he had already made `mattlippey` an owner.[56][57]

Three weeks later, on 5 November 2019, a list owner posting as **"Mattsplatt"** founded `paradoxsailboat` on groups.io as the successor group (see History, above) — the same address `mattlippey` had posted this exchange from.[35] `mattlippey` had told the list as much once before, in 2013, unprompted: "No. This is another Matt entirely. In Australia."[45] He is a long-standing, distinct correspondent, not designer [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md); see [The "Mattsplatt" identity](/matt-layden/biography.md#the-mattsplatt-identity) for the fuller disambiguation.

Getting that Yahoo-side access sorted took another eleven days. `mattlippey` replied to Smith that he would tackle the larger group first in case he could only manage one migration, and asked whether Smith could grant him the narrower access groups.io's transfer tool also accepted in place of full ownership — "either the Owner of the Yahoo group, or a Moderator with invite and promote privileges."[58] Direct requests to [Glen Maxwell](/people/glen-maxwell.md) himself then went unanswered for over a week: a first, on 29 October, asked him to check an email already sent three days earlier, and a second, more urgent one followed on 4 November.[59][60] Maxwell replied on 6 November, apologising to the group for the delay — he had just returned from a trip to the Azores and had been occupied ever since — and confirmed `mattlippey` was now an owner, clearing the last obstacle to the transfer; `mattlippey` thanked him the same day.[61][62] Eight days later, on the new platform itself, `mattlippey` confirmed the transfer was complete, and separately queued the smaller, little-used `paradoxbuilders2` file-overflow group (see below) for transfer as well, "to preserve its content here too," though it had never shared `paradoxbuilders`' full membership.[63]

Two years on, the group's own upkeep drew a fresh appeal: `paradoxsailboat`'s membership and file storage had outgrown groups.io's free tier, so "Mattsplatt" asked members to sponsor its $20-a-month ($220-a-year) hosting cost via the platform's own sponsorship feature, having already covered a first year's renewal himself in October 2020 and being willing to do so again if needed.[82] By the following week enough had come in that the subscription could switch to monthly billing with four months already in reserve, closing the loop on the founding story above: "Mattsplatt" thanked **William** again — the same member who had funded the Premium upgrade that let the original move to groups.io go ahead — for having "allowed the move to Groups.io in the first place."[83]

# The photo-storage limit, and the founding of paradoxbuilders2

`paradoxbuilders`' Yahoo Groups **photo storage limit** — first flagged as a display-resolution problem in March 2004 — was reached in earnest the following month, while the founder was away on an Atlantic crossing aboard "Hogfish Maximus" (see [an Atlantic crossing](/people/glen-maxwell.md#an-atlantic-crossing-aboard-hogfish-maximus)). New "Whisper" photos uploaded during this window triggered a recurring group error that locked the uploader out of the photo section entirely, a problem independently confirmed by another member the same day.[6][7]

The acting moderator filling the administrative gap diagnosed the underlying cause: the group had hit its Yahoo-imposed storage ceiling outright, rather than just a display-resolution limit as in March. As an immediate step, large-format photos were deleted from the "Swash" album, then a genuinely new group, **`paradoxbuilders2`**, was founded on 10 May 2004, dedicated to photo and file storage overflow — asking members to keep posting to `paradoxbuilders` itself and treat the new group purely as backup capacity, so the discussion itself would not split across two groups, and restricting the new group's photos and files to material specific to Paradox and other Matt Layden designs. This settles when and by whom `paradoxbuilders2` was founded: the acting moderator, once the storage limit had gone from a nuisance to a hard block.[8][9]

Even after the new group existed, the photo section remained inaccessible for removing the offending pictures. Further investigation found that **deleting**, rather than merely resizing, three photos was needed to bring the section back under its limit — chosen for being the least Paradox-specific of the group's content rather than for any fault of their own: a steel dinghy, a Bris rudder, and a "water in my backyard" photo. With those removed, general access to the photo section was restored. A division of labor followed: use `paradoxbuilders2`'s **files** section (rather than its photos section) for posting photos in folders, since the original group's files area had been kept free of photos, while reserving the original group's own files section for data.[10][11]

With access restored, new "Whisper" photos continued over the following days — first a further batch to `paradox2`, then a complete set of sailing photos to `paradoxbuilders2` itself, taken with a chase boat to capture the boat "on all points of sail."[12][13]

A year later, the original group's files section again neared capacity, raising the question of a `paradoxbuilders2`-style overflow group — answered by the fact that `paradoxbuilders2` already existed, with its files section still empty, confirming that the May 2004 overflow group had seen little actual use for its intended purpose.[14][15] The exchange also prompted encouragement to keep "ParaPlastic" build photos coming[16] (see [the "BRIS Paradox" — window test-fitting in polycarbonate](/boats/bris-paradox.md#window-test-fitting-in-polycarbonate)).

By February 2006 the original group also reached Yahoo's separate member-capacity limit — distinct from the 2004 storage ceiling — after which new members were directed to `paradoxbuilders2` instead.[17][18]

`paradoxbuilders2`'s own first message, posted the same week as the near-identical announcement above, opened the new group in near-identical terms: overflow for photos and files specific to `paradoxbuilders`, the Paradox design, and other works by Matt Layden, with discussion itself to stay on `paradoxbuilders`.[64] That division of labor was tested within days: a newcomer, Leon, asked the new group directly whether enlarging Paradox by 25% would fit himself, a second person, and a dog, and was told to raise it on `paradoxbuilders` instead, where the question had already been argued both ways in the archives; Dave Bolduc followed up with a specific starting point there.[65][66][67] Leon's question went unanswered on the new group itself for more than two years, until Stephen Yahn gave his own account there of trying a 25% stretch — in full at [Stretched Paradox](/design/ideas/stretched-paradox.md#a-25-stretch-judged-too-big-and-heavy-and-a-narrower-steel-bar-center-frame).[79] `paradoxbuilders2` stayed the quieter group throughout its life, kept going largely by reminders that discussion belonged on `paradoxbuilders` and only photos and files on the overflow group, repeated three months apart in 2004[68] and once more the following spring,[72] and by Yahoo's own requirement of some sign of life roughly every three months to avoid a group's automatic deletion — spelled out that same August, with a nod to David Beard's own restored "Whisper" photos as the kind of post that counted.[69] A November 2004 offer to let those same "Whisper" photos be deleted rather than keep `paradoxbuilders2` up any longer was met with reassurance that a token post every few months, nothing more, was all the group actually needed.[70][71]

A year later, in May 2005, a new correspondent reported that "[Whisper](/boats/whisper.md)" photos alone were taking up 47% of the group's allotted photo space, and that Yahoo showed an uploaded photo at a reduced size — as little as 15 KB — to everyone except its own poster and the group's moderator, who alone could see the original at up to roughly 900 KB, asking that the photos be moved to the files section instead.[73] Glen Maxwell began downloading and re-uploading them himself at a standard 800x600 resolution and asked the group to pre-resize their own photos the same way to keep file sizes manageable;[74] David Beard, whose "Whisper" photos had prompted the exchange, offered to have them deleted outright rather than cause any trouble, an offer Maxwell declined.[75][76] A full-size copy went into the files section within the week, alongside a smaller one left in the photo album.[77][78] A server outage briefly interrupted the last of the move overnight, but by 25 May 2005 the whole set of photos had been relocated to the files section.[80][81]

# The 2024 hosting question

On 22 January 2024, "Mattsplatt" gave the fullest public account yet of the group's running costs: 67 messages had been posted in 2022 and 63 in 2023, groups.io's hosting cost $20 a month or $220 paid annually, and the group held 1.8 GB of data, mostly photos, against the free tier's 1 GB cap. William's funding of the first year after the 2019 migration and Matt's own funding of the second were already on record;[82][83] recapping the 2021 sponsorship appeal, he noted that nine members had contributed, and that he had been paying the $20 a month himself since May 2022. His own situation had since changed, and no longer able to keep funding the group, he put the question of what to do next to the whole membership.[84]

The response converged quickly on downgrading to the free tier, with the storage cap the only real complication: at 1.8 GB against a 1 GB limit, roughly half the group's stored photos would need to be cleared out or moved elsewhere.[85][86][87][88][90][91] Storage proposals ranged from Google Drive — offered independently by two members with their own unused capacity, one raising a shared dedicated account or several redundant hosts with public access, the other a personal 100 GB account already half-used — to Dropbox, and to simply posting a Google Drive share-link back to the group.[86][89][90][91][92] A further Google Drive method arrived the next day: sharing a specific folder so only invited members could reach it, opening a second free account for another 15 GB, other cloud hosts offering up to 50 GB, and a link-shortener to compress the resulting long addresses — "my contribution to the general confusion."[93]

The downgrade itself was carried out within two days of the question being raised: on 24 January Matt reported the response had been unanimous, the group had already been switched to groups.io's free tier with the changeover expected the next day or so, and a full backup of the group's data was already in hand. No host had yet come forward for the photo collection itself, so his own fallback, pending one, was simply to delete the oldest photos until the collection fit the free tier's 1 GB cap, reasoning that other copies of the material already existed; he put that plan to the membership directly.[94] The one direct reply on record endorsed it.[95] The funding question that opened the thread was settled by the downgrade itself rather than by a new sponsor; no single storage host or long-term photo solution had been confirmed as of this record.

# A January 2025 moderation note, and a Facebook cross-link question

Replying within the "Current Email Address to Buy POlans" thread the following January (see [Paradox building plans — a January 2025 recurrence](/references/building-plans.md#a-january-2025-recurrence)), "Mattsplatt" apologised for several messages sitting unapproved, explaining that every new member's first message is held for a moderator to see before it goes through, and confirmed once more, "I am in Australia."[96] In a separate reply to the same thread twenty minutes later, he raised an unrelated point: he had not known the Paradox Sailboat Facebook group existed, wondered why the mailing list had not simply moved there back in 2019 — guessing Facebook groups might not support being used as an e-mail list server — and, having no Facebook account of his own, asked whether any member belonged to both and could help arrange a cross-link between the groups.io home page and the Facebook group.[97]

A reply the same week took the question as raising a possible move rather than just a cross-link, and argued for keeping both platforms running in parallel: groups.io stores files and photos clearly and is easy to search back through, while Facebook's stronger visual appeal comes at the cost of a growing thread becoming hard to search as it ages — in this view, "having both resources is ideal."[98] No further reply followed for over a month. When the thread resurfaced in late February, two further correspondents weighed in against a move: one hoped the forum would not be moved to Facebook outright, since he does not use it and would lose track of what was happening;[99] a second agreed, reporting his own move away from Facebook and a preference for groups.io, adding that his own build was moving at a glacial pace and that having this space to follow it would be useful "for quite a while."[100] The specific cross-link request itself was never taken up on record.

## Whether WaterTribe racing tests the boat or the crew

A recurring question about Matt Layden's WaterTribe results — is a race like the Everglades Challenge really a test of the boat, or mainly of the crew? — drew answers on both sides. A skeptical aside dismissed WaterTribe racing as an easy "blow up canoe" contest, testing crews rather than boats.[19] That was answered, first, with a pointed rebuttal on the course itself: the Gulf of Mexico leg is far from trivial — a large, deep body of water that can carry a 35 mph wind, is only rarely as calm as a "duck pond," and turns genuinely dangerous in a hurricane.[20] A clarification followed that the "blow up canoe" remark had only meant that WaterTribe's organizer thoroughly vets the boats and equipment entered, not that the race itself was easy.[21] Separately, a fuller answer conceded that the month-long Ultimate [Florida] Challenge probably does test paddlers more than boats, though even so the Everglades Challenge has weeded out boats through mechanical failure, and a swamped racing boat with a lead keel sinks fast while a small boat built with positive flotation does not; a further parallel was drawn to an English Channel race where a storm forced crew rescues while the abandoned racing boats themselves stayed afloat.[22]

**Most competitors never actually leave sight of land** — the course runs mostly through sheltered, shoal water, and many entrants are kayakers or canoeists with no offshore sailing experience at all. **Matt Layden was among the first WaterTribe competitors to work the deeper water** rather than hug the shoal coastline, on the reasoning that deeper water is often calmer than the shallows closer in.[21] Two further factors were judged to matter as much as boat choice: not needing to land and make camp each night (landing and departing through surf, and sleeping with the bugs, cost real time compared with heaving-to or drifting through a nap afloat), and a cabin or sprayhood's shelter from wind, rain, cold, and sun alike. Matt himself raced in shorts, a T-shirt, and bare feet, with a fleece top only on cold mornings, carrying rain gear and footwear mainly for the overland portages, and never wore a high-tech life vest — only a couple of old ten-dollar horse-collar types, to the organizer's own amazement. Being able to rest aboard and stay sheltered from the elements were credited with the energy reserves that let him keep sailing home after one race's finish, rather than stopping at the finish line itself — see [Matt Layden's WaterTribe racing record](/matt-layden/biography.md#the-watertribe-years-enigma-sand-flea-and-elusion) for the full racing history.[21]

**A correction to the record on which race used which boat:** the inflatable-kayak result credited above to a Florida Challenge was actually sailed in the **Michigan Challenge** (August 2003); in the 2004 Florida Challenge itself, Matt instead used a small kayak of his own building called the "Rob Royoid," built to make the Everglades' inland portage sections more bearable — both already covered in full at [the 14 ft Rob Roy-based canoe, and the 2005 Everglades Challenge](/matt-layden/biography.md#the-14-ft-rob-roy-based-canoe-and-the-2005-everglades-challenge). WaterTribe describes its own purpose as encouraging "the development of boats, techniques, skills, equipment, and fitness for safe and efficient coastal expeditions and wilderness tripping using minimal impact human and wind powered watercraft based on kayaks, canoes, and small multihulls."[23]

## Citing this source

The list is hosted today at [groups.io/g/paradoxsailboat](https://groups.io/g/paradoxsailboat) — roughly 1,004 members and 2,201 topics, its archive visible to members only. It began 7 July 2001 on Yahoo Groups as `paradoxbuilders`, and its content migrated to groups.io in 2019. Message numbering is not chronological: message 1 is the 2019 groups.io welcome post, and the migrated 2001–2008 Yahoo-era history occupies roughly messages 2–5600. Each message's own number is its permanent address, `https://groups.io/g/paradoxsailboat/message/N` (for example, message 3899 is Paul VandenBosch, "Whaleback, Paradox derivative," 2006-08-28).

Other pages cite this source in the form:

> [Paradox builders mailing list](/references/mailing-list.md) message N (account / "Handle" / Name, `group`, YYYY-MM-DD) — [detail].

Citations on this page itself are bare — `message N (account / "Handle" / Name, group, YYYY-MM-DD)` with no self-link — since this page is the hub the rest of the bundle links to.
