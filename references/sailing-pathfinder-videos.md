# Sailing-Pathfinder (Benjamin Bloss's YouTube build log)

**Sailing-Pathfinder** is the YouTube channel on which **[Benjamin Bloss](/people/benjamin-bloss.md)** documents the building of his [Paradox](/paradox.md) **"[Pathfinder](/boats/pathfinder.md)"** near Seattle, Washington. It is a **talk-to-camera build log** of twelve videos running from **24 November 2021** — before the first cut — to **1 May 2025**, and it was begun in conscious imitation of **[Sven Yrvind](/people/yrvind.md)**, whose short daily workshop videos Bloss cites as the model and recommends to viewers in his opening minutes.[1]

Its particular value to the archive is that it is a **record of an unfinished build, kept while the builder is still inside the problem.** Nearly every other first-person Paradox source in the bundle was written by someone who got to the water — [Pete Martin's blog](/references/pete-martin-blog.md), [Bill's Log](/references/bills-log.md), [Dave Skaife's blog](/references/dave-skaife-blog.md) — and a finished builder narrates the route that worked. Bloss narrates the attempts that did not: the plywood that would not take the baffle's curve, the pre-calculated bevels he later judged a mistake, the wax paper that let epoxy bond a side panel to its support beam. That makes it the archive's fullest account of the **hull-assembly stage** as experienced rather than as recommended.

## What the videos are worth as evidence

They are **contemporaneous, builder-authored, and self-critical**. Each video is recorded within days of the work it describes and is explicit about what went wrong, and Bloss states the motive plainly: as painful as it is to record work in progress, he shares it so other builders have some insight into what was tried, "might not be the best, but at least it's some insight."[2] Several passages are of a kind that written build logs rarely carry — decisions being weighed aloud, before the outcome is known, with the plans open on the bench.

They also have real limits. This is **one builder, on one boat, not yet finished**: as of the last video the hull is a decked-over-nothing — bulkheads, sides, stem, transom, sheer logs and chine logs assembled, bottom not yet on — so nothing here speaks to how a Paradox sails, and much of what is described is a **plan** rather than a completed piece of work. A substantial fraction of the running time is not about the boat at all but about the builder's life around it, and only the boat-relevant material is drawn on here. Bloss is also candid that his own memory of his methods decays: recording the stem a month after making it, he could recall only part of how he did it, and said so on camera — which is itself an argument for the source, and a caution against reading its method descriptions as complete.[3]

## Provenance and dating

The videos are dated in their titles and spoken aloud in their opening seconds, and the two agree throughout **except in one case**: the eleventh video is titled an "Intermission — Winter 2022/2023" but was **recorded on 2 May 2023**, looking back at a winter in which no work was done. Where this bundle dates that video, it uses the **recording date**, not the title's season.

The source read here is the **auto-generated transcript** of each video, so punctuation, capitalisation, and proper nouns are approximate — "Okeanos Explorer" appears as "okionus," "Yrvind" as "irvin" and "a svenjorvind," "Emiliano" for the sailmaking author. Wording is therefore cited as paraphrase unless it is clearly verbatim. The transcripts carry no picture, and several passages refer to photographs, plan sheets, and parts held up to the camera, so the visual content of the videos is **not** captured here.

## Relationship to the Facebook record

Bloss is also the archive's most methodical builder-poster in the [Paradox Sailboat Facebook group](/references/paradox-facebook-group.md), and the two records **interlock rather than duplicate**. Three of his group posts are announcements of these videos, so the group's own record points at them.[4] Cross-read, the two sources divide neatly: the **group posts carry his questions to other builders and the answers he got**, while the **videos carry his reasoning, his methods, and his verdicts on them afterward.** Where the group has a conclusion, the video usually has the working.

Two reconciliations are worth stating, because in both the video is the later and better source:

- **The vent-trunk drain.** In March 2023 Bloss put to the group a viewer's report that [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md) had told Yrvind he would not fit the drain again, and said he was "tempted to just leave the tube plugged."[5] The group thread ends there. The video of **2 May 2023** records the resolution: having asked other builders and found no documentation, he decided to **fit the drain as per plans**, judging the boat too stable for the failure mode to be a realistic worry and a plug an available remedy if it ever were — and noting the hole was drilled already, "that's the hard part."[6] The bundle follows the video.
- **The deck beams.** The group records only that he was "experimenting with laminating 1/4" Sandee for the deck beams. It's a lot of lamination."[7] The video records that he laminated **seven** for a boat needing six, that the spare was deliberate, and that the spoiled one was lost to **peel ply wedged between two laminations** rather than to any fault of the method.[8]

On dates, the two sources also bracket each other: the video record opens **a week before** the group's first build post and closes in May 2025, while the group's Bloss record runs on to **August 2025**, and it is there — not here — that the build's most recent recorded state is found.

## Citing this source

Each video resolves at `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=<id>`, with the ids listed in the table above. Cite in the form:

> [Sailing-Pathfinder build videos](/references/sailing-pathfinder-videos.md) (YouTube), Benjamin Bloss — video #N, YYYY-MM-DD — [detail].

As a dated record of a build in progress, its content is cited in the past tense and dated to the video it comes from, consistent with the bundle's historical-archive convention. Statements of intent are reported as intent: much of what these videos describe had not been done yet when they were recorded, and several such plans were later reversed on camera.
