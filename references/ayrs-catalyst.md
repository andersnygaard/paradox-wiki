# Catalyst — journal of the Amateur Yacht Research Society

**Catalyst** is the quarterly journal of the **Amateur Yacht Research Society** (AYRS), a UK registered educational charity (No 234081) "for the furthering of yacht science," published from BCM AYRS, London, and edited in this period by **Simon Fishwick** (ISSN 1469-6754). It is a technical research journal rather than a cruising magazine: its subject is yacht *science* — hydrofoils, kites, wing masts, speed-record craft, lateral resistance — written up by the experimenters themselves and edited by specialist correspondents.

That editorial character is what makes it useful here. Where [Messing About In Boats](/references/messing-about-in-boats.md) documented Paradox for an American small-boat readership and [microcruising.com](/references/microcruising-website.md) is the Bolducs' own site, Catalyst approached the [chine runner](/chine-runners/index.md) as a **research question** — and its editor was willing to set Matt Layden's invention against prior art and to say plainly what was still unknown about it.

## The Paradox content: issue no. 32, October 2008

The issue relevant to this bundle is **no. 32 (October 2008)**, which carries two distinct items.

**Sven Yrvind, "A Small Voyaging Boat" (pp. 8–13)** — a feature by [Sven Yrvind](/people/yrvind.md) describing *Yrvind*, the 16 ft ocean-voyaging boat he was then building, "in designing her I have had invaluable help from Matt Layden."[1] Roughly the last two pages turn to Paradox directly: how he came to the design, his month sailing her in October 1997, his return visits, the three-source account of her leeway resistance, her stability, her reefing boom, and her 2003 race record. The article was an entry for the **2008 John Hogg Prize**, the society's own annual award.[1][2]

Its value to this bundle is **date and independence**. The same material is on record from Yrvind elsewhere — on [yrvind.com](/references/yrvind-website.md), and in a [2025 video](/references/yrvind-chine-runner-video.md) — but this is the account **fixed in edited print in 2008**, written by Yrvind in English for a technical readership, roughly a decade after the events it describes and years before the web versions. Where it agrees with those sources it dates their claims; where it adds detail (the second correspondent's name, the Everglades finishing margin, the stability mechanism) that detail is contemporaneous.

**Simon Fishwick's editorial, "Lateral Thinking and Resistance" (p. 2)** — the editor's introduction to the Yrvind feature, and the only source in this bundle that sets the chine runner in a **wider research history**: similar devices used by **Prof. Walter Castles in 1976** (AYRS booklet #83A) and since fitted "with some success on a few Wharram catamarans," with Layden credited for bringing the idea to *monohulls*. It also puts on record the editor's own judgment that efficient runner design has unresolved subtleties, and an open call for readers who know better.[2] This is an outside, non-partisan voice on the feature — neither the designer, nor his friends, nor his builders.

### The drawings

Ten of the twelve figures in the Yrvind feature are **Matt Layden's own work for another designer's boat** — eight dimensioned hand drawings initialled **ML** and dated **2007** (rudder layout, daggerboard, chine runner, two bulkhead-dimension sheets, storm-sail and sail dimensions), plus two signed perspective sketches, one inscribed *"16' Voyaging Sailboat for Sven Yrvind — Matt Layden 2007"* and one *"'Yrvind' Sailing, Stuart FL 2007, Matt Layden."* The drawing titles read **"Yrvind 5.0"** / **"Yrvind 5.0m,"** matching the article's 16 ft (≈4.9 m). Yrvind's closing paragraph thanks Matt for "doing the drawings and artwork."[1]

Among them is a **dimensioned chine-runner drawing in Layden's own hand** — plan and profile views with stations along the hull — and a bulkhead section that labels the chine runners and annotates the sandwich as "30–90 mm Divinycell foam core."[1] They are the fullest published drawings by Layden the bundle records, though they are drawings of *Yrvind*, not of Paradox, and are cited only for what they show about his hand and his chine-runner practice at that date.

## Citing this source

AYRS publishes Catalyst back issues as free PDFs at **`https://www.ayrs.org/catalyst/`**, named by issue number and date — issue 32 resolves at `https://www.ayrs.org/catalyst/Catalyst_N32_Oct_2008.pdf`. The **issue number, date, article title, author, and printed page number** together are the complete identifier; the journal's own printed pagination is the authority, not any position within the file.

Cite in the form:

> [Catalyst](/references/ayrs-catalyst.md) (AYRS), no. 32 (October 2008), "[Article title]," [author] (pp. N–N) — [detail].
