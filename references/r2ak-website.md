# Race to Alaska official race record (r2ak.com)

The **Race to Alaska** (`https://r2ak.com`) is the roughly 750-mile engine-free race from Port Townsend, Washington to Ketchikan, Alaska, run by Northwest Maritime; **r2ak.com is the race's own publishing platform**, and with the race's `@racetoalaska` Instagram account it forms a single official record of who entered, on what, and how they finished. For the [Paradox](/paradox.md) it matters for one reason: it records a Paradox **entered in the race**, [Jolyn Barley](/people/jolyn-barley.md)'s Team Forget Me Knot in 2026, and what became of that entry.[1][4]

It is an **organisational record, not an authored account**. Nobody at the race writes about the Paradox as a design; the value is registry-grade fact — name, skipper, boat, length, propulsion, hometown, result — published by the body that verified it.

## What the record establishes

Its strength is that it is **the registrar speaking**. A team page is filled from the entrant's own registration, so "Race vessel: Paradox … LOA: 13′ … Human propulsion: Hobie Mirage Drive and Yuloh" is the sailor's own declaration of what was going north, recorded by the race before the fact rather than reconstructed after it.[1] The printed **team cheat sheet** issued at the start carries the same entry checked into a numbered fleet list, with the design's true length — 13′ 10″ — rather than the team page's rounded 13′.[3] And the **stage results** are the sole record of outcome: the race publishes them as two Google Sheets, and a boat's absence from them is as informative as its presence.[4]

It has real limits too. The record is **thin by design** — a team page is seven fields and, in some years, a two-line joke of a bio; nothing there describes how a boat was built, sailed, or modified.[5] It **spells its own teams inconsistently**: the 2026 entry is "Forget Me Knot" on the team page and in the race's Instagram, and "Forget Me Not" on the printed cheat sheet and in the results sheet — one entry, two spellings, matched by skipper, boat, length and hometown.[1][3][4] The results record **outcomes without reasons**: a team is Finished, DNF, or DNS, and the sheet never says why.[4] And the race's Instagram, while it is the race's own voice, is a **promotional** channel — its fleet-statistics posts are written for effect ("What's a Paradox? Google it"), so the numbers in them can be cited and the framing cannot.[2]

## Provenance and dating

Team pages are dated WordPress posts under a per-year category, so each entry carries a published and modified date — the 2026 Paradox entry was published **9 April 2026**, the same sailor's 2024 entry **10 April 2024**.[1][5] The cheat sheet is a PDF reissued in the days before the start.[3] The two results sheets are published Google Sheets embedded in the results page and are updated as boats finish; they are read here as at **4 August 2026**, a month after the 2026 race closed.[4] The Instagram posts carry no visible timestamp; theirs are recovered from the post shortcode and corroborated against the reshare of the same content into the [Paradox Sailboat Facebook group](/references/paradox-facebook-group.md) the following day.[2]

## What it adds to the bundle

- **The 2026 entry itself** — the first Paradox on record entered in the R2AK, listed as the smallest boat in a fleet of about seventy, and rigged with **both** a Hobie Mirage Drive and a [yuloh](/components/yuloh.md), the only real-world instance on record of a pedal drive fitted to the design.[1][2][3]
- **The result** — Stage One records the entry as **DNS**, did not start, and it appears nowhere in the Stage Two results.[4]
- **The attempt before it** — the same sailor's 2024 entry on a Hobie Tandem Island and the sailor's own account of withdrawing from it, which is what the Paradox entry was a return from.[5][6] The leaks and flooding described in that withdrawal happened to the **Hobie**, not to a Paradox.[6]

The person is drawn out at [Jolyn Barley](/people/jolyn-barley.md), and the race and both entries at [A Paradox and the Race to Alaska](/merits/race-to-alaska.md).

## Citing this source

The record resolves at fixed addresses — team pages at `https://r2ak.com/<year>-teams-full-race/<team-slug>/`, results at `https://r2ak.com/<year>-race-results/`, Instagram posts at `https://www.instagram.com/p/<shortcode>/`. Cite in the form:

> [Race to Alaska official race record](/references/r2ak-website.md) — [which record], [date] — [detail].

naming the channel (team page, cheat sheet, results sheet, Instagram post) so the weight of the citation is legible. As a dated snapshot of a race record, its content is cited in the past tense and dated to the record it comes from, consistent with the bundle's historical-archive convention.
