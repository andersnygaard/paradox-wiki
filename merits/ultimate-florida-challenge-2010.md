# The 2010 Ultimate Florida Challenge, followed from the list

The **1,200-mile WaterTribe Ultimate Florida Challenge** of March 2010 — a circumnavigation of Florida, engine-free, including a roughly forty-mile overland portage between the St. Marys and Suwannee rivers — is the most closely followed single event in the whole archive. Matt Layden raced it in his newest and smallest design, the 9-foot **[Elusion](/matt-layden/elusion.md)**, under the WaterTribe name **Wizard**, and won his class; at one point he led Class 4 (Small Sailboats) outright.[13]

What makes the record worth a page of its own is that the list did not merely report the result. Correspondents followed the SPOT tracker hour by hour for eight days, one of them had inspected the boat in person the evening before the start, another had raced the same season himself, and a third was in the race, chasing Matt in Matt's own old Enigma. The result is a contemporaneous, outside account of how a Layden micro-cruiser actually performs under sustained race pressure — speeds, tactics, and the boat's limits — set against two purpose-built paddling craft. Elusion's own design, particulars, and place in the Layden lineage are at [matt-layden/elusion.md](/matt-layden/elusion.md); the design's wider racing record is at [merits/everglades-challenge.md](/merits/everglades-challenge.md).

The convention that gave Matt the name "Wizard" also gave [Dave Bolduc](/people/bolduc/dave.md) the name "Freebyrd," under which he raced Matt's old [Enigma](/matt-layden/enigma.md) in the Everglades Challenge.[13]

## A dockside inspection on the eve of the start

A visit to the boat on the Thursday evening before the start, kept to about ten minutes so as not to intrude, gives the archive its only firsthand physical description of Elusion. The hull was finished to a high standard — assumed to be 6 mm marine plywood glassed on the exterior — with construction and testing still being completed close to race day. Matt was weighing how much lead-shot ballast to add on top of the gear already stowed, "a fine line between too much and too little in such a little skinny boat." Two pipes protruding from the rear of the cabin were larger than expected and appeared to be tied into a mainsheet drainage channel running beneath the deck.[6] Matt's own updates during the race reported the vent system working very well.[5]

## Underway: the first days, and the spray-skirt question

Once the challenge was under way, Matt kept ahead of two experienced kayak paddlers while running inside the Intracoastal Waterway, where the sheltered course cost him wind; progress at that stage was relayed through tracker updates thought to have come from his wife.[1] Watching from outside, one remark captured just how compact the boat is next to her larger sister: "Enigma looks big compared to Illusion and you don't climb into Illusion as much as pull it on."[1]

For the days that followed, a good, sometimes strong, west wind was expected, with the course kept close to the lee side of the land and its foliage to avoid having the wind blanketed — a strategy the trailing kayakers were expected to share unless the wind swung behind them; strong gusts funnelling through gaps in the foliage could knock the boat down, though she was judged tender but inherently safe.[2] Once under way, Matt was observed to favor sailing close along the beach rather than standing further out to sea, and faced strong, fluky forecast wind for the days ahead — gusting hard one moment and dropping the next where it funnelled over the trees along the shore.[7]

Whether the cabin's kayak-style opening closed with a spray skirt for paddling, already floated as a guess from the pre-race photographs, remained unconfirmed once the race was under way: nobody had put the question to Matt directly, a further photograph captioned simply as the boat "with her lid on" again only supported the same guess rather than settling it, and no fitting for bracing to scull could be seen aboard either, leaving the paddling arrangement itself "still a mystery."[3][4][5] The race remained a close contest between three leaders as of mid-March 2010, its lead narrowing rather than resolving outright over the days that followed.[5]

## The lead narrows, and further photographs (15–17 March 2010)

Matt's lead over the same two named rivals, **Whitecaps** and **Manitou Cruiser**, narrowed as the challenge wore on: tracker readings put him about 30 miles ahead on the morning of 15 March, a margin judged too slim once the course turned upriver, and down to about 10 miles by the evening of 16 March, with Matt resting for a spell while Whitecaps pushed on at over 4 knots.[8][9] The same tracker readings guessed the trailing pair were most likely Kruger-design sailing canoes fitted with a Balogh BOSS sailing rig rather than plain kayaks — a combination judged to struggle making progress upwind and so needing long hours of straight-ahead paddling, where Matt was judged able to tack upwind and paddle less.[10] One reading of Matt's pace at that point took it for a deliberate choice rather than any pressure to defend the lead — sailing "within his own personal plan and limits," with "a great deal of respect for the other challengers" — an inference drawn from the tracker rather than anything Matt said himself.[9]

Further comment on the same photographs wondered whether the vent system reflected an exchange of ideas with [Sven Yrvind](/people/yrvind.md), who is deeply invested in keeping a small boat's interior dry, and judged Elusion's light weight would make her easier to portage through river stretches choked with fallen trees — while hoping a spare set of wheels was aboard, the originals having seemed flimsy.[11] Separately, even a 20-knot wind offshore was judged enough to leave "those little boats... a hand full," reinforcing the case for keeping to the sheltered inside route rather than the open coast, with anticipation building for how Matt would actually be paddled when the time came — "what a grand experiment."[12] Further photographs also circulated during this stretch showing Matt and Elusion under way — sailing with his wife before the race, and arriving at and leaving the Flamingo checkpoint.[14]

Matt's narrowing lead held only a few hours longer: reacting to the 16 March update, one correspondent reckoned Matt had lost "about 40+ miles advantage since yesterday," adding a sentiment shared through the thread regardless of finishing order — "anyone that finished wins, in my book... two days or two minutes don't mean nothing. They are all heros to me."[15]

## Whitecaps and Manitou Cruiser take the lead (17–18 March 2010)

By mid-morning on 17 March, Whitecaps had moved about half a mile ahead of Wizard, with Manitou Cruiser still some six miles back; both rivals were making a knot or more faster than Wizard on their longer waterlines, and all three were staying inside the Intracoastal Waterway, expected to leave the Atlantic altogether by day's end if they kept up the pace — Manitou Cruiser's own crew were reported determined to "push on no matter what the tide says."[16] By mid-afternoon both Whitecaps (2.97 kt) and Manitou Cruiser (4.13 kt) had passed Wizard (1.77 kt) as the fleet threaded a narrow canal, dropping Matt to third place; the two leaders were thought to be paddling double-bladed against Matt's single blade.[17] By the next morning Matt remained roughly 10 miles behind in third, though he had returned to sailing rather than paddling and might yet close the gap, while Manitou Cruiser held the reported lead with her own tracker briefly gone quiet; a tracker message relayed as Wizard's own words that morning read, "Tide is with me for now, but not for long. I may stop for a nap, waiting for the tide to change," adding that he was warm and sailing from inside Elusion with the hatch closed.[18]

## Fort Clinch, Dave Bolduc's chase, and the spray-skirt question narrows

By the evening of 18 March, a closer look at the same dockside photograph that had earlier suggested a spray skirt found the material more likely sunbrella fabric, with no spray-skirt opening for paddling and no transparent panel for viewing the sail from below decks visible in it — narrowing, though not finally settling, the standing question of how the cabin closes off for paddling.[19]

That same evening, all three leaders converged at **Fort Clinch**, at the mouth of the St. Marys River, where the two leading boats rested while Wizard kept going, guessed to be a response to what had been a rough, upwind slog for all three.[20] [Dave Bolduc](/people/bolduc/dave.md), sailing Matt's old Enigma as Freebyrd, gave his own account of the race's early going: he closed to within about ninety minutes of Matt by the second checkpoint before falling quickly behind once Matt began paddling, and a volunteer stationed at the third checkpoint reported Wizard leaving the Flamingo checkpoint at such a pace that "it looked like he had a 2 hp outboard on the back of Elusion."[20] A fellow competitor's photograph settled at last how Matt was propelling the boat: a long, narrow, straight paddle likened to those used by old-time voyageurs in their big canoes, rather than a sculling oar or yuloh. Matt weighed about 140 lbs at the time.[20]

## An outside verdict on Matt's sailing

Watching Matt's pace throughout the race left at least one fellow racer with a firsthand verdict. Describing Elusion's snugness: "you put the boat on like waders or boots, you don't get in it," agreeing the boat "must come in sizes." Of Matt's own sailing: "Matt is the most efficient sailer I have ever seen. I could pass him but every time I started screwing around and not paying attention... I would look up and there he was... again."[21]

## A tracker-reported lead reversal, and the shift from sailing to paddling

A tracker-based report circulating late on the evening of 18 March suggested Wizard had moved back ahead of Whitecaps and Manitou Cruiser on the river, a reading followed within minutes by the same observer's own caution that the two rivals' tracker positions looked stale and might already have advanced further than the comparison showed, leaving the apparent reversal unconfirmed rather than settled.[22][23] The same message wondered whether Elusion's own lightly ballasted displacement might let her turn a favorable current to particular advantage, while acknowledging an adverse current could just as easily work against her.[22]

By this stage the fleet's progress depended on the St. Marys River's own current. After the leaders turned into the river they were paddling **upriver against the flow**, a stretch that would last until the roughly forty-mile portage carried them into the Suwannee River, from which point they would run downstream with the current, including a stretch of rated rapids; the same read judged Wizard had likely been able to sail as far upriver as the wind allowed but would now have to paddle the rest of the leg, his sail becoming more hindrance than help and needing to come down.[24]

## Comfort as an explanation for pace

A firsthand comparison drawn from sailing [Little Jim](/boats/little-jim.md), a similarly small, lightly ballasted Paradox, offered one account of how Wizard could sustain the pace on so little sleep: a boat that size asks for no restrictive wet-weather gear, wastes no energy keeping its sailor warm, and lets him go from dropping anchor to curled up in a sleeping bag within seconds, sparing him the discomfort of sitting exposed to wind and spray that a bigger boat's cockpit would impose.[25]

## Speed: a guess, firsthand observation, and the tracker record

Elusion's shortness invited skepticism about her pace: asked how her potential speed compared with Paradox or Enigma, one correspondent guessed a mere 9 ft hull could make no more than about 2 knots.[26] The race's own record told a different story.

A firsthand observer at the first leg's Saturday start judged Wizard could average about **4 knots or better** running downwind, and had separately seen readings as high as **2.4 knots paddling against the river current**, a pace judged impressive for paddling alone.[27] Watching Wizard hold his own for days against two boats built specifically for river paddling, all while paddling standing up himself, struck at least one follower as remarkable.[28] Wizard's own SPOT tracker, read over the race's first couple of days while the fleet ran the outside coastal route, showed consistent readings of **4.5 to 4.8 knots** with several bursts above 5 knots and one reading of **5.6 knots**, with further fast stretches recorded after Key Largo and across Biscayne Bay; how much of this owed to tidal current was left an open question, and video of Wizard paddling, alone and alongside the other two leaders, circulated alongside the tracker reports.[29] One correspondent reading those same bursts above 5 knots guessed they reflected surfing down the face of a wave rather than steady speed, recalling that comparable readings on his own race that same season had come in the approach to Venice Inlet — a stretch he found frightening enough that he stayed conscious throughout of how easily a slight steering error there could capsize a boat into cold water.[30]

## The 18 March reversal proves temporary, and the leaders reach the portage

The tracker-suggested reversal reported late on 18 March did not hold. By late morning on 20 March, Whitecaps had pulled about three-quarters of a mile ahead of Manitou Cruiser, while Wizard had fallen back substantially and was running roughly a knot slower than the pair on average; a fellow competitor, Crazy Russian, was by then nearing the mouth of the St. Marys River and — like another competitor, Pelican — was thought to be carrying a folding bicycle, which the two hoped would let them cover the forty-mile portage road in four to six hours rather than the better part of two days needed to walk a boat over by hand.[31]

The portage itself runs from **Traders Hill** on the St. Marys River, at Mile 770, where classes 4-6 may switch to a kayak or canoe for the upper river and the long overland crossing, to **Fargo, Georgia**, at Mile 840, where competitors re-enter the water on the Suwannee River for a faster run downstream with the current.[32] By 1 PM, Whitecaps was reported about five miles from the portage with Manitou Cruiser close behind, while Wizard, having picked up his own pace, remained some 20 to 25 miles further upstream; within the hour both leaders were confirmed starting the portage itself, with Crazy Russian by then about three miles from the river's mouth.[33][34] By 4:45 PM the two leaders were about four miles along the portage road together, Manitou Cruiser ahead of Whitecaps by roughly 500 yards, both making about 3 knots on foot.[35]

## Manitou Cruiser opens a clear lead, and a capsize near Fort Clinch (20–21 March 2010)

By midnight, Manitou Cruiser had opened a lead of at least ten miles over Whitecaps on the portage and intended to keep moving through the night at an average of about 2.8 knots, while Wizard remained behind both, still on the river; elsewhere in the fleet, Crazy Russian was well up the St. Marys and stopping to sleep, and Pelican was still out on the ocean approaching the river's mouth. One follow-on assessment, reading the tracker rather than anything confirmed on the ground, judged the race now Manitou Cruiser's to lose barring a misstep, while allowing that a long way still remained.[36]

Pelican's own crossing did not go smoothly: attempting Fort Clinch from the Atlantic side after dark, a wave caught the boat sideways and flipped it. Pelican was reported unhurt and safely ashore, with help on hand getting the boat off the sandbar, and planned to wait until morning to assess the damage.[37]

## The portage completed, and where the archive's record ends (21 March 2010)

By 03:45 that same morning, Manitou Cruiser had completed the roughly forty-mile portage altogether and re-entered the water at Fargo, Georgia, on the Suwannee River; Whitecaps was still about fifteen miles out on the portage road and going strong, Wizard was reported resting and still on the river behind both, and neither Crazy Russian's nor Pelican's tracker showed a current position.[38]

The archive's own account closes there, on 21 March 2010, with Manitou Cruiser through the portage and running the Suwannee, Whitecaps in pursuit, and Wizard still on the river in third — no finish, placing, or withdrawal reported on the list for any of the three leaders. That Matt Layden **won his class** is known from the designer's own reference site rather than from the list's day-by-day following of the race; see [matt-layden/elusion.md — racing history](/matt-layden/elusion.md#racing-history).
