# Apalachicola Bay, the Iowa messabout, a DCA week at Cobnor, and the Havasu Pocket Cruisers Convention

Four gathering points, of very different scale, round out the Paradox community's meetup record: a solo-scouted Apalachicola Bay, an unconnected Iowa messabout, a Dinghy Cruising Association week at Cobnor, and the much larger annual Havasu Pocket Cruisers Convention.

## Apalachicola Bay: a proposed meeting ground, scouted solo (2002)

**Apalachicola Bay**, with campgrounds on St. George Island, was an early proposed second gathering ground for Paradox owners — "a wonderful place to camp and sail," with each owner able to inspect and sail the other's boat and swap ideas, and open to other list members and to a more central location if one made better sense. Apalachicola is roughly an 8-hour drive from Memphis.[1]

Rather than wait for a scheduled group date, one owner made a solo scouting trip of the bay in February 2002, launching "[Zoë](/boats/zoe.md)" from Carrabelle Beach on the eastern side of St George Sound (about 30 miles long) and anchoring near St George Island State Park, with an open invitation for anyone in the area to meet up over the weekend — the first record of a Paradox owner actually acting on a proposed group cruise, even though it played out as an individual scouting trip with an open invitation rather than the originally proposed joint outing. Retrieval at low tide there proved uncertain enough to shape later trailer designs (see [trailering — ground clearance for Gulf Coast ramps](/operations/trailering.md#ground-clearance-for-gulf-coast-ramps)).[2]

## The Iowa messabout: a third, unconnected gathering point (proposed 2002)

A general small-boat-builder gathering, not Paradox-specific, was proposed for **Lake Okoboji, northwest Iowa**, for the summer of 2003 — a third, geographically distinct small-boat gathering point alongside Cedar Key and Apalachicola Bay, with no recorded connection to either.[3]

## A DCA week at Cobnor, Chichester Harbour (August 2007)

Two Paradoxes, "Faith" and a second boat, sailed and anchored together for a week at a **Dinghy Cruising Association** meeting at Cobnor, Chichester Harbour, alongside a fleet of DCA dinghies — matching the dinghy fleet's performance on a reach and doing marginally better to windward, and riding out a near-gale at anchor on good holding ground. "Faith"'s own day-by-day log of the same week, including a man-overboard incident, is at [boats/faith.md](/boats/faith.md#the-cobnor-week-a-dinghy-cruising-association-meeting-and-falling-overboard).[4][5]

## A proposed all-Paradox gathering (2010)

A newer builder, working through his own hypothetical future cruising plans — trailering to the Gulf coast and on to the Bahamas, or a longer-shot solo passage to Hawaii once the boat was finished — floated the idea of a large, dedicated Paradox gathering somewhere, hoping for 2010; a reply wondered, half in jest, whether he might already be in Hawaii by then. See [people/rodney-cannon.md — a distant cruising dream, and a proposed Paradox gathering](/people/rodney-cannon.md) for the fuller exchange.[6][7][8]

A practical wrinkle in staging any such gathering in the Bahamas surfaced the following month: getting builders from outside the mainland US ("the other side of the Lake") and their own boats to the location at all, absent sponsorship from a well-funded outside backer. One proposed workaround was a **time-share/accommodation-sharing scheme** — whoever had a finished boat ready by the gathering's date offering to share it, with camping or overnight accommodation arranged jointly, so a builder unable to bring a boat of his own could still fly in, sail, and pick up hands-on tuning insight from owners who had already sorted their boats out.[9]

## The Havasu Pocket Cruisers Convention (Lake Havasu City, Arizona)

An annual trailer-sailer gathering held each February at **Lake Havasu City, Arizona**, founded by the owner of the Paradox "[Scout](/boats/scout.md)" (see [Sean Mulligan](/people/sean-mulligan.md)), drew roughly **70 boats** in 2010. By August that year, with the 2011 edition (**14–21 February**) still six months off, **40 boats and crews from seven Western US states and Canada** had already signed up, toward a stated goal of **100 or more**.[10] By late October, three months out, registration had grown to **102 boats and crews from 14 US states and 2 countries**, averaging 535 miles of driving distance and spanning 39 different boat models — a Montgomery 17 the most common, at 17 boats.[11]

## Three UK Paradoxes together at Poole Harbour (July 2013)

A Dinghy Cruising Association rally brought all three of the UK's then-existing Paradoxes to **Poole Harbour** on the same July 2013 weekend — [Little Jim](/boats/little-jim.md), [Faith](/boats/faith.md) (by then owned by Jim Hall), and [JoHanna](/boats/johanna.md) — reported at the time as probably the first time three Paradoxes had ever gathered together in one place, sailed in sunshine and a light wind.[12][13]

## Sail Oklahoma 2014: a joint builder/owner program on "Scout"

**Sail Oklahoma**, an annual messabout organised by **Jackie and Mike Monies**, became in **October 2014** the one event on record where a Paradox's builder and her sailor presented her together. Announcing it that August, the organisers said **[Sean Mulligan](/people/sean-mulligan.md) and [Lezlie Henson](/people/lezlie-henson.md) would give a joint program** on "[Scout](/boats/scout.md)" — her build, her finishing, and her sailing — across the **8–13 October** event: "We are excited to have the two of them together and this will be a great program."[14] Henson had built the hull as "Tardis" and sold her almost complete without ever sailing her; Mulligan finished her, renamed her, and by then had a full season and a [Texas 200](/merits/texas-200.md) behind him, so the two halves of the boat's story had never before been told from one platform.

The same event put a second Layden design and the designer himself in the same place: **[Chris Curtis](/people/chris-curtis.md)** — who would buy "Scout" from Mulligan the following March — showed his **Enigma 460** at Sail Oklahoma 2014, where **[Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md)** inspected her in depth and "quietly gave me the OK, saying it was well built."[15]
