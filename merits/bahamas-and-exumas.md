# The Bolducs' Bahamas cruising, and the 2013 engine-free crossing

David and Mindy Bolduc's cruising log is the archive's longest continuous record of what a Matt Layden micro-cruiser is actually used for: seven winter voyages to the Bahamas aboard [Little Cruiser](/matt-layden/little-cruiser.md), each of roughly eight hundred to a thousand miles over two and a half months, and a final solo crossing made in an eleven-foot boat with no outboard aboard at all.[1][2][3][4]

## The Bolducs: decades of Bahamas cruising, and a 2013 engine-free solo crossing

David and Mindy Bolduc's cruising log — kept mostly aboard "Little Cruiser," a sister design to Paradox — documents a **1993** first Bahamas trip (Bimini–Chub Cay–Cat Cay); a **1994** joint Bahamas cruise with [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md) sailing the [original Paradox](/boats/original-paradox.md) (Bimini–Chub–Nassau–Exumas–Long Island–Conception–Rum Cay–Spanish Wells–Eleuthera), during which the party met a family living aboard the 32-foot sharpie "Hogfish Lips" (see [Chris Morejohn](/people/chris-morejohn.md)); further Bahamas cruises in **1995** (an Abaco circumnavigation), **1996**, **1998**, and **2001** (the 2001 trip departing Key Largo, crossing the Gulf Stream, and taking in the Exumas, Cat Island, Georgetown, and Andros); a **2003** circumnavigation of Andros; and a **2006** three-week cruise of North Carolina's Outer Banks. Each Bahamas cruise averaged roughly 800 miles sailed over about 2.5 months. The last of these, in **2013**, was sailed solo in "Enigma" without an outboard at all — "a voyage for the purist with sail and oar only," and on the site's own account "the smallest sailboat to have ever cruised the islands" — the log-keeper's seventh trip to the islands overall (six of the prior trips had been two-up). The 2013 passage's engine-free technique is covered at [operations/sculling-and-yuloh-technique.md](/operations/sculling-and-yuloh-technique.md#a-fully-engine-free-solo-passage-2013); the same log's record of the community's [Cedar Key](/merits/cedar-key.md#the-bolducs-cruising-log-cedar-key-attendance-across-the-years) attendance is covered on that page.[1][2][3][4]

A 2006 interview adds the cadence and the cost from the couple's own memory. Their Bahamas trips came in **four consecutive years from 1993**, then at widening intervals — two years, then three, then three again — matching the 1993/1994/1995/1996 and 1998/2001/2003 pattern in the written log above. Each trip ran roughly **a thousand miles**, seven thousand miles at least across seven trips, and each began by **towing the boat from North Carolina to Key Largo** rather than sailing south, on the reasoning that limited leave is better spent in the pretty part than on the delivery. The cruising itself cost about **$600 a month**, and the couple's settled finding is that they **spend less money cruising than living at home** — utilities turned down, and nothing much to spend money on afloat.[5]

## The seven voyages, narrated

A retrospective magazine account by the same author fills the log's year-by-year skeleton with destinations, and confirms the seven-trip tally from the other side.[6]

The **1993** trip began by towing the boat **600 miles to Cedar Key, Florida** in January to join [Matt Layden](/matt-layden/biography.md) for a fortnight's sailing along the shallow coast — where the lesson was watching Matt read the water, "interpreting the subtle changes in the water's color and little disturbances on its surface to gauge what hid below." A week followed in the **10,000 Islands National Park** out of Everglades City, navigating a labyrinth of mangrove channels that would have been hopeless without GPS. Only then did they tow south to Miami and cross, reaching a protected anchorage off **Cat Cay** just after sunset, clearing customs at North Cat Cay next morning, riding the current twelve miles to **North Bimini** — where the boat "looked like a dinghy compared to the 40-foot yachts around us" — and finally sailing the **75 miles across the Grand Bahama Banks**, beating a cold front into the anchorage at **Mama Rhoda channel** and Chub Cay.[6]

The **1994** cruise is the joint one with Matt sailing the [original Paradox](/boats/original-paradox.md), taken after the couple deliberately **left their jobs** at the end of the year for a three-month absence, with no guarantee of being able to return to them.[6]

**1995** took in more of the Berry Islands and the Abacos: starting at **Sandy Point** on Great Abaco, up through the barren Bight, out through the narrow man-made cut in Little Abaco known as **"the haulover,"** then the well-travelled route from Allans-Pensacola down to **Green Turtle Cay, Man of War Cay and Hope Town**. It was a frigid winter, and it taught them why those places are better visited in the warmer months. **1996** returned to the Exumas. **1998** was deliberately sailed in **spring rather than winter**, to catch the **Family Island Regatta** at Georgetown and find the popular anchorages empty and the weather settled. **2001** brought Little San Salvador, the lee of Cat Island, the "wintering fleet" at Georgetown, and a **first visit to Andros**, whose blue holes they resolved to come back to. **2003** was **Andros entire** — a circumnavigation including the seldom-visited west coast and the three great bights that bisect the island.[6]

Between them, these cruises taught the skills the archive records elsewhere as technique: sailing in high winds and big seas far from shelter, and **perfecting the yuloh after a broken outboard left them stranded mid-trip** — see [sculling and yuloh technique](/operations/sculling-and-yuloh-technique.md).[6]

## The 2013 solo cruise, leg by leg

The same account gives the fullest published itinerary of the engine-free solo *Enigma* cruise. Leaving **Key Largo in March 2013** over calm seas, he dodged shipping for **17 hours** to North Bimini, waited 24 hours at anchor, then set out for the Grand Bahama Bank on the guess that a stalled cold front would hold — and was **caught by it 30 miles out from the Berry Islands**, riding out squalls and near lightning strikes before making Mama Rhoda Cut by nightfall. Next came the New Providence Channel to **Nassau** to replenish his water, and on to **Allans Cay** in what proved his **fastest passage yet to the Exumas**.[6]

From there the pace slackened deliberately: Easter at **Shroud Cay**, a slow southeasterly beat down-island with time spent at anchorages earlier trips had sped past, Georgetown (skipping the regatta), back to **Staniel Cay**, then east to the uninhabited **Green Cay** and into **South Bight**, Andros. From Lisbon Creek he **sailed and sculled the whole bight through to the unspoiled west coast**, past schools of game fish and bales of sea turtles, staying until the trade winds faded and biting flies drove him back east. **Moxey Town** at the mouth of Middle Bight gave him local spongers to watch; **Fresh Creek** gave him provisions and email. He then worked up inside the barrier reef to **Morgan's Bluff**, waited a week there with other boats for a weather window, and crossed with them — a **140-mile passage** across the Bank broken by a few hours' rest at South Cat Cay for want of a second helmsman, then the Gulf Stream, where the wind died **12 miles from land** and he **sculled the last hours into Fort Lauderdale**. Six weeks, and fifteen pounds lighter.[6]
