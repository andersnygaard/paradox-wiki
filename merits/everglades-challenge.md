# The Everglades Challenge, and the wider Layden racing record

A Paradox hull won the WaterTribe Everglades Challenge outright in 2003, and Matt Layden went on to race three further, lighter WaterTribe-tested designs of his own in the years that followed.

## The 2003 Everglades Challenge: Matt Layden's outright win

Matt Layden entered the original Paradox in the 2003 Everglades Challenge and finished first overall — ahead of the entire paddled field of kayaks and canoes, racing solo on sail and yuloh power alone, sleeping while underway rather than making camp, and pausing mid-race to rescue a capsized kayaker. The result was widely read at the time as proof that a heavy, engine-free cruising monohull could out-race light paddled and multihull competition. Full checkpoint-by-checkpoint record, the official results table, and the discussion the win prompted are at [boats/original-paradox.md](/boats/original-paradox.md#the-finish-checkpoint-4-and-the-win).[1][2][3]

### The 2008 print account: elapsed time, margin, and field size

A published account five years later, written by a designer who knew Matt Layden personally and cited the result as evidence "that Matt's boats work," puts three figures on the win that the contemporaneous list traffic does not: an elapsed time of **3 days 9 hours 51 minutes**, a winning margin of **16 hours 6 minutes over the second boat**, and a field of **thirty starters**.[4]

The **margin and the field size are new** to the record here — the mailing-list account measures the lead at Checkpoint 3 (9 hours 20 minutes over Team Weed Warrior), not at the finish. The **elapsed time is one hour short** of what the official start and finish times on record imply: a 07:00 start on 8 March and a 17:51 finish on 11 March give **3 days 10 hours 51 minutes**.[2] The discrepancy is a single hour in an otherwise exactly-matching figure — the minutes agree — so it reads as an arithmetic or timezone slip in the retelling rather than a competing account, and **the official times are treated as controlling**.

### How the win read to the rest of the fleet

The 2003 result is corroborated from outside the design's own community, and the terms in which competitors put it are worth recording. Listing the boats that had won the Everglades Challenge up to 2007, a WaterTribe racer names them as **"a Laser, a Paradox mini-cruiser, a Texas Water Safari canoe (in terrible weather with below freezing temperatures in the 'Glades and winds over 40 knots at times), a schooner rigged canoe, a NorseBoat, and the Core Sound 17"** — a Paradox second in a list otherwise made up of racing dinghies, purpose-built race canoes, and a professional designer's own boats.[5]

The same competitor's summary of what Matt Layden brought to the race treats the boat as secondary to the sailor. Beside the 2003 Paradox win, he records that Layden **"once showed up for a challenge with a blowup boat from West Marine and a sheet sail and left everyone behind"** — a story unrecorded anywhere else in the archive, and consistent with the second-place finish in a 10-foot inflatable kayak already on record below. His considered comparison is unusually specific about where the Layden approach does *not* win: **"Downwind or on a reach in a strong wind he can't stay with Graham's designs, but weather plays a role"** — the Layden advantage being an ability to keep moving, under yuloh at 3–4 knots hour after hour, when everyone else is becalmed or resting.[5] See [sculling and yuloh technique](/operations/sculling-and-yuloh-technique.md#a-yuloh-against-oars-comparison-from-the-boat-that-lost-it).

## Beyond Paradox: the wider Layden racing record

Matt Layden went on to design three lighter, cartoppable micro-cruisers after Paradox, and raced each of them in WaterTribe events the same way he had field-tested Paradox. They are not Paradox hulls, and their own design and build history lives under [matt-layden/](/matt-layden/index.md); this is a results summary only, given here as context for the racing tradition the 2003 win began.

- **Enigma** (built 2005) won her class and placed third overall in the 1,200-mile 2006 Ultimate Florida Challenge, breaking Matt Layden's own 2003 solo record along the way. Sold in 2008, her new owners completed three further WaterTribe races in her — a **2009 NC Challenge class win** (sculled roughly 11 of the miles), a **2010 Everglades Challenge class win**, whose hardest test was sculling through Gasparilla Pass at night against a foul tide and 5-foot breaking seas, and a **2010 NC Challenge** in which she was sculled 14 miles/7 hours on the first day alone. Full racing record at [matt-layden/enigma.md](/matt-layden/enigma.md#racing-history-and-change-of-ownership); the post-2008 seasons are also cited as technique evidence at [operations/sculling-and-yuloh-technique.md](/operations/sculling-and-yuloh-technique.md#watertribe-evidence-a-micro-cruiser-can-be-cruised-motor-free-under-race-pressure) — see that page for what the results demonstrate about engine-free propulsion, not repeated here.[6][7][8][9]
- **Sand Flea** (an 8-foot cruising dinghy, built after Enigma) raced two 300-mile Everglades Challenges (2007, 2008) and the 2012 Ultra Marathon, "finishing near the top as usual" each time; in 2007 Matt Layden sailed her solo, paused to rescue a capsized kayaker, and still finished less than half a day behind a crewed boat. Full record at [matt-layden/sand-flea.md](/matt-layden/sand-flea.md#race-record).[10][11]
- **Elusion** (2010) won her class in the 1,200-mile Ultimate Florida Challenge, at one point leading Class 4 (Small Sailboats) outright. Full record at [matt-layden/elusion.md](/matt-layden/elusion.md#racing-history).[12]
- Matt Layden also raced non-Layden-design **kayaks and canoes** in WaterTribe events in other years — a second-place finish in a 10-foot inflatable kayak (2003 Michigan Challenge), and class wins in a self-built decked canoe (2005, 2009). Summarized at [matt-layden/biography.md](/matt-layden/biography.md#the-watertribe-years-enigma-sand-flea-and-elusion).[13][14]

## An owner who trained for it, and did not enter

No Paradox is recorded as having started an Everglades Challenge after Matt Layden's own 2003 win, but one owner spent two years deciding whether to try. **[Sean Mulligan](/people/sean-mulligan.md)**, sailing "[Scout](/boats/scout.md)" on Lake Havasu, said plainly in **November 2013** that he was "positive that Scout is up to the task of the Everglades Challenge" — Layden having "proved that handily" — while doubting he was himself "a quarter the sailor he is," and confirmed that the progressively longer engine-less sails he was making in her were partly a way of judging whether to attempt it.[15] The [Texas 200](/merits/texas-200.md) he sailed in her in June 2014 — 200 miles in five days, in winds to 34 knots apparent — was run explicitly as that trial, and he judged afterwards that it had gone well enough to leave a **2016 attempt** a real possibility.[16] The archive carries no record of a start.

This is worth setting beside the design's reputation: the boat's fitness for the event was never the doubt, in her owner's own framing — the question he put to himself was whether the *sailor* was ready, which is the same distinction the 2003 win's readers drew at the time (above).
